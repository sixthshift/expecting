---
title: Running warm
read: [feeling-hot-and-overheating, hot-weather]
---
Your metabolism speeds up to support the baby and raises your body temperature. When you're hot, more blood goes to your skin to cool you, and less may reach the placenta.

What helps on hot days is staying out of the sun, in air conditioning in the middle of the day if you can, and sipping water regularly even when you're not thirsty; dark wee is a sign of dehydration. Exercise suits the early morning or later in the day.

Heat exhaustion looks like heavy sweating, great thirst, headache, cramps, dizziness and pale, clammy skin: lying down somewhere cool, loosening your clothes and sipping water help, and see a doctor if it doesn't improve. See a doctor or urgent care clinic for dizziness, nausea, a fast pulse or shortness of breath. Heatstroke can be life-threatening: call 000 for hot, dry skin with no sweating, a temperature over 40 °C, vomiting, confusion or slurred speech, or collapse or seizures.
