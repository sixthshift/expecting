---
title: What a doula does
read: [doulas]
---
As you work out who'll be at the birth, a doula is one option: a non-medical companion who supports you and your family before, during and after it. Before, they give you information and help with a birth plan. In labour they reassure you, keep you informed, help with positions, the shower or bath, massage and breathing, and support your partner. Some visit after, helping with meals, older children, feeding and settling.

A doula doesn't replace a birth support partner; they work with them. Nor are they a midwife: they have no medical training, and giving medical advice isn't part of their role.

They charge a private fee, and what Medicare or your health insurer covers is worth checking. The industry is unregulated and no qualification is required, though many complete training, so the first question to ask one is what training or experience they've had.
