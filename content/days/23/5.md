---
title: Building your village
read: [building-your-village]
---
Many parents live away from their families and don't have a close network of friends, so there's less help when it's most needed. That's why the advice is to build a support network on purpose, as part of planning for the baby, and asking for help doesn't mean you're not coping.

The list is one made before the baby comes: people who are actually available and can listen without judging, a backup for when they can't come, and outside support services. Friends who can drop in, family on the phone or video, other expectant parents, your midwife or GP can all be on it.

People often want to help but don't know how, and it helps to have the words: a meal that reheats easily, some shopping or cleaning, taking an older child to the park, checking before visiting, a message that doesn't need a reply, or just listening.
