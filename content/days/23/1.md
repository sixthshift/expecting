---
title: Wee, or the waters?
read: [fluid-leaking]
---
Waters can be hard to tell from wee or discharge. When they break, the fluid may gush or trickle, or your underwear may just feel damp. It's usually clear, though it can be yellow, straw-coloured or pink, and it smells different to wee. Your midwife can test it.

Before 37 weeks, waters breaking brings a risk of infection and an early birth. Any clear fluid leaking from your vagina means contacting your doctor or midwife immediately, with a pad on to collect it; you'll need to be examined straight away. Call them or go to hospital immediately if the fluid is green, brown or blood-coloured, or smells bad, or with bleeding, a temperature of 37.5 °C or more or feeling unwell, a change in the baby's movements, or regular painful contractions or belly pain.

If you feel something in your vagina after your waters break, call 000 for an ambulance: the cord may have slipped down.
