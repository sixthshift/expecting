title: The waters, at their fullest

The amount of amniotic fluid rises through pregnancy until 34 weeks: there's close to the most of it now. Healthy amounts range widely, from 500 to 2000 mL. Early on your body made it; now the baby makes most of it themselves, from their urine and fluid from their lungs, and has been swallowing it and passing it back out since around week 12.

It isn't just padding. It helps the baby move around, helps their lungs, digestive system and muscles develop, keeps the temperature around them normal, and cushions them from pressure or injury.

It's checked on ultrasound, often at routine scans, by looking for pockets of fluid around the baby, and given in centimetres as the amniotic fluid index (AFI). Too much or too little can sometimes be a sign of a complication, and your doctor or midwife will explain what yours means.
