---
title: The CTG
read: [ctg-monitoring, term-fetal-distress]
---
If you have an extra check for the baby, it may involve a CTG, a cardiotocograph. It records the baby's heart rate and your contractions continuously, through 2 round sensors held on your tummy by soft belts, and it's sometimes used to check the baby's health in pregnancy. Your doctor or midwife looks at whether the heart rate is too fast, too slow, or responding as expected. Your consent is always needed.

It's also how fetal distress is spotted: changes in the baby's heart rate suggesting they're unwell, usually because they aren't getting enough oxygen through the placenta. The team may then give you fluids, have you change position or slow the contractions, and sometimes the baby needs to be born quickly.

If your waters break and they're green or brown, tell your midwife or doctor straight away.
