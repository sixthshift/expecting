---
title: What premature means from here

A baby born before 37 weeks is premature, and in Australia about 8 in 100 are born early. The line that matters most is 32 weeks. Before it, babies are very premature: many need help breathing and are usually cared for in a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) at first. Babies born between 32 and 37 weeks may need care in a special care nursery, and most don't have any serious long-term problems.

Each day in the uterus is likely to mean better health. If labour starts early, it may be possible to slow or stop it, and you may be offered injections to lower the baby's risk of breathing difficulties.

Before 37 weeks, contact your midwife, doctor or birthing centre immediately with any sign of labour: contractions, your waters breaking, mucus, blood or fluid leaking, pressure in your pelvis, lower tummy cramps or lower back pain. If your waters break, contact them and go to the hospital.
