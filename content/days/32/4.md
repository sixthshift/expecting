---
title: Painting the nursery
read: [painting-and-renovating, cleaning-products]
---
If the room needs painting, the risk of fumes from modern household paint harming the baby is low. The bigger risks are solvent-based paints and old paintwork: many Australian houses painted before 1971 used lead paint, there's no safe level of lead, and it can cross the placenta. Stripping or sanding old paint is the work to avoid; old paint can be tested, and removed by a professional if needed.

The safer way to paint: water-based paint, windows and doors open, long sleeves, gloves, a mask and goggles, and no eating or drinking in the room. If the fumes worry you, the job can go to someone else.

Many cleaning products are safe in pregnancy, with the label checked, gloves and a mask on and fresh air; chemical jobs can be passed on too. Being exposed once is very unlikely to cause harm; if you're worried about contact with a chemical, the Poisons Information Hotline is 13 11 26.
