---
title: The birth support partner
read: [birth-support-person, card-support-person-helps]
---
If the partner will be the birth support, it matters more than it might seem: the right support in labour can shorten it, reduce how much pain relief is needed, lower the chance of an assisted birth or a caesarean, and make the whole experience more positive. The birth support partner is there to help, not simply to watch.

Before the day, that means learning what to expect, through antenatal classes, reading about labour and touring the birth unit; talking the birth plan through; and knowing how and when you'd like them to speak to the staff for you. How many support people the hospital or birth centre allows is worth knowing too.

And if the partner doesn't think they can do it, saying so beforehand is better: you might choose someone else, or want more than one support person. A backup is a good idea anyway, in case they're unwell or away.
