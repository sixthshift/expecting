---
title: If the iron comes back low
read: [anaemia-in-pregnancy, iron]
---
The full blood count around 28 weeks checks your haemoglobin, which carries oxygen to you and the baby. When it's low, low iron is the main cause: your body needs extra iron in the second and third trimesters. Anaemia can leave you weak, tired or dizzy, and make it harder to cope with blood loss at the birth.

If your result shows iron deficiency, your doctor or midwife will likely recommend a supplement, and the advice is changing: a draft national recommendation is to offer iron to everyone who is pregnant, not only those who are low. The dose matters, since more than recommended can cause iron poisoning. Vitamin C may be suggested with it. Iron can darken your poo and cause constipation or nausea; a different form or dose may help. If tablets don't work, an iron infusion is an option.

Chest pain or severe trouble breathing: call 000.
