---
title: An itch with no rash
read: [itchy-palms-and-soles, itchy-skin]
---
As the skin over your belly stretches it can feel itchy, and itching is common in pregnancy. A cool shower, loose cotton clothing and a moisturiser can help; check with your doctor before using any treatment for it.

An itch without a rash is different. It can be the first sign of cholestasis of pregnancy, a liver condition. The itch often starts on the palms and soles and can spread. It's rare, usually starts in the third trimester, and can be dangerous for the baby. It's found with blood tests, usually treated with medicine and extra monitoring, and goes away after the birth.

See your doctor or midwife if you're itchy, especially without a rash, or it won't go. After 20 weeks, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if the itch is extreme, especially on your hands and feet. With cholestasis, if the baby is moving less, contact your midwife or doctor immediately; don't wait until the next day.
