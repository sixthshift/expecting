---
title: Three vaccines, one visit
read: [rsv-vaccine, vaccines-in-one-visit]
---
RSV is a common virus that can cause bronchiolitis and pneumonia in babies, most likely to be severe in the first 6 months, often needing a hospital stay. A single dose of the maternal RSV vaccine is recommended from 28 weeks in each pregnancy, and it's free. Antibodies pass through the placenta, so the baby is protected from birth. It works best if the baby is born at least 2 weeks after it; if not, the baby is recommended an infant RSV antibody.

The pregnancy vaccines' windows overlap: flu at any stage, whooping cough from 20 to 32 weeks, and RSV from 28 to 36 weeks. So between now and 32 weeks you can have all three. The Department of Health says they can safely be given in the same visit, each in a different site. All three are free through the National Immunisation Program while you're pregnant.
