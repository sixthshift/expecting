---
title: Eating more variety, not more
read: [eating-well-in-pregnancy]
---
The Royal Women's Hospital puts it simply: you don't have to eat more, but you do have to eat more variety. Pregnancy asks more of certain nutrients, including iron, calcium, iodine and many vitamins.

Iodine is key to the baby's brain growth: fish one to three times a week, limiting the high-mercury kinds, and iodised salt. Iron makes red blood cells, and you need more because your blood volume rises: at least two serves a day of meat, chicken, fish, legumes or nuts. Calcium, for bones, comes from 2 to 3 serves of dairy or calcium-fortified milk.

Omega-3, for the baby's brain, nerves and eyes, comes from fish 2 to 3 times a week, and from walnuts, chia, linseed and soybeans.

Water and milk are the safest drinks, and about 2.3 L of water a day is the aim: you hold more fluid in pregnancy, and it matters for the baby and the amniotic fluid.
