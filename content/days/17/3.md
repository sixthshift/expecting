---
title: How hard to push
read: [exercise-intensity-and-heart-rate]
---
Moderate is the aim, and the test is simple: during moderate activity you can still hold a conversation; during vigorous activity you'd find it hard.

The reason is your heart. It already works harder in pregnancy, enlarging and pumping faster, and your resting heart rate goes up, which leaves a smaller range between resting and a safe maximum. Hard exercise also heats you up, and your core temperature can rise to a level that isn't safe for the baby.

So regular moderate exercise is better than occasional intense sessions, and hot weather, especially humid, is the time to avoid it. For athletes, or anyone already doing more than the recommended amount, the advice is guidance or supervision from a qualified health professional. Vigorous exercise is possible for some, but MotherSafe says to weigh your fitness before pregnancy and check with your doctor or midwife first.
