---
title: The partner's job in the room

For the partner: in labour you're there to help, not just to watch, and the right support person can lower the need for pain relief and the chance of an assisted birth or a caesarean. Most of it is practical: staying close, timing contractions, offering drinks, snacks and ice, wiping a face, holding a hand, helping into positions or the shower, and keeping the room calm, even if that means leaving it when you're asked to.

Somewhere in labour, the pregnant parent may not be able to say what they want. Then it's you who tells the maternity team their needs and wishes, brings the team's advice back, and helps them decide. A birth plan written together makes that easier, and so does knowing beforehand how and when they'd like you to speak up for them.

It can be exhausting: your own snacks and drinks, and breaks when you can take them, are part of the job too.
