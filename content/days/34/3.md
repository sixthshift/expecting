title: Hands, heat and a TENS

Massage and touch can distract from labour pain and ease tension, but one that felt good a minute ago can suddenly become annoying. Practising together in pregnancy is how the partner learns what the pregnant parent likes, knowing it may change on the day. Heat helps too, especially with backache. Moving freely, rocking the pelvis and a birth ball are among the most helpful things for pain.

A TENS machine is another option: pads on the lower back send small electrical pulses that feel like tingling. There's no harm in it and many find it helpful, though it doesn't work for everyone. It can't be used in a bath or shower, or with a pacemaker, a cochlear implant, epilepsy or spinal rods. Not every hospital or birthing centre has one, and your doctor or midwife can say whether it's safe for you.
