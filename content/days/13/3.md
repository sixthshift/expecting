---
title: Fish oil, and who it's for
read: [fish-oil]
---
Omega-3 fats, DHA and EPA, support the baby's brain, nerve and eye development, and your body can't make them. Fatty fish such as sardines, salmon, mackerel, herring and trout are the best source; flaxseed and canola oil have some omega-3, but not DHA or EPA.

Enough omega-3 is linked with fewer premature births when you're carrying one baby, so a supplement is likely to lower that risk if your diet is short of it. If your diet already has enough, a supplement has no demonstrated benefit, and some data suggest too much omega-3 may increase the risk of a premature birth.

Where one is needed, the recommended dose is 1000 mg a day in total, from before 20 weeks, so this is the stretch when it counts. Cod liver oil isn't the one: it also contains vitamin A, which should be avoided in pregnancy.
