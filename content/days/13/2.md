---
title: That stab in the groin
read: [round-ligament-pain, card-round-ligament-stab]

That sudden stab low in your belly when you stand, sneeze or laugh is round ligament pain. The round ligaments hold your uterus in place, and as it grows they stretch and sometimes spasm, giving a stabbing, aching or pulling pain near your hips and groin, more often on the right. It's felt most in the second trimester, it's common, and it isn't dangerous for you or the baby.

Changing position slowly, a warm compress, an elastic belly band and gentle stretches can help. If it keeps going, see your doctor to rule out other causes.

Other things can feel similar. Call 000 if you have vaginal bleeding, strong pain or feel very unwell. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately for tummy pain with bleeding or discharge, contractions, pain or pressure in your lower back, severe pain that won't go, chills or fever, light-headedness, trouble walking, or vomiting that isn't morning sickness.
