---
title: The cord, complete
read: [the-umbilical-cord]
---
The umbilical cord runs between the placenta and the baby. It brings the nutrients and oxygen the baby needs to grow, and takes waste products away.

After the birth the cord is clamped and cut, leaving a stump that eventually falls off and heals into the belly button.

What's left in the cord and placenta afterwards is cord blood. It's normally thrown away, but it can be collected, which takes about 3 minutes after the cord is cut and doesn't hurt you or the baby. In Australia you can donate it free to an AusCord public cord blood bank, which needs a participating hospital, or store it with a private bank, which charges fees. Either has to be arranged before the birth, and deciding to bank it makes no difference to your birth plan.
