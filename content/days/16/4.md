title: Off your back

From about 16 weeks, it's best to avoid exercises lying flat on your back. The weight of the baby can press on the major blood vessels returning blood to your heart, which can make you feel faint, light-headed or sick and reduce blood flow to the baby. An instructor who knows how many weeks you are, or a physiotherapist, can suggest a version that doesn't have you on your back.

It's the same reason for the side-sleeping advice later: from 28 weeks, going to sleep on your side, either side, at night and for naps.

And sleeping on your front, while the bump still allows it? The Australian sources Expecting checked don't say either way, so there's no guidance to give. If you're worried, ask your doctor or midwife. A pillow between your knees, another under the bump or a pregnancy pillow can make side-lying easier to get used to.
