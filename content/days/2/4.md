---
title: Carrier screening, best done first
read: [carrier-screening]
---
Carrier screening is a small sample of blood or saliva that shows whether you carry a gene for an inherited condition. A carrier doesn't have the condition, but if both parents carry the same one, each child has a 1 in 4 chance of being born with it. Around 1 in 20 Australians carry cystic fibrosis, spinal muscular atrophy or fragile X syndrome, and around 9 in 10 carriers have no known family history.

The best time is before pregnancy; it's also recommended in the first trimester, and results usually take about 2 to 8 weeks. Medicare rebates screening for those three conditions for anyone eligible who is pregnant or planning a pregnancy: the pregnant parent is tested first, and the partner only if that shows a carrier of CF or SMA. Expanded screening covers hundreds of conditions and costs more. Whether to have any of it is up to you.
