---
title: Vaccines, a month ahead
read: [live-vaccines-to-avoid]
---
Some vaccines contain a tiny amount of live, weakened virus or bacteria, and because of a very small chance of a risk to the baby, they aren't given in pregnancy: MMR (measles, mumps and rubella), chickenpox and some travel vaccines among them. So the time to catch up is before: have any live vaccine at least 28 days (4 weeks) before you get pregnant.

Your vaccination history is on your immunisation history statement in myGov or My Health Record, or your doctor can look, and a blood test can check your immunity to some diseases. Before any vaccination, tell the doctor or nurse if you may be pregnant.

If you had one without knowing, the risk to the baby is theoretical: the Australian Immunisation Handbook says you don't need to consider ending the pregnancy, and should be reassured.
