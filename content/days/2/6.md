---
title: Private cover and the 12-month wait
read: [private-health-waiting-periods]
---
If private care is part of the plan, its clock starts before the pregnancy does. A new hospital policy, or an upgrade, comes with waiting periods, and the maximum for pregnancy and birth is 12 months, which is what most policies have. The pregnant parent has to have served the full wait before being admitted to hospital to claim, so for private obstetric care the cover needs to be in place at least 12 months before becoming pregnant.

Not every policy covers pregnancy at all. It's a minimum only for Gold; Silver and Bronze may include it as an option, and Basic only on a restricted basis. Many cheaper covers leave out obstetrics, or cover you only as a private patient in a public hospital.

If the baby is due inside the wait, public care is fully covered by Medicare for everyone with a Medicare number, or you pay privately.
