---
title: Smoke-free, both of you
read: [smoking, partner-smoking]
---
There's no safe amount of smoking in pregnancy, and quitting completely is the aim, not just cutting down. Nicotine and other harmful chemicals pass through the placenta, raising the risk of miscarriage and stillbirth, a baby born early or small, and problems with the placenta. No kind is safer: not low-tar, roll-your-own or "natural" tobacco. Vaping isn't safe in pregnancy and isn't recommended as a way to quit.

It's the partner's too. Breathing in someone else's smoke in pregnancy can affect the baby's growth, health and lung development, and another room isn't enough: smoke moves through the house and clings to clothes, skin and hair.

Quitline is on 13 7848 for either of you. Some quitting medicines aren't recommended for the pregnant parent but may suit the partner; a doctor can say.
