---
title: Practice contractions
read: [braxton-hicks]
---
The uterus rehearses. Braxton Hicks are a tightening, as if the muscles across your belly are pulling tight, most often felt in the third trimester. They come at irregular times, no more than once or twice an hour, last about 30 seconds, are uncomfortable rather than painful, and stop if you change position, walk or have a warm shower. They don't cause labour.

Before 37 weeks, contractions can be a sign of premature labour: contact your doctor or midwife immediately if you feel pain or discomfort in your pelvis, belly or lower back, or have any signs of labour. At any stage, contact them immediately for persistent belly pain, vaginal bleeding, worry about the baby's movements, or feeling very unwell. And if you're not sure whether it's Braxton Hicks or labour, contact your midwife or doctor; if in doubt, don't hesitate to call.
