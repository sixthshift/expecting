title: A fall or a knock

A rule worth knowing before you need it: any injury to your belly in pregnancy needs checking, even a minor one. The most common serious complication is placental abruption, the placenta starting to separate from the uterus, and you may need to stay in hospital at least 4 hours so the baby's heart rate can be monitored for signs of it.

After a fall, a car accident or any blow to your belly, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately, and go to the nearest hospital and tell the staff you're pregnant; straight away if the baby is moving less than normal. If contractions start or your waters break afterwards, contact your doctor, or call 000 in an emergency. If you're RhD negative, tell them: you may need an anti-D injection.

With pain in your belly, a firm or tightening uterus or vaginal bleeding after an injury, call 000, ask for an ambulance and say you're pregnant.
