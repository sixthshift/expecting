---
title: Haemorrhoid creams
read: [haemorrhoid-treatments]
---
Haemorrhoids are common in pregnancy, particularly in the second and third trimesters. Hormone changes, pressure from the baby, changes in blood flow and constipation all contribute, and most go away after the baby is born.

The simple things come first: a deep warm bath, ice packs on an external haemorrhoid, fibre and fluids, and not straining. Constipation is the most common treatable cause, and laxatives are considered safe in pregnancy.

Creams and suppositories with an anaesthetic or steroid, such as Proctosedyl and Rectinol, are considered safe at any stage. KEMH's directions are up to three times a day, best after a bowel motion, for no longer than seven days: with longer use, steroids and local anaesthetics may change the skin or raise the risk of infection. They ease the symptoms but won't cure the haemorrhoids.

See a doctor to be sure what it is, particularly if there's bleeding and significant pain.
