---
title: The 12-week scan
read: [nuchal-translucency-scan, combined-first-trimester-screening]
---
The scan people call the 12-week scan is the nuchal translucency scan, usually between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days. It's optional, safe for you and the baby, and takes around 20 minutes, longer if the baby is busy. The wand may go on your tummy or inside your vagina.

The sonographer measures the baby's length, to check the timing, then the small fluid-filled space behind the baby's neck. That measurement helps estimate the chance of Down syndrome and other chromosomal differences, and the scan can also find early signs of heart and other structural differences and help screen for pre-eclampsia.

As part of combined screening it's put together with a blood test and your age, and picks up about 85 to 90% of babies with Down syndrome. The result is a chance, reported as low or high risk, not a diagnosis. Sometimes the picture isn't clear and you'll need to come back.
