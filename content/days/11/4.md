---
title: What the placenta does, and where it sits
read: [the-placenta]
---
The placenta has been growing since the fertilised egg implanted, and by now it does a lot. Besides passing oxygen and nutrients to the baby, it carries waste such as carbon dioxide back to your blood for your body to get rid of, and stops some harmful things, such as viruses, reaching the baby. It doesn't stop everything: alcohol, nicotine, some medicines and illicit drugs can cross it and affect the baby.

Where it attaches varies from person to person and pregnancy to pregnancy: the top of the uterus, the sides, the front (anterior) or the back (posterior). Its position is checked on ultrasound in the first and second trimesters.

That position changes how you'll feel the baby move. With an anterior placenta, movements tend to be felt around 20 weeks and may not feel as strong; with a posterior one, they're often felt earlier and may feel stronger.
