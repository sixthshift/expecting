---
title: What an epidural involves
read: [epidural, card-epidural-package]
---
An epidural can take away the pain of contractions for hours while you stay awake and aware, able to push. An anaesthetist places a very thin soft tube in your lower back, between contractions, and it takes 25 to 40 minutes to set up and work fully. You can have one at almost any stage of labour unless there's a medical reason not to. Most hospitals offer them; birth centres and home births don't.

It comes as a package: a drip in your arm, continuous monitoring of the baby on a CTG, a catheter because you won't feel when you need to wee, and staying in bed. Pushing may be harder and the second stage longer. It doesn't make a caesarean more likely, and the medicines don't harm the baby.

In a long labour it can let you sleep and get your strength back. Whether your hospital offers one, and whether there's a cost, is worth asking now.
