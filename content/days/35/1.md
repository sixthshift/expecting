---
title: The Group B strep swab
read: [group-b-strep-swab]
---
Group B strep is a common bacterium: about 1 in 5 people carry it in the vagina, usually with no symptoms or health problems. There's a small chance it passes to the baby during birth, and most babies who come into contact with it don't become unwell.

The national pregnancy care guidelines strongly recommend offering everyone a swab of the vagina and rectum at about 35 to 37 weeks, the timing that's most accurate and avoids overtreatment with antibiotics in labour. You can usually take it yourself. Not every provider tests routinely yet; some recommend antibiotics in labour if there are risk factors instead.

If the swab is positive, your team will probably recommend antibiotics through a drip once your waters break or labour starts, even for a planned caesarean. The choice is yours.
