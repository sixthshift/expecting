---
title: Tears, plainly
read: [perineal-tears, card-tears-first-baby]
---
Tearing is common: about 8 in 10 people who give birth vaginally have some form of tear. It's more likely with a first baby, a baby over 4 kg, or a forceps or vacuum birth. Most tears are first or second degree. A first-degree tear is small and skin-deep and can often heal without stitches; a second-degree tear reaches the muscle and usually needs stitches. The rarer third- and fourth-degree tears reach the anal sphincter and are repaired surgically, often in an operating theatre.

Pelvic floor exercises and perineal massage from 35 weeks may help lower the chance. In the birth, warm compresses on the perineum, breathing techniques and moving around can encourage a slow, controlled birth. It's worth asking your hospital what it does to lower the chance of a serious tear.

After the birth you're checked for tears, and none is repaired without talking to you first.
