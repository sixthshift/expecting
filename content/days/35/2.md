---
title: Labour in three stages
read: [stages-of-labour]
---
Labour is described in three stages, common to every labour and felt differently by everyone. The first is the longest: the cervix thins, softens and opens to about 10 cm. It starts with the latent phase, which can last hours or even days and can usually be spent at home. Then comes the active phase, from about 4 cm, with stronger, more regular contractions around 3 to 4 minutes apart. Last is transition, at 8 to 10 cm, when you may feel shaky, sick, pressure in your bottom, or that you "can't do it anymore".

The second stage runs from full dilation to the birth, with longer contractions, longer breaks between them, and an urge to push. It can last up to 3 hours, usually less if you've had a baby before.

The third is the placenta: up to about 30 minutes with an oxytocin injection, up to about an hour without. Some hospitals call the recovery afterwards a fourth.
