---
title: Upright and moving
read: [positions-for-labour, card-upright-helps]
---
There's no one best position for labour, and unless there's a medical reason you can move and choose what's comfortable throughout. It used to be common to give birth lying on your back in bed; research now shows being upright has benefits for you and the baby. In active labour it can mean less pain and a shorter first stage, a lower chance of an epidural or a caesarean, and it can help the baby's head move down.

Lying flat on your back in the first stage is generally not a good idea: it can reduce the baby's blood supply and could lead to a longer labour. Kneeling, all fours, a birth ball, swaying in the shower: you might like to practise some beforehand.

Some things limit moving. An epidural usually means the bed, though lying on your side may still be better than your back, and continuous monitoring can too, so ask if there's a mobile monitor.
