---
title: You won't be cut as a matter of course
read: [episiotomy, card-episiotomy-not-routine]
---
An episiotomy is a cut in the perineum to make more room for the baby. It isn't recommended for every vaginal birth, and it's only done with your consent. It may be suggested during a forceps or vacuum birth, when the baby is distressed and needs to be born quickly, or when a severe tear looks likely; then it can lower the risk of a severe tear, though you may still tear as well.

If it happens, the area is numbed first, and it's closed afterwards with dissolvable stitches.

If you'd rather not have one, that can be talked over with your midwife or doctor before labour and put in your birth preferences, and your team will do their best to support them. If something unexpected happens and one is recommended, they'll explain the risks and benefits, and the final decision is yours. Having one doesn't mean you'll always need one next time.
