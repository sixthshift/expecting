---
title: If your blood group is RhD negative
read: [rhd-negative, anti-d]
---
Around 17 in every 100 people in Australia are RhD negative, and on its own it isn't usually a problem. It matters if the baby is RhD positive: a little of the baby's blood getting into yours can make your immune system produce antibodies. They usually won't affect this pregnancy, but can cause serious complications for a future baby. That's rare these days, because anti-D injections can usually prevent it.

If you're RhD negative, anti-D may be offered at 28 and 34 weeks, and again after the birth if the baby is RhD positive. It's safe for you and the baby. If an RhD NIPT blood test shows the baby is RhD negative, you might not need it.

It may also be recommended after heavy, frequent bleeding or an injury to your belly. Always speak to your doctor or midwife about any bleeding, and tell them you're RhD negative.
