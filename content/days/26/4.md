---
title: The gap down the middle
read: [abdominal-separation]
---
As the bump grows, the two long muscles down the middle of your tummy can separate. It's called abdominal separation, or diastasis recti, and it's common in the later stages of pregnancy, a natural adaptation to the growing baby. It isn't painful, though you may see a gap, feel your core is weak when you lift something, or get lower back pain. It's more likely if you've had more than one pregnancy.

While the muscles are separated, sit-ups and crunches are out; pelvic tilts and abdominal bracing are better, and a supportive brace or special underwear can help support your back.

It often gets better in the weeks and months after the birth, though up to 1 in 3 still have it a year later. If you think you have it, see your doctor or maternal health nurse; your doctor, midwife or physiotherapist can check it, and you may be referred to a physiotherapist for an exercise program.
