A baby is one of the changes Moneysmart names for making or updating a will. A will can say who will look after your children if they're under 18. Without one, the government decides who gets your assets, which can take longer and may not be what you wanted. A solicitor can prepare one, your state's Public Trustee does it at no cost for some people, and a will kit should be checked by one of them.

Your super, and any life insurance held through it, sits outside the will: each fund needs its own beneficiary nomination, or the trustee decides. ASIC found almost 60% of members hadn't nominated anyone, and binding nominations generally expire every 3 years.

Life cover helps when someone depends on your income, and a baby is someone. Moneysmart's way to size it: what the family would need, less what it would already have.
