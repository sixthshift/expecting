---
title: Closeness in the middle months
read: [sex-and-intimacy]
---
Feelings about sex change through the stages of pregnancy. In the second trimester some people notice their sex drive rise, with less tiredness and nausea, hormone changes and extra blood flow to the genitals. It's also OK if desire stays low or drops, for either of you, and sex should only happen when you both agree to it.

Having sex doesn't harm the baby, unless your maternity care provider has told you otherwise. Partners are told it might feel different but won't harm the baby; if either of you has doubts, that's one for your maternity care provider.

It's normal for couples to feel disconnected or anxious during pregnancy. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests talking about how you feel, and about the other ways to feel wanted and secure: kissing, cuddling, massage. Making time to share thoughts and feelings, being open about the changes and keeping a sense of humour can help.
