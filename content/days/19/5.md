---
title: The cervix, holding on
read: [the-cervix-in-pregnancy]
---
Your cervix, the muscular channel between your uterus and your vagina, has one job for now: holding the baby in the uterus. From the start of pregnancy the mucus on its surface has been thick and sticky, a plug that stops bacteria, viruses and other germs getting into the uterus.

Its length can be seen on a routine ultrasound, usually between 16 and 24 weeks. Some people have a short cervix, under 25 mm, which can raise the risk of going into labour and giving birth before 37 weeks. If that's you, your doctor may recommend treatment to lower the risk, and will watch your cervix closely through the rest of the pregnancy.

Its other job comes at the end. In labour it softens, thins and opens, to about 10 cm. As it begins to open you may see a clear, pink or slightly bloody discharge, the "show", and the mucus plug may come away.
