---
title: Headaches, from next week
read: [headaches]
---
Mild headaches are common in pregnancy, most of all in the first few months, and they usually get better as it goes on. Rest, water or a cold or heat pack come first, and if it doesn't settle, you can take paracetamol, for the shortest time you can; it's best not to take ibuprofen or aspirin.

From 20 weeks, a headache can mean more: it can be a sign of pre-eclampsia. Call your doctor or midwife straight away for a very bad headache, blurred vision, pain below your ribs, vomiting, or sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet. A severe headache that isn't usual for you needs medical help immediately, and frequent headaches are worth telling your doctor or midwife about. And call 000, or go to your nearest hospital emergency department, for a sudden, severe headache, or one with vomiting, confusion, a stiff neck, fever, loss of balance, a seizure or changes in your vision.
