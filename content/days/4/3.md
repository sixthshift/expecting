---
title: If there's spotting
read: [spotting-and-bleeding-in-early-pregnancy]
---
As the fertilised egg attaches to the lining of the uterus, some people have light spotting, called implantation bleeding. Bleeding in early pregnancy is common: about 1 in 4 pregnant people bleed early on, and many go on to have a healthy baby. But it has other causes too, including a miscarriage or an ectopic pregnancy, which can be life-threatening.

Contact your doctor or midwife straight away for any spotting or bleeding; if you haven't started pregnancy care yet, call your GP. Call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department immediately for heavy bleeding (soaking 1 or more pads in an hour, bleeding you can't catch or contain, or large clots), severe pain in your belly, pain in the tip of your shoulder, smelly vaginal discharge, a fever, feeling faint, dizzy or unwell, or trouble breathing; and go to emergency immediately for chills or a fast heartbeat with bleeding.
