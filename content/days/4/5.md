The same question comes up about tablets. The answer depends on the medicine and when you took it, so tell your doctor, pharmacist or midwife what it was.

Every pregnancy starts with a 3 to 5% chance of a birth defect, called the background risk, and medicines account for only a small part of it. Timing matters. In very early pregnancy, a harmful exposure most likely causes an "all or nothing" response: either a miscarriage, or no significant effect on the pregnancy. Through the first trimester, when the baby's major structures form, harmful exposures are most likely to cause birth defects.

Anti-inflammatories such as ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and aspirin are no longer recommended in pregnancy except under medical supervision, but MotherSafe says people who've used one by accident can generally be reassured.
