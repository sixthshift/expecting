---
title: More isn't better
read: [pregnancy-supplements]
---
Folic acid and iodine are the two supplements recommended for everyone in Australia. Beyond them, a healthy, balanced diet gives you most of what you need, and anything else is worth a word with your pharmacist, doctor, midwife or dietitian before you add it.

A pregnancy multivitamin often has extra folate, iodine and iron, but amounts vary by brand, so read the label and check how many tablets to take. The Royal Women's Hospital says to take only one designed for pregnancy, and that costlier ones aren't always better.

High doses of some vitamins can be dangerous. It's best not to take vitamins A, C or E as supplements in pregnancy: extra vitamin A might increase the chance of birth defects, so no more than 10,000 international units a day, and avoid foods very high in it, such as liver and pâté. Fish liver oils have too much vitamin A as well.
