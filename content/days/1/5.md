---
title: Iodine, for the baby's brain
read: [iodine]
---
Folic acid has a companion. The National Health and Medical Research Council recommends that everyone who is pregnant, breastfeeding or considering pregnancy takes 150 micrograms of iodine a day, from when you're thinking about pregnancy right through pregnancy and breastfeeding.

The thyroid uses iodine to make hormones that are vital for the normal development of the baby's brain and nervous system, before birth and in the first years of life. Even mild to moderate deficiency can cause learning difficulties and affect physical development and hearing. Bread in Australia, except organic, has been made with iodised salt since 2009, which is enough for most people; pregnancy and breastfeeding bring the greatest need, which is why a supplement is recommended on top.

Two exceptions: with a thyroid condition, get your doctor's advice before you take one, and skip kelp or seaweed tablets, where the amount of iodine varies.
