---
title: The first blood tests
read: [first-blood-tests, blood-group-and-antibodies, rubella-immunity]

Around the first visit you'll be offered a set of blood tests, and it's your choice whether to have each one. Offered to everyone: your blood group and antibodies, a full blood count for anaemia, and an infectious diseases screen, for immunity to rubella, and for syphilis, hepatitis B and C, and HIV. Others are added depending on you, and a urine test goes with them.

A few results change your care. Almost 2 in 10 people in Australia are RhD negative; if you are and the baby is RhD positive, your body may make antibodies against the baby's blood, usually affecting a later pregnancy, so you'll be offered anti-D injections at 28 and 34 weeks. Rubella is mild for adults but very dangerous for an unborn baby, and its vaccine is live, so if you're not immune you'll be offered it as soon as possible after the birth.
