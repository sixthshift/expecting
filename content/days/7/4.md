---
title: Vitamin D, and whether to test
read: [vitamin-d, vitamin-d-test]
---
Vitamin D helps your body take up and use calcium, for your bones and the baby's bones, and your skin makes most of it in sunlight. Many pregnant people in Australia are low, and if you're low, the baby will be low at birth too. It's more likely if you spend little time outdoors or have dark skin, but many people are low without either.

Whether you get a test for it depends on the service: some include it in the first tests, others offer it if you're likely to be low. The national pregnancy care guidelines (August 2026) recommend against testing everyone or giving everyone a supplement, because the evidence that it improves outcomes is weak.

Most pregnancy multivitamins contain some vitamin D. If a test shows you're low, your midwife or doctor may suggest a tablet, and supplements at recommended doses haven't been shown to cause harm. Ask what applies to you.
