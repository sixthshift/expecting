---
title: Eggs, cooked firm
read: [raw-eggs, custard-and-desserts]
---
Raw and lightly cooked eggs can carry salmonella, which causes food poisoning. In pregnancy it can, rarely, cause miscarriage, so eggs are cooked until both the yolk and the white are firm, about 72°C. Hard-boiled eggs and cooked omelettes are fine.

Raw egg turns up in more places than you might think: homemade mayonnaise and aioli, mousse, lightly poached eggs, milkshakes made with egg, homemade custard, and raw cake or biscuit batter. Commercially made mayonnaise is fine, and pasteurised eggs are a safer choice. Batter is for baking, not tasting.

Custard depends on how it's made. Shop-bought custard can be eaten cold if freshly opened; once open, it's kept in the fridge, reheated to at least 60°C and used within a day. Home-made custard needs cooking to at least 71°C and eating hot. Chocolate mousse and other desserts made with raw or undercooked egg are out until after the birth.
