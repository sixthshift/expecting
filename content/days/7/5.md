---
title: When things slow down
read: [constipation]
---
Constipation is very common: up to 2 in 5 pregnant people have it. Progesterone relaxes your muscles and slows food moving through your gut, and some medicines and supplements, including iron and some multivitamins, can add to it; a change of dose or brand can sometimes help, so ask your doctor. It isn't harmful to the baby.

First steps: at least 7 to 8 glasses of fluid a day, more fibre, exercise, and going as soon as you get the urge. A footstool helps. Not all laxatives are recommended in pregnancy, so check with your doctor or pharmacist first.

See your doctor if it's severe (3 or more days between, tummy pain, long straining, or a feeling of a ball blocking your rectum), isn't getting better, or you're worried. Get urgent care for constipation with severe tummy pain, cramping or bloating, vomiting, bleeding from your rectum or blood in your poo, or weight loss you can't explain.
