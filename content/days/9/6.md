---
title: A cervical screen, if it's due
read: [cervical-screening-test]
---
The cervical screening test checks for HPV, the virus that can cause changes in the cervix that sometimes lead to cancer. It's usually every 5 years, for anyone aged 25 to 74 with a cervix who has ever had sex.

Pregnancy isn't a reason to put it off. It's safe and shouldn't be delayed, so if you're due, or have never been screened, your doctor should offer it at your first visit, and your GP can do it. You can collect the sample yourself with a vaginal swab, in a private place at the clinic, unless you've had an abnormal result before. It may feel a bit uncomfortable but shouldn't hurt, and a little spotting afterwards is normal and won't harm you or the baby.

A normal result means no HPV, and the next test is in 5 years. If HPV is found you may need another test in 12 months or a colposcopy, which is safe in pregnancy too.
