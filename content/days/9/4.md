---
title: The urine sample, and what it finds
read: [urine-culture, uti-found-on-testing, urinary-tract-infection]
---
The urine sample at or soon after your first visit goes to a lab, which checks it for bacteria. It can find a urinary tract infection even when you feel completely well, and that matters in pregnancy: a bladder infection is more likely to reach the kidneys, which can be serious for you and the baby. Bacteria in the urine are also linked to pre-eclampsia, and an infection to the baby being born early or small, a risk that goes away once it's treated, with antibiotics that are safe in pregnancy.

UTIs are the most common bacterial infection in pregnancy, about 1 in 10. See your midwife or doctor with any signs, and don't delay antibiotics: pain or burning when you wee, needing to go more than usual, cloudy, bloody or very smelly urine, pain low in your belly, or a fever. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have a fever or chills.
