---
title: Skin like a teenager's
read: [acne, acne-treatments]
---
Hormone changes can trigger acne, and it may flare in the first trimester, on your face, neck, back or chest. It won't affect the pregnancy or the baby, and it usually improves, or even disappears, by the third trimester.

What you treat it with is what matters. Some acne medicines can harm the baby and must be avoided: prescription retinoids, medicines similar to vitamin A such as isotretinoin and tretinoin, whether pills, creams or ointments, and spironolactone. Over-the-counter retinol is best paused too, though past use isn't a concern. Benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid spot treatments and glycolic acid are safe; salicylic acid peels are not.

Gentle care helps: a soap-free cleanser, oil-free make-up and moisturiser, no picking, and good sun protection. Check with your midwife, doctor or pharmacist before starting any new product, and see your doctor or midwife if over-the-counter products aren't controlling it, it's moderate to severe, or it's getting you down.
