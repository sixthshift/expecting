---
title: Moving, even when you're tired
read: [staying-active-in-pregnancy, walking]
---
Unless your doctor or midwife says otherwise, you can start or keep exercising now. If you weren't very active before, start small and build up. The aim is to be active most days, adding up to 2.5 to 5 hours of moderate activity a week, and it can help prepare your body for birth and reduce back and pelvic pain.

Walking is the easy way in: a good moderate activity that puts minimal stress on your joints. Supportive shoes help, and strenuous exercise in hot or humid weather is best avoided.

Call 000 for chest pain or severe trouble breathing. Stop and see your doctor immediately for unexplained shortness of breath, dizziness, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain, sudden swelling of the ankles, hands or face, or nausea and vomiting. Stop, too, for pain in your tummy, back or pelvis, and for vaginal bleeding stop and contact your midwife or birth unit straight away; for heavy bleeding, call 000.
