---
title: A chance, not an answer
read: [screening-vs-diagnostic, first-trimester-screening]
---
The tests you're weighing up this week are screening tests, and it helps to know what that word means before you choose. A screening test, such as combined first-trimester screening or NIPT, estimates the chance your baby has a condition such as Down syndrome. A high-chance result isn't a diagnosis, and a screen can miss some babies who have the condition.

A diagnostic test, CVS or amniocentesis, can tell you for certain. They're generally safe but carry a small risk of miscarriage, so most people have a screening test first, and a diagnostic test may be recommended if a screen shows a higher chance, or if there's a known reason, such as a genetic condition one of you carries.

All of it is optional. The windows are short, though: the combined test's blood test runs from 10 to 13 weeks, which is why it's worth talking it through now.
