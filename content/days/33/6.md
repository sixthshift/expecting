---
title: Heavy, aching legs
read: [varicose-veins]
---
Up to 4 in 10 pregnant people get varicose veins. You make more blood than usual, the uterus presses on the veins bringing it back from your legs, and hormones soften the vein walls. They mostly show in the legs, though they can affect the vulva or rectum too. Your legs may ache, feel heavy, throb or burn, usually worse by the end of the day. They usually get better without treatment by the time the baby is 3 to 4 months old.

To ease the aches: professionally fitted compression stockings, ankle exercises, shifting your weight from foot to foot when standing, legs up when you can, lying on your left side, cold packs, and no tight clothes or high heels.

Varicose veins raise the risk of a blood clot. Pain or swelling in one leg, especially if it's red or warm, means calling your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away.
