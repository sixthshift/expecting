Haemorrhoids are swollen varicose veins in the rectum and anus, more common in the second and third trimesters as the growing uterus presses on the veins. They can bring pain or burning when you poo, and sometimes minor bleeding. They won't affect the baby, and most go within a few days of the birth.

Avoiding constipation is the best way to treat and prevent them: plenty of water and a high-fibre diet. An ice pack can reduce the swelling of external ones, and a warm bath may ease the pain. Paracetamol can be taken as directed, and creams and laxatives are options, but check with your doctor or pharmacist first. If you're worried about pushing in labour making them worse, talk to your midwife.

See your doctor or midwife if it hurts to poo, or you see blood on the toilet paper or in your poo, so they can rule out other causes.
