---
title: If they say the baby is big
read: [large-for-dates]
---
The baby is gaining a lot of weight now. Estimating a baby's weight by feeling and measuring the bump is imprecise, so if it measures big, for example 3 cm more than expected from 36 weeks, Queensland's guideline recommends an ultrasound to estimate the weight. Scan estimates have improved, with random errors of less than 10%, but they are estimates, not a weighing.

There's no single definition of a big baby, but one is a birth weight over 4000 to 4500 g at 40 weeks. High blood sugar in pregnancy raises the chance of one, and having had a large baby before puts you at higher risk of gestational diabetes, so tell your doctor or midwife if you have.

The guideline suggests discussing induction from 38 weeks if a scan estimate is high enough, but not on a hands-on examination alone. Hospitals differ, so ask yours what your result means for you.
