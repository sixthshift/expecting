---
title: Birth preferences, on paper
read: [birth-preferences]
---
Birth preferences, or a birth plan, tell your healthcare team the kind of birth you'd like, written down or just talked through. Not everyone makes one, but research shows people who prepare them are more likely to be satisfied with how the birth goes.

The usual ground: who's there and how your support person speaks to staff for you, moving and positions, a bath or shower, pain relief offered early or kept on hand, what you'd want if you need an induction or a caesarean, skin-to-skin and feeding, and any cultural practices. With a first baby you don't need answers to all of it.

Two things make one work. Talking it through with your midwife or doctor, since what's possible depends on where you give birth and your health, with it put in your notes. And flexibility: you can change your mind at any time, even when the baby is close to being born.
