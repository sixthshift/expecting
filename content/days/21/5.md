---
title: The COVID-19 vaccine, a choice
read: [covid-vaccine-in-pregnancy]
---
Unlike the flu, whooping cough and RSV vaccines, a COVID-19 vaccine isn't routinely recommended in pregnancy. It's a choice. The Australian Immunisation Handbook says anyone pregnant can consider a dose from 20 weeks in each pregnancy, to protect the baby; if you've never had a COVID-19 vaccine, vaccination is recommended.

A dose may reduce your risk of severe COVID-19 and its complications, and the antibodies cross the placenta and may help protect a young baby from being hospitalised with it. On the other side, the risks of severe outcomes from Omicron, the only variant now circulating, are substantially lower than with earlier variants.

mRNA COVID-19 vaccines are safe in pregnancy, with side effects much like anyone else's, and they can be given with other vaccines, including flu. If you want one this pregnancy, it's from 20 weeks; if you have questions, your doctor or midwife can talk them through.
