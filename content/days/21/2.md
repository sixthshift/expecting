---
title: When antacids aren't enough
read: [reflux-medicines, heartburn]
---
With heartburn, in up to 80% of pregnancies, the steps go in order: changes to how you eat, then antacids at the recommended dose, then stronger reflux medicines considered safe in pregnancy. H2 blockers such as famotidine are generally used when antacids haven't fully worked. Proton pump inhibitors have been widely used, with no increased risk for the baby in the available reviews; omeprazole and esomeprazole are the most commonly suggested. A short course is available over the counter, and longer needs a prescription.

From 20 weeks, though, heartburn that antacids don't fix can be a sign of pre-eclampsia. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if they don't fix it, or if it comes with severe pain below the ribs, sudden swelling of your hands, face or feet, a headache simple pain relief doesn't fix, or vision problems. With chest pain or shortness of breath, call 000. Vomiting blood, losing weight or painful swallowing need care straight away too.
