title: Going overseas

Overseas brings more to weigh. Travel to very remote areas or developing countries isn't recommended in pregnancy, and places where malaria, Zika or similar mosquito-borne infections are a problem are ones to avoid. Many developing countries have no intensive care for very premature babies, which is why being within reach of hospitals matters, and which travel vaccinations are safe for you is a question for your doctor.

Insurance is the other half. Policies differ on pregnancy: you may only be covered until week 32, and for cover in pregnancy you may need to pay extra or show medical evidence. What matters is whether a policy covers the pregnancy and to what stage, complications or a premature birth while you're away, the baby if you go into labour, and cancelling because of the pregnancy. Some cover a newborn too, which matters if the baby comes early.
