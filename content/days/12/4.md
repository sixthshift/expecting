---
title: X-rays, CT and MRI
read: [medical-x-rays-and-ct]
---
Sometimes the rest of life needs an x-ray or a scan. Most radiation from medical testing is unlikely to harm the baby, but before any test, tell your doctor and the radiology staff that you're pregnant.

Any harm depends on the dose, the stage of pregnancy (the further along, the better) and where it's aimed: tests of your abdomen or pelvis carry a higher risk. For scale, Australians get about 2 mSv of background radiation a year; a chest x-ray is up to 0.1 mSv and a CT scan up to 8 mSv. CT scans are best avoided in pregnancy because of a small chance the radiation could harm the baby.

Ultrasound and MRI use no ionising radiation and may be alternatives; MRI is generally safe in pregnancy, but not in early pregnancy. And if you had an x-ray before you knew, talk to your doctor: a single low dose is often not likely to be harmful.
