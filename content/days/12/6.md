---
title: The muscles holding it all up
read: [the-pelvic-floor]
---
Your pelvic floor is a sling of muscle from your pubic bone to your tailbone, holding up your bladder, uterus and bowel. In pregnancy it has an extra job, supporting the baby, and it can be weakened, stretched or strained along the way; a weak one can show as leaking urine, needing to wee often, or a feeling of pressure or a bulge.

To find it, sit or lie relaxed and squeeze as if stopping yourself passing wind or holding on from weeing. You should feel a lift inside, around your anus and vagina, and a definite let go. The exercises are slow holds of 3 to 5 seconds and quick squeezes, best done every day and tied to something daily, like waiting for the kettle.

If you can't feel a distinct tightening, a pelvic floor physiotherapist or continence nurse can help. If you have bladder or bowel problems, see your doctor.
