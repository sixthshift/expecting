---
title: Pushing, and the birth
read: [second-stage, term-urge-to-push, term-crowning]
---
The second stage starts when the cervix is fully open at 10 cm and ends with the birth. Contractions get longer, with longer breaks between. The urge to push can be overwhelming, like a strong urge to do a poo, and you may push without meaning to; with an epidural it may be weaker or absent, and the midwife guides you instead. Pushing is more effective upright: squatting, kneeling, sitting, standing or in water.

Near the end you're likely to feel stretching and burning as the head crowns, visible at the opening of the vagina. With your consent, the midwife may press gently on the head so it comes slowly, and offer a warm compress on your perineum, to lower the chance of tearing.

Birth is generally expected within 3 hours of full dilation for a first baby, or 2 if you've had a baby before. Then, if the baby is well, they're given to you, skin to skin.
