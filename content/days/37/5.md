---
title: If the baby needs a hand
read: [assisted-birth]
---
Almost 1 baby in 10 in Australia and New Zealand is born with help from a vacuum cup or forceps, most often in a first birth. It's offered when it's the safest way: the baby isn't moving down as expected, needs to be born quickly, or is in a hard position to be born without help, or you're exhausted and can't push effectively. It's done only with your consent.

A vacuum is a soft cup on the baby's head, used more often; forceps are two smooth, spoon-shaped blades around it. Either way the doctor gently pulls as you push. Forceps usually mean an episiotomy; a vacuum may. Most assisted births happen without complications, and a mark or bruise on the baby's head or cheeks soon fades.

In the moment you can ask why it's needed, what it involves, the risks of having it and of not, and what else there is. It's worth talking about before labour.
