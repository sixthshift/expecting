---
title: Active labour, then transition
read: [active-labour, term-transition]
---
Active labour is when contractions become strong, painful and regular, every 3 to 4 minutes and lasting around 60 seconds, and the cervix, now fully thinned, opens steadily towards 10 cm. If your pregnancy has been uncomplicated, the time to contact the birth unit is when contractions are regular and about 5 minutes apart, or you can no longer manage at home.

When you arrive, the midwife feels your tummy for the baby's position, listens to the baby's heartbeat, checks your blood pressure and, with your consent, may examine you to see how open your cervix is. Standing and moving can help with pain and progress, and you can ask for pain relief any time.

Near the end comes transition, at about 8 to 10 cm. Contractions get faster and more intense, and you might feel shaky, sick, irritable or out of control, or that you "can't do it anymore". Calm reassurance and someone close help.
