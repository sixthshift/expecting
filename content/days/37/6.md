---
title: If it becomes a caesarean
read: [emergency-caesarean, caesarean-what-happens, card-skin-to-skin-in-theatre]
---
Even with a vaginal birth planned, a caesarean is sometimes decided on during labour: there's concern for you or the baby, labour isn't progressing, or there's severe bleeding or severe pre-eclampsia. Your doctor explains why and asks for your consent, in writing or, if there's no time, out loud, and you can still ask questions. The most urgent happen within about 30 minutes; others can wait longer.

You're usually awake, with a spinal or epidural anaesthetic, behind a screen across your chest: you'll feel tugging and pulling but no pain. The baby is often born in the first 10 minutes, and the operation takes 30 to 40. Usually one support person, often the partner, can be with you, in scrubs, unless you need a general anaesthetic.

If the baby is well, you can have skin to skin on the operating table, and the partner can remind the team that you'd like it.
