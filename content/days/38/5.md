---
title: The placenta is a stage too
read: [third-stage-placenta, card-placenta-is-a-stage, term-third-stage]
---
After the baby, the placenta still has to be born: the third stage of labour. It usually comes with mild contractions and a gentle push, and isn't usually painful, though it can be uncomfortable. The baby is usually on your chest by then, and a first breastfeed releases oxytocin, which helps.

There are two ways to manage it, a choice to make before the birth. Active management is an oxytocin injection and a gentle pull on the cord once the placenta has separated, usually up to 30 minutes; it cuts the risk of heavy bleeding by about half, and RANZCOG recommends it for everyone. Physiological management lets it come with your own contractions, up to an hour; you can choose it if you're well informed, knowing events at the birth may change your risk.

Afterwards the placenta is checked to make sure it's complete. Families have their own practices around it, and those choices should be respected.
