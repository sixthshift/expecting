---
title: What the centimetres mean
read: [vaginal-examinations, term-dilated, term-effaced]
---
In labour you'll hear centimetres. In a vaginal examination the midwife or doctor feels inside your vagina to check how far your cervix has opened (dilated) and how thin it has become (effaced), and how far down the baby's head has come.

Up to about 4 cm is early labour, while the cervix softens, shortens and thins. From about 4 to 5 cm, with the cervix completely thinned, is active labour. At 10 cm you're fully dilated, and the second stage, pushing, begins. Progress isn't judged on the cervix alone, though: your contractions, how far the baby has come down, your wellbeing and your birth preferences count too.

Every examination needs your consent, written or spoken. RANZCOG's guideline says they shouldn't be planned more often than every four hours in the first stage, though more may be recommended if there are concerns, or if you ask. In the second stage, some of the signs don't need one at all.
