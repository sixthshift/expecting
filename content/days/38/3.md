---
title: Eating, drinking and being sick
read: [eating-and-drinking-in-labour]
---
Labour takes energy, and early labour, which can last many hours or even days, is when it's built up: the advice is light snacks such as fruit or toast, regularly, and water to stay hydrated, while you rest at home.

Later, appetite may go. Vomiting is quite common in labour, and nausea is one of the things you may feel around transition, so it's no surprise if it happens. In the second stage the advice is to keep up fluids and rest when you can, and a cold face washer can soothe if you feel hot.

What you can eat and drink once you're in the birth suite, or with an epidural or a planned caesarean, is a question for your midwife. The partner's part is offering snacks, drinks and ice, and encouraging both; and bringing snacks and drinks of their own.
