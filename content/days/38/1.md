---
title: Morphine, and why timing matters
read: [morphine-and-opioids, card-morphine-drowsy]
---
Morphine is one of the medical options for labour pain, alongside the gas and an epidural. It's an injection that reduces how severe the pain is without taking it away completely; some find it helps, others that it made little difference. It can take up to 30 minutes to work and lasts three hours or more, and unlike an epidural it needs no drip, catheter or CTG.

It can make you drowsy, sick and less alert, which can make it harder to take part in decisions, and you can't use a bath or birth pool for 4 hours afterwards. It reaches the baby too, who may be sleepy for a short time.

Given within two hours of the birth, it may contribute to breathing problems in the baby: uncommon, and reversed with an injection. That's why, once labour is well advanced, an opioid may not be recommended.
