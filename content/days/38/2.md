On the day, the midwife's advice decides when to go, and a call to say you're on your way helps the hospital plan, even if things seem to be going well. The time of day and peak-hour traffic change how long the trip takes, and a long one is hard when you can't move around in the car.

The bags go with the paperwork: Medicare card, insurance details, the birth plan and hospital paperwork. Older children and pets need someone, with a stand-by or two.

If you're checked in early labour, you may be sent home for a while. That's common practice, and early labour can be long and tiring, so try not to be disappointed. And call triple zero (000) for an ambulance if you think you won't make it in time, or there's heavy bleeding, a severe headache or blurred vision.
