---
title: If the baby goes to special care
read: [special-care-nursery]
---
Almost 1 in 5 babies in Australia spends time in a special care nursery or intensive care. Babies go when they need more care than a normal ward can give: for jaundice, low blood sugar or breathing problems, for example. Special care isn't intensive care; babies there are less unwell.

The baby may start in a humidicrib before a cot, with machines watching their oxygen, heart rate and breathing, and the staff explain each one. You can be with your baby as often as you like, and holding, feeding, changing and bathing are encouraged. Ask about kangaroo care, the baby in just a nappy against your bare chest: it helps steady their heartbeat, breathing and temperature, and supports bonding and breastfeeding.

The baby comes home once they can keep their own temperature, are a suitable weight and feeding well. Sometimes you go home first.
