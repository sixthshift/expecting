---
title: After a caesarean
read: [caesarean-recovery, breastfeeding-after-caesarean]
---
Recovery after a caesarean usually takes longer than after a vaginal birth. A nurse or midwife first helps you out of bed, usually in the first day or two; then gentle walking every day lowers the chance of a chest infection or blood clots. Breastfeeding can usually start soon, and holding the baby under your arm, with a pillow over the wound, can be more comfortable. At home the advice is to lift nothing heavier than the baby.

Contact your doctor or midwife straight away for a fever of 38°C or more, shivering or sweating, redness or swelling around the wound, smelly discharge from the wound or vagina, or pain when you wee. If you're soaking a pad every hour or two or passing large clots, go to your hospital's emergency department straight away; call 000 if you gave birth in the last 24 hours, or feel unwell, dizzy or clammy, your heart is racing or you're short of breath.
