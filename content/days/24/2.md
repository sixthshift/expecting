---
title: Flying from here
read: [airline-pregnancy-limits]
---
If a trip means a flight, the airlines' rules start to matter soon. As at October 2026, Qantas wants a letter from your doctor or registered midwife after 28 weeks, and Virgin Australia from 28 weeks, giving your due date, whether it's one baby or more, and that there are no complications; Virgin also wants it to say you're fit to fly, dated no more than 10 days before you travel. Any complications mean formal medical clearance as well.

With one baby, both let a routine pregnancy fly to the end of week 36 on flights of 4 hours or more, and to the end of week 40 on shorter ones; with twins or more, 32 and 36. Those are the airlines' minimums, not a promise that flying is safe then, and RANZCOG doesn't recommend travel beyond 36 weeks. Other airlines can be stricter: Smartraveller warns that most restrict travel after 28 weeks, and each sets its own rules.
