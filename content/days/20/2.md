---
title: If they say your placenta is low
read: [low-lying-placenta]
---
One thing the scan notes is where the placenta has attached. Sometimes it's low, close to or over the cervix, and it's often found at this scan. Often it resolves: as the uterus expands and the baby grows, the placenta can "move up" away from the cervix.

If yours is low, you'll be offered another scan at about 32 to 34 weeks to see where it has ended up. If it was in a normal position at this scan, it's likely to stay there.

With a low-lying placenta, contact your doctor or midwife immediately about any vaginal bleeding, and tell them it's low. If the bleeding is heavy, or comes with pain or contractions, call 000 and ask for an ambulance, or go to your nearest emergency department.
