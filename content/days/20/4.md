---
title: Covered in cream, and a little furry
read: [term-vernix, card-baby-is-furry]
---
From about 19 weeks the baby's skin is covered in vernix, a white, sticky coating that protects it from infection and from the amniotic fluid all around. It's very common for it still to be there at birth, so don't be surprised by a creamy-looking baby; past the due date there's likely to be much less.

Under it is lanugo, a fine, soft hair that has covered the baby since about 15 weeks. Nobody knows exactly why, though it may help keep the baby at the right temperature. It starts to disappear by about 30 weeks and is usually shed before birth; any left then soon goes on its own.
