---
title: The first feed
read: [first-feed]
---
If you've chosen to breastfeed, the first feed usually happens during that skin-to-skin time. Many babies, on your bare chest, can move towards the breast, attach and start feeding on their own. You might see the baby move their head from side to side, bring their hands to their mouth, lick or nuzzle, and wriggle closer. There's no need to rush: some feed in the first hour, others take longer, and medicines or interventions in the birth can mean a baby needs more time.

What comes is colostrum, thick and yellow and made in small amounts, which suits a very small tummy. Afterwards many babies fall into a deep sleep for a while; then feeds come often, perhaps every hour, 8 to 12 times or more in the first 24 hours.

If the first feed is delayed, you can still breastfeed, and your midwife can show you how to express until the baby is ready.
