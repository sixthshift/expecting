---
title: The partner, once you're home
read: [partner-after-the-birth]
---
For the partner, a preview of the first weeks home. Your part in the baby's care starts from the beginning: dressing, washing, settling, nappies. Sharing it helps you both bond with the baby, and your one-on-one time with the baby gives the one who gave birth a chance to rest. If an instant, overwhelming attachment doesn't come, that's okay: connection takes time, and small moments build it.

Feeding is where backup counts, and active support can help them breastfeed for longer: a drink, a pillow, company. At night it can be you who brings the baby to them, then burps, changes and settles; after feeds, the baby may settle better in your arms, away from the smell of milk. Feeding that hurts beyond the first 30 to 60 seconds isn't normal, and is worth getting help for.

The house and the door are yours too: meals, laundry, other children, and visitors gone by the time feeding or rest is due.
