---
title: The first hour, skin to skin
read: [skin-to-skin, term-golden-hour]
---
Many hospitals encourage skin to skin straight after the birth, if you and the baby are well: the baby in just a nappy, maybe a hat, on your bare chest, wrapped up together. The first hour or so of it is often called the golden hour. It isn't a clinical term, and it's a goal, not a rule.

Skin to skin can help with bonding and breastfeeding, keeps the baby warm, and steadies their heartbeat and breathing. The Australian Breastfeeding Association suggests keeping it up for at least the first hour and until after the first feed, and says some checks, like weighing, can often wait.

After a caesarean you should still be able to have it. If you need medical care, the partner can hold the baby skin to skin until you're well enough. And if it's missed at the start, it's never too late.
