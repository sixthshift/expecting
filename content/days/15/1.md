---
title: The second-trimester blood test
read: [second-trimester-serum-screening, neural-tube-defect-screening]
---
If you didn't have screening in the first trimester and would like it, there's a second chance from this week. Second-trimester serum screening, also called maternal serum screening, is a blood test between 15 and 20 weeks.

It estimates the chance of the baby having Down syndrome, or a neural tube defect such as spina bifida, and the Royal Women's Hospital says it also estimates the chance of trisomy 18. Like every screen it can't diagnose: it tells you whether the chance is higher, and it can miss some babies who have the condition. Neural tube defects are usually found in the second trimester, and the ultrasound at around 18 to 20 weeks can diagnose most of them.

If the chance comes back high, your doctor or midwife will talk you through what's next, which may be genetic counselling and a diagnostic test. Screening is offered to everyone, isn't compulsory, and is your choice.
