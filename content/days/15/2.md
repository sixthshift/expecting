---
title: Amniocentesis
read: [amniocentesis]
---
From 15 weeks a diagnostic test becomes possible: amniocentesis. It can confirm whether the baby has a genetic condition or a chromosome difference, and it might be offered after a higher-chance screening result, if you already have a child with one, or if both of you are known carriers. It's your choice whether to have it.

An ultrasound checks the baby and placenta first, then an obstetrician with specific training passes a fine needle through your tummy, guided by ultrasound the whole time, and takes 15 to 30 mL of fluid. It usually takes a few minutes, may be mildly uncomfortable, and you can have someone with you. Mild cramping, spotting or a few drops of fluid afterwards can happen; the risk of miscarriage is very small.

After one, contact your maternity care provider immediately or go to the nearest emergency department for ongoing fluid loss or bleeding, severe cramping for a few hours, or a fever.
