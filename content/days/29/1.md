---
title: The cord blood
read: [cord-blood-banking]
---
After the birth, the blood left in the placenta and cord is normally thrown away. It's a rich source of stem cells, used in transplants for blood cancers, blood disorders and severe immune deficiencies, and in Australia it can be collected and stored.

There are two ways to keep it. Donating to a public AusCord bank is free, and the blood goes to patients who need a transplant; you won't find out who. A private bank charges fees, and keeps it for your baby or other family members. Collection takes about 3 minutes, after the cord is cut, and doesn't hurt you or the baby.

The catch is timing, twice over. Donating has to be arranged before the birth, at a participating hospital, so it's a decision for these weeks. And it can't be done with delayed cord clamping, because the cord must be clamped early to capture as many stem cells as possible.
