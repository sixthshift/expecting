---
title: Up under your ribs
read: [the-uterus-in-pregnancy]
---
Before pregnancy the uterus is pear-shaped, sitting in the middle of your pelvis behind your bladder. It grows enormously: at 20 weeks the top of it, the fundus, is usually roughly level with your belly button, and by 36 weeks it reaches all the way up to your ribcage.

Its wall has a layer of muscle in the middle, and that muscle is already rehearsing: Braxton Hicks are the uterus practising. In labour the same muscles contract in waves from the top down to the cervix, opening it and moving the baby down the birth canal.

Near the end, if the baby engages, moving down into your pelvis, your bump may move down slightly. And after the birth the uterus keeps contracting as it shrinks, which can feel like period pain; you may notice it when you breastfeed.
