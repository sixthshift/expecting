---
title: If your platelets come back low
read: [low-platelets]
---
The full blood count at 26 to 28 weeks checks your platelets as well as your iron. Platelets are tiny blood cells that clump together to stop bleeding, and around 1 in 10 people have a low count in pregnancy. You might have no symptoms; if you do, it may be bleeding gums, a skin rash or small bruises.

Most of the time, in up to 8 in 10 cases, it's gestational thrombocytopenia: caused by the normal changes of pregnancy, usually mild, gone after the birth, and not usually a problem for you or the baby. Other causes need more care, so a low result may bring more tests.

It touches labour in one way: low platelets can raise the chance of bleeding around the spinal cord, so your doctor or midwife will discuss whether an epidural is safe for you. See them straight away if you have any symptoms that concern you.
