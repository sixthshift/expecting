---
title: The checks after the date
read: [past-your-due-date-appointment]
---
Most babies are born between 37 and 42 weeks, and going past the due date is more likely in a first birth, over 30, if you've gone overdue before, or if it runs in the family.

If you do go past, the appointments change shape. The longer a pregnancy goes on, the higher the risk that the placenta won't supply the baby with enough oxygen and nutrients, so your midwife or doctor will recommend regular checks of you and the baby: perhaps twice a week, an ultrasound and a CTG to check the baby's heart rate. These show how the baby is at that moment; they can't predict whether that might change. Past 42 weeks you'll probably be offered monitoring more often.

So movements matter more than ever once you're overdue. Tell your doctor or midwife straight away if they suddenly speed up, slow down or stop. Don't wait.
