---
title: Vitamin K, and your yes
read: [vitamin-k, card-vitamin-k-consent]
---
In the first hours after the birth, someone will ask whether the baby can have vitamin K, because it's only given with your consent. Vitamin K helps blood clot, and babies don't get enough of it in pregnancy or from breast milk. Without it there's a risk of vitamin K deficiency bleeding: very rare, fewer than 1 in 100,000 babies, but very serious, because it can cause bleeding in the brain.

Australian guidelines recommend one injection for every baby soon after birth. Drops are an option, but need 3 doses at the right times, are absorbed less well, and don't suit every baby. Parents can also choose none, and then watch closely for signs of bleeding.

It has been given in Australia for over 30 years with no known problems. Whatever you choose, see your doctor, midwife or child health nurse if the baby has unexplained bleeding or bruising, or yellow skin or eyes after 3 weeks old.
