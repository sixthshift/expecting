---
title: The heel prick
read: [newborn-bloodspot-screening, card-heel-prick-72-hours]
---
Within the first 72 hours, usually when the baby is 48 to 72 hours old, you'll be offered newborn bloodspot screening: the heel prick. A midwife or nurse pricks the baby's heel and puts a few drops of blood on a special filter paper, which goes to a laboratory. It screens for some rare conditions, cystic fibrosis and congenital hypothyroidism among them, that are easier to treat if found early.

It's free, recommended and not compulsory, and it can't be done without your consent. It isn't comfortable for the baby: holding and comforting them helps, and breastfeeding has been shown to soothe them.

If the results are normal, you won't be contacted; you'll only hear if the doctors are concerned. Some babies need a second test, usually because the first didn't give a clear result.
