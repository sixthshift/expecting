---
title: The stretch and sweep
read: [membrane-sweep]
---
Close to or past your due date, you may be offered a stretch and sweep, a membrane sweep, to try to start labour. It's part of a vaginal examination: if the cervix is open, the midwife or doctor sweeps a finger around to separate the membranes from the wall of the uterus. If it's too uncomfortable, you can ask them to stop at any time, and they should. Waiting for labour to start on its own is a choice too.

Afterwards you might have a show, mucus with some blood mixed in, and cramps or contractions, a sign that the hormones that prepare for labour have been released; paracetamol and a warm bath can help. If it works, you can expect labour within 48 hours. If not, it can be repeated.

It isn't offered with a low-lying placenta, a vaginal infection, waters already broken, or a baby who isn't head down, unless you're planning a vaginal breech birth.
