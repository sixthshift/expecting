---
title: What a contraction is like
read: [contractions, timing-contractions]
---
A contraction is the muscle of the uterus tightening and relaxing. It starts like a wave at the top and moves down, builds, peaks for about 15 to 20 seconds, then eases off, and you can rest before the next. People feel them low in the belly like strong period pain, or in the back or thighs.

Braxton Hicks stay irregular and short, and lying down may make them go away. Labour contractions get stronger, longer and closer together, walking may make them stronger, and lying down doesn't stop them. In active labour they usually come every 2 to 3 minutes and last 50 to 60 seconds.

Timing them is a job for the partner: how often, from the start of one to the start of the next, how long each lasts, and how strong. Until 37 weeks, though, contractions or other signs of labour mean calling your midwife or doctor for advice and going straight to hospital.
