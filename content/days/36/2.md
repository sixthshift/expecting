---
title: Colostrum, before the baby
read: [antenatal-expressing]
---
Colostrum, your first milk, has been made since about 16 weeks: thick, sticky and yellowish, and very high in protein, antibodies included. From 36 weeks, some people express it by hand and freeze it in syringes, a store of extra feeds instead of formula top-ups. It can help if you have diabetes, the baby may need special care, or your supply may be low; whether it's a good idea for you is a question for your doctor or midwife.

Not before 36 weeks, because stimulating the breasts may trigger premature labour, and not if you're at risk of it, have a short cervix or a cervical suture, have had bleeding in the pregnancy or have placenta praevia. It's done by hand, and colostrum comes in small amounts, so a little, or nothing at first, is no reason to be discouraged. Stop if you feel contractions or notice vaginal bleeding. It goes in with you, frozen, in a cool bag.
