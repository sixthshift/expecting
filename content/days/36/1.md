---
title: If the baby is still breech
read: [ecv]
---
If the baby is still bottom first around now, the next conversation is an ECV, an external cephalic version: a specially trained obstetrician puts one hand over the baby's bottom and one behind the head and presses to encourage a roll to head down. It's usually done after 37 weeks, or after 36 for a first baby, in a hospital that can do an emergency caesarean.

It may feel uncomfortable, but shouldn't be painful. It works about half the time, more often if you've given birth before, and it doesn't start labour. About 1 in 200 need an emergency caesarean because of complications.

Afterwards, contact your doctor or midwife immediately if you have pain in your abdomen, contractions, bleeding or fluid leaking from your vagina, or the baby moving less than usual. And if your waters break when you've been told the baby isn't head first, call 000 and ask for an ambulance: there's a higher risk of cord prolapse.
