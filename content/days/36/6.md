title: Water, and four stings in the back

Heat and water can ease tension and backache in labour. A warm bath or shower helps at home in early labour, and many hospitals let you labour in a bath, which many people find helps with relaxation and pain; your midwife still needs to monitor you there. It isn't possible for everyone, and a TENS machine can't be used in or around water.

For lower back pain that persists through labour, midwives can give sterile water injections: usually four, just beneath the skin of your lower back. Each stings strongly, like a bee sting, for up to 30 seconds, then the sting goes along with the back pain, for up to two hours, though you'll still feel the contractions. There are no side effects for you or the baby, but not yet enough evidence that they reduce the need for other pain relief.

Not every birth place offers every method, so it's worth asking yours.
