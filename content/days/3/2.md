---
title: One egg, or two
read: [term-zygote, term-monozygotic, term-dizygotic]
---
The fertilised egg has a name, a zygote: an egg cell and a sperm cell joined, holding the DNA of both parents to make a baby.

Twins start here too, in one of two ways. Identical twins happen when one fertilised egg splits in two after fertilisation. They have the same genes, which is why they're always the same sex, and there are different types, depending on whether they share a placenta or an amniotic sac.

Fraternal twins happen when two eggs are fertilised by two different sperm. They're also called non-identical twins: they don't usually look identical, they may or may not be the same sex, and each has a separate placenta and sac as they develop in the uterus.
