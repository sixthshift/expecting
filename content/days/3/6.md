---
title: Warm, not hot
read: [hot-baths, spas-and-hot-tubs, saunas]
---
A significant rise in your temperature may affect your unborn baby, so heat is worth thinking about early. Pregnancy also makes you feel warmer, with hormonal changes and more blood flowing to your skin, and your body may be less effective at cooling itself down.

That's why spas, hot tubs, heated hydrotherapy pools and saunas are on the avoid list until after the birth: they can raise your core temperature and lead to overheating, dehydration or fainting. If you've already had a soak or a session, MotherSafe says a short one is unlikely to raise your temperature enough to cause problems; if you're worried, ask your midwife or doctor.

A warm bath is perfectly safe, as long as you don't get too hot: warm water rather than hot, and slow getting up afterwards, since getting up quickly from a hot bath is when you're most likely to feel faint.
