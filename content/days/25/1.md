---
title: The glucose test
read: [glucose-tolerance-test, gestational-diabetes]
---
Gestational diabetes is diabetes that starts in pregnancy, and it affects around 1 in 10 pregnancies. The placenta makes hormones that help the baby grow but also reduce the action of insulin, and if your body can't keep up with the extra insulin it needs, your blood sugar runs high. It often causes no symptoms, which is why everyone is offered screening between 24 and 28 weeks.

The usual test is the oral glucose tolerance test: 8 to 10 hours of fasting, usually overnight, then a blood test, a sugary drink, and more blood tests at 1 and 2 hours. You'll usually need to stay at the lab for the whole test.

If it does show gestational diabetes, it doesn't mean the baby will be born with diabetes. Managed well, it lowers the risks for you and the baby.
