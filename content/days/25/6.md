---
title: Pins and needles in your hands
read: [carpal-tunnel]
---
Up to 1 in 2 people get carpal tunnel syndrome in pregnancy, more often in the second and third trimesters. Hormones cause fluid to build up and soften the ligaments, and swelling in the wrist squashes the nerve that runs through it: pins and needles, numbness, pain or weakness, mostly in the thumb, index and middle fingers, often worse at night or early in the morning. In most cases it gets better within 4 weeks of the birth.

Raising your hands, keeping your wrists straight, and not sleeping with a wrist bent under your chin all help. See your doctor or midwife if you have it, as early treatment helps; a wrist splint, especially overnight, is one of the best ways to relieve it, though the evidence for it is limited.

Sudden, severe swelling in your hands, feet or face after 20 weeks is different: contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately.
