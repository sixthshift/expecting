---
title: If an induction is offered
read: [induction]
---
An induction is when your midwife or doctor starts labour for you, with your consent; about one in three have one. Going past the due date, at 41 to 42 weeks, is one of the common reasons. It's an offer: the decision is always yours, and you can change your mind or ask for a second opinion. Some people choose to wait, and your doctor may ask you to weigh that the risk of stillbirth and other problems rises the longer a pregnancy goes past term.

What it's like: induced contractions can build quickly and be more intense, and you're more likely to have continuous monitoring and to want pain relief sooner.

The questions to bring: why now, what happens if we wait, what else could we do, how would you do it and what are the risks, and what pain relief is there? And whatever you choose, tell them straight away if you notice the baby moving less.
