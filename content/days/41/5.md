---
title: If labour is slow
read: [slow-progress]
---
Sometimes labour takes longer than expected, more likely in a first birth or after an induction or an epidural. The evidence on what's normal and what's slow isn't strong, and experts define it differently; one measure is the cervix opening less than 2 cm in four hours once labour is active.

You can try changing position, moving around, relaxation, and pain relief if you need it. Your team may then offer breaking the waters or an oxytocin drip to make contractions stronger, or turning the baby's head by hand into a better position. If the baby needs help to be born, forceps or a vacuum, or a caesarean, may be recommended.

Slow labour can lead to complications, including severe tears, heavy bleeding after the birth, infection and too little oxygen for the baby, which is why you're both watched closely. Your team explains the risks and benefits of each option, and it's your choice whether to have any.
