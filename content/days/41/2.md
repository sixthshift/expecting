---
title: Getting the cervix ready
read: [prostaglandin, balloon-catheter, card-induction-takes-time]
---
An induction usually starts with the cervix. A vaginal examination shows how ready it is, and that decides the method. Often it's prostaglandin, a hormone placed in the vagina to soften and open the cervix: a gel, which may be given in up to three doses, or a pessary on a tape, like a tampon, that releases it slowly over 12 to 24 hours. Once it's in, you stay in hospital until the baby is born.

A balloon catheter is the other way: a thin tube with a small water-filled balloon, placed in the cervix, whose pressure softens and opens it. It's the preferred way after a previous caesarean. It stays in for up to 12 hours, and some hospitals let you go home and come back.

Once an induction starts, the birth can be a few hours or more than a day away. Tell your doctor or midwife straightaway if you start bleeding or feel the baby moving less.
