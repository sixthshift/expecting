---
title: The waters, then the drip
read: [breaking-the-waters, oxytocin-drip]
---
Once the cervix is soft and open enough, the next step is often breaking the waters: during a vaginal examination, and only with your consent, a small hook-like instrument makes a hole in the sac around the baby. That lets the head come lower and press on the cervix, which may be enough to start labour. The examination can be uncomfortable.

If contractions don't start, an oxytocin drip follows, and most people need one. It's a synthetic version of the hormone your body makes in labour, given through a drip in your arm and turned up until contractions are strong and regular. The baby is monitored on a CTG throughout, which makes moving about hard: you can stand or sit, but not have a bath. Contractions can become strong quickly, and there's usually no restriction on the pain relief you can have.

Tell your doctor or midwife straightaway if you start bleeding or feel the baby moving less.
