A baby born after the due date can look a little different. It's very common for vernix, the white, sticky coating that protected their skin in the uterus, to last until birth, but past the due date there's likely to be much less, and an overdue baby's skin may be a little dry and cracked. There may be blood on their skin too, and small pink or red stork marks on the forehead, eyelids or neck, which fade gradually.

The first poo is black and sticky, meconium, then over the first days it turns greenish-brown and then mustard yellow, more yellow and formed in a formula-fed baby. There should be at least one wet nappy within 24 hours of birth.

Babies normally lose up to 10% of their birth weight in the first few days, then start to regain it; they're weighed again a few days after birth to check.
