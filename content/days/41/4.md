---
title: How the baby is watched in labour
read: [monitoring-in-labour]
---
Through labour, the baby's heart rate is checked to see how they're coping, and most cope well. There are two main ways, and a choice about which.

In a labour with no complications, the usual way is listening at intervals: a hand-held doppler on your tummy for about a minute, every 15 to 30 minutes in early labour and every 5 minutes or after each contraction once you're pushing. It leaves you free to move, and works anywhere, in water included.

Continuous CTG, two discs held on by belts, is recommended when something in the pregnancy or labour might affect how the baby copes, and you'll need it with an epidural or an oxytocin drip. It picks up changes more readily, but keeps you connected; it's worth asking if wireless CTG is available. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says routine CTG isn't recommended in an uncomplicated labour, because it can lead to unplanned interventions. Either way, it's decided with you.
