---
title: Tight shoes, tight rings
read: [swollen-feet-and-ankles, sudden-swelling]
---
About 8 in 10 pregnant people get swollen ankles, feet and fingers, more noticeable late in the day and late in the pregnancy, and shoes and rings may feel tight. It usually goes down overnight. Resting with your legs raised, loose shoes and compression stockings in the day all ease it.

Swelling that comes on suddenly, or is severe, in your face, hands or feet is different: it can be a sign of pre-eclampsia. Contact your doctor, midwife or maternity hospital immediately for sudden swelling, especially with a severe or dull headache that won't go, vision changes, severe pain just below your ribs, feeling sick or vomiting, or heartburn antacids don't fix; for leg swelling that doesn't improve overnight; and for one leg more swollen than the other, with pain, redness or warmth. A headache with vision changes: call 000. Swelling early in the day, or more than slight: tell your midwife or doctor.
