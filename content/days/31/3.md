---
title: The thinking part switches on
read: [the-babys-brain]
---
The baby's brain started forming in the first weeks of pregnancy, and in the second trimester it grew fast: by 23 weeks the baby could recognise light, sound and pain. In this trimester the cerebral cortex, the part for thinking and planning, becomes more active, getting the baby ready for learning. By birth they can hear, will know your voice, and can see a little.

What you eat still feeds it. Iodine is key to the baby's brain growth, and the Royal Women's Hospital recommends a pregnancy multivitamin with at least 150 micrograms. Omega-3 fats, in fish, especially oily fish, and in walnuts, chia and linseed, are needed for brain, nerve and eye development. If you eat no animal foods you'll need a vitamin B12 supplement.

And the brain keeps developing for years after birth, through use: talking, singing, reading, play and loving relationships all help it form new connections.
