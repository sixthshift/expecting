---
title: Calcium, mostly from the fridge
read: [calcium, milk-and-yoghurt]
---
Calcium matters for the baby's growth, but your body adapts to make more efficient use of the calcium in your usual food, so if you eat a healthy diet there's usually no need for a supplement. MotherSafe's aim is 3 to 4 serves of dairy a day, or alternatives fortified with calcium. If that isn't possible, a supplement is worth talking over with your doctor or midwife; some contain vitamin D as well.

There's one group for whom it's more than that. The national pregnancy care guidelines strongly recommend a calcium supplement for anyone at risk of high blood pressure in pregnancy, such as pre-eclampsia, so it's worth asking whether that's you.

Milk, yoghurt and cream are fine as long as they're pasteurised: they bring calcium and iodine, with protein and several vitamins. Check the dates and keep them in the fridge. Raw milk and kefir are on the NSW Food Authority's "do not drink" list.
