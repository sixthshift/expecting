---
title: The litter tray is the partner's
read: [cat-litter]
---
If there's a cat in the house, it can stay. Cat faeces can carry the parasite that causes toxoplasmosis, a common infection that usually doesn't make healthy people unwell. In pregnancy, though, it can cross the placenta and infect the baby, affecting their brain, vision and hearing, and it can lead to miscarriage or stillbirth. Early infection is less likely to reach the baby, but more severe if it does.

So the litter tray is the partner's until after the birth: emptied daily, and disinfected with boiling water regularly. If you have to do it yourself, it's with gloves, and hands washed well with soap afterwards.

Contact your doctor or midwife if you've been in direct contact with cat faeces, raw meat, or contaminated soil or water. With flu-like symptoms (fever, headache, aching muscles, swollen glands, tiredness), see your doctor or midwife; if you think you may have toxoplasmosis, see your doctor immediately.
