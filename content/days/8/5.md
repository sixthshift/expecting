---
title: Fish, two or three times a week
read: [safe-fish, high-mercury-fish, canned-fish]
---
Fish is one of the foods worth keeping, not cutting. It has protein, iodine, vitamin B12 and omega-3 fatty acids, which matter for the development of the baby's nervous system, and it's safe to eat two to three serves a week in pregnancy, a serve being 150 g. Canned tuna and canned salmon count, and are low in mercury.

Most fish in Australia is low in mercury. Only a few build up enough to limit, because a growing baby's nervous system is especially vulnerable to it: shark (flake) and billfish such as swordfish and marlin, no more than one portion a fortnight with no other fish that fortnight; orange roughy and catfish, one a week with no other fish that week. Cooking, canning and freezing don't reduce mercury, so ask what the fish is at the takeaway.

Fresh fish is cooked to at least 63°C and eaten straight away, and an opened tin is used within a day.
