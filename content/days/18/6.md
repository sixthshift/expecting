---
title: Light-headed, and why
read: [dizziness-and-fainting]
---
Blood pressure commonly falls below normal in the first trimester and keeps falling in the second, rising back towards your usual level by the birth. Low blood pressure can leave you tired and feeling faint. It's common, and not usually a cause for concern, though in rare cases it can be dangerous.

You're most likely to feel it getting up quickly from a chair or a hot bath, or standing too long. Standing up slowly, plenty of water, eating at regular intervals and lying on your side rather than your back all help. At the first sign of faintness, sitting or lying down until it passes is the advice.

If you feel dizzy or faint, call your midwife, or the Pregnancy, Birth and Baby helpline on 1800 882 436. Contact your doctor or midwife if it happens often, and mention any symptoms of low blood pressure at your next visit.
