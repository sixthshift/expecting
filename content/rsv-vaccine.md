RSV (respiratory syncytial virus) is a common cold-like virus, but in babies under 6 months it's a leading cause of hospital stays, from bronchiolitis and pneumonia. Many people have never heard of the vaccine that helps.

A single dose of the maternal RSV vaccine (Abrysvo) is recommended **from 28 weeks in each pregnancy**, and it's free under the National Immunisation Program. Antibodies pass through the placenta, so the baby is protected from birth for up to about 6 months. It works best if the baby is born at least 2 weeks after the vaccine; if not, the baby is recommended an infant RSV antibody (nirsevimab) for extra protection.

Some older material gives the window as 28–36 weeks; the current Handbook says that if 36 weeks has passed, it's given as soon as possible. Ask your midwife or OB.
