Nitrous oxide gas mixed with oxygen, also called Entonox, laughing gas or just "gas", is breathed in through a mouthpiece or mask connected by a tube to a gas supply.

## How you use it

You use it only during contractions. It works best if you start breathing it about 30 seconds before a contraction begins, or at the start of one. Breathe deeply at your normal rate, not faster, and stop when the contraction eases. It can take a few contractions to get the hang of it.

Only you should hold the mouthpiece or mask, so you can stop straight away when you don't need it, you've had enough, you feel any side effects or you feel drowsy.

## What it does

It can reduce pain intensity and anxiety, and help you relax. RANZCOG says it helps take the edge off the pain. It's only partly effective for some people and may only dull the pain, and it doesn't work for everyone. It's fine to stop and try something else.

- You control the amount, and it can be used at any stage of labour.
- It can be used with some other forms of pain relief, and you can move and change position between contractions.
- It quickly clears out of your body within a few minutes.

## Baby and side effects

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says nitrous oxide has no known harmful effects on your baby and doesn't affect breastfeeding, so skin-to-skin and feeding can happen straight away. Side effects for you can include dizziness, light-headedness, nausea and vomiting. Breathing in too much can make you drowsy or briefly unconscious, but you usually recover quickly once you stop. Your midwife checks on you while you use it.

## Who can't use it

People with certain medical conditions, including vitamin B12 deficiency, recent ear surgery, conditions that cause trapped air in the body (such as pneumothorax or blocked middle ears), or trouble keeping oxygen levels up because of a chest infection or lung disease. You also need to be able to hold the mouthpiece, which can get harder as you tire.
