Services Australia says you need to enrol your newborn in Medicare as soon as possible. It's an after-the-birth job, but it's quick if you know how it works.

**The form.** After the birth, your hospital or midwife gives you a Parent Pack with a Newborn Child Declaration form in it. A doctor or midwife must sign the form on the last page. That declaration is your proof of birth for Medicare.

**Two ways to send it:**

- **With a family payment claim.** If you're claiming Parental Leave Pay or Family Tax Benefit (including the Newborn Upfront Payment and Newborn Supplement), use the completed declaration in that claim, in myGov or the Express Plus Centrelink app.
- **On its own.** If you're not claiming a family payment, upload it through your Medicare online account in myGov, or enrol the baby in the myGov app.

Either way, Services Australia enrols the baby, adds them to your Medicare card and to the Australian Immunisation Register, and posts a new card that should arrive in 3 to 4 weeks. Enrolling online, you can get a digital version of the updated card straight away in the myGov app. You can also register the baby for My Health Record and your Medicare Safety Net family.

**Both cards.** The baby can go on both parents' Medicare cards, but on 2 cards at most. That takes a separate form: Application to copy or transfer from one Medicare card to another.

**Your own card first.** If you're still on your own parents' Medicare card, you need to transfer to your own card before you can enrol the baby.

**Lost the declaration?** You'll need the baby's birth certificate, or another proof of birth such as a letter from the doctor or midwife who attended the birth.
