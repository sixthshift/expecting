Yes, for most. Throat lozenges with antibacterial and anaesthetic (numbing) ingredients are considered safe in pregnancy at the recommended dose. Two ingredients are the exception: benzydamine and povidone-iodine.

## Why

- **Benzydamine** is an anti-inflammatory. KEMH says it shouldn't be used in pregnancy unless your doctor directs it; MotherSafe says lozenges containing it are best avoided.
- **Povidone-iodine**, in some gargles, may affect the developing baby's thyroid. KEMH and healthdirect say to avoid it. MotherSafe says iodine gargles can affect the baby's thyroid if used long-term, and a few days at the recommended dose isn't expected to, but they're still generally not recommended.

## What to do

- **Start simple:** drink warm lemon and honey, or gargle warm salty water.
- **Lozenges are fine** at the recommended dose, but don't overdo them: too many can cause diarrhoea.
- **Read the label** for benzydamine or povidone-iodine, and ask your pharmacist if you're unsure.
- **After the birth,** MotherSafe says benzydamine lozenges may be used while breastfeeding.
