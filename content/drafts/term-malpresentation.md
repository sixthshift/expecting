Malpresentation is when your baby isn't head first, with the crown of their head against the cervix (vertex presentation), as birth approaches. Breech is the most common kind; others are face and brow presentation, a transverse (sideways) or oblique (diagonal) lie, and an unstable lie, where the baby keeps changing position after 36 weeks.

More than 9 in 10 babies in Australia are head first at birth. Malpresentation makes a more complex vaginal birth or a caesarean more likely. If your waters break and you have been told that your baby is not in a head-first position, seek medical help immediately; there is a serious risk of cord prolapse.
