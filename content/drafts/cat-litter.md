There's no need to give away your cat. But avoid changing the litter yourself while you're pregnant: ask someone else to do it. If you have to, wear gloves and wash your hands well with soap afterwards.

## Why

Cat faeces can carry the parasite that causes toxoplasmosis, a common infection that usually doesn't make healthy people unwell. In pregnancy, though, it can cross the placenta and infect the baby. Early infection is less likely to reach the baby but more severe if it does; later infection is more likely to reach the baby but milder. It can affect the baby's brain, vision and hearing, and can lead to miscarriage or stillbirth.

## What to do

- **Pass on the litter tray.** Make sure it's emptied daily and disinfected with boiling water regularly.
- **Feed your cat store-bought or cooked food,** and discourage it from hunting; a bell on its collar can help.
- **Wear gloves for gardening** and wash your hands with soap after touching soil. Cover sandpits when they're not in use.
- **Contact your doctor or midwife** if you've been in direct contact with cat faeces, raw meat, or contaminated soil or water. A blood test can show whether you've been infected.
- **Flu-like symptoms** (fever, headache, aching muscles, swollen glands, tiredness)? See your doctor or midwife. healthdirect says that if you're pregnant and think you may have toxoplasmosis, see your doctor immediately.

**For the partner:** the litter tray is yours until after the birth.
