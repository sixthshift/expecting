## What's usual

Itching is common in pregnancy. As the baby grows, the skin over your belly stretches and can feel itchy. An itchy vagina can be a sign of thrush, and an itchy bottom can be haemorrhoids; both are common in pregnancy.

Some itches come with a rash. Eczema and prurigo (a red, bumpy rash on the belly, arms or legs) usually show up in the first or second trimester. Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy (PEP), a very itchy, red, bumpy rash that often starts in stretch marks, usually starts late in the third trimester of a first pregnancy.

An itch **without** a rash is different. Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy is a liver condition that causes itchy skin with no rash, usually starting on the palms and soles and spreading. It's rare, usually starts in the third trimester, and can be dangerous for the baby.

For relief: a cool shower, loose cotton clothing, a moisturiser (such as one with menthol). Check with your doctor before using any treatment for the itch.

## Contact your hospital now

After 20 weeks, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have extreme itchiness of your skin, especially your hands and feet.

## See your doctor or midwife

If you're pregnant and feel itchy, see your doctor or midwife; they can check for the conditions that cause an itch or a rash. Contact your doctor or midwife for persistent itching, as it can be a sign of a more serious problem.
