Hormone changes can trigger acne, and it may flare in the first trimester and usually improves or may even go away by the third. It won't affect your pregnancy or your baby, but what you treat it with matters.

## Why

Some acne medicines can harm your baby and must be avoided, including retinoids (medicines similar to vitamin A) as pills, creams or ointments, and spironolactone.

Safe to use: treatments containing benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid spot treatments and glycolic acid. Avoid salicylic acid peels.

## What to do

- **Wash gently** with a soap-free cleanser, and keep your hair off your face.
- **Choose oil-free** or matte make-up and non-comedogenic moisturiser, and take make-up off every night.
- **Don't pick,** pop or scratch.
- **Use good sun protection.**
- **Ask before you buy.** Check with your midwife, doctor or pharmacist before starting any new medicine or skin product.
- **See your doctor or midwife** if over-the-counter products aren't controlling it, if it's moderate to severe, or if it's getting you down. They may prescribe a cream such as clindamycin, or for severe acne, erythromycin tablets.
