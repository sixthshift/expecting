Your baby is checked a few times in the first days. Most of it is quick and happens while you're still in hospital or with a midwife at home.

## Straight after the birth

- **The Apgar score.** A check of your baby's heart rate, breathing, colour, muscle tone and reflexes, done at 1 minute and 5 minutes after birth, and sometimes at 10 minutes. The maximum is 10, and 7 or above usually means your baby is doing well. It helps the team know whether your baby needs extra care straight away, and it doesn't predict your baby's health or development later on.
- **Weight, length and head size.** Your midwife weighs and measures your baby and records the numbers on growth charts. They're taken again at your baby's later health checks.

## In the first days

- **The heel prick (newborn bloodspot screening).** Done within the first 72 hours, usually when your baby is 48 to 72 hours old. A midwife or nurse pricks your baby's heel and puts a few drops of blood on filter paper. It screens for some rare conditions that are easier to treat if found early. It's free, recommended and not compulsory, and holding or breastfeeding your baby eases the sting. If the results are normal you won't be contacted.
- **The hearing screen.** Offered at about the same time, because hearing loss isn't easy to see in a newborn. You're given the result as soon as the test is done.
- **Your baby's Infant Health Record.** Every baby in Australia is issued one at birth, to track growth and development. Your baby's first health check is usually at birth, then again in the first 1 to 4 weeks, with follow-ups at 6 to 8 weeks, 6 months and 12 months.

## Your agreement

You'll be asked for permission before the heel prick and the hearing screen. Your midwife or doctor will give you information first so you can ask questions.
