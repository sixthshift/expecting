There are two ways to get Parental Leave Pay: Services Australia pays it to you directly, or your employer pays it to you in line with your regular payment cycle.

## When your employer pays it

Your employer pays it if all of these apply:

- you've worked for them for at least a year before the birth
- they're based in Australia
- you've asked for a **continuous block** of payment
- you'll stay employed by them until at least the end of the block
- the block is within a year of the birth

The continuous block must be your first Parental Leave Pay for the child, start on a weekday, run 5 weekdays a week with no weekends, and last at least **8 weeks in a row**. If you choose a block based on part-time days, or one that includes weekends, you still get paid, but by Services Australia.

If you haven't worked there a year but meet everything else, your employer can still pay if you both agree. Tell Services Australia in your claim, and your employer must register and opt in **before you submit your claim**, or they can't pay it.

Changing your block's start date or days during it, or working a weekday in it, may move payment to Services Australia, and you can't switch it back.

## When Services Australia pays it

Services Australia pays you if you don't have a job, your employer isn't paying it, you're self-employed, or you get an income support payment. It pays into your bank account every 2 weeks, after the days you've chosen.

## The tax

Parental Leave Pay is taxable: declare it in your tax return.

- **From your employer:** PAYG tax at your usual rate. They don't have to add superannuation, but can.
- **From Services Australia:** 15% PAYG tax, unless you ask for a different rate. Depending on your total income, 15% might not be enough, and you'd pay the rest at tax time. You can raise the rate online.

If your baby is stillborn or dies, you can ask Services Australia to pay you instead of your employer.
