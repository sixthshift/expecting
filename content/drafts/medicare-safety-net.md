The Medicare Safety Nets can lower your out-of-pocket costs for **out-of-hospital services**, such as seeing a doctor or specialist, and some tests and scans like blood tests.

## How it works

When you spend over a certain amount in a calendar year, Medicare gives you a higher amount back. The tally runs from **1 January to 31 December**, and the threshold amounts are set each year from 1 January, so check the current figures on the Services Australia thresholds page.

There are three thresholds:

- **Original Medicare Safety Net**, for everyone in Medicare: once your gap amounts reach it, you get back 100% of the schedule fee for out-of-hospital services.
- **Extended Medicare Safety Net (general)**, for everyone in Medicare: once your out-of-pocket amounts reach it, you get back 80% of out-of-pocket costs, or up to the benefit caps.
- **Extended Medicare Safety Net (concessional and Family Tax Benefit Part A)**: a lower threshold for concession cardholders and families eligible for FTB Part A.

**Only verified payments count**: ones you've paid in full before you claim from Medicare. Unpaid accounts count only once you've paid them.

## Register as a family

If you're on your own with no dependants, you're registered automatically. **A couple can register as a family to combine your costs**, so you're more likely to reach the thresholds sooner. Even if you're both on the same Medicare card, you still need to register. You only register once, with the Medicare Safety Net couples and families registration form or by calling the Medicare program line.

When your baby is born, you can add them to your Safety Net family when you enrol them in Medicare. If your family situation changes, such as having a child, let Medicare know.
