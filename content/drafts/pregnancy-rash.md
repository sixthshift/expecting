## What's usual

A few itchy rashes are linked to pregnancy. None of these three is harmful to you or your baby.

- **Eczema**: dry, scaly, itchy, red skin, often on the face, wrists, elbows or knees. It's the most common skin condition you can develop in pregnancy. Some get it for the first time (atopic eruption of pregnancy), others a flare-up. If it starts in pregnancy it usually gets better after the birth.
- **Prurigo of pregnancy**: a dry, bumpy rash that can cover large areas of your stomach, arms or legs, usually starting in the first or second trimester. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby calls it the most common itchy rash of pregnancy. It usually improves after the birth.
- **Polymorphic eruption of pregnancy (PEP, or PUPPP)**: a red, bumpy, itchy rash that starts in or near stretch marks on your tummy. It affects about 1 in 160 pregnancies, usually between 36 and 39 weeks. It usually goes within a few weeks of the birth.

To ease the itch: moisturise often, have one short, cool shower a day, use soap-free wash, wear loose, soft clothes and stay cool. An oatmeal bath or calamine can help. Check with your pharmacist that any cream or antihistamine is safe in pregnancy; your doctor may suggest a steroid cream.

## Contact your hospital now

After 20 weeks, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have extreme itchiness of your skin, especially your hands and feet.

## See your doctor or midwife

- **Any itchy rash or itchy skin in pregnancy.** Some other causes of itch, such as intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, can be harmful to your baby, and need to be ruled out.
- Treatment isn't working, or the rash is making you anxious.
