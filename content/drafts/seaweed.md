In moderation. FSANZ says pregnant and breastfeeding people should eat no more than one serve a week of brown seaweed, such as kelp.

## Why

**Iodine.** Your baby needs iodine for a developing brain and nervous system, but brown seaweed can be naturally so high in it that eating a lot can make you ill. An Australian survey found most seaweed and seaweed products have safe levels of iodine, but some, particularly kombu, had very high levels, which can be unsafe for pregnant people.

**Arsenic in hijiki.** In an Australian survey, most seaweed was below the limit for inorganic arsenic; one hijiki sample was over it. FSANZ says people who regularly eat a lot of hijiki face a higher potential health risk. Imported hijiki is tested at the border for arsenic, and brown seaweed for iodine.

## What to do

- **One serve a week** of brown seaweed, at most.
- **Kombu** is the one FSANZ singles out for very high iodine.
- **Don't rely on seaweed for iodine.** Kelp and seaweed supplements aren't recommended, because the amount of iodine in them varies. The NHMRC recommends a 150 microgram iodine supplement each day in pregnancy instead.
- **Have a thyroid condition?** Ask your doctor before taking an iodine supplement.
