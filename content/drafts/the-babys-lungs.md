Your baby is still getting all their oxygen from the placenta, but they practise breathing in the uterus, and the amniotic fluid they swallow and pass back out helps their lungs develop.

**Surfactant.** This is a substance that helps the lungs stay inflated when your baby is breathing air after birth. The pages describe it arriving gradually: at week 23 the lungs have started to produce it, at week 24 they're growing branches and getting ready to produce it, and by week 28, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says the lungs are mature enough to breathe air and are producing surfactant. The pages are not consistent on the exact week, so treat it as somewhere between weeks 23 and 28.

**Full maturity.** By week 37 your baby is considered full term, with fully mature lungs.

## If a baby is born early

At 28 weeks the page says a baby would have a good chance of surviving but would most likely need help to breathe, with a higher chance of having a disability. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says many babies born before 32 weeks may need help breathing and are usually cared for in a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) at first. Breathing problems are among the health problems premature babies may have.

If you're at risk of an early birth, your doctor or midwife may offer injections of corticosteroids or other medicines, to reduce your baby's risk of some complications of being born very early, such as breathing difficulties.
