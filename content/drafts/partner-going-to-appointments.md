Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's a good idea to be as involved as you can throughout the pregnancy, and its first suggestion is to offer to go with your partner to antenatal appointments with doctors or midwives. It also suggests that you do your own research and share what you learn, and talk about how you're both feeling.

## Later in pregnancy

Check-ups become more frequent towards the end of pregnancy, and it suggests going along to them. It's also a chance to listen to your baby's heartbeat, learn what's going to happen at the birth, attend antenatal classes together and tour the place where your baby will be born.

## Time off

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says a pregnant employee is entitled to take time off for check-ups, scans and tests. For your own time off, it suggests planning some leave so you can be there for the birth and support your family; what you're entitled to depends on your award, agreement or contract.

## If you feel left out

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says some partners feel left out during pregnancy, with all the attention going to the pregnant person. The Gidget Foundation lists common experiences for dads and non-birthing partners: feeling helpless when the birthing parent is uncomfortable or in pain, unsure about parenting, loss from changes to your relationships or lifestyle, concerns about the changing relationship, and a belief you should be "the strong one", while struggling to ask for support.

Partners can experience depression and anxiety too. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to look after your own physical and mental health, to talk openly with your partner, family or friends, and to speak up before your feelings build up.
