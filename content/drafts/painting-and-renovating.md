With care. The risk of fumes from modern household paints harming your baby is low. The bigger risks are solvent-based paints and old paintwork that may contain lead.

## Why

Paint containing lead was used in many Australian houses before 1971 (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby; healthdirect says homes built before 1970). There is no safe level of lead exposure, and lead can cross the placenta to your baby. It isn't clear how much paint exposure is safe, so it's best to be cautious.

## What to do

- **Stay away from renovations that disturb lead-based paint**, including stripping or sanding old paintwork. In an older home you can have the paint tested, and removed by a professional if needed.
- **Use water-based paint**, not solvent-based.
- **Open all the windows and doors.**
- **Cover up**: long sleeves and trousers, gloves, a face mask and goggles.
- **Don't eat or drink in the room**, and wash your hands when you finish.
- **Arsenic-treated timber**: wash your hands after touching it, don't breathe in its sawdust or smoke, and don't burn it.
- **If fumes worry you**, consider skipping painting and decorating while pregnant, particularly in the first trimester, and hand the job to someone else. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby also suggests considering not renovating at all while pregnant or breastfeeding.
