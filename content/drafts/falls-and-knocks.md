Get it checked. Even a minor injury in pregnancy can lead to complications, so it needs to be assessed.

**Call 000 and ask for an ambulance** if you've had an injury to your abdomen and you have pain in your abdomen, a firm or tightening uterus, or vaginal bleeding. Tell the operator you're pregnant.

## Why

The most common serious complication after a blow to the abdomen is placental abruption, when the placenta separates from the uterus before birth. Other complications include preterm labour and bleeding from the baby's blood supply into yours.

## What to do

- **Any injury to your abdomen** (a fall, a bump, a car accident): go to the nearest hospital and tell the staff you're pregnant. An obstetrician will check you and the baby. You may need to stay at least 4 hours so the baby's heart rate can be monitored for signs of abruption.
- **If it isn't an emergency,** you can contact your GP, obstetrician or an Urgent Care Clinic first, and contact your midwife or doctor immediately after any trauma to your abdomen.
- **After any injury, even a minor one,** go to hospital straight away if the baby isn't moving as much as normal, you have vaginal bleeding, or you have pain in your abdomen.
- **Contractions or your waters breaking** after an injury: contact your doctor. If you can't wait, try an Urgent Care Clinic; in an emergency, call 000.
- **Rhesus negative?** After an accident, tell the doctor: you may need an anti-D injection.
