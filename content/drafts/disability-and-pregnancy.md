More than 15% of Australian households have at least one parent with a disability: physical, sensory, intellectual, a mental health condition or a chronic illness. Parents with disability are highly capable, and the creativity, adaptability and problem-solving they've built navigating a world not designed for them are real strengths. This applies whichever of you has the disability.

## The assumptions you may meet

Parents with disability often face discrimination, and some people assume a disability affects your ability to parent. It can come from family, from strangers, and from health services: some providers focus more on your disability than on your ordinary pregnancy and postnatal needs, and Gidget notes this happens more often when a disability could be passed on. That pressure to keep "proving" yourself is exhausting, and it can put people off asking for help.

## What to look for

Many health services don't automatically accommodate parents with disability, so it's worth seeking out professionals and groups that are accessible and disability-aware:

- wheelchair-accessible rooms and spaces
- an Auslan interpreter
- information in Easy English, Braille, audio or large print
- adaptive parenting equipment; a specialised occupational therapist can help

The NDIS doesn't always cover adaptive equipment for parenting.

## Build your network

Every parent needs a support network, and for parents with disability it's vital. Groups that can help include Disability Maternity Care, Positive Powerful Parents, Women with Disabilities Australia, People with Disability Australia, the Disabled Parenting Project and the Disabled Mum Group online, and the Royal Women's Hospital's Women with Individual Needs Clinic in Melbourne.

## When to get help

Feeling overwhelmed, prolonged stress, or signs of depression or anxiety are strong signs that outside support would help. Getting help early stops things escalating. The Gidget Foundation offers free psychological therapy for expecting and new parents, in person or by telehealth, subject to Medicare eligibility.
