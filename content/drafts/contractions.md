A contraction is the muscle of your uterus tightening and then relaxing. In labour, contractions open (dilate) your cervix and move your baby down, until the cervix is fully open at 10 cm.

## What they feel like

Each contraction starts like a wave at the top of the uterus and moves downward. It builds up, makes your uterus feel tight, peaks for about 15 to 20 seconds, then eases off and disappears before the next one. You can rest between them; the pain eases in between.

Everyone feels them differently, from slight discomfort to intense pain. You might feel them:

- low in your abdomen, like strong period pain
- in your lower back or thighs
- in your back, spreading around to the front and down your thighs

At first they're usually spaced apart; as labour establishes they get stronger, longer and closer together. In active labour they usually come every 2 to 3 minutes and last 50 to 60 seconds. With a first baby it may take a few hours to reach active labour.

## Real or practice?

Braxton Hicks ("practice contractions") can happen through pregnancy but don't open the cervix.

| Braxton Hicks | Labour contractions |
|---|---|
| Irregular and short (usually about 30 seconds) | May start irregular, then become regular and longer |
| Usually uncomfortable, not painful | Usually more painful as they go on |
| Walking doesn't make them stronger | Walking may make them stronger |
| Lying down may make them go away | Lying down doesn't make them go away |
| Don't get stronger or closer together | Get stronger and closer together |

## When they start

If you've had a normal pregnancy and you're comfortable, you can usually stay home and rest in early labour. A warm bath or shower, heat, a TENS machine, slow breathing, changing position and gentle movement can help. Contact your midwife or doctor any time you're unsure.

**Contact your birth unit or midwife** if your waters break, your contractions become painful and regular (2 or more in 10 minutes), you need extra support with the pain, you've planned a caesarean and you're in labour, your baby's movements change, or you have any concerns.

## When to get help

**Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance** if your labour is progressing quickly and you think you may not make it to your planned birthing place in time, you have heavy bleeding from your vagina, or you have a severe headache or blurred vision (signs of pre-eclampsia).

If you have contractions or other signs of labour **before 37 weeks**, call your midwife or doctor for advice and go straight to hospital.
