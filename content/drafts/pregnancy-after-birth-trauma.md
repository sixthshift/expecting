If your last birth felt traumatic, it was traumatic, and it's absolutely understandable to feel fearful about being pregnant again or about the birth ahead. A previous birth trauma can influence how you experience the next birth, and getting early care and support can help. It's never too late to ask for help, even months or years on.

## Work through the last birth

Processing your last birth may well reduce your fear of the next one. If you're not sure about something that happened, meet with your doctor or midwife: talking to the people involved in your care can help you understand what happened and why. If you're unhappy with the care you received, you can contact your birth hospital or your state or territory's health complaints body.

A social worker, counsellor, psychologist or psychiatrist can help. Your doctor, midwife or child health nurse can refer you.

## Choose your team

Birth Trauma Australia recommends, ideally, a team of a midwife, a doctor, a pelvic health physio and a mental health clinician, though this isn't readily available through public services. Look for a care provider who treats you as an individual and supports your choices. Being on the same page as your team lets you make plans for possible situations and outcomes: "if X happens, we'll try our best to do Y."

## Ask for trauma-informed care

Trauma-informed care recognises past trauma and responds to it, so you feel physically and emotionally safe. It uses neutral, supportive language, and you should never feel judged. It's collaborative: you're supported to make informed choices, and informed consent means you agree to care while understanding the potential benefits and risks. It helps to write down your concerns before appointments and bring a trusted person.

## How you'll give birth

A previous birth trauma has been linked to requests for a caesarean. If you had birth injuries, ask your trusted health professional about the risks of another vaginal birth. If you'd like a vaginal birth after a caesarean, ask about the pros and cons of both, and consider a pelvic health physio during pregnancy if one is available to you.

## The two of you

Whether and when to have another baby is yours and your partner's decision, and it doesn't need to be rushed. You may not be ready at the same time. Talking openly about the last birth helps, and a couples counsellor can support that.
