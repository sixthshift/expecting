Your job is to stay with them through labour and the birth and give emotional and physical support. You're there to help, not just to watch. Having the right support person can shorten labour, lower the need for pain relief and lower the chance of an assisted birth or caesarean.

## Before the day

- Go to antenatal classes together, and know the birth plan, including what to do if things don't go to plan.
- Ask how they'd like you to support them, including when and how they want you to speak to staff for them.
- Tour the birth suite and ask questions.
- If you don't think you can give the support they need, say so beforehand.

## Early labour

Stay with them so they're not alone. Go for a walk together, encourage eating and drinking, time the contractions, give a massage, and help them rest. Dim the lights, put on calming music, and help with hot or cold packs and breathing.

## As labour goes on

- Encourage and comfort them, and guide their breathing.
- Help them into positions, and into a bath or shower.
- Massage, hold their hand, wipe their face, and put a cool compress on their forehead. Massage may feel good one moment and irritating the next; talk about this before labour.
- Offer snacks, drinks, ice, and sips of water while they push.

## Speaking for them

You may need to tell the maternity team their needs and wishes, talk through with them what the team advises, keep them told what's happening, help them make decisions, and update family if they want.

## Keep the room calm

There are times they'll need peace and quiet, so it may be best not to have too many people around. They need to feel free to say what they need, even if that means telling you to leave the room.

## Look after yourself

It can be exhausting. Take breaks when you can, and bring your own snacks and drinks, and swimwear if you might get into a pool or shower with them.

## After the birth

You may be there while they learn to breastfeed or bottle-feed the baby.
