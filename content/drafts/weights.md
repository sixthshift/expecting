Strength training is recommended in pregnancy, about twice a week. Keep the weights light: Better Health says to choose low weights with medium to high repetitions and to avoid lifting heavy weights altogether.

## Why

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby and Better Health both list weightlifting among activities to avoid, Better Health because of the pressure it puts on the abdomen. Your ligaments also loosen in pregnancy, so heavy weights carry more chance of injury.

## What to do

- **Go light.** Resistance training with light weights is fine (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby); the Royal Women's lists a 'Pump' class with light weights among good group choices.
- **Get off your back.** After 16 weeks, avoid exercises lying on your back; Better Health says some of these exercises can be modified by lying on your side.
- **Skip wide squats and lunges,** which Better Health lists to avoid.
- **Stretch with control** and don't over-extend.
- **Tell the instructor** you're pregnant and how many weeks, and ask for modifications. Some gyms run pregnancy classes.
