The Newborn Upfront Payment and Newborn Supplement are a lump sum and an increase to your Family Tax Benefit Part A when a baby comes into your care. To get them you must:

- care for the baby
- be eligible for Family Tax Benefit (FTB) Part A
- **not be getting Parental Leave Pay for the same child.**

So it's one or the other for each child. If you're not eligible for Parental Leave Pay, you may still be able to get both of these.

## How much

The amount depends on your family's income and how many children you have.

- **Newborn Upfront Payment:** a lump sum of $708 per child.
- **Newborn Supplement:** an ongoing payment for up to 13 weeks. For your first child the most you can get is $2,125.76 over the 13 weeks; for later children, $709.80.

Neither is taxable. If you're eligible for the FTB Part A base rate or more, you get the maximum Newborn Supplement; less than the base rate, and it's reduced. It stops if you stop being eligible during the 13 weeks. They're paid the way you've chosen to get FTB Part A.

## How to get it

There's no separate claim. When you claim FTB for your child, Services Australia checks whether you're eligible, but only if you don't receive Parental Leave Pay for the child. If you're eligible, they'll let you know with a letter to your myGov inbox.

You can claim FTB up to 3 months before your baby's due date.
