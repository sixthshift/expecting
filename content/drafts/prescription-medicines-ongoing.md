If you take a medicine for an ongoing condition, such as asthma, depression, diabetes, epilepsy or high blood pressure, the answer is the same: don't stop taking it until you have spoken with your doctor.

## Why

Some prescription medicines must not be taken when you're pregnant, and others should be used carefully. But stopping some treatments can also cause problems that affect you and your baby. Your doctor weighs the medicine against the condition: if they think the medicine will cause more harm than the condition itself, they may change the medicine, change the dose or stop it altogether. They may also leave it as it is.

Pregnancy itself can change how your body processes medicines, which can change the amount you need.

## What to do

- **Talk before you change anything.** KEMH's advice is that starting, continuing, changing or stopping a medicine in pregnancy should be discussed with a doctor, pharmacist or midwife, and the decision made with them.
- **If you're planning a pregnancy,** it's a good idea to talk to your doctor then, if you take regular medicines.
- **Already pregnant?** Your doctor, pharmacist or midwife can advise on what you take.
- **For questions in between,** healthdirect's nurses are on 1800 022 222, 24 hours a day.
