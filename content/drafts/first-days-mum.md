After the birth you may feel elated, exhausted, emotionally drained, or all of them at once. That's normal: you've just been through a big, life-changing event.

## Straight after

Your baby will likely be lifted onto your chest for skin-to-skin contact. Your midwife will check your pulse, your blood pressure, how your uterus is contracting and how you're feeling, and can help with your first breastfeed. After a vaginal birth you'll probably stay in the birth room for a meal and a shower before moving to the postnatal ward or going home.

## Bleeding

The bleeding from your vagina is called lochia. For the first few days it's bright red, like a heavy period, and may have clots; change your pads often. It gets lighter and reddish-brown, then white or yellow. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it lasts up to 6 weeks; RANZCOG says it may go on for about two months. It may be redder and heavier while you breastfeed. Use maternity pads or period underwear until your 6-week check: tampons and menstrual cups may increase the chance of infection.

## Afterpains

Your uterus contracts back to its usual size, which can feel like period pain. It's more likely if this isn't your first baby, and more noticeable while breastfeeding. A warm pack on your tummy helps, or paracetamol 30 minutes before a feed.

## Your vulva, stitches or wound

Your vulva and vagina may be tender for a few days. An icepack, pain relief, lying down and firm supportive underwear can help. If you have stitches, bathe the area often in clean, warm water and dry carefully; they dissolve in 1 to 2 weeks. After a caesarean, the scar may be sore for a few weeks; see [the first days after a caesarean](caesarean-recovery).

## The toilet

It's safe to use the toilet. Drinking lots of water makes urine sting less. You probably won't poo for a few days: eat fruit, vegetables and wholegrains, and don't strain. Your midwife may suggest laxatives if you get constipated.

## Feelings

Your hormones change fast. Feeling teary, irritable or more sensitive than usual is known as the baby blues, and usually passes in under 2 weeks.

## When to get help

**Talk to your doctor or midwife straight away** if you feel unwell and have sudden heavy bleeding, a high temperature, pain in your chest, pain, swelling or redness in one calf, a severe headache, blurred vision or vomiting. These need immediate treatment.

**If you pass large clots,** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says tell your midwife; RANZCOG says contact your midwife or doctor or go to an emergency facility, and keep the pad to show them. Tell your midwife too if you notice a bad smell.

**If you have thoughts of hurting yourself or your baby,** call your doctor or midwife right away. For the blues that hang on, the sources differ: RANZCOG says call your doctor or midwife right away if they last more than a few days; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says call your doctor if you still have them 2 weeks after the birth.
