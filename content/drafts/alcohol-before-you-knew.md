You might have drunk alcohol before you even knew you were pregnant. Plenty of people have. The risk from low-level drinking before you knew is likely to be low.

## Why

There's no safe level of alcohol established in pregnancy, and alcohol crosses the placenta. MotherSafe says the level of risk to the baby is likely to be low if only small amounts were drunk before you knew you were pregnant. The risk goes up with frequent drinking and large amounts over a short time, so binge drinking may be especially harmful, and chronic heavy drinking raises the risk of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD).

## What to do

- **Stop now.** Once you know you're pregnant, it's safest to stop drinking. Stopping at any time in the pregnancy reduces the risk to the baby.
- **If you're worried, say so.** Speak to your doctor or midwife. NOFASD Australia gives confidential information and support on 1800 860 613.
- **For advice about your own situation,** call MotherSafe: Sydney metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848.
- **If it's hard to stop,** or you're concerned about how much you drink, talk to your doctor or midwife, or call the Alcohol and Drug Information Service (ADIS), a 24-hour confidential line, on 1800 250 015.
