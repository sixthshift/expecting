The placenta sometimes attaches low in the uterus, close to or over the cervix. It's often found at the 18 to 20 week scan, and often it resolves before the baby is due: as the uterus expands and the baby grows, the placenta can "move up" away from the cervix.

## The words

- **Normal placental location**: more than 2 cm from the cervix.
- **Low-lying placenta**: within 2 cm of the cervix, without covering it.
- **Placenta praevia**: covering the cervix. ("Placenta praevia" is also used for both.)

## The repeat scan

If your placenta is low or covering the cervix, you'll be offered another ultrasound later, at around 32 to 34 weeks, to check where it has ended up. If it was in a normal position early on, it's likely to stay there.

There's often no clear cause. It's more common if you've had placenta praevia or a caesarean before, smoke, are over 35, are having twins or triplets, have had a short gap between pregnancies, or have diabetes or high blood pressure. It's unusual in a first pregnancy, and nothing can prevent it.

## If it stays low

It can't be treated directly, but regular monitoring reduces the chance of complications. You may:

- have extra scans, and be advised to plan the birth at a major hospital, so the right care is there if you bleed
- be advised to rest more, and to avoid tampons and penetrative sex
- have blood tests or iron tablets to watch for anaemia if you bleed
- need to stay in hospital until any bleeding settles, possibly more than once

Your doctor may recommend a caesarean, because the placenta can block the baby's way out; the Royal Women's Hospital says most people with placenta praevia will need one. Heavy bleeding may mean the baby is born early, with steroid injections for the baby's lungs before 34 weeks. Your doctor or midwife will advise on the safest way and time for the birth.

## When to get help

The most common sign is **painless vaginal bleeding after 28 weeks**. It may start light and get heavier, and some people have no symptoms at all.

**Call 000** and ask for an ambulance if the bleeding or pain is severe. Also call 000 and ask for an ambulance, or go to your nearest hospital emergency department, if the bleeding is heavy and you have pain or contractions with it.

**Contact your doctor or midwife immediately** about any vaginal bleeding, and tell them you have a low-lying placenta.
