Yes, you can. ARPANSA says that if proper guidelines are followed, the risk to your unborn child is negligible.

## Why

Once you declare a pregnancy, your working conditions must make it unlikely that the extra dose to your baby goes over 1 mSv for the rest of the pregnancy. Below that limit, the risk of health effects is insignificant.

Your personal dosimeter (badge) may overestimate your baby's dose: from external exposure, the dose to the baby is unlikely to be more than 25 per cent of what the badge records.

## What to do

- **Tell your employer as soon as possible**, so extra protections can be considered.
- **Talk through your duties** with your employer or radiation safety officer. The usual options are no change, a move to an area with lower exposure, or a job with no radiation exposure. You don't have to stop working with radiation altogether.
- **Ask about closer monitoring**, such as monthly instead of three-monthly readings, or an electronic dosimeter set to alarm.
- **Aircrew**: the unborn child is treated as a member of the public, with the public limit of 1 mSv. People who fly very often, such as 10 to 20 hours a week on long-haul flights, can approach and exceed 1 mSv a year.
