Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's week-by-week pages trace the senses as they arrive. They don't agree exactly on hearing, so treat the weeks as approximate.

**Taste.** By week 9 the face has a mouth and tongue with tiny taste buds. By week 17 the taste buds are working and your baby can tell sweet from bitter. By about week 24 they're becoming more sensitive.

**Hearing.** The week 9 page says the inner ear is forming but your baby won't be able to hear until about 24 weeks. The week 15 page says the bones in the ears are developing, so your baby is starting to hear for the first time: your heartbeat, digestive system and voice. By week 24 your baby can hear sounds from outside the uterus. The bonding page says hearing develops in the first 20 weeks and becomes fully functional at about week 25, when your baby may react to noises inside and outside the womb and may be soothed by your voice. It adds that some research suggests very early language development may start before birth.

**Sight.** At week 24 your baby's eyes respond to light. The bonding page says your baby begins to sense light during weeks 29 to 32, and that their eyes keep developing after birth.

**Touch.** At 26 weeks your baby can suck, see, hear and taste, and might move in response to you running your hand firmly over your tummy. The bonding page says that from week 24 your baby may be able to feel pain.

## Talking and singing

The week 15 page says it's a great time to start talking to your baby, and that you might feel silly at first but will get used to it. The bonding page suggests you can talk and sing to your baby, play calming music, and gently touch or rub your belly, and that partners and other people close to you can do the same. It says bonding in pregnancy can help your baby's development after they're born.
