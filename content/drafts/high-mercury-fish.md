## Why

Most fish have low levels of mercury, but a few big, long-lived or predatory fish build up more. High levels of mercury can damage the nervous system, and unborn babies are especially vulnerable because they're growing so fast. Flake is shark, one of the higher-mercury fish.

## What to do

If you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy, a portion is 150 g, and:

- **Shark (flake) or billfish (swordfish, broadbill, marlin):** no more than 1 portion a fortnight, and no other fish that fortnight.
- **Orange roughy (deep sea perch) or catfish:** no more than 1 portion a week, and no other fish that week.

Cooking, canning and freezing don't reduce mercury. If you're not sure what the fish is, at the shop, a restaurant or the takeaway, ask.

Keep eating fish, though: there's no need for anyone, pregnant or not, to stop altogether. Most fish in Australian waters is low in mercury, and the NSW Food Authority's guidance is 2–3 serves a week of other fish and seafood, such as salmon, canned tuna, sardines, snapper or prawns.
