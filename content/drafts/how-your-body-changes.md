Pregnancy, Birth and Baby groups the changes into two kinds: physical changes from the size and pressure of the baby growing in your uterus, and hormonal changes, which cause physical symptoms and can affect your mood. If you're ever unsure whether a change is normal or something to worry about, speak to your doctor.

**Circulation.** You may feel dizzy, especially going from lying to standing. Get up slowly, and sit or lie down if you feel faint.

**Swelling.** Feet and ankles may swell, especially after long standing or in hot weather, and more towards the end of pregnancy. If you notice swelling early in the day, or in your fingers, hands or face, speak to your midwife or doctor.

**Veins, heartburn and wees.** Varicose veins and heartburn are common. You may need to wee more, from hormones early on and the baby's weight on your bladder later. Some leaking towards the end is possible, as pressure from the baby on your pelvic floor, which may also weaken, builds.

**Joints and balance.** Hormones soften the ligaments, most noticeably in the pelvis and lower back, but also the feet, wrists and upper back. That can mean pelvic girdle pain, sciatica, back pain and feeling unsteady. A physiotherapist can teach exercises that strengthen your muscles.

**Skin and breasts.** Skin may glow from extra blood flow, or get red or dry patches or worse acne; moles, freckles, areolae and other areas may darken, and stretch marks are common. Breasts get larger, heavier and more tender, with more visible veins, and colostrum may leak later on. If you have significant breast pain, redness or a lump, speak to your midwife or doctor.

**Hormonal symptoms.** Nausea and vomiting usually starts around week 6 and improves by around week 14; if it's severe you may need to go to hospital. Hormonal changes can also cause food cravings and constipation, and pregnancy can affect brain function and memory ("brain fog"), especially in the third trimester.

**Mood.** A range of emotions, positive and negative, is normal. You may also have strong emotions and mood swings, such as anxiety or depression. If you're having suicidal thoughts and are in immediate danger, call 000; for support, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 or PANDA on 1300 726 306.
