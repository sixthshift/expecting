Yes. Vaginal creams and pessaries containing clotrimazole (such as Canesten) or nystatin are safe in pregnancy and the treatments of choice.

## Why

Thrush is twice as common in pregnancy, because of higher oestrogen and other changes in your body. It isn't harmful to the baby, but it's uncomfortable and sometimes painful, so it's worth treating.

The single-dose oral tablet (fluconazole) is different. MotherSafe says it isn't a recommended treatment in the first trimester; after that it's a second-line option if creams haven't worked or you can't use them. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says not to use the single dose oral tablet unless your doctor tells you to, and KEMH says a single dose of oral fluconazole is not routinely recommended unless your doctor advises it. If you took a single 150 mg dose before you knew, MotherSafe is reassuring about it.

## What to do

- **See your doctor first,** even though these are sold without a prescription. Other infections, such as genital herpes or a UTI, can cause similar symptoms, and a swab may be needed.
- **Choose the longer course.** A 6-day course of clotrimazole, or 14 days of nystatin, works better than the shorter ones.
- **The applicator:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's best not to use it with pessaries; MotherSafe and KEMH say applicators may be used with care.
- **For itch,** a low-strength hydrocortisone cream applied sparingly to the vulva for a short time is considered safe.
- **See your doctor again** if symptoms continue after treatment.
