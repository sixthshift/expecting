The first few days are exciting and tiring, and every baby is different. Here's what's usual for a healthy baby born at full term.

## At birth

Your baby may have blood and vernix on their skin. Vernix is a white, sticky substance that protected their skin in the uterus. After an assisted birth there may be some swelling and bruising on the head, which soon goes. An overdue baby's skin may be a little dry and cracked. Small pink or red marks on the forehead, eyelids or neck (stork marks) fade gradually.

The midwife checks your baby's condition at 1 and 5 minutes (the Apgar score), and weighs and measures them.

## Feeding and sleeping

Your baby may want to feed often, perhaps every hour: as a rough guide, 8 to 12 times or more in the first 24 hours, and 8 to 14 or more feeds a day after that, including overnight. Signs of hunger include getting restless, sucking their hands, murmuring, and turning their head with an open mouth.

Most newborns sleep 14 to 17 hours a day, in stretches of 2 to 4 hours, and don't know day from night yet. Don't set strict schedules in the first couple of months.

## Nappies

The first poo is black and sticky (meconium). Over the first week it turns greenish-brown, then mustard yellow; formula-fed babies' poos are usually more yellow and formed. Your baby should have at least one wet nappy within 24 hours of birth.

## Weight

Babies normally lose up to 10% of their birth weight in the first few days, then start to regain it. Your baby is weighed again a few days after birth to check they haven't lost too much.

## The cord stump

Keep it clean and dry. Only wash around it if it's dirty with wee or poo: clean water, then pat dry. When it falls off, it can go in the bin.

## Tests and injections

You'll be offered vitamin K and the first hepatitis B vaccine in hospital. Usually when your baby is between 48 and 72 hours old (the newborn screening page says within the first 72 hours), you'll be offered the heel prick test (newborn bloodspot screening) and a hearing screen. You'll be asked for permission first.

## The baby book

Every baby in Australia is issued an Infant Health Record at birth, a different colour in each state: blue in the ACT, NSW, SA and Tasmania, yellow in the NT, red in Queensland, green in Victoria and purple in WA. It records your child's health, growth and development for life.

## Safe sleep from the first night

Always put your baby to sleep on their back, with their head and face uncovered, in a cot next to your bed for at least the first 6 to 12 months. Keep them away from cigarette and vape smoke.
