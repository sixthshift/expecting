Two different records start when your baby is born: a paper book and a digital record. They do different jobs.

## The infant health record book

It's a coloured book that records your baby's growth and development, vaccinations and health issues, from birth to when they start school. It's in the newborn information pack you get in hospital. Its colour depends on where you live, according to Pregnancy, Birth and Baby: blue in the ACT, NSW, SA and Tasmania; red in Queensland; green in Victoria; purple in WA; yellow in the NT.

Take it to every appointment: doctor, midwife, child health nurse, specialists, dentist, immunisation clinic, hospital, and childcare or school. Health professionals record weight and measurements in it, and you can add things yourself, such as medicines or milestones. It also has tips on registering the birth and a place for your family's health history. Keep it safe, as it's a permanent record. If you lose it, ask your doctor or child health nurse how to get a new one.

## My Health Record for your baby

My Health Record is a secure digital record that you and your healthcare providers can add to. You can get one for your newborn by completing the Newborn Child Declaration form in your newborn information pack, or by registering your newborn when you enrol your baby for Medicare. Providers can add immunisations, allergies and other health issues, and you can add developmental milestones.

## Your own record

Pregnancy Birth and Baby says that during pregnancy, with many providers, tests and scans, My Health Record helps your doctor see relevant information. It can store test and scan reports and pathology reports.

The Newborn Child Declaration form is also the proof of birth you use to enrol your baby in Medicare; a doctor or midwife must sign its last page.
