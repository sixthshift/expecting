Where possible, buy a new car restraint. A second-hand one can be used only if it passes a few checks.

## Why

The risk with a second-hand car seat is that its history is unknown. Once a car seat has been in an accident, it must be thrown away and not resold, as it may not protect a child properly in another one.

## What to do

If you choose a second-hand car seat, make sure it:

- is undamaged, with all parts, including the shell and straps, in good condition
- is less than 10 years old: check the date of manufacture
- has a sticker showing it meets the 2004, 2010 or 2013 AS/NZS 1754 standard

Install it to the manufacturer's instructions for that seat. If you're unsure, an authorised restraint fitting station in your state or territory can help.

**Hiring is the other way to avoid buying.** Kidsafe Queensland and Kidsafe SA hire baby capsules for up to about 6 months, and the hire includes fitting by their qualified fitters.
