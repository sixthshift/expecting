Adoption permanently transfers the legal rights and responsibilities of parenting from a child's birth parents to the adoptive parents, through a court's adoption order. Each state and territory has its own laws and process. Australian adoptions are open: adopted children grow up knowing they were adopted, and where possible know about their birth family and culture.

## If you're thinking of adopting

- **Two kinds.** Domestic adoption is of a child born or living in Australia, including by a step-parent, relative or foster carer. Intercountry adoption is of a child from overseas, matched through formal processes.
- **Eligibility** is set by the state or territory where you apply, and can include citizenship, age, where you live and how long you've been in a relationship, plus criminal history, child protection and health checks and interviews. Single people can adopt, though rules vary, and same-sex couples can adopt.
- **It takes time.** Intercountry adoption often takes several years, in many cases around 3 to 4 from approval to placement.
- **Money.** You may be eligible for government payments after adopting; check with Services Australia.

Start with your state or territory's adoption authority.

## If you're thinking of placing your baby for adoption

You don't need to decide right away, whether you've just found out you're pregnant or have already had your baby. You can get information, advice and counselling before the birth, but nothing is confirmed until after.

- You can't consent straight after the birth: there's a waiting period, which varies by state.
- Before consenting you must complete pre-adoption counselling and read the required information.
- After you sign, there's a period, usually around 30 days, when you can change your mind.
- An adoption plan can set how much contact you have with your child and their adoptive family.

Other options include parenting your child yourself, shared care with family or friends, or foster care. A hospital social worker or your state's adoption services are a good place to start.

Considering adoption can bring up many feelings, and they can change over time. Talking with someone you trust helps. Parentline is 1300 30 1300 and Lifeline is 13 11 14.
