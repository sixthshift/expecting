Pregnancy asks more of certain nutrients, including iron, calcium, iodine and many vitamins. You don't have to eat for two. In the first three months you probably won't need many extra kilojoules; in the second and third trimesters you'll need a bit more vegetables, lean protein and wholegrains each day. The Royal Women's Hospital puts it simply: you don't have to eat more, but you do have to eat more variety.

## The five food groups, serves a day

| Food group | Pregnancy, Birth and Baby | The Royal Women's Hospital |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetables and legumes | 5 | 5 |
| Fruit | 2 | 2 |
| Grains, mostly wholegrain or high fibre | 8 | 8½ |
| Lean meat, fish, chicken, eggs, tofu, nuts, legumes | 3½ | 3½ |
| Milk, yoghurt, cheese or alternatives | 3½ | 2½ |

The two sources differ on dairy and grains; both are given here. A serve is, for example, 1 slice of bread, ½ cup of cooked rice or pasta, 1 cup of milk, 2 slices (40 g) of cheese, 1 medium apple, ½ cup of cooked vegetables, 2 eggs or 100 g of fish (the Women's table).

## The nutrients that matter most

- **Folate** helps build your baby's cells and lowers the risk of spina bifida. It's in green leafy vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, fortified cereals, legumes and nuts, but food alone is rarely enough.
- **Iodine** is key to your baby's brain growth. Eat fish one to three times a week (limiting high-mercury kinds), use iodised salt, and don't take kelp tablets.
- **Iron** makes red blood cells; you need more because your blood volume rises. Have at least two serves of meat, chicken, fish, legumes or nuts a day, and vitamin C-rich foods with plant sources of iron.
- **Calcium**, for bones: 2 to 3 serves of dairy or calcium-fortified milk (at least 100 mg per 100 ml), canned salmon or sardines with bones, or firm tofu.
- **Vitamin D**, for your baby's bones and teeth: mostly from sunlight, a little from oily fish and eggs.
- **Omega-3**, for your baby's brain, nerves and eyes: fish 2 to 3 times a week, plus walnuts, chia, linseed and soybeans.

The Women's says everyone should take a pregnancy supplement with at least 500 micrograms of folic acid, 150 micrograms of iodine and 400 IU of vitamin D, because food and sunshine alone are unlikely to be enough. Supplements have their own entry.

## Vegetarian or vegan

Lentils, beans, tofu, eggs and soy milk can replace animal foods. If you eat no animal foods, you'll need a vitamin B12 supplement, as your baby needs it for brain development.

## Drinks

The safest drinks are water and milk. Aim for about 2.3 L (10 cups) of water a day: you hold more fluid in pregnancy, and it matters for your baby and the amniotic fluid.

## Making it work

- If big meals won't fit, eat smaller meals with healthy snacks through the day.
- Easy snacks: eggs or peanut butter on toast, baked beans, yoghurt on fruit, tuna on toast, soup and toast, a smoothie.
- Avoid fast food and sugary drinks, which give you and your baby few nutrients, and limit high-fat and salty processed foods.

What's safe to eat (listeria, mercury, caffeine, alcohol) has its own entries.
