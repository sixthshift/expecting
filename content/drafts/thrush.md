## What's usual

Thrush is a common yeast infection in pregnancy. You're twice as likely to get it while pregnant, because higher oestrogen helps the yeast grow. You may have itching or pain in the vagina or vulva, redness and swelling, pain when you wee or have sex, and white discharge that may look like cottage cheese. It won't harm your baby.

Treatment is antifungal vaginal cream or pessaries (clotrimazole or nystatin), which are safe in pregnancy. A 6-day course is better than shorter ones, and the single-dose oral tablet shouldn't be used unless your doctor says so. If you're using pessaries, it's best not to use the applicator. These are sold without a prescription, but it's best to see your doctor first to make sure nothing else is causing your symptoms, and to speak to your pharmacist before using any medicine.

To help prevent it: cotton underwear, loose clothing, wiping front to back, and no soap or scented products on the vulva.

## See your doctor

See your doctor if you think you have thrush, particularly if it's your first time with these symptoms, symptoms continue after treatment, sex is still painful after treatment, you get it frequently, you feel pain in your pelvic or vulval area, or you have unusual vaginal bleeding. Genital herpes and urinary tract infections can have similar symptoms.
