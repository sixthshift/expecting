## What's usual

A fever is a temperature of 38°C or higher. In pregnancy it matters more than usual: a fever can increase the baby's chance of congenital anomalies (birth differences) and miscarriage, and it can be a sign of an infection that needs treating quickly.

Some infections to have in mind:

- **A kidney infection** can follow a urinary tract infection, with a fever above 38°C, back pain and vomiting.
- **Sepsis** is when your immune system has a big reaction to an infection, of any kind. Its signs can include a fever, or a temperature below 36°C, confusion or drowsiness, belly pain, a fast heart rate and fast breathing. In pregnancy you might also notice the baby's movements change, or pain in your uterus. Sepsis needs urgent treatment.

There are two different numbers to know. Generally, a fever is 38°C or higher. Once your waters have broken, the line to call is lower: **37.5°C**.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you have signs of sepsis, such as an infection, a fever or difficulty breathing, see your doctor urgently, or go to your nearest emergency department or call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance.

If you have a fever with seizures, confusion or difficulty breathing, go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately. healthdirect's fever page also says to get emergency care for a fever with confusion or drowsiness, difficulty breathing, or seizures or muscle spasms.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have a fever or chills at any time in pregnancy.

If your waters have broken, call your midwife, doctor or hospital immediately for a temperature of 37.5°C or above, or if you feel unwell or have flu-like symptoms.
