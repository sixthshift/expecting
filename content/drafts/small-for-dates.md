At your check-ups, your doctor or midwife measures and tracks the growth of your uterus and baby. If your bump isn't growing as expected, or a routine ultrasound shows the baby isn't growing as expected, you'll be looked after more closely. A baby measuring small is one of the things that can make a pregnancy higher risk.

## Placental insufficiency

The placenta delivers oxygen and nutrients to the baby. Placental insufficiency is when it doesn't work properly. It's uncommon, but it can be serious: the baby may not grow as expected (fetal growth restriction, FGR), and it raises the risk of pre-eclampsia, premature birth and stillbirth. Babies with FGR may have breathing, feeding, temperature or other problems after birth.

Signs may include slower growth than expected and reduced movements. If you've been pregnant before, you might notice your bump is smaller than last time.

There's often no clear cause, and it isn't your fault. It's more likely with diabetes, pre-eclampsia, high blood pressure, blood clotting conditions, autoimmune disease, kidney problems or severe anaemia. Other things that can affect the baby's growth include smoking, alcohol or drugs, a low weight before pregnancy, being over 35, a previous small baby, pre-eclampsia or stillbirth, and six months or less between pregnancies.

## The plan

There's no treatment to stop placental insufficiency, but it can be managed. Diagnosing it early matters for you and the baby.

- **Closer monitoring.** Extra ultrasounds to follow the baby's growth, and an umbilical artery Doppler during the scan to check blood flow to the placenta. You and the baby may be seen more often.
- **Where to give birth.** You may be advised to give birth at a major hospital with the right support, especially if the baby may come early.
- **When.** Your doctor may recommend ways to help you carry the pregnancy to term. If there are signs the baby isn't well, they may recommend an induction or a caesarean before your due date.

Your midwife and doctor will explain any tests and treatment, and it's your choice to consent.

It can be upsetting to hear. PANDA and COPE offer support, and you can call PANDA on 1300 726 306.

## When to get help

**If you're concerned about the baby's movement pattern or notice a decrease in their movements, contact your midwife or doctor immediately.** Don't wait until the next day.

If you have bleeding or significant pain, **call 000** and ask for an ambulance, or go to your nearest hospital emergency department.
