See your midwife or doctor if you have any symptoms of a UTI. Don't delay getting antibiotics: an infection can develop quickly and cause problems for you and your baby.

## Why

In pregnancy a bladder infection is more likely to become a kidney infection, partly because the tubes from the kidneys to the bladder get wider. Kidney infections in pregnancy can be serious. A UTI also raises the chance of an early birth or a small baby, and that risk goes away once it's treated.

**Ural and Citravescent** make urine less acidic and can ease the burning. There's limited safety data in pregnancy, though they're not expected to be harmful. But there's no evidence they treat the infection.

**Cranberry** is likely safe in the amounts found in food, but there's little information on whether it works, and a poorly treated UTI can have serious consequences.

**Bacteria without symptoms** are treated too. Your urine is tested early in pregnancy, and your doctor will decide whether you need treatment.

## What to do

- **See your midwife or doctor** for burning or stinging when you wee, needing to go more often, leaking, cloudy, bloody or very smelly urine, lower tummy pain or a fever. Fever above 38 °C, back pain or vomiting can mean the kidneys are involved.
- **Antibiotics commonly used for UTIs** aren't considered a concern in pregnancy.
- **To lower your chances:** drink plenty of fluids, especially water; wee when you need to and straight after sex; and avoid douching, sprays or powders in your genital area.
