Energy drinks aren't recommended during pregnancy. In Australia they must carry a label saying they're not suitable for pregnant or breastfeeding women, young children or people sensitive to caffeine.

## Why

Energy drinks are a concentrated source of caffeine. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby puts a 250 mL can at about 80 mg, and the Food Standards Code allows up to 320 mg of caffeine per litre. The pregnancy limit is no more than 200 mg of caffeine a day from all sources, and too much caffeine may increase the risk of miscarriage, stillbirth and low birth weight.

Guarana is a plant that's naturally high in caffeine, so foods that contain it must be labelled as containing caffeine. Energy drinks often contain a lot of sugar too.

## What to do

- **Leave energy drinks for now,** including the ones that list guarana rather than caffeine.
- **If you need a pick-me-up,** coffee and tea are fine within the 200 mg a day limit. Alternatives without caffeine include water with lemon or lime, caffeine-free herbal tea, diluted fruit juice or a glass of milk.
- **Questions about caffeine?** Talk to your doctor or midwife.
