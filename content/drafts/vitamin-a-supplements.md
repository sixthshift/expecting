Avoid vitamin A supplements in pregnancy. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's best not to take vitamin A as a supplement, and never more than 10,000 international units (IU) a day.

## Why

Extra vitamin A from a supplement might increase the chance of birth defects. Supplements usually contain it as retinyl esters, which your body turns into retinol, and retinol can harm your growing baby.

Beta-carotene, the plant form of vitamin A, is not linked with birth defects.

## What to do

- **Get it from food.** Eggs, dairy, orange and yellow vegetables, leafy greens, oily fish such as salmon, and meat give you what you need.
- **Avoid foods very high in vitamin A.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says this might include liver and pâté.
- **Skip cod liver oil.** It contains vitamin A as well as fish oil, so it should be avoided in pregnancy.
- **Check any multivitamin's label,** or ask your pharmacist, midwife or doctor whether it suits pregnancy.
- **Retinol in skin care:** MotherSafe says retinol in skin care products is poorly absorbed through the skin and isn't expected to be a concern in pregnancy.
