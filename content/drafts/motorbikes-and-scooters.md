Ask your midwife or doctor. We haven't found an Australian health source that speaks to riding a motorbike, scooter or e-bike in pregnancy, so this is the general advice on falls and knocks applied to it.

## Why

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid activities with a risk of collision, tripping or falling. Injuries to your abdomen can lead to serious complications, including the placenta separating from the uterus (placental abruption) and preterm labour, and falls and road accidents are common causes.

## What to do

- **Talk it over** with your midwife or doctor before you keep riding.
- **On holiday overseas,** Smartraveller says things can go wrong quickly when you're pregnant: stay within reach of hospitals and doctors. Check your travel insurance covers your pregnancy, and that it covers riding: you may need to pay extra to be covered for riding a motorcycle, most policies are void if you were breaking any road law when the accident happened, and some won't cover you if you ride without an authorised licence.
- **After any knock to your abdomen,** contact your midwife or doctor immediately. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby also says that if you have any injury to your abdomen, you should go to the nearest hospital and tell staff you're pregnant. Even after a minor injury, go to hospital straight away if your baby is not moving as much as normal, you have vaginal bleeding or you have pain in your abdomen.
- **Call triple zero (000)** and say you're pregnant if, after a knock to your abdomen, you have pain in your abdomen, a firm or tightening uterus, or vaginal bleeding.
