We're so sorry. Whatever has happened, you don't need to do anything here right now.

## What Expecting does

When either of you says "something has happened", from Settings or the capture button, Expecting goes quiet. The timeline pauses. The week notes, the baby's size, the countdown and every task are put away. Nothing cheerful comes back until you choose.

It won't ask you anything it doesn't need. It already knows the week. A name for the baby is yours to give, or not.

Bookings and claims in the Planner are put aside, not deleted. When you're ready, there's a note on which ones you might want to cancel and which may still matter.

The same quiet mode is there for news that isn't a loss but turns everything over, such as a worrying screening result or a baby in intensive care, with next steps that fit.

## Someone to talk to

- **Red Nose Grief and Loss, 1300 308 307**, pregnancy and infant grief and loss support.
- **PANDA, 1300 726 306**, for expecting and new parents and the people around them.
- **Lifeline, 13 11 14**, to talk to someone now. If you or someone close to you is in crisis, or at immediate risk of harm, call 000.

## Your body

After a loss it's normal to bleed, from light spotting to heavier bleeding with clots, and to have some cramping. Bleeding usually gets lighter and stops within about 2 weeks. If you have very heavy bleeding, strong pain or feel very unwell, call 000 and ask for an ambulance.

## How you might feel

Shock, numbness, sadness, guilt, anger, fear, loneliness, even relief: there's no right or wrong way to feel, and your feelings may change over time. In most cases a loss isn't caused by anything you did or didn't do.

Partners grieve too, sometimes differently, and sometimes later. A partner who didn't carry the baby may put their own feelings aside to support the other. Counselling, together or on your own, can help.

Reminders can be very hard, especially early on. It's okay to turn off social media for a while, to stay away from babies and pregnant friends until you're ready, and to grieve in your own way, at your own pace. If grief is so consuming that you can't get through the day, an experienced professional with bereavement training, such as a psychologist, can help.

## When you're ready

Expecting has these, one at a time, whenever you want them:

- what happens to your body now
- the medical choices, if they're still open
- time off work: under the National Employment Standards (Fair Work Ombudsman) employees, including casuals, are entitled to 2 days of compassionate (bereavement) leave each time they have a miscarriage or their partner does, or a baby in their immediate family or household is stillborn; it's paid for full-time and part-time employees and unpaid for casuals, and awards and agreements can give more
- certificates and ways to remember your baby
- telling people
- the partner's grief
- a pregnancy after loss, if and when you choose

Later, if you'd like, Expecting can help you mark the due date and anniversaries gently.
