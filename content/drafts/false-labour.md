Braxton Hicks contractions are your uterus tightening and relaxing. They're a normal part of pregnancy that can come and go, and are thought to help your uterus and cervix get ready for labour. They're sometimes called "false labour" or "practice contractions". You may notice them from the second trimester. They're more common at night, and they may become more regular as the birth gets closer.

Unlike labour contractions, they don't open or change the shape of your cervix.

## Telling them apart

**Braxton Hicks:**

- irregular and short, usually about 30 seconds
- usually uncomfortable but not painful (they can be strong, and uncomfortable in the third trimester)
- walking doesn't make them stronger
- lying down may make them go away
- don't get stronger or closer together

**Labour contractions:**

- may start irregular, then become regular and last longer
- usually more painful as they go on
- walking may make them stronger
- lying down doesn't make them go away
- get stronger and closer together

RANZCOG puts it simply: Braxton Hicks disappear with a change of position or activity, such as a warm bath or shower.

## Easing them

Lie down or change position, take a walk, relax in a warm bath, drink enough to stay hydrated, and go to the toilet.

## Still not sure?

Your midwife can tell whether you're having Braxton Hicks or are in labour by doing a vaginal examination. At any stage of pregnancy, contact your midwife or doctor if you're concerned or unsure.

## When to get help

**Call your doctor or midwife** when your waters break, or your contractions are getting stronger, closer together and more regular.

If you have contractions or other signs of labour **before 37 weeks**, call your midwife or doctor for advice and go straight to hospital.
