**Pholcodine is no longer sold in Australia.** In March 2023 the TGA cancelled 55 pholcodine cough medicines, and the ones still on pharmacy shelves were recalled. Some older pages still list it.

Other cough medicines are considered safe in pregnancy. Which one depends on the kind of cough, and it's best to check with your doctor or pharmacist first.

## Why

- **Dry, tickly cough:** dextromethorphan is considered safe in pregnancy. MotherSafe also lists dihydrocodeine.
- **Chesty, productive cough:** bromhexine and guaifenesin are considered safe.

MotherSafe notes there are no large published studies on these medicines, but they've been widely used for many years and are considered safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

**Why pholcodine went.** The TGA found a link between pholcodine and severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) to some muscle relaxants used in general anaesthesia. Pholcodine was used in dry-cough syrups and lozenges and in some cold and flu products. If you've taken it in the past 12 months and need a general anaesthetic, tell your health professional.

## What to do

- **Try the simple things:** rest, plenty of fluids, and warm drinks with honey and lemon.
- **Take only the recommended dose,** and see your doctor if your cough doesn't go away.
- **Check cold and flu combinations,** which contain several ingredients, including in many cases an oral decongestant (see Sudafed and oral decongestants).
- **If you think you have the flu, see your doctor.** Flu can be serious in pregnancy, and an antiviral works better the earlier you start it.
