Ask first. Herbal medicines, although natural, are not necessarily safe, especially in pregnancy. Talk to your doctor or midwife about any herbal preparation you plan to take, and about any you're already taking.

## Why

- **Little is known.** There's very little information on the safety of herbal preparations in pregnancy or their effect on the baby. Much of what is known comes from practitioner experience rather than scientific research, and most herbal treatments don't have to be tested to show they work.
- **They can reach the baby.** Like conventional medicines, herbal medicines may cross the placenta and carry potentially harmful ingredients to the baby.
- **The label may not match.** Herbal medicine in Australia is generally not regulated as strictly as other medicines, so the ingredients on the label may not always reflect what's actually in the product.
- **They can interact.** Herbal medicines may interact with other medicines and have harmful side effects, just as conventional ones can.
- **Chinese herbal medicine** has limited research on its safety and benefits in pregnancy.

## What to do

- **Discuss it first** with your midwife, GP or obstetrician, or ask a pharmacist.
- **If you do use one,** make sure it's from a reputable source.
- **Seeing a complementary practitioner?** Ask whether the treatment is safe at your stage of pregnancy, what the risks are, and check they're registered.
- **For a specific herb,** call MotherSafe: Sydney metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848.
