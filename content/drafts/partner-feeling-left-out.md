Some partners feel left out during the pregnancy. The attention goes to the person who's pregnant, and you may feel they're more interested in the baby than in you. It's a normal feeling, and there are ways in.

## Bond with the baby now

You don't have to wait for the birth. Partners can bond with the baby during pregnancy too:

- feel the baby kicking as often as you can
- massage the bump, if your partner is happy for you to
- come to the ultrasound appointments
- read and talk to the baby so they get used to your voice; from around 25 weeks their hearing is fully working

## Be part of the pregnancy

You can also bond by supporting your partner. PBB suggests you:

- go to antenatal appointments and classes together
- talk about the birth plan and meet the maternity team
- develop the birth plan together, ready to make important decisions during the birth with your partner or on their behalf
- do some of the household jobs or make healthy meals, so they have time to rest
- ask how they're feeling and really listen to the answer, and check in with your own needs too

## After the birth

MensLine's advice if you feel your partner has the bigger role and you're on the outside: be present at the birth, then change nappies, burp, bath and dress the baby, take them for walks, and settle them for sleep. Ask your partner what help they need, and talk to friends who are fathers. Take on the housework, the cooking, the shops, and the calls and messages while your partner sleeps.

PBB adds: try not to hand the baby back every time they cry. The more you practise caring and settling, the more confident you'll get.

## When it's more than that

Feeling resentful, distant or disconnected can come with the changes. Talk with your partner, and speak up before feelings build. If you're feeling very tired, irritable, angry, overwhelmed or can't cope, talk to a healthcare professional. You can call MensLine on 1300 78 99 78 or Lifeline on 13 11 14 any time, or PANDA on 1300 726 306 (7 days: until 7:30pm Monday to Friday, 9am to 4pm on weekends, AEST/AEDT).
