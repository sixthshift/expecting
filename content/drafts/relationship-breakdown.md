A break-up during pregnancy can be very hard. If you'd hoped to raise your child as a couple, you may feel angry, hurt or hopeless. Look after yourself and talk about your feelings; it's better to talk to someone than let things make you sad.

## For now: the pregnancy

- **Find your people.** If you're pregnant and on your own, it's important to have people you can share your feelings with. Ask for help from friends and family, and talk to your midwife about the services available.
- **Choose who'll be with you.** Think about whether you'd like a friend, sibling or parent at antenatal visits and in labour. Your midwife or doula may know of antenatal classes run for single people.
- **Plan for after the birth.** Think about who'll be around to help. If there's nobody, talk your situation over with someone.
- **Look after yourself:** eat well, exercise and sleep as well as you can.

## Later: co-parenting

Co-parenting is when separated parents share raising a child. You'll need to decide things like living arrangements, health and education, how you'll stay in contact, and special occasions. PBB's advice is to put the child first, even when you feel very emotional, and to keep negative feelings about your ex-partner to yourself.

Australia's Family Law Act says both parents are responsible for their children's care and welfare. The law changed in May 2024; the Attorney-General's Department website or a lawyer can tell you more.

- A **parenting plan** sets out what you've agreed, without going to court. It isn't legally enforceable.
- A **consent order** is like a parenting plan but is approved by the court and enforceable; neither of you has to attend.
- If you can't agree, a family relationship centre, a counsellor or a dispute resolution service is cheaper, easier and less stressful than court. You can go straight to court if the child may be at risk of abuse or violence, or the matter is very urgent.

## Money

Services Australia can tell you what help is available to you as a single parent, and you may be able to get a parenting payment. Child support can be set by an agreement between you, or by a child support assessment based on both your incomes; get legal advice before signing an agreement.
