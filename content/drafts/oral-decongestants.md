Avoid them. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says oral decongestants such as pseudoephedrine are not safe to use in pregnancy. KEMH says oral medicines such as pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine are not recommended in pregnancy, and healthdirect says you should not use oral decongestants such as phenylephrine.

## Why

MotherSafe, at the Royal Hospital for Women, says pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine narrow blood vessels, which may raise blood pressure in some people. It's more measured on early pregnancy: there's no conclusive data that they're harmful in the first trimester. But it says not to use them in the second and third trimesters if you have high blood pressure, and it prefers nasal sprays over tablets for a blocked nose in pregnancy.

They're the decongestants in many combination cold and flu tablets, so check the ingredients on the box.

## What to do

- **For a blocked nose,** try steam and saline nasal sprays or drops.
- **Decongestant nasal sprays** (oxymetazoline or xylometazoline) are considered safe in pregnancy because so little is absorbed. Use them only briefly: KEMH and healthdirect say no more than 3 days at a time, MotherSafe no more than 5, because overuse can make the congestion worse.
- **If you've already taken one,** MotherSafe, writing about the first trimester, says accidental exposure at the recommended dose shouldn't be regarded as a cause for concern.
- **Not sure what's in a product?** Ask MotherSafe, your doctor or your pharmacist about its ingredients.
- **While breastfeeding,** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says decongestants aren't recommended either: they can reduce your milk supply and make the baby irritable.
