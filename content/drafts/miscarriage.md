A miscarriage is when a pregnancy ends before 20 weeks. It is common: up to 1 in 4 pregnancies end in miscarriage before 14 weeks. Most happen in the first trimester; a late miscarriage is one in the second trimester, up to 20 weeks.

## Why it happens

In most cases no one knows why. Around 50 to 80 per cent of miscarriages come from random chromosomal abnormalities, which happen more often in the sperm and eggs of people over 35. It is unlikely that you did anything to cause it. Sadly, if a pregnancy is developing abnormally, nothing can be done to prevent or stop a miscarriage. People who miscarry because of a random chromosomal abnormality have a high chance of a healthy pregnancy later.

## The kinds you might hear about

- **Threatened:** bleeding or pain, but the cervix stays closed. It may settle and the pregnancy carry on, or it may become a miscarriage.
- **Inevitable:** the miscarriage has started and the cervix is open, with bleeding and strong cramps.
- **Incomplete:** some pregnancy tissue has passed and some is still in the uterus.
- **Complete:** all the pregnancy tissue has passed. If it happened at home, see a doctor or midwife to check.
- **Missed (silent):** the pregnancy has ended but stays in the uterus, often with no symptoms. You may find out at a routine visit or scan.
- **Blighted ovum (anembryonic pregnancy):** a fertilised egg implants but an embryo doesn't develop.

## What it can look like

Usually vaginal bleeding, from light spotting to heavy, with cramping or pain low in the tummy, and sometimes clots or tissue. But bleeding in early pregnancy is common and doesn't always mean a miscarriage. If you're bleeding now, read [I think I'm having a miscarriage](miscarriage-signs-what-to-do). The choices that come after a diagnosis are in [the three options](miscarriage-management-options).

## How it can feel

You may feel shock, sadness, anger, guilt, anxiety, numbness or confusion, or lose trust in your body. Pregnancy loss can affect your partner too, and the two of you may react in different ways.

## When to get help

If you have very heavy bleeding, strong pain or feel unwell, call 000 and ask for an ambulance.

If strong feelings last a long time, or you think you might have depression, reach out to a counsellor or mental health professional. Medicare Mental Health is on 1800 595 212 and Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636.
