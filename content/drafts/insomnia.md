## What's usual

Sleep problems affect about 1 in 2 pregnancies and may get worse as pregnancy goes on. You need more sleep than usual, but it's often lighter and you wake more. Late in pregnancy, toilet trips, heartburn, kicks and discomfort all break it up, and you may have disturbing dreams in late pregnancy.

**From 28 weeks, sleep on your side**, at night and for daytime naps; either side is fine. Lying on your back late in pregnancy presses on major blood vessels and is linked to a higher risk of stillbirth. If you wake on your back, don't worry: just roll over. A pillow between your bent knees and one under your belly help.

What else helps:

- a pregnancy pillow
- regular bed and wake times, short naps, caffeine only in the morning
- a quiet, dark bedroom, no clocks to watch, no screens for an hour before bed
- for heartburn, raise your head on pillows; for night wees, drink less in the evening

Your doctor or midwife will likely recommend a non-medicine treatment first, such as behavioural and psychological treatments; cognitive behaviour therapy is a treatment of choice. Herbal remedies aren't recommended in pregnancy, nor are melatonin or suvorexant. Speak with your doctor before taking anything.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you're having suicidal thoughts and are in immediate danger, call triple zero (000). If you're extremely distressed or having suicidal thoughts, Lifeline is on 13 11 14.

## See your doctor or midwife

- you have trouble sleeping and are worried it's affecting your or your baby's health
- you started snoring during pregnancy
- heartburn is causing severe discomfort, or comes with swelling of your hands, feet or face, a headache or distorted vision
- you still have trouble sleeping after trying these tips
