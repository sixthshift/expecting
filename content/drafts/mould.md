NSW Health's mould advice doesn't mention pregnancy at all. Mould is a breathing and allergy issue, and most people have no health problems from it.

## Why

Mould spores can cause a runny or blocked nose, eye and skin irritation and sometimes wheezing in people who are sensitive or allergic. People with asthma, allergies or other breathing conditions are more sensitive, and people with weakened immune systems or chronic lung disease are more at risk of infection.

## What to do

- **Fix the cause**: run exhaust fans, open windows, repair leaks, and fully clean and dry water-damaged carpet or throw it out. Mould comes back otherwise.
- **For routine cleaning**, use mild detergent or vinegar (4 parts vinegar to 1 part water).
- **If that doesn't shift it**, use diluted bleach (250 ml in 4 litres of water) with rubber gloves, safety glasses and good ventilation.
- **Dry the surface completely** after cleaning.
- **Testing is rarely needed**, since most mould is visible.
- **With cleaning products in pregnancy**, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to wear gloves and a face mask, avoid breathing fumes and, if a task involves chemicals, ask someone else to do it.
- **See your doctor** if you have health problems after contact with mould. In a life-threatening emergency, call 000.
