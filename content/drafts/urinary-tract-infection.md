## What's usual

A urinary tract infection is the most common bacterial infection in pregnancy: about 1 in 10 pregnant people get one. You can have one with no symptoms at all, which is why you're offered a urine test at your first antenatal visit or soon after.

The signs:

- pain or burning when you wee
- needing to wee more often than usual, or leaking before you reach the toilet
- feeling your bladder is still full after you've been
- cloudy, bloody or very smelly urine
- pain low in your belly or above the pubic bone
- a fever

It can also cause cramping that feels like early labour. If the infection has reached your kidneys, you may have a fever above 38°C, back pain and vomiting.

A bladder infection is more likely to become a kidney infection in pregnancy, and kidney infections can be serious for you and your baby. UTIs are treated with antibiotics that are safe in pregnancy. To lower your chances: drink plenty of water, wee when you need to and straight after sex, and wipe front to back.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have a fever or chills at any time in pregnancy.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your midwife or doctor if you have any symptoms of a UTI. Don't delay getting antibiotics: an infection can develop quickly and lead to problems for you and your baby.
