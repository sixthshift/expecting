Most people who become pregnant after 40 have healthy pregnancies and healthy babies. Some conditions become more common with age, and your care team can help monitor and support you. These are possibilities rather than certainties.

## What's more common

After 40 you may be more likely to have high blood pressure, deep vein thrombosis (a blood clot) or heartburn. Other complications that increase include:

- ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage
- premature labour
- gestational diabetes
- pre-eclampsia
- placenta problems
- a higher chance of needing a caesarean

You're also more likely to be carrying twins or triplets. For the baby, there's a higher chance of being small, needing care in a neonatal intensive care unit, low Apgar scores, and some genetic conditions.

## Screening and testing

Screening tests are offered to everyone and show whether your baby is more likely to have a condition such as Down syndrome, Edwards syndrome or Patau syndrome. A diagnostic test, such as chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis, can confirm it. You choose what feels right, and a genetic counsellor can help at any stage: before screening, after results, or when weighing further tests.

## Your care and the birth

Early, regular antenatal care helps you stay informed. You can choose your model of care: public hospital care, shared care with your GP, a midwifery group practice, or a private obstetrician. Worth asking: where can I give birth, who will I see, and how are decisions made if things change?

Some care providers suggest planning the birth around 40 weeks, because the chance of stillbirth increases slightly after that, which may mean an induction. You can discuss it with your provider and decide based on your situation and preferences.

## Your wellbeing

If getting here took longer than you hoped, it can be emotionally hard. Counselling and peer support can help, and Beyond Blue is on 1300 22 4636.
