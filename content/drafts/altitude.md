Talk to your doctor before you book. High places are on the list of travel risks that make pregnancy complications more likely.

## Why

At altitude there's less oxygen in the air, which means less oxygen for your baby, and pregnancy makes you more vulnerable to altitude sickness.

The sources draw the line at different heights:

- **MotherSafe** lists high-altitude areas, above 2,500 metres, among destination risks that significantly increase the chance of complications, and says to consult your doctor before long-distance or overseas travel, especially then. Anyone pregnant who has travelled to high altitude should put off exercise until acclimatised.
- **Better Health** lists high-altitude activities such as mountain climbing above 3,000 metres among activities to avoid.
- **Pregnancy, Birth and Baby** says to avoid exercise more than 2,000 metres above sea level until you've acclimatised.

## What to do

- **Ask your doctor or midwife** about the heights on your itinerary.
- **If you go,** don't exercise until you've acclimatised.
