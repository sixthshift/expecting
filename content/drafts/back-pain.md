## What's usual

Backache affects up to 7 in 10 pregnant people, especially in the third trimester. Hormones loosen your ligaments, so your joints aren't in their usual alignment, and your growing baby's weight pulls your spine forward.

What helps:

- **Move gently:** walking, swimming, stretching and strengthening. A physiotherapist can suggest exercises; check with your doctor first, and stop anything that hurts.
- **Posture:** stand and sit tall, don't slump, and draw in your tummy muscles when you walk or lift.
- **Sleep on your side** with a pillow between your legs, and keep your knees together as you roll to get out of bed.
- **Medicine:** paracetamol is considered safe, at the lowest dose for the shortest time. Don't take anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen or aspirin unless your doctor advises it.

If pain persists, see your GP or a physiotherapist.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) immediately and ask for an ambulance if you have:

- **signs of preterm labour** (before 37 weeks): back pain with tummy pains or cramps, pressure in your pelvis or lower back, or fluid leaking from your vagina
- **signs of cauda equina syndrome**, a very rare compressed nerve in the spine: back pain with lost feeling or weakness in your limbs, pins and needles or tingling in your limbs, or problems controlling your bowels or bladder

## See your doctor or midwife

See your midwife or doctor if you are concerned, feel "pins and needles" in your legs, or have leg weakness or incontinence.

See your doctor or midwife if you're concerned about lower back pain, including pain that:

- disrupts your day
- happens while exercising
- is ongoing from previous back surgery
- is new to you

Back pain can sometimes be a urinary tract infection. See your doctor or midwife if it comes with painful urination, blood in your urine, nausea and vomiting, or changes to your bowel or bladder.
