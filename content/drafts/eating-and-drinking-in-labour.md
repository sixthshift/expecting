In early labour, the latent phase, which can last many hours or even days, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to eat light snacks such as fruit or toast to maintain your energy, and drink water to stay hydrated. The Royal Women's Hospital likewise says to have regular snacks to build your energy reserves while you rest at home.

Vomiting is quite common during labour, according to the Royal Women's, and nausea is one of the things you may feel around transition.

## Later in labour

For the second stage, the Royal Women's says to keep up your fluids and rest when you can. A cold face washer can soothe if you feel hot.

## Early labour at home

The Royal Women's says to stay at home for as long as you can, rest as much as possible (sleep if it's night), try a bath or shower, and go to the toilet regularly, emptying your bowels if you can.

## At the hospital or birth centre

Once you arrive, your midwife will monitor you and make sure your labour is progressing as it should.

## For the partner

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says a support partner can offer snacks, drinks and ice, and encourage you to eat and drink. It also says to bring snacks and drinks for yourself.

## What the sources don't cover

None of the sources sets rules for eating once you're in the birth suite, or with an epidural or a planned caesarean.
