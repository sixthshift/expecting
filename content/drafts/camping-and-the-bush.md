Yes. The thing to plan for is mosquitoes.

## Why

Mosquitoes in Australia can carry diseases that can harm you or your baby, including Ross River virus, Barmah Forest virus, Murray Valley encephalitis and Japanese encephalitis (JE). MotherSafe says pregnant women are more likely to be bitten.

JE has been found in people and pigs in New South Wales, South Australia, Victoria and Queensland. The local risk is likely to be highest for people working at or living near piggeries that tested positive, and for people camping, fishing or hiking near lots of mosquitoes, particularly near rivers and lakes. JE infection in the first and second trimesters has been linked with miscarriage.

## What to do

- **Use repellent.** Personal repellents are usually safe in pregnancy, and the benefits of avoiding bites outweigh the risk. MotherSafe prefers medium-strength DEET to high strength (avoid 80%); picaridin can also be used.
- **Cover up and screen out:** long sleeves and long pants, nets and screens, and keep outdoor time from dusk to dawn to a minimum.
- **Ask about the JE vaccine** if you'll be at high risk. The inactivated vaccine (JEspect) can be given in pregnancy if needed; the live one (Imojev) can't.
- **Mind the heat:** you're more sensitive to it when pregnant. Stay in the shade, drink water and rest.
