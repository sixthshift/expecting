Your skin changes because of higher hormone levels and extra blood flow. Some people find it looks healthier and "glows"; others get dry, blotchy or more sensitive skin, or acne. Most changes slowly improve or go away after the birth, and a skin condition that got better or worse in pregnancy will probably go back to how it was. The usual ones are not dangerous for you or your baby.

## The usual changes

- **Darker areas.** Nipples, armpits, inner thighs, the genital area and under the eyes can darken.
- **Linea nigra.** A dark line down the middle of the abdomen, from the belly button towards the pubic area, often starting in the first trimester. It isn't dangerous, needs no treatment and usually fades after the birth, though it might not disappear. Sun on your abdomen can make it darker.
- **Melasma (chloasma, "the mask of pregnancy").** Symmetrical brown patches, usually on the face, most often starting in the second or third trimester. It won't cause health problems, but sunscreen and sun protection help. There's a good chance it fades after the birth, and it can come back with sun or in a later pregnancy.
- **Stretch marks.** Red, pink or purple lines that almost everyone gets, mostly on the tummy, thighs, breasts, hips and upper arms. They may itch at first and fade to pale silvery streaks. No skin product has been scientifically proven to prevent them.
- **Acne, eczema, prurigo.** Acne is likely to flare in the first trimester if you've had it, and usually improves in the third. Prurigo is an itchy, dry, bumpy rash that usually appears in the first or second trimester.
- **Moles.** They can get larger and darker, though they are not more likely to become dangerous during pregnancy. Check them for changes in shape, size and colour, and show your doctor any that change. Itchiness is a sign to look out for.

## Itching and creams

If your skin is itchy, even without a rash, seek medical advice: Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says itchy skin can be a sign of a more serious condition that can harm your baby.

Most moisturisers and cosmetics are safe, but check ingredients. It's best to avoid products containing vitamin A (retinol); other products to avoid include hydroquinone bleaching products, minoxidil lotions and tretinoin acne treatments. Waxing and hair removal creams are safe, though skin may be more sensitive. It's never safe to sunbathe; cover up and use sunscreen.
