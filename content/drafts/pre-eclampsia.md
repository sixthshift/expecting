Pre-eclampsia is a serious condition that causes high blood pressure and can affect the liver, kidneys and brain. It usually comes on suddenly after at least 20 weeks, and can happen any time in the second half of pregnancy or the first few days after the birth. It affects about 1 in 10 pregnancies, and for most it stays mild, but it can get worse very quickly. It can also affect the placenta, and so how well the baby grows.

## How they watch for it

Most people with pre-eclampsia don't notice any symptoms; it's usually picked up at a routine visit. That's why your blood pressure is checked at every antenatal visit, and compared with your first reading. If it's high, your doctor will suggest tests: a urine test for protein, blood tests and an examination. The baby is checked too, with ultrasound scans of growth and wellbeing, and heart rate monitoring (CTG).

## Who is at higher risk

Your risk is higher if you had pre-eclampsia before, have long-standing high blood pressure, diabetes, kidney disease or an autoimmune condition such as lupus, or are pregnant with twins or more. It's also higher if this is your first pregnancy, you're 40 or older, it's been more than 10 years since your last pregnancy, you were very overweight at the start of the pregnancy, or pre-eclampsia runs in your family.

If you're at increased risk, your doctor may recommend low-dose aspirin during pregnancy, calcium or vitamin D supplements, blood pressure medicine or regular moderate exercise, and more frequent check-ups. Check with your doctor or midwife before taking any medicine or supplement.

## If you're diagnosed

You'll be monitored closely, with more blood pressure checks, and you may be advised to go to hospital for rest and monitoring. Treatment can include blood pressure medicines and treatment to prevent seizures. The only complete cure is the birth of the baby, and with severe pre-eclampsia your doctor may recommend the baby is born early; they'll talk with you about the risks first.

After the birth your blood pressure is watched closely for the first few days, you'll usually stay a little longer, and it's normal for your blood pressure to go up and down. Have regular checks and go to your 6-week postnatal check to make sure it's back to normal.

## When to get help

Seek medical attention urgently or go to your local emergency department if you have symptoms such as a severe headache, blurred vision, sudden swelling of your hands, feet or face, or pain under your ribcage.

See your midwife, doctor or pregnancy care provider straight away if you have any symptoms of pre-eclampsia, including:

- sudden swelling of your hands, face or feet
- a headache that doesn't go away with simple pain relief
- vision problems, like blurring, flashing lights or spots
- severe pain below the ribs or in your upper tummy
- heartburn that doesn't go away with antacids
- feeling very unwell

HELLP syndrome, the severe form, needs urgent medical attention.
