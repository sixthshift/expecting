A stretch and sweep, or membrane sweep, is a way of trying to start labour. Your midwife or doctor may suggest it when you're close to or past your due date, and you may also be offered one before being induced. It should be a shared decision: you don't have to have one, and you may choose to wait for labour to start on its own.

## What happens

It's done as part of an internal vaginal exam. With gloves and lubricant, the midwife or doctor puts a finger in your vagina and checks your cervix is open. If it is, they sweep their finger around to try to separate the membranes from the wall of your uterus. If it's too uncomfortable, you can ask them to stop at any time, and they should.

## Afterwards

You might have a "show" (a plug of mucus, with some blood mixed in), cramps or contractions, a sign that the prostaglandin hormones that help prepare your body for labour have been released. Paracetamol and a warm bath can help if you're uncomfortable, following the packet or your midwife's or doctor's advice. If it works, you can expect to go into labour within 48 hours. If it doesn't, it can be repeated over the next couple of days, and your doctor or midwife may talk to you about being induced.

## Risks

For an uncomplicated, low-risk pregnancy there is very little risk, but it can be uncomfortable and can cause vaginal bleeding and irregular contractions.

## When it isn't offered

You won't be offered one if you have a low-lying placenta (placenta praevia), your baby isn't head down (unless you're planning a vaginal breech birth), you have a vaginal infection, or your waters have broken. It's safe with a positive group B strep result, but may not be safe if you have HIV or active genital herpes.
