People become single parents for many reasons, including separation, the death of a partner, or choosing to have a child on their own with help such as IVF. Children raised by single parents can be just as happy and emotionally healthy as children with two parents. What matters is consistent, sensitive, nurturing care, and some compassion for yourself.

## In pregnancy

If you're pregnant on your own, it's important to have people you can share your feelings with; problems can be harder to solve alone. Talk to your midwife about the services available to you.

## Your birth support person

Think early about who you'd like with you at antenatal visits and in labour: a friend, a sibling or a parent. Choose someone you feel safe and comfortable with, who's calm, can be there when labour starts, and can speak up for you with the maternity team. A backup person is a good idea in case they're unavailable. Some people choose a doula, a non-medical birth support professional. Your midwife or doula may know of antenatal classes run for single people.

## What's harder, and what helps

Gidget names the challenges and some answers:

- **Time off.** Without helpful support, it's harder to look after yourself, get to a medical appointment or take a night off. Take turns with other single parents, or find a local high school student to give you a break.
- **Money.** Ask friends, family or your local council about child-minding, get legal advice if you need it, and check what benefits are available. Services Australia can tell you what help you can get as a single parent, which may include a parenting payment. Childcare can sometimes be subsidised.
- **Sleep.** When you're the only adult, it's all on you. Take short naps and rests when you can; a sling or a walk can break up the day.
- **Isolation.** You may not talk to another adult for days. Other single parents, in person or online, can ease the loneliness.
- **Expectations.** Babies don't need a lot at first. "Good enough" is absolutely fine.

PBB says the best source of support may be other single parents. Local groups and your council can connect you; a trusted relative or friend may be glad to babysit regularly.

## The upside

You make all the decisions, and you can choose the kind of parent you want to be.

## Plan ahead, and where to get help

Think about how you'll manage after the birth and who'll be around. If there's nobody, talk it over with someone, and don't be afraid to ask friends and family. If you're struggling, call PANDA on 1300 726 306, Medicare Mental Health on 1800 595 212, or Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636. Gidget Foundation can help too.
