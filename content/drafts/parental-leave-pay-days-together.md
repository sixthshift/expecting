There's a limit to how many Parental Leave Pay days you can take at the same time as your partner. For a child born from 1 July 2025 it's **20 days** (it was 10 days for a child born before then). With an exemption, you may be able to take more together.

## When an exemption may apply

- **A multiple birth:** you can choose to take more than 20 days at the same time.
- **A stillbirth or the death of your child:** you can also choose to take more than 20 days at the same time.
- **The birth mother had a caesarean,** or developed a complication or medical condition at birth.
- **Your baby stayed in hospital** after the birth, or was hospitalised immediately after it, for at least 14 days.
- **Your baby developed a complication or illness** during the pregnancy, at birth or straight after, and needs an extended period of medical care, resulting in higher care needs.
- **You or your partner have a medical condition.**

In deciding, Services Australia may consider whether your circumstances affected your ability to care for your child on your own, caused significant stress, or meant your child needs more than one parent's care.

## How to ask

You can request an exemption as part of your claim, or ask Services Australia to assess one after you've claimed by calling the Centrelink families line on 136 150 (Monday to Friday, 8 am to 8 pm).

If asked, you'll need evidence. For example:

- a caesarean or birth complication: a letter from the doctor or hospital saying so
- a baby in hospital for 14 days or more: a letter giving the date they went in and the date they left, or the expected stay
- a medical condition: a letter from the doctor saying what it is, when it started and how long it's expected to last
- a multiple birth: the Doctor or Midwife Proof of Birth Declaration, or a letter saying your child was part of a multiple birth
