To get [Parental Leave Pay](parental-leave-pay), you need to have worked enough in the time before the birth. Because it's counted back from the birth, it's worth checking now, while there's still time to plan around a job change or a break.

## The test

You need to have worked both:

- **10 of the 13 months** before the birth (Services Australia counts 10 months as 295 days and 13 months as 392 days), and
- **at least 330 hours** in those 10 months, around one day a week.

And you **can't have a gap of more than 12 weeks** between workdays in that 10-month period.

You can include work for more than one employer, and many activities count as work, including paid leave. If you're self-employed, you may still meet it.

## Who has to meet it

The birth mother, as Services Australia puts it, must meet the work test to claim. If you're the partner, for you to get Parental Leave Pay, both you and the birth parent must meet the work test.

## Exceptions

If something stopped you working during those 13 months, you may still qualify. Exceptions include pregnancy-related illness and complications, premature birth, a severe medical condition for you or someone you care for, family and domestic violence, dangerous jobs or workplace hazards, and natural disasters. You'll need documents to prove it, provided when you claim online.

## What to do

Count back 13 months from your due date and check your work history, each of you. If it's close, or you're planning a job change or unpaid break, look at the gaps now.
