## What's usual

Abdominal separation (diastasis recti, or DRAM) is when the two long muscles down the middle of your tummy separate. It's common in the later stages of pregnancy, a natural adaptation to your growing baby, and it isn't painful. You may see a gap, or feel your core is weak when you lift something. It can also bring lower back pain. It's more likely if you've had more than one pregnancy or are overweight.

It often gets better in the weeks and months after the birth, though up to 1 in 3 women still report it a year later. A supportive brace or special underwear can help support your back. Don't do sit-ups or abdominal crunches while your muscles are separated; pelvic tilts and abdominal bracing are better.

## See your doctor, midwife or physiotherapist

If you think you may have abdominal separation, see your doctor or maternal health nurse. Your doctor, midwife or physiotherapist can check it, and your doctor may refer you to a women's health physiotherapist for an exercise program.
