Yes, you can keep your coffee. You don't need to cut caffeine out completely, but have no more than 200 mg a day, in total, from all sources.

## Why

Caffeine crosses the placenta, so it reaches your baby. Too much caffeine in pregnancy may increase the risk of complications, including miscarriage, stillbirth and low birth weight.

## What to do

- **Count it in milligrams.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's caffeine chart gives about 145 mg in a 50 mL espresso, 80 mg in a 250 mL cup of instant, 10 to 50 mg in a cup of tea, 35 mg in a 375 mL can of cola, 20 mg in a 100 g chocolate bar and 80 mg in a 250 mL energy drink. (Its healthy-diet page gives lower figures for some: 100 mg a shot of espresso and 60 mg a cup of instant.)
- **In cups,** 200 mg is about 1 to 2 espresso coffees, or 2 to 3 cups of instant (3, on the foods-to-avoid page).
- **Skip double shots.** Energy drinks and double shots of espresso aren't recommended in pregnancy.
- **Check other sources.** Tea, cola, chocolate and energy drinks all count, and some cough, cold, headache and slimming medicines contain 20 to 100 mg a dose. Check labels, and ask your pharmacist before taking any medicine.
- **Cutting back?** Withdrawal can bring headaches, irritability, tiredness and feeling on edge. It's temporary and may last up to a week.
