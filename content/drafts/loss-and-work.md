After a loss you may need time to recover physically and to look after yourself. Here's what the law gives you, and how to handle going back.

## After a miscarriage

Under the National Employment Standards, every employee, casuals included, can take compassionate leave if they have a miscarriage or their spouse or de facto partner does. That's 2 days each time, taken as a single continuous 2-day period, 2 separate days, or any separate periods as agreed with your employer. Full-time and part-time employees are paid at their base rate; casuals' compassionate leave is unpaid. Tell your employer as soon as you can, even after the leave has started. They can ask for reasonable evidence.

Some employers give more than 2 days, so ask your employer or HR about their policies. For many people 2 days won't feel like enough. Talk to your doctor and your employer about other leave you could take before going back.

## After a stillbirth

The same 2 days' compassionate leave applies when a baby in your immediate family or household is stillborn. If your baby is stillborn or dies in the first 24 months, you can still take up to 12 months' unpaid parental leave, and your employer can't call you back or cancel it. You can cancel it yourself and return: with written notice if it hasn't started, or at least 4 weeks' written notice if it has, unless you agree on an earlier date.

If your employer offers paid parental leave, it can't refuse or cancel it because of a stillbirth or a child's death, unless you ask or an exception applies. This protection applies where a child is stillborn or dies on or after 7 November 2025.

## Telling work

You don't have to tell your workplace about your loss. If colleagues knew you were pregnant, you can ask your employer to tell them for you, or send an email yourself so you don't have to answer questions face to face.

## Going back

Don't feel you need to rush back unless work is a welcome distraction. Talk to your workplace and make a flexible plan to return: some people go part-time or work from home for a while. It's normal to find it hard to concentrate, to have good days and bad, and to be upset by colleagues' pregnancy announcements or work events with children. When it's overwhelming, a walk, a coffee or a call to someone kind can help.
