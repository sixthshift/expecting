## What's usual

It's common for blood pressure to fall below normal in the first trimester and keep falling in the second; it rises back towards your pre-pregnancy level by the time the baby is born. Low blood pressure can leave you tired and feeling faint (syncope: feeling faint and passing out). It's common and not usually a cause for concern, but in rare cases it can be dangerous.

You're most likely to feel faint when you get up quickly from a chair or a hot bath, or stand for too long. In late pregnancy, lying on your back can make you feel faint; turning onto your side helps.

To avoid it: drink plenty of water, stand up slowly after sitting or when getting out of bed or the bath, have something to eat at regular intervals, lie on your side instead of your back, and try not to stand for long periods (if you need to stand, leg exercises and support stockings can improve your blood flow). At the first sign of feeling faint, sit or lie down until it passes.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you're in early pregnancy and you faint, feel dizzy, or have bleeding or abdominal pain, it could be a sign of an ectopic pregnancy. Go straight to your nearest emergency department, or call triple zero (000) for an ambulance (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby; healthdirect's ectopic pregnancy page says the same for dizziness or fainting).

## Contact your hospital now

Before 20 weeks, healthdirect says to contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately for dizziness or fainting.

## See your doctor or midwife

Contact your doctor or midwife if you have frequent dizziness or fainting. If you feel dizzy or faint, call your midwife, or the Pregnancy, Birth and Baby helpline on 1800 882 436.

## Mention it at your next visit

If you notice symptoms of low blood pressure, let your midwife or doctor know; you can also discuss your blood pressure at your regular antenatal checks.
