## What's usual

Haemorrhoids (piles) are swollen varicose veins in the rectum and anus, and they're common in pregnancy. They're more common in the second and third trimesters, as your growing uterus presses on the veins. Straining because of constipation can trigger or worsen them. They can cause pain or burning when you poo, and sometimes minor bleeding. They won't affect your baby, and in most cases they go away within a few days after the birth.

The best way to treat and prevent them is to avoid constipation: plenty of water and a high-fibre diet. An ice pack on external haemorrhoids can reduce swelling and a warm bath may ease the pain. Paracetamol can be taken in pregnancy, as directed. Laxatives and haemorrhoid creams are options too, but check with your doctor or pharmacist before using medicines or creams while pregnant.

Some people worry the pushing stage of labour will make them worse. Speak to your midwife if you're concerned.

## See your doctor

If you have pain when passing stools, or see blood on the toilet paper or in your poo. It might be haemorrhoids, but it could be a sign of a more serious condition, so they can confirm it and rule out other causes.
