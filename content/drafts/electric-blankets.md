The Australian pages we checked don't give a rule on electric blankets, heat packs or hot water bottles. MotherSafe lists electric blankets, along with hot tubs, saunas and sustained exercise, among the things that may raise your body temperature.

## Why

Body temperature matters in pregnancy. MotherSafe says a sustained fever of more than 38.9 °C for at least 24 hours has been associated with an increased risk of miscarriage and certain malformations, including neural tube defects like spina bifida in early pregnancy, and stillbirth in later pregnancy. That figure is about fever, not about a warm bed; the pages don't say how much heat from a blanket matters.

The Royal Women's advises keeping your temperature down when you exercise (a cool ventilated environment, no spas or saunas).

## What to do

- **If you're unsure,** ask your doctor or midwife about how you use your blanket or heat pack.
- **If you have a fever,** MotherSafe says to drink adequate fluids and rest, and to contact your doctor if it is prolonged.
