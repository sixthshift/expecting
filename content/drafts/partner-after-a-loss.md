It didn't happen to your body, but it happened to you. A loss can bring partners great sadness, helplessness and anxiety, and that is normal. Your cultural beliefs may shape how you feel, and the feelings can be especially strong if getting pregnant was hard or this isn't the first loss.

## Grieving differently

You may grieve in a different way, or with a different intensity, from your partner. You may not feel comfortable showing the same anguish. Or you may have been deeply invested from the very start, and be hit hard now. Not everyone feels sad; some feel relief or confusion. There's no right or wrong way to feel.

## Being the strong one

Many partners feel they need to be strong for the one who carried the pregnancy, or that they shouldn't be as upset. That can leave you isolated. Sometimes partners set their own feelings aside at first, believing their job is to support, and feel sad or anxious later, while planning another baby. If you hadn't told work, family or friends about the pregnancy yet, they may not be there to lean on.

Acknowledge your feelings and give yourself time to grieve while you support your partner. You may be coping in different ways, and that's all right. It can take time for either of you to want intimacy again, or to feel ready for another pregnancy, even after your partner has physically recovered. Talk openly, and be patient with each other.

## Looking after yourself

Some partners find comfort in rituals that matter in their culture, in talking to a friend or family member, in a support group, or with a mental health professional. Counselling can help, on your own or as a couple.

Time off is yours too. Under the Fair Work rules, you can take compassionate leave if your spouse or de facto partner has a miscarriage, or if your baby is stillborn.

## Support for partners

- **Red Nose Grief and Loss** runs support groups for non-birthing parents (PBB's wording). Its line is 1300 308 307, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
- **Miscarriage Australia** has a page for men, and one for LGBTIQA+ partners and families.
- **SMS4dads** sends free text messages for dads, including a set for grieving dads.

## When to get help

If you're struggling, talk to a mental health professional or your GP. If you're in crisis, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636, or 000.
