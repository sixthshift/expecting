The sources point two ways. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says some plastics contain bisphenol A (BPA), found in water bottles, toys and the lining of some food and drink products. It says exposure in pregnancy and infancy has been suggested to affect a baby's development, organs and immune system and to increase the chance of childhood asthma. It then adds that Food Standards Australia New Zealand says BPA in food packaging is unlikely to be a health risk.

## Why

Most chemicals you come across in daily life won't harm your baby, usually because the amount is so small you don't need to worry (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby). The same page says flame retardants, added to furniture, carpet, clothes and electronics to make them less flammable, can impact your baby's brain development.

## What to do

- **Keep your home well ventilated** and wash your hands regularly, as the page advises for reducing chemical exposure in general.
- **Look for alternatives** if you want to: the page suggests products with low levels of chemicals that may be harmful in pregnancy.
- **Non-stick pans and PFAS:** the page doesn't mention them, so there is no guidance here.
