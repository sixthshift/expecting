It's important your baby is covered from birth, in case they need hospital treatment or other medical care straight away. Every fund has different rules for newborns, so the answer is in your fund's rules.

## What to ask your fund

- **Will the baby be covered automatically** under your policy after the birth, or do you need to **change to a family policy**?
- **When do you need to switch?** Many funds ask you to upgrade to a family policy 1 to 3 months before the baby is born. Some ask up to 12 months before, so that any congenital conditions are covered.
- **Will the waiting periods** on the upgrade be served in time to cover your newborn?

## Why it matters

If your baby is healthy, they usually won't be formally admitted to hospital. If they're premature or very unwell, they may need care in a Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU), usually in a large public hospital. As a public patient in a public hospital, most costs are covered by Medicare. As a private patient, your fund may cover the costs, depending on your policy, but if the baby is born within a waiting period they may not be covered. Your baby is charged separately for their nursery care, on top of your own hospital stay.

Twins or more raise the chance of an early birth and a NICU stay, which can be very expensive, so check with your fund what's covered.

## After the birth

Tell your fund about any change in your family circumstances, such as adding a newborn to your policy. Keeping it up to date helps avoid problems when you need to claim.
