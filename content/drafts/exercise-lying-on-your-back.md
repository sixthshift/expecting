After 16 weeks, it's best to avoid exercises lying flat on your back. That's the advice from the Royal Women's Hospital, MotherSafe and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's sport page. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's exercise page puts the emphasis later, saying lying flat is dangerous particularly after 28 weeks.

## Why

The weight of the baby can press on the major blood vessels returning blood to your heart. That can make you feel faint, light-headed or sick, and can reduce blood flow to your baby.

It's the same reason you're advised to sleep on your side in the second half of pregnancy.

## What to do

- **Swap the position.** Better Health says some of these exercises can be modified by lying on your side. For core exercises it also gives sitting, standing and hands-and-knees versions.
- **In a class,** tell the instructor how many weeks you are so they can modify the moves.
