Platelets are tiny blood cells that clump together to stop bleeding. Around 1 in every 10 people have a low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) during pregnancy. Most of the time it doesn't cause problems, though that depends on the cause.

## How it's found

Usually on the full blood count, a routine blood test done early in pregnancy and again at 26 to 28 weeks. You might have no symptoms at all. If you do, you may notice bleeding gums, a skin rash or small bruises. If your platelets are low, you might have more blood tests to look for the cause.

## The causes

**Gestational thrombocytopenia** accounts for up to 8 in 10 cases. It's caused by the normal changes of pregnancy, is usually mild and goes away after the birth, and doesn't usually cause problems for you or the baby. It isn't linked to heavier bleeding after the birth or to low platelets in the baby.

**Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)** is an autoimmune condition that can get worse in pregnancy and cause very low platelets. You'll be referred to a haematologist, may need treatment before the birth, and may be advised to give birth in hospital. There's a higher chance of heavy bleeding for you or the baby, and the baby has a blood test at birth.

**HELLP syndrome**, a severe form of pre-eclampsia, can cause very low platelets and liver problems. Other causes include some viruses, low vitamin B12 or folate, some medicines, liver disease and an overactive spleen.

## What happens next

Treatment depends on the cause. You'll be offered more full blood counts; how often depends on how low the count is and how far along you are. Treatments that can raise platelets include a steroid (prednisolone), intravenous immunoglobulin and a platelet transfusion. If the count keeps falling, you might be advised to have your labour induced.

**Epidurals.** Low platelets can increase the chance of bleeding around the spinal cord, so your doctor or midwife will discuss whether an epidural is safe for you.

After the birth you'll be advised to have blood tests at about 2 and 6 weeks to check your platelets have recovered.

## When to get help

See your doctor or midwife straight away if you have any symptoms that concern you.
