Yes, you can breastfeed after a caesarean, whether it's planned or happens unexpectedly. The Australian Breastfeeding Association says that whatever anaesthetic you have, it usually doesn't stop you from breastfeeding.

## Skin-to-skin and the first feed

Ask for your baby to be placed on your chest as soon as they've been checked over, even while you're still in the operating room. If that isn't possible, you may be able to have skin-to-skin time in the recovery room. Your partner or support person can remind the team that you'd like to hold your baby skin-to-skin and start feeding as soon as possible.

The Royal Women's Hospital says that unless you've had a general anaesthetic, skin-to-skin is encouraged straight after the birth, and the midwife will help you with breastfeeding.

## Positions

You'll have a wound, but you can still find a position that works. The Australian Breastfeeding Association suggests:

- the underarm (football) hold, with your baby's feet towards your back and a pillow on your lap to support them and protect your wound
- a laid-back position with your baby off to one side (you might need help to get comfortable)

However you hold your baby, keep them close, with their chest against your body.

## If feeding has to wait

With a general anaesthetic, or if your baby needs extra care, feeding may not happen straight away. You can express your colostrum for your baby in the meantime. The Royal Women's Hospital says that if your baby can't feed from the breast you'll be encouraged to express as soon as possible, about 8 to 10 times every 24 hours, and that you should be able to hold and feed your baby as soon as you're more alert.

## Looking after yourself

A caesarean is major surgery, and it's normal to need more time to recover. The Australian Breastfeeding Association says to try not to lift anything heavier than your baby for at least six weeks or until your postnatal check-up. The Royal Women's Hospital says you'll be offered pain-relieving medications after surgery as you need them.
