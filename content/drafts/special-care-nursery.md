Almost 1 in 5 babies in Australia is admitted to a special care nursery (SCN) or a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU). Even though your baby can't leave hospital yet, they're on the road to becoming stronger.

## Why babies go

A baby goes to special care when they need more care than a normal ward can give, for example because they:

- were born early and need extra care
- have jaundice, low blood sugar or breathing problems
- were born with a low birth weight
- have moved from intensive care as their health has improved

An SCN is not the same as a NICU: babies in the NICU are more seriously ill and need more care. If the hospital where you gave birth can't care for your baby, they may be transferred to another hospital by road or air, and it may be possible for you or your partner to travel with them.

## What you'll see

Your baby may start in an incubator (a humidicrib) before moving to a cot. Machines may monitor their oxygen, heart rate and breathing, and there may be equipment to treat jaundice. The staff will explain any equipment or procedure your baby needs. Doctors who specialise in newborns, neonatal nurses and midwives, lactation consultants and others work there, and you're considered an important part of your baby's healthcare team.

## Being with your baby

You can see your baby as often as you like. Staff will encourage hands-on contact: holding, feeding, changing nappies and bathing. The nursery is kept quiet, so you'll be asked to talk softly. Visitors may be limited to 2 or 3 at a time; rules differ between hospitals. Wash or sanitise your hands, and stay away if you have a cold, diarrhoea or vomiting.

Ask about **kangaroo care**: holding your baby in just a nappy against your bare chest. Even babies on a ventilator can have it. It helps regulate their heartbeat, breathing and temperature, and supports bonding and breastfeeding. Check with staff first; your baby needs to be stable, and you may need 2 staff to help with the transfer.

## Feeding

Your baby may be fed through a tube at first, then from the breast or a bottle as they get stronger. You may be shown how to express by hand, use a breast pump, and breastfeed.

## Going home

Every baby is different. Your baby may go home when they've grown enough not to need medical help, can keep their own temperature, are a suitable weight, are feeding well and have finished any drip medicines. Ask the staff when that might be. You may be sent home before your baby.

## Looking after yourself

Grief, anxiety, disbelief or guilt are all natural, and you may feel overwhelmed by the equipment. Don't blame yourself. Talk to the nursery staff; they can arrange a social worker. Miracle Babies Foundation's 24-hour NurtureLine is 1300 622 243, and PANDA's helpline is 1300 726 306.
