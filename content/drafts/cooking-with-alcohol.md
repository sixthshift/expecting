We haven't found an Australian health service that gives advice on dishes cooked with wine, beer or spirits, or on desserts with alcohol in them. What there is covers alcohol itself, so if you're unsure about a dish, ask your doctor, midwife or MotherSafe.

## Why

MotherSafe, at the Royal Hospital for Women, says there's currently no safe level of alcohol established in pregnancy. Alcohol in your bloodstream crosses the placenta, producing levels in the baby's bloodstream as high as yours. The Australian guidelines it quotes say that for anyone pregnant or planning a pregnancy, not drinking is the safest option.

The same sheet says the risk is likely to be low if only small amounts of alcohol were had before you knew you were pregnant or during pregnancy, and that the risk of harm increases when alcohol is consumed frequently and in large amounts over a short period.

## What to do

- **If you're unsure about a dish,** ask your doctor or midwife.
- **If you've already had some alcohol,** MotherSafe says the risk to the baby is likely to be low after small amounts, and that stopping at any time in the pregnancy reduces the risk. Its sheet is about alcohol itself, not food cooked with it.
- **For individual advice,** call MotherSafe: Sydney Metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848.
