Yes. Mosquito repellents approved by the Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority (APVMA) are safe to use when you're pregnant or breastfeeding.

## Why

Mosquitoes can carry diseases that can harm you or your baby, including Ross River virus, Barmah Forest virus, Murray Valley encephalitis and Japanese encephalitis. The benefits of avoiding bites outweigh the risk of using a personal repellent. MotherSafe notes that pregnant women are more likely to be bitten than women who aren't pregnant.

DEET poorly crosses the placenta and hasn't been shown to cause harm at any stage of pregnancy. Picaridin can also be used.

## What to do

- **Choose a repellent** containing DEET, picaridin or oil of lemon eucalyptus. MotherSafe prefers medium-strength DEET to high strength (avoid 80 per cent): it protects as well with less exposure.
- **Put it on after sunscreen**, and keep it away from your eyes, mouth and broken skin.
- **Cover up** outdoors in loose, light-coloured long sleeves and pants, socks and shoes, and avoid being outside when mosquitoes are most active.
- **Keep them out**: insect screens without tears or holes, or mosquito nets over where you sleep if you don't have screens.
- **Japanese encephalitis vaccine** is only recommended for people at highest risk, such as those living or working near affected piggeries. If that's you, the killed JEspect vaccine is considered safe in pregnancy; ask your doctor.
