Not necessarily. Many people who've had a caesarean plan a vaginal birth next time (a VBAC); others plan another caesarean. For most, both are safe ways to give birth. The choice is personal, and it's made with your partner and your healthcare team.

## What goes into the decision

Talk to your doctor, midwife or obstetrician about future births. Things worth discussing:

- when and why you had your last caesarean, and the type of cut made in your uterus
- whether there were complications last time, or are any now
- how you feel about your last birth
- how many children you hope to have

A VBAC may not be recommended in some situations, such as 3 or more previous caesareans, a previous uterine rupture, or a low-lying placenta. Most healthcare professionals recommend a hospital birth if you plan a VBAC, and some regional and remote hospitals don't offer it. If yours doesn't, you can ask to go to one that can.

## Questions to take with you

- What are my options for labour and birth?
- Where can I have a planned VBAC?
- How likely is a successful VBAC for me?
- What are the risks and benefits for me and my baby of each choice, and how likely are they?

## How you feel about it

How you feel about your last birth is part of the conversation. If the last birth was traumatic, see Pregnant again after a traumatic birth.
