Being pregnant after a miscarriage, stillbirth or the death of a baby can bring joy and fear in the same breath. Becoming pregnant after a loss is a known source of stress in pregnancy. Whatever you feel is valid, and you don't need to justify it.

## Deciding when

Trying again is a complex decision. Some people want to try straight away; others need more time. Listen to what feels right for you. Sometimes there's a medical reason to wait, for example until the results of medical or genetic tests are back. If your doctor has advised you to wait a certain time, follow that advice.

## The feelings that come back

Grief isn't a straight line. It can return around due dates, birthdays, anniversaries, medical appointments and other people's pregnancy announcements. Planning or carrying a new pregnancy can bring up anxiety, grief and even conflict between you. Anxiety and fear about a future pregnancy or medical care may come too.

For the partner: if you set your own grief aside last time to be the support, it may surface now, while you plan for or carry another baby.

## Extra care

After a stillbirth or neonatal death, you may be offered extra antenatal care, and there are specialist clinics throughout Australia for pregnancy after loss. Your doctor may suggest:

- more frequent and flexible appointments
- extra ultrasounds to check the baby's growth and pick up complications
- genetic testing
- cervical length screening between 16 and 24 weeks if you've had a spontaneous preterm birth

Your doctor will also talk with you about the timing of the birth and a birth plan, and you may be able to contact your team between appointments. Share your worries and preferences with your doctor; they will use them, with your history and test results, to plan your care.

## Support

- **Gidget Foundation** offers therapy for birthing and non-birthing parents after a stillbirth, including for fears about a future pregnancy.
- **Pink Elephants** has a private online Pregnancy After Loss group, moderated by peer companions who have had their own losses.
- A counsellor or psychologist can help you work through your feelings, and support groups let you talk with people who've been there.

## When to get help

Seek counselling or support if you feel overwhelmed or your grief doesn't ease over time, or if your symptoms feel unmanageable, intensify, or affect your ability to function or feel safe. If you are having thoughts of self-harm or suicide, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 for immediate support. If you or your baby are in immediate danger, call 000.
