About half of full-term babies get some jaundice: a yellow tinge to the skin and the whites of the eyes, from a build-up of bilirubin. It usually shows from about day 3, is usually harmless, and fades within 1 to 2 weeks. Because it often appears **after you've gone home**, it's easy to miss — and some babies need to go back to hospital for light treatment (phototherapy).

Every case should be checked. Contact your midwife, child health nurse or GP if you notice:

- yellow skin, or yellowing in the whites of the eyes
- a baby who is **very sleepy** or hard to feed
- **fewer wet nappies**
- **very pale or white poo**, or dark urine
- jaundice getting **more noticeable**, or lasting past 2 weeks

Go to every follow-up blood test you're booked for. Frequent feeds help, but **sunlight is not a treatment** — don't put the baby in the sun to clear it. Ask your midwife or child health nurse.
