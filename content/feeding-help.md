Feeding is learned, by both of you, and it often takes a few weeks. But pain and struggle aren't something to push through alone. Ask for a feeding assessment if:

- feeding is still **painful**, or nipples are sore or misshapen after feeds
- the baby **can't stay attached**, or tires quickly
- feeds take a **very long time**
- the baby **clicks**, dribbles or leaks milk, or swallows a lot of air
- the baby **isn't gaining weight** as expected

A **lactation consultant**, your **child health nurse** or your **GP** can check attachment and how much milk the baby is getting, and look for things like **tongue-tie**. Tongue-tie doesn't always need treatment — for many babies it loosens as they grow — and a simple procedure (a frenotomy) is usually only considered if it's making feeding difficult.

The Australian Breastfeeding Association's helpline, **1800 686 268**, is free, any time. Ask your child health nurse or GP.
