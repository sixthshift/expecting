Seeing a private obstetrician can be expensive, and the costs vary a lot depending on the care you get. As a public patient you won't usually pay to see an obstetrician; as a private patient, Medicare covers only part of the costs.

## Where the bills come from

- **Your pregnancy visits** in the obstetrician's rooms. Health insurance doesn't apply to pregnancy visits or expenses, but Medicare covers part of them.
- **Tests and scans,** such as blood tests and ultrasounds.
- **The hospital stay** for the birth, where your insurance comes in, though it may not cover all of it, and your fund may charge an excess or co-payment.
- **Other doctors:** the anaesthetist and the paediatrician, including the paediatrician's check before you go home.
- **Your baby,** whose nursery care is charged separately from your stay. A healthy baby isn't usually formally admitted, but a premature or unwell one is, which can raise costs significantly; with a single policy, the baby's admission isn't covered. If you have twins, at least one will be admitted.

Services that are free to public patients, such as room accommodation, blood tests, ultrasounds, and access to diabetes educators, physiotherapists, paediatricians and anaesthetists, may cost you as a private patient.

## Insurance

Most policies have a 12-month waiting period for pregnancy, and during that time you can't claim any pregnancy-related health expenses, so buy or upgrade to a policy with the right cover at least 12 months before you become pregnant. Some policies don't cover obstetrics at all, or pay restricted benefits, for example only as a private patient in a public hospital. Without cover, a private hospital birth means high out-of-pocket costs: you can claim part of the obstetrician's fees from Medicare, but you pay the hospital.

## Private in a public hospital

You can be a private patient in a public hospital, but you're not guaranteed a single room, and you're responsible for the hospital, medical and diagnostic costs, which your insurance may not fully cover.

## What to do

- **Ask before your first appointment** what the obstetrician charges and what out-of-pocket costs to expect, including for the hospital, the anaesthetist and the paediatrician, and tests.
- **Call your fund** to check what it covers for the pregnancy, the birth and your newborn.
- **Look up expected costs** on the Medical Costs Finder.
- **Consider registering for the Medicare Safety Net:** if you spend above a certain amount on healthcare in a calendar year, you can get more money back from Medicare.
