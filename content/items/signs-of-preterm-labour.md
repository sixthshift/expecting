## What's usual

Early signs of labour usually start from around 37 weeks. In Australia, about 8 in every 100 babies are born before then, and often nobody knows why. If labour starts early, it may be possible to slow it down or stop it, and each day your baby stays inside, the better their health is likely to be.

Signs of labour can include:

- cramps that feel like period pain, or pain low in your tummy
- low backache, or pressure in your pelvis
- frequent bowel motions or loose poo
- a change in vaginal discharge, or a bloodstained "show"
- fluid leaking, or your waters breaking
- contractions

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you have back pain along with other signs of preterm labour, such as tummy pains or cramps, pressure in your pelvis or lower back, or fluid leaking from your vagina, call triple zero (000) immediately and ask for an ambulance. Call 000 too if you think the baby may come before you can get to your hospital or birth centre.

## Contact your hospital now

If you're less than 37 weeks pregnant and have any sign of labour, contact your midwife, doctor or nearest birthing unit immediately. That includes regular, painful contractions, and pain or discomfort in your pelvis, tummy or lower back.

Call your midwife or doctor straight away if you have contractions or other signs of labour before 37 weeks, bleeding from your vagina, waters that are green or brown, or strong pressure in your vagina or bottom or an urge to push.
