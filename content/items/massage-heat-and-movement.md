A support person can make a real difference to pain in labour. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says they can give practical help with non-medical pain relief such as touch or massage, emotional support and encouragement, and can help communicate your partner's needs to the healthcare team.

## Massage and touch

Massage and touch can help distract from the pain, reduce muscle tension and anxiety, and keep the room calm. Sometimes it will feel good and at other times be irritating, and the Royal Women's says that's important for you to know: even a massage that felt good a minute ago can suddenly become annoying. Talk it over, and practise together during pregnancy to find how they like to be massaged.

## Heat and cold

Both hot and cold packs are useful, and so is a shower or bath. Heat on the body can reduce pain, especially backache. A cold face washer can soothe if they feel hot.

## Moving about

Moving freely, walking, changing positions, rocking the pelvis and using a birth ball are among the most helpful things for pain.

Ask your midwife whether your hospital or birthing centre provides a birth ball for use in labour.

## Being there

Having someone who knows you during labour may help you feel more comfortable. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby also says a support person can alert the healthcare team if they notice anything.

## Other things you can do

- Encourage them to eat and drink, and offer snacks, drinks and ice.
- Wipe their face, hold their hand and guide their breathing.
