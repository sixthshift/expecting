Some midwives work privately rather than for a hospital. The Royal Women's calls them midwives in private practice (MIPP). You can employ one for the whole of your pregnancy, labour, birth and postnatal care, or for a one-off consultation before pregnancy or after the birth.

## Why people choose one

Mostly for continuity: the same midwife through the pregnancy, at the birth, at home or in hospital, and afterwards. Others want to choose their own carer, or a service a private midwife offers. People choosing private midwife-led care are often low risk, want more choice, and are sometimes planning a home birth or a birth-centre birth.

## What a midwife can do

Midwives are trained to support you through pregnancy, labour and birth and to care for you after. An **endorsed midwife** has an extra postgraduate qualification and can prescribe some medicines, order certain tests and refer you to other specialists, such as an obstetrician or paediatrician. Midwives can't give epidurals: only an anaesthetist in a hospital can.

Some private midwives work jointly with a private obstetrician through pregnancy, birth and afterwards.

## Referral

You don't need a referral to see a private midwife. You do need a referral from your doctor to see a midwife based in a public hospital, including one working in a maternity group practice.

## What it costs

You pay a fee, so ask about costs before your first appointment. Some private midwives are registered Medicare providers, so you may get a rebate. For a home birth, Medicare covers some of the care given by a midwife with a Medicare provider number: pregnancy and postnatal care may be covered, the birth itself isn't. If you have private health insurance, check what it pays towards your pregnancy care.

## Hospital back-up

If complications arise in pregnancy, labour or birth, you may need to see an obstetrician or transfer to a maternity hospital. Ask your midwife about their arrangements with local maternity hospitals; they may suggest you book into your local hospital too, in case you need to transfer.

## Finding one

You can find a midwife on the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) website, or ask your doctor to recommend one. Ask whether they're registered with the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia. In Victoria, the Royal Women's notes, all midwives, including those in private practice, must have professional indemnity insurance, which private midwives arrange themselves.
