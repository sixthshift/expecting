## What's usual

Palpitations are when you become aware of your heartbeat: it may feel like it's pounding, sinking, skipping beats, or fast and fluttering, in your chest or neck. They're common and usually not serious. They can come with exercise, feeling unwell, anxiety or stress, caffeine, alcohol or some medicines (including asthma relievers), and with conditions such as anaemia, fever or an overactive thyroid, as well as heart problems.

If you know the cause isn't serious, sit and rest somewhere quiet, try to stay calm, and loosen tight clothing. Keeping track of when they happen and what sets them off helps your doctor.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) if you have palpitations along with these symptoms:

- shortness of breath
- chest pain
- dizziness or light-headedness
- fainting or blackouts

## Contact your hospital now

At any time in pregnancy, healthdirect lists **shortness of breath or a racing heartbeat** among the symptoms to contact your doctor, midwife or hospital about immediately.

A fast heart rate is also a sign of sepsis, a severe reaction to infection, along with fever (38 °C or higher) or a temperature under 36 °C, confusion or drowsiness, tummy pain or fast breathing. If you have symptoms of sepsis, see your doctor urgently, go to your nearest emergency department or call 000.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor **urgently** if your palpitations last more than a few minutes, happen often, or you have a family history of heart rhythm problems.

Even when palpitations seem harmless, see your doctor so your heart can be checked and the cause worked out.
