Yes, eat them. It's fine to eat highly allergenic foods in pregnancy if you haven't had an allergic reaction to them before.

## Why

Cutting these foods out hasn't been shown to prevent allergies in your baby, and it isn't recommended. The Australasian Society of Clinical Immunology and Allergy (ASCIA) advises not avoiding common allergy foods in pregnancy. Several studies show that avoiding them doesn't lower the baby's risk of food allergy, and may mean lower weight gain in babies.

The common allergy foods are egg, cow's milk, peanut, tree nuts, sesame, soy, wheat, fish and other seafood. Nuts and seeds bring calcium and omega-3 fatty acids, and a balanced diet with plenty of wholegrains, fruit, vegetables, legumes and nuts is the most useful thing you can do, especially if allergies run in the family.

## What to do

- **Keep eating a varied diet**, allergy foods included.
- **If you have a true food allergy yourself**, keep avoiding that food through the pregnancy.
- **Later, for the baby:** the current advice is to give common allergy foods, including peanut butter, cooked egg, dairy, tree nuts and wheat, before their first birthday. That can reduce the risk of allergy to those foods.
- **Sesame is the exception for a different reason.** Hummus, tahini and halva are on the pregnancy avoid list for food-poisoning risk, not allergy.
