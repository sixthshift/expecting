Everyone who's pregnant is offered a urine test, usually at the first antenatal visit or soon after. It's a routine screening test, and it can find bacteria in your urine even when you feel completely well. That's called an asymptomatic urinary tract infection (UTI).

## Why treat it if you feel fine

Pregnancy changes your hormones, bladder, urine and immune system in ways that make infections more likely and make it easier for bacteria to reach your kidneys. A bladder infection is more likely to become a kidney infection when you're pregnant, and a kidney infection in pregnancy can be serious for you and the baby: it can cause sepsis, blood pressure problems and kidney damage.

Bacteria in your urine, even with no pain or other symptoms, is also linked to pre-eclampsia. And with a UTI or kidney infection in pregnancy, the baby has a higher risk of being born early or underweight. That risk goes away when the infection is treated.

So if bacteria are found, your doctor will decide whether you need treatment. It's important to treat asymptomatic UTIs to prevent problems later for you or your baby.

## The treatment

UTIs are treated with antibiotics that are safe in pregnancy. Your doctor chooses one based on the type of bacteria in your sample; if they're concerned, they may start one straight away and possibly switch once results are back. Your doctor or midwife may recommend another urine test a few weeks after you finish, to make sure the infection has gone.

You may need repeat urine tests if you've had UTIs before, have symptoms, had an unclear result, or are at high risk of a UTI. If you have many infections, you may need more tests, such as a kidney ultrasound, or regular antibiotics through the pregnancy to prevent them.

## When to get help

See your midwife or doctor if you have any symptoms of a UTI, such as pain or burning when you wee, needing to go more often, cloudy, bloody or smelly urine, or a fever. Don't delay getting antibiotics: an infection can develop quickly and lead to problems for you and your baby. Signs it may have reached your kidneys include a fever above 38 °C, back pain and vomiting.
