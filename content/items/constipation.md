## What's usual

Constipation is very common: up to 2 in 5 pregnant people have it. The hormone progesterone relaxes your muscles and slows food moving through your gut. Some medicines (for nausea, heartburn or strong pain) and some supplements, including iron and some multivitamins, can also cause it. If you take one of these, speak with your doctor: a change of dose or brand can sometimes help.

First steps: drink at least 7 to 8 glasses of fluid a day (more in hot or humid weather), eat more fibre (wholegrain foods, fruit and vegetables), exercise, and go to the toilet as soon as you get the urge. A footstool helps. It isn't harmful to your baby.

If that's not enough, your doctor may recommend fibre supplements or laxatives. Not all laxatives are recommended in pregnancy, so check with your doctor or pharmacist first.

## See your doctor or midwife

**Get urgent care** if you have constipation as well as unexplained weight loss, bleeding from your rectum or blood in your stool, severe tummy pain, cramping or bloating, or vomiting.

See your doctor if your constipation is severe (3 or more days between bowel movements, tummy pain, long straining, or a feeling of a 'ball' blocking your rectum), it isn't getting better with simple lifestyle changes, or you're worried.
