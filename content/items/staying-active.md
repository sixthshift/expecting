What the Australian advice says, for a pregnancy without complications:

- **How much:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests being active each day of the week, with strengthening exercises twice a week.
- **What changes:** lying flat on your back, particularly after 28 weeks, can make you feel faint and reduce blood flow to the baby. Strenuous exercise in hot or humid weather, and classes heated on purpose, such as hot Pilates or yoga, are ones to avoid.
- **When to stop:** stop and see a doctor immediately for unexplained shortness of breath, dizziness, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain, sudden swelling of the ankles, hands or face, or nausea and vomiting. For chest pain or severe trouble breathing, call 000. Stop for pain in the abdomen, back or pelvis. Stop and contact your hospital straight away for vaginal bleeding, or if the baby is moving less: [The baby is moving less](baby-moving-less).

**Classes.** Your gym may offer classes designed for pregnancy, and swimming or water-based classes help because the water supports your weight. In any class, check the teacher is properly qualified, knows you're pregnant and knows how many weeks.

Your midwife, doctor or a physiotherapist can say what suits you, especially if you've had complications or a sore back or pelvis.
