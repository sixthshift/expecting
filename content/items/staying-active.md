What the Australian advice says, for a pregnancy without complications:

- **How much:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests being active each day of the week, with strengthening exercises twice a week. The Royal Women's Hospital suggests a moderate session on all or most days, or at least 150 minutes over a week.
- **What changes:** lying flat on your back, particularly after 28 weeks, can make you feel faint and reduce blood flow to the baby. Strenuous exercise in hot or humid weather, and classes heated on purpose, such as hot Pilates or yoga, are ones to avoid.
- **When to stop:** the advice is to stop and see a doctor straight away for chest pain, unexplained shortness of breath, dizziness or vaginal bleeding, and to stop for pain in the abdomen, back or pelvis, or if the baby is moving less.

**Classes.** Many gyms, pools, hospitals and physiotherapists run classes designed for pregnancy: aqua, yoga, Pilates and strength. If you'd rather stay in your usual class, check the instructor is qualified and knows you're pregnant and how many weeks. Classes fill up, and the second trimester is when many people start one.

Your midwife, doctor or a physiotherapist can say what suits you, especially if you've had complications or a sore back or pelvis.
