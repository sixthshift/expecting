A high-risk pregnancy is one where you or your baby has a greater chance of problems or complications during the pregnancy or birth. It often means extra tests and scans. The specific risks depend on your situation and are different in every case.

## Why a pregnancy gets the label

Your midwife or doctor may consider your pregnancy high risk if:

- **your health or circumstances** raise the chance of complications: drinking alcohol, smoking or using drugs, living with obesity, being under 17 or over 35, high blood pressure or a high risk of pre-eclampsia, an existing condition such as epilepsy, diabetes, asthma, kidney or heart disease, a mental health condition, or things like limited social support or past experiences of violence
- **something about the baby**: measuring small at checks, twins or more, or signs you may give birth early
- **a previous pregnancy** had complications: a loss, a preterm baby, diabetes in pregnancy or pre-eclampsia

## What changes

Your midwife or doctor will watch the pregnancy more closely. That may mean more blood tests, more scans, and appointments with obstetricians who specialise in high-risk pregnancies, such as maternal-fetal medicine specialists. You may also need to start a medicine. With high blood pressure, you'll go to the hospital or health centre for blood tests, blood pressure checks and monitoring of your baby's heart rate.

Public hospitals have a model of care designed for high-risk or complex pregnancies (for example twins or a previous caesarean), led by specialist obstetricians or maternal-fetal medicine specialists together with midwives. In midwifery group practice, if risk factors come up your midwife works closely with doctors and other specialists.

## Where and how you give birth

You may need to give birth in a hospital or birth unit with the right staff and equipment for your or your baby's condition. If continuing the pregnancy adds risk, your team may recommend an induction, or a caesarean, planned or in an emergency.

## It's still your care

Your midwife or doctor will explain why they're recommending something, and the risks and benefits of having it or not, so you can give informed consent.
