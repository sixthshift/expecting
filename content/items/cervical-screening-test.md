The cervical screening test checks for HPV, the virus that can cause changes in the cervix that sometimes lead to cancer. It replaced the Pap smear and is usually every 5 years, for anyone aged 25 to 74 with a cervix who has ever had sex.

**In pregnancy.** Cervical screening is safe in pregnancy and should not be delayed. If you're due, or have never been screened, you should have one while you're pregnant. Your doctor should offer it at your first visit if you're due, and your GP can do it.

**How.** Your doctor or midwife can take the sample with a speculum and a small brush, or you can collect it yourself with a vaginal swab, in a private place at the clinic. You can't self-collect if you've had an abnormal result before. It may feel a bit uncomfortable but shouldn't hurt. A little bleeding or spotting afterwards is normal and won't harm you or the baby.

**Results.** A normal result means no HPV, and your next test is in 5 years. If HPV is found, you may need another test in 12 months or a colposcopy, which is safe in pregnancy. If a self-collected test finds HPV, a health professional will take a second sample to check the cells.

**See your doctor** if you have abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge, or any signs of cervical cancer, at any age.
