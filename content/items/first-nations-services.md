Culturally safe care means healthcare that respects your culture, values and identity: you're listened to, included and involved in decisions, and your care team recognises and addresses racism. Feeling safe and respected makes it easier to get the care you and your baby need, and regular antenatal care from early in pregnancy is linked to better health for you both.

## Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Workers

An Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander Health Worker or Health Practitioner can support you through pregnancy, birth and early parenthood. They work alongside doctors and midwives, and can:

- help you understand your care and answer your questions
- explain health information in a way that's easy to understand
- help you talk with your healthcare team
- connect you with local health and community services
- support you with breastfeeding, caring for your baby and understanding their health

You might meet one at an Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation (ACCHO), an Aboriginal Medical Service, a hospital or a community health service. Research shows Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people who get pregnancy care through an Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Service are more likely to start antenatal care early and have 5 or more visits, and their babies are less likely to be born premature or need care in a neonatal nursery.

If you have your baby in hospital, an **Aboriginal Hospital Liaison Officer** can support you and your family during your stay, including involving your family in your care if you choose. Ask your midwife, nurse or other hospital staff.

## Birthing on Country

Birthing on Country is a model of culturally safe maternity care developed and delivered with Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander families and communities. It brings together maternity care, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledges, connection to Country, family support and continuity of care. NACCHO's national ACCHO finder can help you find an Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation near you.

State programs include Aboriginal Maternal and Infant Health Services in NSW, the Koori Maternity Service in Victoria, Waijungbah Jarjums in Queensland, the Aboriginal Family Birthing Program in South Australia and Moort Boodjari Mia in WA.

## Looking after your mind and spirit

There is no shame in speaking up. If you feel stressed, anxious, sad or ashamed in pregnancy or after the birth, you can have a yarn with an Aboriginal Health Nurse, a doctor or a GP.

- **13YARN** (13 92 76) is free, confidential crisis support, 24/7, with an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander Crisis Supporter. You can also text 0447 13 92 76, 4pm to midnight. Call 000 if life is in danger.
- **SMS4dads** sends free text messages, support, information and tips to dads and dads-to-be, and has a Deadly Dads stream.
