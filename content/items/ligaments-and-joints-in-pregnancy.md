Pregnancy hormones soften the ligaments, the connective tissue that holds the bones of your joints together. You'll notice it most in your pelvis and lower back, but feet, wrists and upper back can be affected too. Together with changes in your posture and weight gain, softer ligaments can cause pelvic girdle pain, sciatica and back pain, and balance issues and feeling unsteady. A physiotherapist can teach you exercises that strengthen your muscles, and can give specific exercises if a joint is painful or making you waddle.

## Round ligament pain

Your round ligaments sit on either side of your uterus and support it in the pelvis. As the uterus stretches, they stretch too, which can cause aching, cramping or even a sharp, stabbing feeling. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests gentle stretches, moving slowly and an elastic belly band for support. It's quite common but can be alarming, and other pregnancy complications can cause similar sensations, so if you're worried about pain or have any questions, it says it's always best to talk to your doctor or midwife.

## Pelvic girdle pain

This is pain in the bones and joints of the pelvis, most often where the pelvis meets the lower spine and at the pubic bone. It's very common, usually starts between 14 and 30 weeks, isn't dangerous for you or your baby and usually goes away within 3 months after the birth. It can range from mild discomfort to severe pain that makes walking hard. If you have back, pelvic or hip pain in pregnancy, see your doctor, who will check whether it's pelvic girdle pain, as some other causes can be serious. See your doctor immediately if you have pain that occurs without movement, an unexpected fever, bleeding or fluid leaking from your vagina (after 20 weeks, contact your hospital straight away for any bleeding or if your waters may have broken), weight loss without trying, difficulty controlling your bladder or bowels, numbness or weakness in your legs, or have had a fall or an injury.

## Feet

Around week 19, you may find your feet grow too, because your ligaments are stretching to help carry the extra weight.

## Protecting your joints when you exercise

The Royal Women's Hospital says to avoid high-impact exercise such as netball, tennis, aerobics or running, and to exercise in a pool or on a fitball instead. Wear supportive shoes, take shorter strides, bring your knees together when changing positions, change position smoothly, and keep good posture. It says to be alert to signs you need to stop exercising, including strong pain, especially from your pelvis or back.
