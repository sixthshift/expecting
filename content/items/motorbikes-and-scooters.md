Ask your midwife or doctor. We haven't found an Australian health source that speaks to riding a motorbike, scooter or e-bike in pregnancy, so this is the general advice on falls and knocks applied to it.

## Why

Activities with a risk of collision, tripping or falling are best avoided in pregnancy. Injuries to your abdomen can lead to serious complications, including the placenta separating from the uterus (placental abruption) and preterm labour, and falls and road accidents are common causes.

## What to do

- **Talk it over** with your midwife or doctor before you keep riding.
- **On holiday overseas,** things can go wrong quickly when you're pregnant: stay within reach of hospitals and doctors. Check your travel insurance covers your pregnancy, and that it covers riding: you may need to pay extra to be covered for riding a motorcycle, and most policies are void if you were breaking any road law when the accident happened, even if it wasn't your fault.
- **After any knock to your abdomen,** contact your midwife or doctor immediately, or go to the nearest hospital and tell staff you're pregnant. If the baby is moving less, contact your midwife or birth unit straight away, don't wait: [The baby is moving less](baby-moving-less).
- **Call triple zero (000)** and say you're pregnant if, after a knock to your abdomen, you have pain in your abdomen, a firm or tightening uterus, or vaginal bleeding.
