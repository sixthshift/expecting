Chorionic villus sampling (CVS) is a diagnostic test: it can find out whether your baby has a genetic or chromosomal condition, such as Down syndrome, rather than estimating the chance as a screening test does. It isn't part of routine antenatal care and isn't offered to everyone. If it's offered, having it is your decision.

**When.** Usually between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days.

**Why it might be offered.** A screening result that suggests a higher chance of a genetic condition; a child with a genetic or chromosomal condition; you or your partner carrying a genetic condition, or one in the family; being over 35 when the baby is due; or wanting certainty about a diagnosis. You can ask for a referral to a genetic counsellor.

**What happens.** It's usually done at a hospital by a specially trained doctor, and only with your written consent. Guided by ultrasound, the doctor passes a very thin needle into the placenta, through your tummy or through your cervix, and takes a very small sample of cells. You may be offered a local anaesthetic. Some people feel pain or period-like cramping; many find it less painful than they expected. Afterwards the doctor checks the baby's heart rate, the fluid around the baby and where the needle went in, and offers an anti-D injection if your blood type is Rh negative.

**The risks.** CVS is generally safe, but there's a small risk of vaginal bleeding, infection, your waters breaking or miscarriage. Every pregnancy carries a small risk of miscarriage, and a CVS slightly increases it. Sometimes there's a technical problem with the sample, and the CVS is repeated or an amniocentesis is suggested instead.

**The other options.** Amniocentesis later in pregnancy, genetic testing after the birth, or a screening test (NIPT, combined first-trimester screening or second-trimester serum screening), which can only tell you the chance of a condition.

Feeling anxious is common. PANDA offers mental health support during pregnancy, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's maternal child health nurses are on 1800 882 436.
