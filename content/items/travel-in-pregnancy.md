## Why

In most cases it's safe to travel while pregnant, in Australia or overseas, by air, sea or road. The second trimester (weeks 13 to 26) is usually the best time: the risk of complications like miscarriage is lowest then.

It may not be safe if you have a high chance of complications. MotherSafe names placental problems, an incompetent cervix, previous premature labour, diabetes, twins or more, and high blood pressure in this or a previous pregnancy. If any of these is you, ask before you plan anything.

## What to do

- **See your doctor or midwife first.** Smartraveller says at least 8 weeks before an overseas trip. If they advise against it, don't go.
- **Ask for a letter** listing any medical problems and a brief antenatal history. Take a copy of your medical records, and copies of your prescriptions and travel insurance policy.
- **Think about where the nearest hospital is**, and whether it has maternity and newborn care. Things can go wrong quickly in pregnancy.
- **In Australia**, your Medicare card or private health insurance covers care throughout the country.
- **Overseas** brings more to check: insurance, vaccines, Zika and malaria, altitude. See the entries on each.
