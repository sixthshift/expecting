All pregnant employees, **including casuals**, have the right to a safe job. That includes employees who aren't eligible for unpaid parental leave because they haven't worked for their employer for 12 months.

## When you can move to a safe job

You're entitled to be transferred if you're fit for work but your current job isn't appropriate for a period, because of an illness or risk arising from your pregnancy, or hazards connected with the job. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says changes are more likely if you work with chemical or biological hazards or do physically demanding work, such as heavy lifting.

You must give **evidence that would satisfy a reasonable person**, and your employer may ask for a medical certificate.

If there's an appropriate safe job, your employer **must** transfer you to it. It has the same ordinary hours as your current job unless you agree otherwise; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you're entitled to the same pay rate too. You stay in it until it's safe to go back to your normal job, or your pregnancy ends.

## When there's no safe job

You can take **no-safe-job leave**:

- **Paid**, at your base pay rate for your ordinary hours, if you're entitled to unpaid parental leave and have met its notice and evidence requirements.
- **Unpaid**, if you're not entitled to unpaid parental leave. Your employer may ask for reasonable evidence of your pregnancy, such as a medical certificate.

If you're on paid no-safe-job leave in the 6 weeks before your due date, your employer may ask for a further medical certificate saying whether you're fit for work. If you don't provide it within 7 days, or it says you're not fit, they can require you to start unpaid parental leave.

## After your leave

If you were moved to a safe job before your parental leave, you're entitled to return to the job you had **before the transfer**.

For concerns about safety at work, your state or territory work health and safety body can help.
