Family Tax Benefit (FTB) Part B is paid **per family**, not per child. It's for single parents and carers, and for couples who meet a 2-part income test while their youngest child is younger than 13.

## The income test for couples

The **primary earner** is the parent with the higher income; the **secondary earner** is the one with the lower income, or none.

- If the primary earner earns **more than $124,327**, your family can't get FTB Part B.
- If they earn less, the amount depends on the secondary earner's income. They can earn **up to $7,154** a year before it affects the payment; above that, it drops by 20 cents for each dollar.
- You can still get some FTB Part B while the secondary earner earns less than **$35,661** a year if your youngest child is under 5 ($27,777 if they're 5 to 13).

Single parents can get the maximum rate if their income is $124,327 or less.

## The rates

- **Maximum rate:** $200.34 a fortnight per family while your youngest child is 0 to 4.
- **Supplement:** a yearly payment of up to $478.15 per family for 2026–27, paid after your payments are balanced at the end of the financial year.

## Parental Leave Pay and going back to work

**You can't get FTB Part B on days Parental Leave Pay is paid**, to you or your partner. If you get FTB Part B fortnightly, Services Australia adjusts your payments so you're not paid too much.

When the lower earner starts or returns to work for the first time after the birth, you may get the maximum rate of FTB Part B for the part of the financial year before they went back. You need to tell Services Australia you've returned to work within 12 months from the end of the financial year you went back.
