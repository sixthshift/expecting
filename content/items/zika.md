## Why

Zika is a virus spread mainly by mosquito bites, and also through sex. Most people who catch it don't notice any symptoms. In pregnancy it can pass to the baby, with potentially serious consequences, in particular microcephaly: a small head and intellectual disability. It is associated with a range of severe abnormalities in babies.

RANZCOG says pregnant people should avoid travelling to Zika-affected countries. The Department of Health advises anyone who is pregnant or planning a pregnancy to consider delaying travel to countries where an outbreak has been detected.

## What to do

- **Where:** there's a possible risk in parts of Africa, South and Central America, Asia, the Caribbean, the Pacific Islands and Mexico. The list moves: healthdirect points to the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention for the current one, and to Smartraveller for travel advice.
- **If you can't avoid going:** take precautions against mosquito bites, and use condoms during sex while you're in the affected region.
- **After a trip,** even without symptoms, see a doctor about testing and preventing transmission if you're pregnant, planning a pregnancy, or have a pregnant partner, so a partner who travelled without you should go too.
- **Planning a pregnancy:** wait 6 months after leaving the area before trying to conceive.
