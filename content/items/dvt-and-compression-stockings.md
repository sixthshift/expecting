Yes. Wear knee-high fitted compression stockings on long flights, and see your doctor before you go.

## Why

Sitting still for hours on a plane, train, bus or in a car raises the chance of a blood clot in a deep vein of the leg (a DVT), and pregnancy already raises it because of changes in your blood flow. Part of a clot can break off and travel to the lungs. The risk stays higher for up to two weeks after a long flight. Better Health says your risk is higher still if you've had a DVT before, weigh more than 100 kg, are having more than one baby, or a family member has had one.

## What to do

- **Before you go,** see your doctor: they can tell you whether you need medicine to prevent clots. Better Health says that if your risk is raised, you may be advised to have heparin injections before and after any journey longer than four hours.
- **On the way,** drink plenty of water, avoid caffeine and alcohol, wear fitted knee-high compression stockings, and walk around or do calf and ankle exercises often.
- **See a doctor immediately** if you have redness, swelling or pain in your lower leg.
- **Call triple zero (000)** for an ambulance if you have chest pain, trouble breathing or become short of breath, palpitations, feel dizzy or faint, or cough up blood, or have symptoms of a stroke or heart attack.
