Yes. Wear knee-high fitted compression stockings on long flights, and see your doctor before you go.

## Why

Sitting still for hours on a plane, train, bus or in a car raises the chance of a blood clot in a deep vein of the leg (a DVT), and pregnancy already raises it because of changes in your blood flow. Part of a clot can break off and travel to the lungs. The risk stays higher for up to two weeks after a long flight. RANZCOG puts it at fewer than 1 in 100 pregnant air travellers. MotherSafe counts a past blood clot, or carrying more than one baby, as raising the risks of travel, so tell your doctor if either is you.

## What to do

- **Before you go,** see your doctor: they can tell you whether you need medicine to prevent clots. Some people are advised to take medicine to prevent clots (RANZCOG, MotherSafe).
- **On the way,** drink plenty of water, avoid caffeine and alcohol, wear fitted knee-high compression stockings, and walk around or do calf and ankle exercises often. In a car, stop and stretch your legs every two hours (RANZCOG).
- **See a doctor immediately** if you have pain, swelling or tenderness in your calf or thigh, warm or discoloured skin there, or pain when you straighten your leg. A clot doesn't always cause signs.
- **Call triple zero (000)** for an ambulance if you have chest pain, trouble breathing or become short of breath, palpitations, feel dizzy or faint, or cough up blood, or have symptoms of a stroke or heart attack.
