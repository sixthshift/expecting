A blighted ovum, also called an anembryonic pregnancy, is a kind of early miscarriage. A sac and placenta grow in the womb, but no embryo develops. It often shows up on an early routine ultrasound, which can be very distressing when you were expecting good news, or on a scan done because of bleeding.

## What happens

The body recognises that the pregnancy isn't developing and it eventually miscarries, usually in the first trimester, before 12 weeks. Pregnancy symptoms may be there early on and fade over time. Symptoms of the miscarriage include pain low in your tummy and bleeding from your vagina.

## Why it happens

The cells that would become the baby don't grow as expected. The exact cause is often unknown, but it's often random chromosome problems. **It is not caused by anything you have done.** Early pregnancy loss is common and happens to healthy people: around 1 in 8 people who know they're pregnant miscarry before 20 weeks.

## The choices

Your doctor will talk through your options:

- **Let it happen naturally.** Once it starts, the bleeding can take days or weeks to finish. See your doctor if the bleeding gets heavier, you're in pain or you feel unwell.
- **Take medicines** to end the pregnancy.
- **Have a D&C** (dilatation and curettage), a surgical procedure that gently empties the uterus.

Use pads, not tampons, for the bleeding. Paracetamol can help with the pain.

## Next time

In most cases a blighted ovum doesn't change your chances of having a baby in future. After one miscarriage, the next pregnancy is likely to be normal, and you usually don't need extra tests.

## When to get help

**Go to your nearest emergency department or call 000** and ask for an ambulance if you have heavy bleeding, severe tummy pain, dizziness or fainting, or a fever or chills.

**Speak to your doctor or midwife immediately** if you think you're pregnant and have bleeding or pain low in your tummy.

## Grief

It can be extremely upsetting, and you may need support and time to grieve. People feel many things after a miscarriage, including guilt, sadness or relief; there's no right way to feel. Your doctor or midwife can help you find support. Red Nose Grief and Loss is on 1300 308 307, and Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636.
