In pregnancy it's best to treat each of your cold symptoms on its own, rather than reach for a cold and flu tablet that does everything at once.

## Why

Cold and flu tablets contain several ingredients. Many include pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine, oral decongestants. Oral decongestants aren't recommended in pregnancy (see [Sudafed and oral decongestants](oral-decongestants)). Some also contain paracetamol, so if you're already taking paracetamol, a cold and flu tablet can take you past the daily limit. MotherSafe suggests checking the ingredients with MotherSafe, your doctor or your pharmacist.

A cold isn't dangerous for you or your baby, and it usually gets better in a few days.

## What to do

- **Aches, pains or fever:** paracetamol, at no more than 8 × 500 mg tablets in 24 hours. Anti-inflammatories such as ibuprofen should be avoided unless your doctor advises them (healthdirect) and only used under medical supervision (MotherSafe).
- **Blocked or runny nose:** saline spray, steam, or a decongestant nasal spray for no more than 3 days.
- **Cough:** dextromethorphan for a dry cough; guaifenesin or bromhexine for a chesty cough.
- **Sore throat:** warm drinks with lemon and honey, gargling salt water, or throat lozenges.
- **If you think it's the flu, see a doctor.** Flu is more serious than a cold in pregnancy, especially in the second and third trimesters, and can cause pneumonia, premature labour and stillbirth. Your doctor may prescribe an antiviral, which works better the earlier you take it, so see them when you first notice symptoms.
