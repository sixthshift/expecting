## What's usual

Even a minor injury in pregnancy can lead to complications, so it needs assessing, whether it was a fall, a car accident, or being hurt by someone. The most common serious complication after a blow to the belly is placental abruption, when the placenta starts to separate from the uterus before birth. After an injury you may need to stay in hospital for at least 4 hours so your baby's heart rate can be monitored for signs of it.

In the car, wear the lap belt over your hips and below your belly, and the sash between your breasts and above your belly.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you've had an injury to your belly and you have pain in your belly, a firm or tightening uterus, or bleeding from your vagina, call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance. Tell the operator you're pregnant.

## Contact your hospital now

- After any hit or blow to your stomach (a fall, a car accident or family violence), contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately.
- With any injury to your belly, go to the nearest hospital and tell the staff you're pregnant.
- Even after a minor injury, go to hospital straight away if your baby is moving less than normal.

## See your doctor or midwife

After an injury, also watch for contractions or your waters breaking. If you notice either, contact your doctor, or call 000 in an emergency.

If you're experiencing domestic or family violence, 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) is there at any time for information and support.
