Once you and your baby have been checked, the doctor or midwife will tell you when you can go home.

## How long you stay

The sources give different ranges. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's page on being at home says you're likely to go home 4 to 24 hours after a vaginal birth and 48 to 72 hours after a caesarean. Its pages on the first few days and the first 24 hours say you may be able to go home 4 to 6 hours after a vaginal birth in a public hospital or birth centre, and you may need to stay longer after an assisted birth or a caesarean, or if you or your baby are unwell; in a private hospital you might be able to stay longer. King Edward Memorial Hospital in Perth says, if you and your baby are well enough, from 4 hours after an uncomplicated vaginal birth, and 24 to 72 hours after a caesarean. If you stay a couple of days, the midwives can help with feeding, settling, bathing and nappies.

## The discharge list

KEMH lists what's done before you're ready to go. For you:

- a health check
- eating and drinking, and passing urine
- any pain managed, and moving and walking
- understanding any ongoing health needs, with referrals or instructions for follow-up

For your baby:

- a health check and a feeding plan
- vitamin K and hepatitis B considered
- the birth registration paperwork, and the baby's health record book given and explained
- any medicines arranged
- **transport and a baby car seat arranged**
- safe sleep discussed, and the support services you can use

## The first visits at home

If you went home the same day or had a home birth, a midwife will come to your home. Your midwife may also arrange a home visit to help with care and feeding. Then your care is handed over to a child health nurse, and your first appointment with them is about 10 days after you get home. They check on you both and record your baby's weight and measurements in the health record book. Ask lots of questions.

## Ready at home

Set things up so nappies, wipes, spare clothes and any feeding equipment are within easy reach. Some parents freeze a few meals before the baby is due. A close friend or relative staying for a few days can help, and partners can do nappy changes, settling and meals. It's fine to limit visitors.

Two pieces of paperwork follow: add your baby to Medicare as soon as possible, and register the birth, which in most states and territories must happen within 60 days.

## When to get help

If you're feeling down or inadequate, very anxious, having trouble sleeping or worrying excessively about your baby, these can be signs of postnatal anxiety or depression: speak to a health professional. PANDA is on 1300 726 306, Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby on 1800 882 436.
