## What's usual

Your round ligaments hold your uterus in place, and as your baby grows they stretch and sometimes spasm. You may feel cramping, a sharp, stabbing or aching pain, or a pulling feeling low in your belly near your hips and groin, more often on the right but on either or both sides. It can last a few minutes or a few hours, and is often set off by moving suddenly, standing up, sneezing, coughing, laughing or standing for a long time.

It's common, felt most often in the second trimester (some people get it earlier), and not dangerous for you or your baby. It usually goes after the birth.

What helps:

- a warm compress on your lower belly, or a warm bath
- rest, lying on your side with a pillow between your knees
- an elastic belly band for support
- gentle daily stretches, such as bending and flexing your hip
- paracetamol, which most people can take safely; check with your doctor before taking any medicine
- changing position slowly, avoiding heavy lifting and long periods standing
- when you feel a sneeze, cough or laugh coming, leaning forward and supporting your belly with your hands

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you have vaginal bleeding, strong pain or feel very unwell, call triple zero (000) immediately and ask for an ambulance.

## See your doctor or midwife

**Get medical care immediately** if you have tummy pain together with:

- bleeding or vaginal discharge
- contractions, or pain or pressure in your lower back
- severe pain that doesn't go away
- chills or fever
- feeling lightheaded
- trouble walking
- nausea or vomiting not related to morning sickness

If round ligament pain keeps going, see your doctor to rule out other causes.
