Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's important to prepare a toddler for a new baby, because it supports their sense of security and helps them adjust. From their point of view everything has changed, and their parents may seem busier or less available.

## What to expect

It's common for toddlers to feel left out or to blame the baby for the changes in their routine. They might also feel pressure to act like a "big kid" before they're ready, or regress: forgetting how to use the potty or feed themselves, or crying more. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says this happens because they may be trying to show you they're still small and still need your attention. Toddlers differ, so try not to compare yours to others.

## During the pregnancy

- Tell your toddler during the pregnancy but not too soon; later, when they can see your bump, works well.
- Talk about "our baby", look at baby photos, read books about becoming an older sibling, and share stories and pictures of them as a baby.
- If you plan to change their room or furniture, do it early and explain it's because they're growing bigger, not because the baby needs their cot.
- Potty training may be easier for you if it's done before the baby comes home, but don't worry if they're not ready.
- If you're planning a hospital birth, start talking a little before your due date about going away to have "our baby", and explain the arrangements you've made for them.
- Let them feel the baby move, and let them help set up the nursery.

## Around the birth

Plan your older children's care for when you go to hospital, with a few stand-by alternatives. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says arranging for your toddler to visit you and the new baby in hospital is a good idea. It also helps if the parent who isn't caring for the newborn can focus on the toddler.

When you come home, make sure your toddler gets lots of attention, ask someone else to carry the newborn so you can give your toddler a big hug, and ask visitors to greet your toddler first.
