No. The NSW Food Authority says don't drink or use unpasteurised (raw) milk in pregnancy ("high risk"), and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says not to eat or drink unpasteurised milk or its products.

## Why

Raw milk can carry a wide range of organisms that cause illness, including listeria, salmonella, campylobacter, E. coli and the parasite cryptosporidium. Unpasteurised milk is also one of the ways to catch toxoplasmosis. Pregnant people and their babies are among those most vulnerable, and complications can be severe. Even very good hygiene on the farm doesn't make raw drinking milk safe.

Pasteurisation heats milk to kill these bacteria and leaves its nutrients largely unchanged. Australia's Food Standards Code requires milk to be pasteurised, or treated in an equivalent way, with very few exceptions, and it's illegal to sell raw milk as a food in Australia. In NSW, unpasteurised goats' milk is one exception, and it must be labelled as unpasteurised.

## What to do

- **Stick to pasteurised** milk, cream, yoghurt and cheese, kept in the fridge and used by their date.
- **Check the label** on goats' milk: in NSW, unpasteurised goats' milk must be labelled as unpasteurised.
- **Already had some?** If you're worried about something you've eaten, call your maternity care provider or doctor.
