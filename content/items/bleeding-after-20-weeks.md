## What's usual

Bleeding after 20 weeks may be a sign that something needs medical attention, and it's usually caused by a problem with the placenta:

- **Placenta praevia** (a low-lying placenta, close to or covering the cervix). The most common sign is painless bleeding after 28 weeks.
- **Placental abruption**, when the placenta separates from the wall of the uterus before birth. It can bring bleeding, belly pain that is constant rather than coming in waves, contractions, lower back pain, and the baby moving less. The pain is usually severe but may be mild or absent, and the amount of bleeding doesn't show how serious it is. It's a medical emergency.
- **Vasa praevia**, a rare problem with the placenta's blood vessels over the cervix.

Close to the birth you may have a 'show': the mucus plug that sealed the cervix, which can be watery, sticky or jelly-like, and pink, brown or bloodstained. Blood that is bright red, or not mixed with mucus, is probably not a show: contact your doctor or midwife immediately.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance if you have very heavy bleeding, strong pain or feel very unwell. If you have placenta praevia and the bleeding is heavy, with pain or contractions, call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your obstetrician or birth unit, or call an ambulance, immediately if:

- your bleeding becomes very heavy: soaking 2 pads an hour or passing clots (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's page on miscarriage counts 1 or more pads an hour as heavy)
- you have strong pain in your tummy or shoulder
- you feel dizzy or faint, or short of breath

For any other bleeding, contact your doctor, midwife or birth unit straight away. If you know you have a low-lying placenta, tell them.

After any injury to your belly, such as a fall, an accident or violence, contact your midwife or doctor immediately.
