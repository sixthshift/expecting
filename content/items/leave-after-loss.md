We're so sorry. This is what work owes you, so you don't have to ask.

## Compassionate leave, for both of you

You can take compassionate leave if you have a miscarriage, if your spouse or de facto partner has one, or if a baby in your immediate family or household is stillborn. Every employee is entitled to it, casuals included.

It's **2 days each time**, taken all at once, as two single days, or in other periods your employer agrees to. Full-time and part-time employees are paid for it at their base rate; casuals get it unpaid. Tell your employer as soon as you can, even if that's after the leave has started. You can take it during unpaid parental leave too, or use it in place of other leave you're already on.

## After a stillbirth or the death of a baby

If your baby is stillborn (at least 20 weeks or 400 grams), or dies in the first 24 months of life, you can still take **up to 12 months' unpaid parental leave**. Your employer can't call you back to work or cancel it.

The choice to come back is yours. Before the leave has started, you can cancel it with written notice. Once it's started, give at least **4 weeks' written notice** before you return; you and your employer can agree to an earlier date.

**Paid parental leave from your employer** can't be refused or cancelled because of a stillbirth or a baby's death, where that happened on or after 7 November 2025. It can be cancelled only if you ask, or if an exception applies, such as your existing employment terms already allowing it.

## A loss after 12 weeks that isn't a stillbirth

If you're eligible for unpaid parental leave and you have a pregnancy loss after 12 weeks, you can take **unpaid special parental leave** while you're unfit for work.

Fair Work's fact sheet, *Understanding parental leave following the loss of a pregnancy or a child*, sets out the leave and payments for you and your employer.
