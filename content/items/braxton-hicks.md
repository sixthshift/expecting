## What's usual

Braxton Hicks, sometimes called practice contractions, are a normal part of pregnancy. The muscles of your uterus tighten, and it feels as if the muscles across your belly are pulling tight. They don't cause labour and aren't a sign it's starting.

You may not feel them until the second trimester, and they're most often felt in the third. Braxton Hicks usually:

- come at irregular times, no more than once or twice an hour, a few times a day
- last about 30 seconds (up to 2 minutes)
- are uncomfortable but not usually painful
- stop if you change position, go for a walk, or have a warm bath or shower

Labour contractions are different: they become regular and closer together, last longer and get stronger over time, get stronger when you walk, and are painful or need all your attention.

To ease the discomfort, try changing position, a walk, a warm shower, a heat pack or a drink of water.

## Contact your hospital now

Call your midwife or doctor, or go to the hospital, if your waters break, or your contractions are getting stronger, closer together and more regular.

If you're less than 37 weeks pregnant, contractions can be a sign of premature labour. Contact your doctor or midwife immediately if you feel pain or discomfort in your pelvis, belly or lower back, or have any signs of labour.

At any stage, contact your doctor or midwife immediately if you have persistent belly pain, vaginal bleeding, you're concerned about your baby's movements, or you feel very unwell.

## See your doctor or midwife

Call your midwife if the contractions don't ease up. If you're not sure whether it's Braxton Hicks or labour, contact your midwife or doctor; if in doubt, don't hesitate to call.
