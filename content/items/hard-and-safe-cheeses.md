Yes, most everyday cheeses are fine. Hard cheeses such as cheddar, tasty, parmesan and edam are okay to eat. For pasteurised cream cheese, plain cottage cheese, cheese spreads and processed cheese the sources differ a little, below.

## Why

The worry with cheese in pregnancy is listeria, bacteria that can grow even in the fridge and, if your baby is infected, can lead to miscarriage, stillbirth or premature labour. Soft and semi-soft cheeses such as brie, camembert, ricotta, feta and blue are the higher-risk ones. Hard cheeses are a safer alternative.

**Mozzarella, haloumi and paneer.** The sources don't agree here:

- Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's food guide lists pasteurised mozzarella, haloumi and paneer as fine.
- Food Standards Australia New Zealand lists mozzarella, pre-packaged or from the deli, among the higher-risk soft and semi-soft cheeses; its safer alternatives include baked soft cheeses cooked to an internal temperature of 75 °C.

Neither FSANZ nor the NSW Food Authority mentions haloumi or paneer. For soft cheeses in general, the NSW Food Authority says not to eat them unless thoroughly cooked to at least 75 °C and eaten soon afterwards, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you can eat them if cooked to at least 75 °C and eaten straight away. If you'd rather be cautious about mozzarella, ask your midwife.

## What to do

- **Only eat cheese made from pasteurised milk.**
- **Hard cheeses:** keep them in the fridge.
- **Cream cheese, cottage cheese, cheese spreads, processed cheese:** the NSW Food Authority says eat with caution; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says they're fine (cream cheese, cheese spreads and plain cottage cheese in its food guide, processed cheese in its food chart); FSANZ lists pasteurised cream cheese and plain cottage cheese as safer alternatives. The NSW Food Authority and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby both say store them in the fridge and eat them within 2 days of opening.
- **Buy cheese packaged by the manufacturer** rather than cut at a deli counter, and keep it in the fridge (FSANZ).
