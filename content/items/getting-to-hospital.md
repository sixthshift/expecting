Labour often starts unexpectedly. Having a plan for the trip, and knowing where you're going, will help you both feel organised. This one is yours to own: the person in labour will be concentrating on other things, and the advice is that they plan not to drive themselves.

## Before the day

- **The route.** Talk through the way you'll go, and whether it changes with traffic or the time of day. Ideally, do a practice run in the third trimester.
- **A backup.** Make an alternative plan for transport in case you need it.
- **Fuel.** Keep the car topped up.
- **The car seat.** Fit it now, following all the manufacturer's recommendations for safe installation, and practise so you know how it works.
- **Parking.** Be clear where you can park. A tour of the maternity unit helps you know what to expect.
- **The numbers.** Keep the hospital's or birth centre's contact numbers in the pregnant parent's phone and make sure you have them in yours too.
- **The bags and the paperwork.** Bags packed for both of them, and the essential paperwork: Medicare card, insurance details, birth plan and hospital paperwork.
- **Home.** Plan care for any older children, with a few stand-by alternatives, and for pets.

## On the day

Be guided by your midwife or doctor about the right time to go. It's a good idea to ring the hospital to let them know you're on your way, even if things seem to be going well: it helps them plan.

Allow for how long the trip takes and the time of day, including peak-hour traffic. Sitting in the car without being able to move around is hard if the trip is long. First labours are often the longest; if there's been a baby before, this labour may be quicker.

If you're checked at the hospital in early labour, you may be advised to go home for a while. That's common practice, so try not to be disappointed.

You can ring the hospital for support and guidance any time either of you feels concerned or worried.

## When to get help

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance if you think you may not make it to your planned place of birth before the baby is born, or if there is heavy bleeding from the vagina, a severe headache or blurred vision.
