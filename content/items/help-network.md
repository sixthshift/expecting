Many families no longer live near the people who would once have helped, so it's worth building that help on purpose. People often say "let us know if you need anything"; a list turns that into something you can actually say yes to.

**Write down what needs doing,** then who could do it:

- **Meals and shopping:** cooking, a grocery run, an online order.
- **The house:** washing, a clean, the bins, the garden.
- **Older children and pets:** school and daycare runs, an afternoon out, walking the dog.
- **Getting around:** lifts to appointments, hospital parking money.
- **Rest:** someone to hold the baby while you sleep or shower.
- **Company:** someone to talk to who's been there.

**Ask early, and be specific.** "Could you do the Tuesday school run for the first three weeks?" is easier to say yes to than "help out". Ask before the birth, while people can plan around it.

**Think wider than family.** Friends, neighbours, people from antenatal classes, and the partner's own friends all count. Maternal health centres usually run groups for new parents after the birth, and there are online groups too.

**Paid help** is an option too: a cleaner for a few weeks, meal delivery, or extra hours of care for an older child.

Keep the list somewhere you can both see it, with phone numbers, so either of you can call when the time comes.
