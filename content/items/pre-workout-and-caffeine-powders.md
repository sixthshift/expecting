Check before you keep taking it. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's best to speak to your doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine or supplement while you're pregnant, and we haven't found Australian pregnancy advice that names pre-workout, protein powder or other sports supplements.

## Why

The caffeine limit in pregnancy is no more than 200 mg a day from all sources, and too much caffeine may increase the risk of miscarriage, stillbirth and low birth weight. A pre-workout or caffeine tablet counts towards that limit along with your coffee, tea and chocolate.

Pure and highly concentrated caffeine powders are a different matter. They're typically marketed as sports supplements, a single teaspoon can hold as much caffeine as 25 to 50 cups of coffee, and because it's almost impossible to measure a safe dose from a toxic one, they've caused deaths. Foods with 5% or more caffeine as a powder or solid, or 1% or more as a liquid, are banned from sale in Australia and New Zealand. They're usually bought online.

Some slimming medicines also contain caffeine.

## What to do

- **Read the label.** Products with added caffeine must say so, and anything containing guarana must be labelled as containing caffeine.
- **Pure caffeine powder** is banned from retail sale in Australia and New Zealand, and FSANZ says it can have serious health effects, including death.
- **Ask before anything else:** speak to your doctor or pharmacist before taking any supplement. Only take a high-dose vitamin supplement if your doctor recommends it.
