Your cervix is the muscular channel that runs between your uterus and your vagina. In pregnancy, it holds your baby in your uterus, and protects them from conception until just before birth.

## The mucus plug

From the start of pregnancy, the mucus on the surface of your cervix becomes thick and sticky, forming a mucus "plug". It stops bacteria, viruses and other germs getting into your uterus, where they could harm your baby.

## Cervical length

Some people develop a short cervix in pregnancy: a cervical length under 25 mm. Your doctor can see it on a routine ultrasound, usually between 16 and 24 weeks. A short cervix can raise the risk of going into labour and giving birth before 37 weeks. If you have one, your doctor may recommend treatment to lower that risk, and will watch your cervix closely through the rest of the pregnancy.

## In labour

In labour, your cervix changes from long and firm to soft, thin and more stretchy, and starts to open (dilate). This is called effacement, or ripening. Contractions partly trigger the changes.

As it begins to open, you may see a clear, pink or slightly bloody discharge, or spotting: the "show". You may also notice the mucus plug coming away.

How long it takes to open varies from person to person. When it's fully open, about 10 cm (fully dilated), you're in the second stage of labour, when your baby moves down through your pelvis and you push them out.

If labour needs to be induced, there are several ways doctors can ripen the cervix with treatment to start it.
