If someone you love has lost a baby, during pregnancy or after, it's hard to know what to say. You can't take the grief away, but you can help them feel less alone. You don't need the perfect words. What matters most is showing up with kindness, patience and care. Take care you're ready too: you may be grieving as well, and that's normal.

## What to say

A simple, honest acknowledgement is often more supportive than hunting for the right response. You could say:

- "I'm so sorry. I'm here with you."
- "I don't know the right words, but I care about you."
- "Would you like to talk about your baby, or would you prefer some quiet company?"
- "I'm thinking of you today. You don't need to reply."

## How to listen

A supportive conversation isn't about fixing. Let them set the pace, and listen without rushing to reassure, solve the problem or move the conversation on. Use the baby's name if they've shared it and are comfortable with it. Acknowledge both parents' grief, including partners. Allow silence and tears. Ask what would help rather than assuming. If you get it wrong, say sorry and try again.

## What not to say

Avoid anything that starts with "at least": there is no "at least" in a miscarriage. Also avoid:

- "You can always try again."
- "Everything happens for a reason" or "it wasn't meant to be."
- "At least it happened early" or "at least you have other children."
- "I know exactly how you feel" or "it's time to move on."

Don't blame, offer unsolicited advice, or tell them what to do differently next pregnancy.

## Help that helps

Instead of "let me know if you need anything", offer something specific: a meal, groceries, laundry, pet care, minding other children, coming to an appointment, or passing on news so they don't have to repeat it.

Keep checking in over the weeks and months ahead, as everyone else goes back to normal. Note the dates that matter to them, such as the day of the loss and the due date. If they don't want to talk, send short messages that need no reply, and don't take it personally.

## When to get help

There's no timeline for grief. If they seem unable to cope, very isolated, can't sleep or eat, or are struggling to get through the day, gently encourage them to see a mental health professional experienced in perinatal loss. If someone is in immediate danger, call 000; for urgent mental health support, Lifeline is 13 11 14.
