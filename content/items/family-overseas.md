Having a baby can be very challenging when your family lives overseas. You miss the close-by support of the people you love, and it can be lonely. If you're also new to Australia, you may be adjusting to a new language, culture and health system at the same time. With the right support, you can feel more confident and connected.

## Keeping family close

Short but frequent contact helps family feel involved.

- Call or video call at regular times, and send photos, short videos and messages.
- Share rituals and milestones by video: birthdays, religious holidays and other important events.
- Keep your cultural traditions alive, and involve your relatives in the new family rituals you make.

Families apart can disagree, about the health system, cultural practices or parenting. Conflict is normal; working through it together makes families stronger.

## Your culture in your care

It's your right to have care that fits your cultural needs, and those caring for you will respect your wishes as much as possible. Tell your team what matters: who you want at the birth, foods and rituals, and practices for after the birth. In many situations you can choose where to give birth, public or private care, and a female doctor or midwife if one is available. Many antenatal clinics have cultural liaison officers, and in most states and territories multicultural health workers can help you choose a clinic, get to appointments and find support groups.

## Language

If you don't speak English well, ask for an interpreter. Hospitals in Australia provide free interpreters, in person or by phone, and where possible a trained health interpreter is better than a family member. You can also ask for a bilingual health professional, or bring a trusted support person to appointments. PANDA has resources translated into 40 languages.

## Building a local network

- Your child health nurse can put you in touch with a parents' group or playgroup.
- Look for local parenting groups online, and get involved in your community: volunteering, a place of worship, multicultural or faith groups, or settlement services.
- Migrant and multicultural resource centres help new arrivals settle.
- Pregnancy, Birth and Baby (1800 882 436) can answer the questions you might usually ask family; you can call through the Translating and Interpreting Service.

Thinking ahead to work: there's a wide range of childcare in Australia, and you may be eligible for help with fees through the Child Care Subsidy.

## When to get more help

Gidget suggests extra support if worry, low mood or stress last a while or make daily life harder: feeling sad or overwhelmed much of the time, feeling alone or disconnected, trouble sleeping even when you can, not enjoying things, or avoiding appointments because of fear, shame or language. Talk to your GP, midwife or child health nurse. Gidget offers free sessions under Medicare (with a GP referral and plan), and can arrange a free interpreter. If you have thoughts of not wanting to be here anymore, reach out now: call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 if you're in immediate danger.
