Ask first. The national pregnancy care guidelines say there isn't enough evidence yet to recommend probiotics in pregnancy, for or against, and they're watching the research.

## Why

The guidelines note that the Australian Dietary Guidelines make no recommendation on probiotics, and that the usual pregnancy multivitamins in Australia don't contain them.

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's general advice is to speak to your doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine in pregnancy, including over-the-counter ones. It says complementary medicines are often described as natural, but that doesn't mean they are safe, especially in pregnancy, and we often don't know much about their safety.

## What to do

- **Ask your doctor or pharmacist** before starting a probiotic supplement. They can advise which products are safe to use.
- **Foods with live microbes,** such as yoghurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, miso and tempeh, are ordinary foods. Check the food entries for any that carry a listeria risk, such as some cheeses.
