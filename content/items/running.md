It depends who you ask. Better Health lists jogging among activities that are generally safe in pregnancy. The Royal Women's Hospital says to avoid high-impact exercise, including running, to protect your joints, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid exercise that is high-impact or involves jumping or bouncing.

## Why

Pregnancy hormones soften the ligaments that support your joints, most of all in the pelvis and lower back, so they're easier to strain. As the bump grows your centre of gravity moves forward, which changes your balance.

## What to do

- **If you already run,** talk to your doctor or midwife about carrying on. Keep it to a pace where you can still hold a conversation, and don't push harder than your usual level.
- **If you didn't run before,** choose something that needs less effort. Better Health lists walking and swimming as generally safe, even for beginners.
- **Carry on only while it's comfortable.** Better Health says to continue "until it becomes uncomfortable for you to do so".
- **Cut back on hot or humid days,** stay well hydrated, and wear supportive shoes.
- **Stop and see your doctor straight away** for chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, calf pain or swelling, contractions or tummy pain, bleeding or leaking fluid, or if your baby is moving less.
