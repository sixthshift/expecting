## What's usual

You or your partner may notice you snoring in pregnancy, sometimes for the first time in your life. Hormonal changes can also give you a blocked, stuffy nose that affects your sleep, and nasal sprays can help.

Snoring can also be a sign of something more. Snoring, gasping or pauses in your breathing during sleep can be signs of obstructive sleep apnoea, where your airway becomes partly or completely blocked while you sleep and you briefly stop breathing, often without knowing.

Other signs of sleep apnoea include tossing and turning, mouth breathing, waking unrefreshed or with a dry mouth or headache, and feeling sleepy during the day.

If you share a bedroom with someone, take them to the appointment: they can describe your sleep and whether it's noisy or disturbed. Your doctor may refer you for an overnight sleep study, in a sleep lab or at home.

## See your doctor or midwife

- you started snoring during your pregnancy: it could be a sign of other medical problems that can affect your and your baby's health
- you have more pauses in your breathing during sleep
- you're exhausted and more rest doesn't help
- you have any other symptoms of sleep apnoea, or you're worried about your sleep
