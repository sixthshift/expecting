The surprise: the baby won't sleep in the nursery for a while. Red Nose's safe sleeping advice is to keep the baby in their own safe cot in the parents' room for the first 6 to 12 months. Room sharing like this lowers the risk of sudden unexpected death in infancy (SUDI). The nursery can be the change table and the clothes for now.

**The cot**

- Every cot sold in Australia, new or second-hand, must meet the mandatory Australian Standard, **AS/NZS 2172**. Check the label or the instructions.
- Second-hand cots don't need a safety label, so make sure an old cot is known to meet the current standard. If you can't tell, don't use it, however lovely it is.
- Check for recalls on the Product Safety Australia site before buying.

**The mattress**

- One firm, flat mattress that fits snugly, within 20 mm of the sides and ends. Never a second mattress on top, and never tilted or raised.

**What stays out of the cot**

- No pillows, cot bumpers, soft toys or soft bedding. Bumpers and soft bedding have been linked to fatal sleep accidents.

**The safe sleeping basics, for later**

- Baby on their back to sleep, never on the tummy or side.
- Face and head uncovered.
- A smoke-free home, before and after the birth.

Make the cot up and put it in your room a few weeks before the due date, so it's ready if the baby comes early.
