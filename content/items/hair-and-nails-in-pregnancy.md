Changes in hormone levels, such as oestrogen, make your hair grow more and get thicker. Each hair follicle goes through phases of growth and rest, and in pregnancy that cycle changes: your hair stays in its growing phase for longer, so you shed less. You may also grow hair in places you haven't before, including your abdomen, lower back and thighs. Around week 15, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says your hair and nails growing more quickly is all very normal.

## After the birth

By the time your baby is about 12 months old your hair should be back to normal. Around 2 to 4 months after the birth you may start to shed hair, which can be worrying, but you will not go bald. You can keep losing extra hair for up to 6 months. If your hair loss seems excessive, or your hair hasn't returned to normal after a year, speak to your doctor.

## Dye and treatments

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you can safely colour your hair; the idea that dyeing harms your baby is a myth, because the chemicals aren't easily absorbed through the scalp. If you're worried, you may prefer to wait until after the first 12 weeks, or speak to your midwife or doctor. To limit exposure you can use semi-permanent vegetable dye, leave it in for the shortest recommended time, rinse thoroughly, and go to a hairdresser rather than doing it at home.

Some things to avoid: minoxidil and spironolactone, medicines used for hair loss, which should not be taken in pregnancy because they can harm your baby; and hair straightening treatments containing formaldehyde, which has no evidence of harming unborn babies but is a known carcinogen and should be avoided. Hair removal creams, sprays, waxing and laser are safe.
