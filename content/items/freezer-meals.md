The first weeks with a newborn leave little time or energy for cooking. A stock of meals that only need reheating means you eat properly without thinking about it, and it's a job the partner can own.

**Planning**

- Aim for enough dinners for the first two to three weeks, or more if you have the space. Count lunches too.
- Pick things that freeze and reheat well: soups, curries, stews, casseroles, pasta sauces, bolognese, lasagne, pies, fried rice, muffins and slices.
- Favour meals you can eat with one hand from a bowl.
- Clear the freezer first. A second-hand chest freezer is worth thinking about if yours is small.
- Stock the pantry too: rice, pasta, tins, long-life milk and snacks.

**Cooking**

- Cook in double batches over a few weekends rather than all at once.
- Cool food quickly, then freeze it in portions that suit how you'll eat: single serves for lunches, family sizes for dinners.
- Label every container with what it is and the date.
- Write a list of what's in the freezer and stick it on the door.

**Eating**

- Thaw meals in the fridge, not on the bench, and reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

If friends and family ask how they can help, a frozen meal is a good answer.
