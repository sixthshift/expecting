The booking visit is your first appointment at the hospital where you've booked to give birth. Your GP refers you. Timing varies: the Royal Hospital for Women in Sydney, for example, aims to contact you within 2 to 3 weeks of your GP's eReferral, and says its booking appointment is usually when you're about 14 weeks pregnant.

**Before it.** At the Royal Hospital for Women, your GP confirms the pregnancy, sends the referral, organises the routine blood tests and talks through genetic screening first. Bring all those results to the booking visit.

**What happens.** Your first antenatal visit is longer than the ones after it. Your midwife or doctor will:

- ask about your health, earlier pregnancies, illnesses and operations, medicines and allergies, your family's health, and whether you smoke, drink or use drugs
- measure your blood pressure, height and weight, and may suggest a urine test
- offer blood tests
- ask about stress, depression and anxiety, and your support at home and work, through a mental health and social wellbeing screen that also asks about family violence. You don't have to answer, and what you say is kept in confidence.
- go through the tests on offer and their costs. None are compulsory.

**Your model of care.** You'll talk about who looks after you (a hospital team, midwives, shared care or an obstetrician), where you'll be seen, how many visits you'll have and when, and where to find antenatal classes. At the Royal Women's Hospital in Melbourne, think about the options before the booking visit, and tell the midwife there about days or times that don't suit; caseload midwifery is popular, so ask then whether it's available.

**Costs.** As a private patient, ask about the costs of your birth when you book.
