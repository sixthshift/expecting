A keeping in touch (KIT) day is a day of work during unpaid parental leave that helps with your transition back at the end of it. It can be remote or in the office, and the Fair Work Ombudsman gives refreshing skills, a planning day, training or a conference as examples. The work must only be for the purpose of keeping in touch.

**How many.** By agreement with your employer, up to 10 days in the first 12 months of your leave, and up to 10 more in an agreed extended period of up to 12 months. KIT days don't change the length of your leave, and you don't have to use them. A part day counts as a full day against your balance.

**Rules.**
- You and your employer both have to agree to them and when they're worked.
- They're only for a continuous period of unpaid parental leave, not while you're on flexible unpaid parental leave.
- You can't use them in the first 14 days (2 weeks) after the birth. After that you can suggest one; after 42 days (6 weeks) your employer can suggest one too.

**Pay.** You're paid as normal for a KIT day and you accumulate leave entitlements. If you're getting the government's Parental Leave Pay, the Fair Work Ombudsman says to consider how KIT days may affect your payments. Services Australia says Parental Leave Pay is paid for days when you're not working, and it counts you as working on a day if you work for one hour or more, including keeping in touch with your workplace (taking part in a meeting, doing on-the-job training, or doing work to become familiar with the workplace or your role before you return). You must tell it if you plan to work on a day you asked to get Parental Leave Pay for, and either select a new day or return the day to your balance. You can do this in your Centrelink online account through myGov.
