If you're planning childcare after parental leave, the Child Care Subsidy (CCS) is what brings the fees down. Services Australia pays it directly to your child care service, and you pay the rest.

## How much

Your CCS percentage depends on your family income estimate:

- **$0 to $88,520:** 90%
- **more than $88,520 to below $538,520:** between 90% and 0%, going down 1% for every $5,000 your family earns above $88,520
- **$538,520 or more:** 0%

The percentage applies to your hourly fee or the hourly rate cap for the care, whichever is lower. If you have more than one child aged 5 or under, you may get a higher rate for one or more of them. The StartingBlocks website has a calculator.

## How many hours

From 5 January 2026, every family eligible for CCS can get **at least 72 hours** of subsidised care a fortnight. You can get **100 hours**, depending on your family's recognised participation and circumstances.

## Who can get it

You must:

- care for a child 13 or younger who isn't at secondary school
- use an **approved** child care service
- be responsible for paying the fees
- meet residence and **immunisation** requirements.

Approved care is centre-based day care (long day care and occasional care), family day care, outside school hours care, and in home care, which has extra criteria.

## The 5% held back

Services Australia withholds 5% of your subsidy each fortnight, to reduce the chance of an overpayment. At the end of the financial year it balances your payments against your actual income. If you underestimated your income, you may have a debt to pay back; if you overestimated, you'll get the difference, including what was withheld.

## What to do

Use your likely family income to estimate your percentage when you compare centres and join waitlists. How and when to claim has its own entry.
