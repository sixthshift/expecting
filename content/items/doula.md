A doula is a non-medical companion who gives physical and emotional support before, during and after the birth. They're not a midwife or a doctor: they don't give medical advice or clinical care, and they're not registered health practitioners. In Australia the industry is unregulated, and no particular qualification is required, though many have trained as a birth doula, a postnatal doula, or both.

What one does:

- **Before the birth:** information about labour, and help writing a birth plan.
- **In labour:** reassurance, help with positions, breathing, massage and comfort, and support for the partner too. A doula works alongside the partner, not instead of them.
- **After the birth:** a postnatal doula may visit at home to help with meals, chores, older children, feeding and settling.

Doulas charge private fees, and they vary. Ask Medicare and your health fund, if you have one, what (if anything) they cover.

Questions to ask before booking:

- What training and experience do you have, and what's included?
- What are your fees, and what happens if you're unavailable when labour starts?
- Do you hold a Working with Children Check and a police check?
- Are your vaccinations up to date, including whooping cough and flu?
- Can we speak to past clients?

Check the hospital or birth centre's rules on how many support people can be in the room. A doula works with the staff and leaves the room if asked, for example in an emergency.
