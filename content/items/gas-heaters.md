With checks. Carbon monoxide can make anyone sick, and NSW Health lists pregnant women among the people who can get sicker than others. You can't see, smell or hear it, and it can build up inside when doors and windows are closed.

## Why

Burning gas, petrol, wood, charcoal or coal makes carbon monoxide. Small amounts can cause headaches, nausea, vomiting and weakness; larger amounts cause dizziness, fainting, loss of consciousness, long-term brain injury and death.

## What to do

- **Check the instructions** to make sure the heater can be used indoors.
- **Have a registered gas fitter check it** at least every two years.
- **Think about a carbon monoxide alarm** (it's different from a smoke alarm) that meets the US (UL2034) or European (EN50291) standard, near the bedrooms and rooms with gas heaters.
- **Never bring barbecues, grills or outdoor gas heaters inside**, and never run petrol engines, such as generators, in the house or garage, even with doors open.

**If someone is dizzy, fainting or has lost consciousness, call triple zero (000).** If someone has headaches, nausea or vomiting or feels weak, get everyone out into fresh air and call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.
