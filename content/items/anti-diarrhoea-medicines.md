Ask first. Diarrhoea usually goes away without treatment, and what matters most is replacing the fluid you lose. Avoid loperamide (Imodium) in the first trimester. After that it can be used for mild diarrhoea on your doctor's or pharmacist's advice.

## Why

The main risk of gastro in pregnancy is dehydration, which replacing lost water and salts prevents.

Anti-diarrhoeal medicines don't treat the cause. MotherSafe says they should be avoided for moderate to severe diarrhoea, because they may make the illness last longer. For mild diarrhoea at an inconvenient time, such as travel or work, loperamide is the one most often recommended, at the lowest dose that works for the shortest time.

Diarrhoea at or near your due date may be a sign of labour.

## What to do

- **Rest at home, and sip clear fluids** such as water often, every five to ten minutes. Oral rehydration drinks like Gastrolyte and Hydralyte, from the chemist, help replace fluids and salts.
- **Eat if you feel hungry.** Most people are back on a normal diet in two to three days, even if the diarrhoea hasn't stopped.
- **Get urgent medical treatment** if you can't keep any fluids down, aren't passing much urine or feel dizzy when you stand, and it doesn't settle quickly. You may need fluids through a drip in hospital.
- **Not sure what's causing it?** See your doctor. You can also call MotherSafe on 9382 6539 (Sydney) or 1800 647 848 (rest of NSW), or healthdirect on 1800 022 222.
