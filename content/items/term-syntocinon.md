Syntocinon is a medication used to induce labour. It is given through a vein, attached to a bag that continuously delivers a small amount of the medication. The glossary says it may also be used during the managed third stage of labour to help birth the placenta.

Separately, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says that with active management of the third stage an injection of synthetic oxytocin helps your uterus contract, so the placenta comes out more quickly and the risk of heavy bleeding is lower. The glossary says augmenting labour, which is used when contractions aren't strong or regular enough, may be done by giving oxytocin. The Royal Women's Hospital says oxytocin is the hormone that causes contractions, and a synthetic version can be given through a drip in your arm.
