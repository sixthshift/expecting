## Why

It's not recommended to travel to very remote areas or developing countries while you're pregnant. If you're pregnant, avoid countries where mosquito-borne infections like malaria and Zika are a problem: they can raise the risk of miscarriage, premature labour and stillbirth. And if you're unvaccinated, you have a higher risk of severe illness and hospital admission with COVID-19 or flu. Ask your doctor which travel vaccinations are safe for you.

## What to do

- **Check Smartraveller** for your destination's advisory and health risks, and set up an email alert. If it says do not travel, don't.
- **Know where care is.** Stay within reach of hospitals and doctors, and check what maternity and newborn care is there. Many developing countries have no intensive care for very premature babies.
- **Food and water:** "boil, cook, bottle, peel". Use bottled water, even for brushing teeth and ice. Don't use iodine to purify water after 10 weeks, as it can affect the baby's thyroid; chlorine-based tablets are an option. Traveller's diarrhoea can lead to dehydration, low blood pressure and shock in pregnancy.
- **Medicines:** some legal here are restricted or illegal elsewhere. Check before you go, and carry copies of your prescriptions.
- **Language:** take a phrase book or translation app, and translations of common pregnancy problems. If you're in serious trouble and can't get help anywhere else, contact the Australian embassy or consulate.
