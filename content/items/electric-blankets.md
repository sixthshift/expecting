Yes, sensibly. MotherSafe says that unless you're exposed to extremely high temperatures for a long time, things like electric blankets are unlikely to raise your core temperature enough to cause problems.

## Why

Body temperature matters in pregnancy. MotherSafe says a sustained fever of more than 38.9 °C for at least 24 hours has been associated with an increased risk of miscarriage and certain malformations, including neural tube defects like spina bifida in early pregnancy, and stillbirth in later pregnancy. That figure is about fever, not about a warm bed: MotherSafe says that unless you have prolonged exposure at extremely high temperatures, hot tubs, saunas, electric blankets and sustained exercise are unlikely to raise your core temperature enough to cause problems.

The Royal Women's advises keeping your temperature down when you exercise (a cool ventilated environment, no spas or saunas).

## What to do

- **If you're unsure,** ask your doctor or midwife about how you use your blanket or heat pack.
- **If you have a fever** of 38°C or more, contact your doctor or midwife straight away. Drink plenty of fluids and rest.
