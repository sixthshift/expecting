Parental leave under the law is unpaid. Some employees can get payments during it from the government's Parental Leave Pay, from their employer, or from both.

**Where the employer's pay comes from.** Employers can provide parental leave payments in agreements, employment contracts or workplace policies, and the rules about getting it depend on that agreement, contract or policy. So there's no single answer: Fair Work says that if you have questions about your employer's parental leave payments, speak to your employer first.

**It works alongside the government's pay.** Services Australia says you can take Parental Leave Pay before, during or after any paid or unpaid employer funded leave, including maternity or parental leave, annual leave and long service leave. Fair Work's example is a nurse whose enterprise agreement gives 12 weeks of paid parental leave at her usual wage: she gets both that and Parental Leave Pay during her 12 months of leave.

**If the worst happens.** If a baby is stillborn or dies, employers can't refuse or cancel an employee's employer funded parental leave because of that stillbirth or death, though there are exceptions that may apply.

**What to do.** Ask your employer what they offer and the rules in the agreement, contract or policy that applies to you. Knowing helps you choose when your Parental Leave Pay starts: claiming up to 3 months before the due date lets you pick your start date.
