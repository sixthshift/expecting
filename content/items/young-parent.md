Finding out you're pregnant young can bring up all sorts of feelings: confused and scared, happy and excited, often all at once. That's normal. Other people have been here before, and help is available.

## Your health

See a doctor as soon as you can. Good pregnancy care helps keep you and your baby healthy. Teenagers are more likely to have an early birth or a small baby, and have a higher chance of postnatal depression, anaemia and urinary tract infections than adults, though a lower chance of gestational diabetes and blood clotting problems. Being under 17 is on the list of things that make a pregnancy higher risk, which means your midwife or doctor watches more closely. Eat well, and avoid alcohol, smoking and vaping; your pharmacy or Quitline can help you stop.

## Questions worth thinking through

- Who can you call on for emotional and financial support?
- How will a baby change your plans for study or work?
- Who do you tell first, and how? In person is often best for big news. Don't put it off too long.

If you're worried how your parents will react, plan a safe place to go afterwards so everyone has time to take it in. It's normal for them to be shocked, worried or even angry at first. If you tell the baby's father, do it somewhere private if it's safe, and give him time for the news to sink in.

## School and study

Your school should support you to finish. It may be able to adapt how it teaches and assesses you, your uniform, your hours, and how long you take to finish your senior years, both while you're pregnant and after. Distance education, alternative education centres (some with childcare) and TAFE are other options. You have a right to an education.

## Money and housing

Check what you can get through Centrelink: payments for parents include the Newborn Upfront Payment and Newborn Supplement, Parental Leave Pay, Family Tax Benefit, Parenting Payment and the Child Care Subsidy, if you're eligible. If you rent and get a Centrelink payment, you can apply for Rent Assistance. If you can live with a parent, carer or support person, it may help. If you're at risk of homelessness, couch surfing or not safe at home, services such as Ask Izzy, Homelessness Australia, Mission Australia and The Salvation Army can help.

## Support

- The Brave Foundation has resources for young people expecting or parenting.
- Kids Helpline is 1800 55 1800, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, for teenagers and young adults aged 18 to 25 (as Pregnancy, Birth and Baby words it).
- headspace supports young people going through a tough time.
- Relationships Australia offers family counselling and mediation on 1300 364 277.

## Your rights

You have the right to confidential, safe, non-judgemental care, respect whatever you choose, and time to ask questions before you agree to anything.
