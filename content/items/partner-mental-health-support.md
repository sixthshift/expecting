Your mental health counts in this too. Anxiety and depression in pregnancy and after the birth affect up to 1 in 10 expecting and new dads, PANDA says, and Gidget reports that more than half of new dads don't seek support when things get stressful. Men can find it harder to reach out, because of shame, stigma and other barriers. Every service below is there for you in your own right.

## Who to call

- **PANDA National Helpline, 1300 726 306.** Free, for anyone affected by changes to their mental health from pregnancy until the baby is 12 months old. Dads call about their own wellbeing, about how to support their partner, or for resources for another family member. It's a confidential, non-judgemental space; they'll ask a few questions, listen, help you explore your options and email you any resources discussed. They also offer follow-up calls and peer support from a parent who's been there. PANDA says the line is open 7 days a week, until 7:30pm Monday to Friday and 9am to 4pm on weekends (AEST/AEDT). (MensLine's page lists it as Monday to Saturday; PANDA's own page is followed here.)
- **MensLine Australia, 1300 78 99 78.** Free professional counsellors, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, by phone or online chat. Their first-time-dad page covers sleep, the relationship and looking after yourself.
- **SMS4dads.** Free text messages to your phone with tips, information and links, timed to when you and your baby need them. You also report your mood, and if it shows you need extra support, you're offered a call from a trained counsellor. There are message streams for dads in NICU and for dads whose partner is distressed.
- **Gidget Foundation.** A free video, *Understanding Perinatal Depression and Anxiety for Dads*, and free psychological sessions (up to 10 a calendar year, subject to Medicare eligibility). Gidget encourages dads who need extra support to see their GP about a mental health plan.
- **Dad Space**, run by the Parent-Infant Research Institute: information, strategies and tips for fathers.

## Signs to watch in yourself

PANDA lists feeling low, anxious, short-tempered, angry or resentful; losing interest, withdrawing or not communicating; and physical signs like panic, headaches, muscle tension, and changes to sleep or appetite. In men it can also look like not enjoying things you used to, or struggling to concentrate, decide or get things done. If you recognise these, reach out to your GP or one of the lines above.

## If it's urgent

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000. If you're having suicidal thoughts or feel disorientated, get help immediately: PANDA is not a crisis service, so call Lifeline on 13 11 14 (24/7). Beyond Blue is also there 24/7 on 1300 22 4636.
