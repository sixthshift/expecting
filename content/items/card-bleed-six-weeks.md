After the birth you bleed from the vagina; the blood is called lochia. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's bright red and heavy at first and might have clots, so change your maternity pads often. Around 4 to 10 days it becomes lighter and reddish-brown, and from about 10 to 28 days it's white or yellow with very little blood. You can expect lochia for up to 6 weeks. Bleeding is normal, but if you notice a bad smell, tell your midwife. If you're bleeding heavily (soaking a pad every hour or two) or passing large clots, go to your hospital's emergency department straight away; call 000 if the birth was in the last 24 hours, or you feel unwell, dizzy or clammy, your heart is racing or you're short of breath.
