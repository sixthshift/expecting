After the birth you bleed from the vagina; the blood is called lochia. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's bright red and heavy at first and might have clots, so change your maternity pads often. Around 4 to 10 days it becomes lighter and reddish-brown, and from about 10 to 28 days it's white or yellow with very little blood. You can expect lochia for up to 6 weeks. Bleeding is normal, but if you pass large clots or notice a bad smell, tell your midwife. It also says to talk to your doctor or midwife straight away if you feel unwell and have sudden, heavy vaginal bleeding, which it calls a sign that you need immediate treatment.
