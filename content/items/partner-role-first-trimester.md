Finding out you're going to be a parent can be wonderful, or emotional and confusing, or scary. All of that is normal. Supporting your partner through pregnancy is an important job, and it helps them, your relationship and your baby.

## The quiet weeks

Some couples don't announce the pregnancy until the end of the first trimester. That's your choice. Use the time to get used to the idea of being parents and think about how you'll manage the changes a baby brings.

Pregnancy can be exhausting, physically and emotionally. If your partner is tired, has morning sickness or is uncomfortable, offer help and listen.

## What you can do

- **Offer to come to antenatal appointments** with the doctor or midwife.
- **Talk about how you're both feeling.** Pregnancy can be an emotional time.
- **Do your own research** and share what you learn.
- **Don't take mood swings personally.**
- **With morning sickness,** encourage small amounts of food often. If they're vomiting often and you're concerned, contact your doctor.
- **Look after their health with them:** eat well and be active together, cut back on alcohol yourself, and don't smoke, especially if they're trying to quit.

If your partner is very anxious or seems depressed about the pregnancy, encourage them to ask for help.

## Your own feelings

Gidget lists what's common for dads and non-birthing partners: feeling helpless when your partner is sick or in pain, pressure if you are the sole income earner, worry about your parenting skills, about bonding, or about the birth, a sense of loss as your relationship and lifestyle change, a belief that you have to be the strong one, and finding it hard to ask for help.

It can go further than worry. Anxiety affects 1 in 6 fathers during their partner's pregnancy, and depression affects 1 in 10 fathers from the first trimester through the baby's first year. You're more at risk if you've had anxiety or depression before, your partner has, money or work is a worry, or you feel unsupported.

Talk openly with your partner, family or friends, and speak up before feelings build. If you notice signs of depression or anxiety, see your GP, or call PANDA on 1300 726 306 or MensLine Australia on 1300 78 99 78. If you feel you're getting angry, jealous or violent, speak to your doctor or call one of those lines.

## When to get help

If you're thinking about suicide, call 000 for an ambulance or go to the emergency department. For immediate support with thoughts of self-harm or suicide, call Lifeline on 13 11 14.
