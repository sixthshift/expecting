Genetic counsellors are health professionals qualified in both genetics and counselling, with special skills in explaining health information. They often work closely with clinical geneticists, doctors who specialise in genetic conditions.

## When you might see one

**Before pregnancy**, if you have a genetic condition, one runs in your family, or you want to understand the chance of your baby having one. Carrier screening, which finds out your chance of passing on a genetic condition, can be done before you try to get pregnant.

**During pregnancy**, if a screening test shows your baby has a higher chance of a genetic or chromosomal condition. Your doctor may suggest seeing a genetic counsellor to talk through your options, alongside further tests (CVS or amniocentesis) that can diagnose or help rule out a condition. A genetic counsellor can also support you if a condition is diagnosed during pregnancy.

**After the birth**, if your child has signs of a genetic condition, and to understand the chance for any future children.

## What a session covers

Sessions usually involve:

- talking about your family and any conditions common in it
- learning about the condition and how it's passed down
- what tests are available, and organising them
- understanding the results
- how to manage or prevent a condition, and what support is available

In pregnancy, a genetic counsellor can explain what different tests can and can't tell you, talk through possible outcomes and next steps, help you think about how your values fit your options, and give balanced information, including connecting you with people who've been in a similar situation. Genetic counselling can help you decide whether you want further tests; the decision stays yours.

## How to find one

Genetic counsellors work in many hospitals and health clinics in Australia. Your doctor can refer you. The Human Genetics Society of Australasia has a Find a Genetic Counsellor service to find a registered counsellor near you.
