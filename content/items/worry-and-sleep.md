## What's usual

It's normal to feel some anxiety when you're pregnant. Common worries are your baby's health, labour and birth, how life will change with a baby, how your relationship will change, and your work or studies. Short-term insomnia can come with stress, such as emotional pressure or money worries, and usually gets better once the stress passes.

What can help with the worry side:

- **Wind down.** Do something relaxing before bed, and if you can't sleep, go to another room and do something calm, like reading, until you feel sleepy.
- **Less screen time.** Keep screens out of the bedroom and try not to use them for an hour before bed. Low lighting and a regular bedtime help too.
- **Rest when you can,** and don't expect too much of yourself.
- **Breathing exercises,** done regularly.
- **Stay connected** to people, and ask for help.

The physical side of pregnancy sleep is in [Insomnia](insomnia).

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you, or someone you know, is having suicidal thoughts and is in immediate danger, call triple zero (000). For help and support call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

## See your doctor or midwife

Sleep problems and constant worrying can be signs of perinatal anxiety and depression. See your doctor or midwife if you have:

- a low, sad or worried mood for 2 weeks or longer
- negative thoughts and feelings that affect your ability to function normally
- problems with insomnia (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to seek medical advice and treatment)

PANDA's helpline is 1300 726 306.
