Yes, if it suits you. In Australia every pregnant person is recommended to take folic acid and iodine. A pregnancy multivitamin is one way to get both, but it isn't the only way.

## Why

If you eat a balanced, healthy diet, you should get most of what you need from food. Multivitamins made for pregnancy often add extra folate, iodine and iron, and MotherSafe says they cover most vitamin needs.

But formulations vary. MotherSafe notes that not all contain iodine, and the amount of iron varies too.

More isn't better. High doses of vitamins A, C and E can be harmful, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's best not to take them as supplements in pregnancy. Too much vitamin A as retinol may increase the chance of birth defects.

## What to do

- **Check the label,** or ask your pharmacist, midwife or doctor whether a product suits you. Look for:
  - folic acid: 0.5 mg, or at least 400 micrograms
  - iodine: 150 micrograms a day
- **High-dose vitamins A, C and E:** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's best not to take them as supplements.
- **If it makes you feel sick,** try taking it at night with a snack. If you need to stop it, keep taking folic acid and iodine on their own.
