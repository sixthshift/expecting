Going back to work after having a baby is your decision, and there's no "right" timeline. When you do go back, the law protects your job.

## Your old job

After unpaid parental leave, you're entitled to come back to **the job you had before you went on leave**, even if someone else has been doing it as your replacement. Your employer must tell anyone hired to cover you that the job is temporary and that you have the right to return.

If you were moved to a safe job, or reduced your hours because of the pregnancy, you're entitled to return to the job you had **before** the transfer or reduction.

**Fixed-term contracts.** Your employer doesn't have to extend a fixed-term contract because you're on leave. If it ends during your leave, you're not entitled to return to the same job unless your contract says otherwise. If it ends after you come back, you return and finish it.

## If your job changes or goes

If your employer wants to make significant changes to your job while you're on leave, such as to your responsibilities, pay or location, they must discuss them with you and give you a chance to respond, even while you're away.

If your job no longer exists, you must be offered a suitable available job that you're qualified for and that's nearest to your old one in pay and status. A job that no longer exists may mean a redundancy has taken place. If the job still exists and your replacement is doing it, there's no redundancy.

## Coming back early

You can shorten your leave if your employer agrees; if they don't, you return on the planned date. Your employer can only require you back early if you no longer care for the child, with written notice: at least 4 weeks after the notice, and at least 6 weeks after the birth if you were pregnant.

## Breastfeeding

You're allowed to breastfeed at work, and you can't be discriminated against for it. Making you feel uncomfortable about it, or not providing adequate facilities or breaks, may be discrimination. The Fair Work Ombudsman says employees should be given appropriate breaks to breastfeed or express, and a best-practice employer provides a private room, somewhere to store a pump, and a fridge for milk. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests setting up a pumping routine before you go back.

## Childcare and flexibility

You might talk with your employer about part-time hours, working from home, or flexible hours that fit your childcare. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests a short trial of childcare before you start work. A Child Care Subsidy claim can be backdated no more than 28 days before you submit it.
