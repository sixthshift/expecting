If you get Parental Leave Pay for a child born or adopted from 1 July 2025, the ATO pays a superannuation contribution on it, called the Paid Parental Leave Superannuation Contribution (PPLSC). Services Australia puts it at 12%; the ATO says it's based on the superannuation guarantee rate. It includes an interest component and is paid as a lump sum.

**When.** After the end of the financial year in which you received the Parental Leave Pay. The first contributions start in the 2026–27 financial year, and the ATO will let you know when it has paid it to your fund.

**What you do.** Nothing to claim: once you've submitted your Parental Leave Pay claim, you don't need to do anything further. In most circumstances it goes to the fund your super is currently paid to. The ATO asks you to check that your super fund has your current details, including your tax file number, with your name and address in the fund's records exactly matching the ATO's. If you haven't given your fund your TFN, an additional tax may apply. If you've changed your name, update it with both the ATO and Services Australia.

**Sharing the pay.** If you share Parental Leave Pay with another person, each of you gets a contribution, to your own fund, based on your portion.

The ATO says the contribution is generally taxed at 15% in the fund and counts towards your concessional contributions cap. It isn't counted as income for social security, family assistance and child support.
