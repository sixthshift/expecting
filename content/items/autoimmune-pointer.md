Autoimmune disease is on the list of pre-existing conditions that make a pregnancy higher risk. That means your midwife or doctor watches the pregnancy more closely, which may mean more blood tests, scans, or appointments with obstetricians who specialise in high-risk pregnancies. The plan is made with your own specialists and your maternity team; this page is only a pointer.

## Lupus, as an example

healthdirect's advice for lupus: if you're planning a pregnancy, talk to your doctor. They can help get your symptoms under control before you're pregnant, and check that the medicines you take are safe in pregnancy.

Lupus can bring pregnancy complications such as pre-eclampsia, preterm labour and a higher risk of miscarriage. Pregnancy can also be one of the things that trigger a flare. And if certain antibodies pass to the baby during pregnancy, the baby can develop neonatal lupus, which can cause skin rashes, liver and blood problems and sometimes heart problems.

## Questions to bring

- Which of my medicines are safe to keep taking in pregnancy?
- How likely is a flare, and what do I do if I have one?
- Do I have antibodies that matter for the baby, and how will the baby be checked?
- Who leads my care, and how do my specialist and maternity team share it?
- Where should I give birth?
