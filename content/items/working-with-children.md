Yes, keep working, with good hygiene. People who work in childcare are at higher risk of catching cytomegalovirus (CMV) if they haven't had it before.

## Why

CMV usually causes no symptoms, but it can cross the placenta, especially if it's your first infection, and the risk is higher in the first half of pregnancy. Most babies are fine, but a small number have poor hearing or vision, developmental delays or intellectual disability. Children who've had CMV can carry it in their saliva and urine for years. There's no vaccine.

Chickenpox is more serious in pregnancy, and the vaccine isn't given during it.

## What to do

- **Wash your hands** thoroughly with soap and water after nappies, wiping noses, feeding or handling anything with body fluids.
- **Wear gloves** for nappy changes, and clean surfaces that may have body fluids on them.
- **Don't kiss children on the lips** or put dummies in your mouth, and don't share food, cups or utensils.
- **Ask your doctor about a CMV test**: it's recommended for people with frequent contact with young children, such as childcare workers.
- **If you've been near someone with chickenpox**, contact your doctor or midwife. An antibody injection within 10 days of exposure may lower your chance of catching it.
- **If you think you have chickenpox**, call your doctor straight away rather than going to the clinic.
- **Your employer** must reassess the risks of your job in pregnancy.
