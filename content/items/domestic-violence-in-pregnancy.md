**If you are in immediate danger, call 000** for police and ambulance.

Yes, it is a thing. Family violence (also called domestic violence) can begin, or get worse, during pregnancy and after the birth. It is never okay, and it is always dangerous, for you and for your baby.

## It isn't only hitting

Family violence is behaviour that coerces or controls you, or makes you afraid. It doesn't have to be physical. It can be:

- **emotional or verbal:** blaming, name-calling, yelling, making you feel guilty or that you're imagining things
- **financial:** taking control of the money, stopping you working, restricting your access to bank accounts
- **social:** keeping you from family and friends, controlling who you see, checking your phone and messages
- **reproductive:** pressuring you to get pregnant, or to end a pregnancy
- **physical or sexual:** pushing, hitting, driving dangerously, hurting a pet, any unwanted sexual activity

Warning signs include a partner becoming more jealous, possessive and controlling, checking up on you, accusing you of being unfaithful, or making you nervous to say no. Some people feel that unless there's physical violence, they must put up with it. You don't.

## Who you can tell

Talk with your doctor, midwife or a support organisation. Many maternity hospitals have social workers and counsellors who can explain your rights and help you get protection and support.

If a controlling partner comes to every appointment and you can't speak to your midwife or doctor alone, it is vital to reach a specialist service. If there's nowhere safe at home to call, try from the car, a supermarket, or a friend's phone.

## 1800RESPECT

1800RESPECT is free, confidential, and open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week:

- call **1800 737 732**
- text **0458 737 732**
- or chat online or by video call on their website

Their website has a Quick Exit button, and suggests private (incognito) browsing if you don't want your history saved.

## A safety plan

A safety plan is thinking ahead about how to stay as safe as possible. You can make one on your own, with someone you trust, or with 1800RESPECT's help. It might include calling 000 if you're in danger, a trusted person to contact, keeping your phone with you (or a second, safe phone), a safe place to go if you need to leave quickly, and key things packed in case you do. Keep it somewhere private.

## If you're worried about someone

Listen without judging, tell them you believe them and that it's not their fault, ask if they want help from a service, and keep checking in. 1800RESPECT's website has a page on supporting someone, and you can speak with one of its counsellors. Its website also has a section on using violence.
