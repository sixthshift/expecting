There's no law that says you have to tell your employer you're pregnant at any particular time, but some jobs have their own requirements, so check your award, agreement or contract. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's a good idea to tell your employer before they hear it from somebody else.

## The deadlines that do apply

If you're taking parental leave, you need to give your employer **at least 10 weeks' written notice** before it starts, and confirm your leave dates at least 4 weeks before. Your employer may ask for a medical certificate confirming you're pregnant and your due date.

## Why telling them can help

Once your employer knows, they have legal obligations, for example:

- assessing any health and safety risks of your job to your pregnancy
- moving you to a different role that is safe during pregnancy
- discussing more suitable working hours with you.

If you need a safe job transfer, you'll need to give evidence a reasonable person would accept, and your employer may ask for a medical certificate. Pregnant employees can also request flexible working arrangements, and take sick leave or unpaid special parental leave for a pregnancy-related illness.

## Your protection

You can't be discriminated against because you're pregnant. That means you can't be fired, demoted or treated differently from other employees because of it. Under the Fair Work Act, pregnancy is a protected attribute, and adverse action includes changing your job to your disadvantage or not giving you your legal entitlements, such as pay or leave. Even an employer who thinks they're helping, say by cutting your hours, can be discriminating.

When you're applying for a job, employers aren't allowed to ask if you're pregnant or planning a pregnancy.

## What to talk about

If you plan to get Parental Leave Pay, tell your employer, as they may need to pay it to you. Services Australia suggests discussing your leave plans with your employer before your baby is born.

If you need support, the Fair Work Ombudsman can tell you about your rights, and the Australian Human Rights Commission can help if you can't negotiate reasonable arrangements with your employer.
