Your baby must be safely secured in the correct car seat whenever they're in the car, and it's a job worth doing well before labour.

- **The standard:** it must meet the Australian and New Zealand Standard, **AS/NZS 1754**. Buy from a shop that only stocks compliant restraints.
- **Rear-facing:** by law, babies up to 6 months of age must be secured in an approved rear-facing car seat. Ideally, children stay rear-facing for as long as possible, until they outgrow the seat.
- **Fitting:** it's important that the seat is installed properly. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, and if you're unsure, contact an authorised restraint fitting station in your state or territory.
- **Second-hand:** where possible, buy new, because a second-hand seat's history is unknown. If you do, make sure it's undamaged, under 10 years old and meets the standard. A seat that's been in a crash must be thrown away and not resold.

A rear-facing seat can't be used where there's a passenger airbag, and never swaddle a baby when they're in their car seat.
