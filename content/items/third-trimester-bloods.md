Many of your blood tests are done early in pregnancy, usually at your first antenatal visit, but a few are repeated around the third trimester. As with every test, the choice is yours, and your doctor or midwife should explain why each one is recommended.

**A full blood count, around 28 weeks.** This checks your haemoglobin again for anaemia. Anaemia happens in about 1 in 4 pregnancies, mostly from low iron: in pregnancy your blood volume increases and your body needs extra iron. It can leave you tired and make it harder to cope with blood loss at the birth. If your haemoglobin is low, you may have more blood tests to find the cause, and your doctor or midwife can talk about treatment, such as an iron supplement or an iron infusion.

**Syphilis, between 26 and 28 weeks.** It's recommended that you're screened for syphilis before 10 weeks, again between 26 and 28 weeks, and at 36 weeks or the birth, whichever is earlier. Untreated syphilis in pregnancy can lead to miscarriage or stillbirth. Caught early, it's easy to treat with antibiotics.

**Gestational diabetes.** You may be tested for gestational diabetes around this time, with the glucose tolerance test.

**If you're RhD negative.** You may need more tests relating to your blood group, and you'll be offered anti-D injections at 28 and 34 weeks to stop your body making antibodies against an RhD positive baby's blood.
