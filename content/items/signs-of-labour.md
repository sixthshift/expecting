Normal labour starts between 37 and 42 weeks, and you can't know ahead of time when yours will. Early signs usually start from around 37 weeks, and they can be subtle. It's often not easy to tell when labour is starting.

## The early signs

Your body makes physical and hormonal changes as it gets ready. These might include:

- cramps that may feel like period pain
- low backache
- frequent bowel motions or loose stools
- a change in vaginal discharge
- a **show**: bloodstained or pink mucus you might notice on your underwear or when you wipe. It's the mucus plug that sealed your cervix, and it means the cervix may be starting to soften and open. It can come before contractions start.
- your **waters breaking**

You might also notice the baby dropping deeper into your pelvis, more pressure on your bladder, or a sudden burst of energy and the urge to prepare, known as nesting.

## Braxton Hicks are not labour

In the weeks before labour you may have Braxton Hicks contractions, sometimes called practice contractions. They're irregular and usually painless, though they can become uncomfortable in the third trimester, and they don't open the cervix.

## How you know it's labour

You're in labour when contractions become regular, longer, stronger and closer together. At first they may be mild and irregular, and you may hardly notice them. The first part of labour (the latent phase) can last hours or even days, and you can usually stay at home: if you've had a normal pregnancy, home is the best place for early labour, where you're free to move around.

If you're unsure whether it's true labour, contact your midwife or doctor. You can call them any time you're unsure, need support, or want advice about when to travel.

## When to get help

**If your waters break and you've been told the baby isn't head-first, call triple zero (000)** and ask for an ambulance: there's a higher risk of cord prolapse.

**Call 000** and ask for an ambulance if you think you may not make it to your planned place of birth before the baby is born, or you have heavy bleeding from the vagina, a severe headache or blurred vision.

**Call your midwife or doctor straight away** if:

- you notice early signs of labour before 37 weeks
- your waters break and the fluid is green, brown or blood-coloured
- you have bleeding from your vagina
- you're worried about your baby's movements
- you feel strong pressure in your vagina or bottom, or an urge to push
