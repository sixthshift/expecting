Everyone wants to meet the baby. The first days are also when you're sorest, most tired and learning to feed, so it helps to decide the rules before anyone asks.

**In hospital.** Each hospital sets its own visiting hours and rules, and some limit numbers or children. Check yours, and decide whether you want anyone beyond the closest family, or would rather wait until you're home.

**At home.** Some ideas that work:

- **A quiet first week or two,** with visits after that.
- **Short visits,** an hour or less, at times that suit you.
- **Visitors who help:** bring food, hang out the washing, then go.
- **Nobody comes unwell.** A cold for an adult can be serious for a newborn.

**Vaccines.** The Australian Immunisation Handbook recommends a whooping cough booster for adults who'll be in close contact with the baby (partners, grandparents, carers) if their last was more than 10 years ago, ideally at least 2 weeks before they meet the baby. Ask early, so there's time.

**Saying not yet.** The partner is often the gatekeeper: they answer the messages and the door. A few lines ready make it easier:

- "We're keeping it quiet this week. We'll send photos and let you know when."
- "We'd love you to come. Could you bring dinner and stay half an hour?"

Agree it between you first, then each of you tells your own family and friends.
