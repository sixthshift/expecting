Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection (STI) caused by a tiny parasite, *Trichomonas vaginalis*. It's uncommon in Australian cities, and more common in rural and remote areas. Most people who have it have no symptoms, and it can be passed on even then.

In pregnancy, trichomoniasis is linked to the baby being born early, the waters breaking early, and a low birth weight.

## Symptoms

When there are symptoms, they usually start 4 to 20 days after catching it, and can include:

- frothy, yellow or green discharge from the vagina
- a fishy smell
- itching or burning in or around the vagina
- burning or pain when you wee
- pain during sex
- bleeding after sex
- in a partner with a penis, discharge from it

## Testing

It isn't part of the routine tests in pregnancy. The national pregnancy care guidelines say to offer and recommend a test if you have symptoms. It's a swab from the vagina or a urine sample, and you may be tested for other STIs at the same time.

## Treatment

It's treated with an antibiotic, metronidazole, often a tablet twice a day with food for 7 days. Metronidazole is category B2 in pregnancy: there's limited information, but no evidence of harm to the baby. The guidelines say your doctor may seek specialist advice before treating you in pregnancy.

- **Tell your partner,** and anyone else you've had sex with recently, even if they have no symptoms, so they can be tested and treated. If telling them is hard, the Let Them Know website can send an anonymous text or email.
- **No sex,** even with a condom, for 7 days after starting treatment, or until you've finished the tablets and any symptoms have gone, whichever is later.
- **If symptoms don't clear up** after treatment, go back to your doctor.

## When to get help

See your doctor or midwife, or a sexual health clinic, if you have unusual discharge, itching or burning, or pain when you wee or have sex, or if a partner has been told they have trichomoniasis.
