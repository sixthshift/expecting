## What's usual

Needing to wee more often is normal in pregnancy: hormones early on, and later the baby pressing on your bladder. Pain or stinging is not part of that. Any pain or scalding when you pass urine may mean an infection.

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is the most common infection in pregnancy; about 1 in 10 pregnant people get one. Signs include:

- pain or a burning feeling when you wee
- needing to wee more often than usual, or leaking before you reach the toilet
- feeling your bladder is still full after you've been
- wee that looks cloudy or bloody, or smells strongly
- pain low in your tummy or above the pubic bone, or a fever
- cramping that can feel like early labour

If the infection has reached your kidneys, you may also have a fever above 38°C, back pain and vomiting. Your urine is also checked for infection early in pregnancy, because a UTI can have no symptoms.

## Contact your hospital now

If you have a fever or chills, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your midwife or doctor if you have any symptoms of a UTI, and see them as soon as possible if you have urgency, pain or a burning feeling when you wee. It's important not to delay getting antibiotics: an infection can develop quickly and lead to problems for you and your baby, and an untreated UTI can lead to kidney problems.
