Check with your doctor or pharmacist first. The two hospital services that cover this put it differently:

- **King Edward Memorial Hospital (WA)** says anti-inflammatory medicines, including topical ones such as creams, are not recommended in pregnancy, especially in the third trimester.
- **MotherSafe (Royal Hospital for Women, NSW)** says only a small amount of a topical anti-inflammatory is expected to be absorbed through the skin, and they're generally considered relatively safe. But they shouldn't be used in the last 3 months of pregnancy unless your doctor specifically directs you to.

## Why

Diclofenac and ibuprofen gels are non-steroidal anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs), the same family as the tablets, which are no longer recommended in pregnancy except under medical supervision. More of a gel gets into your body if you use it over a large area, on broken skin, or with heat or a covering over it.

## What to do

- **Try other things first.** MotherSafe suggests trying pain relief without medicines first, where that's possible or suitable.
- **Paracetamol** is generally considered the safest medicine for short-term pain in pregnancy, at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time.
- **In the last 3 months,** don't use one unless your doctor specifically tells you to.
- **If pain keeps going,** see your doctor about the best way to manage it. You don't need to suffer needlessly: pain can be treated in pregnancy.
