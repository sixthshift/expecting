Lower your exposure where it's easy. Most places in Australia have very good air most of the time, and if you're well, being in more polluted air now and then is not likely to harm you or the baby. What matters most is how much you breathe in across the whole pregnancy. It matters more if you have asthma or another lung or heart condition, or diabetes.

## Why

Air pollution is gases and tiny particles, mostly from burning things: tobacco, wood heaters, bushfires and planned burns, traffic, and industry. The smallest particles get deep into the lungs and into the blood.

Pregnancy changes how your lungs, heart and immune system work, which can make you more sensitive to pollution, and the baby's organs are still developing. Studies have linked severe pollution, such as bushfire smoke, with gestational diabetes, preterm birth and slower growth of the baby, and a few Australian studies suggest everyday low levels may affect the baby's growth too. The effects found are usually small; in one study after a major wildfire, babies were on average 7 grams lighter. Most of the research comes from places with far worse air than Australia.

**Cigarette smoke is the single most important source of harmful air pollution for pregnant people.**

## What to do

- **Don't smoke, and keep smoke out of the house:** tobacco, incense, mosquito coils, candles and unflued gas heaters all add to the air you breathe indoors.
- **Never use camping stoves or outdoor heaters indoors.** Their gases can build up to deadly levels.
- **Cut down on indoor sources** where you can: frying and grilling, especially without a range hood, cleaning products and wood heating. Have gas appliances serviced so they don't leak.
- **Walk, cycle or drive on quieter streets.** One or two streets back from a main road cuts your exposure a lot, especially in rush hour.
- **On poor air quality days,** stay inside with the doors and windows shut, and use the air conditioner on recycle. Open up again once the air clears: the rest of the time, plenty of fresh air is good. Air-conditioned public buildings like libraries and cinemas are another option. Your state's environment or health department publishes air quality readings, and the AirRater app shows them where you live.
- **For smoky weeks,** such as bushfire smoke or winter wood smoke, a HEPA air cleaner sized for the room can make one clean-air room in your home.
- **Masks:** paper and cloth masks don't protect against air pollution. A properly fitted P2 or N95 mask does, but every mask makes breathing harder, so check with your doctor or midwife before using one in pregnancy.
- **Keep exercising.** If you're otherwise healthy, the benefits of exercise outweigh the harms except when pollution is extreme.
- **If you have asthma or another lung or heart condition,** watch your symptoms on poor air days and follow your plan. **If you have diabetes,** watch your blood glucose more closely.
- **If you're worried,** talk to your doctor or midwife.
