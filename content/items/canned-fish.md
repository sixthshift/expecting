Yes. The NSW Food Authority says it's generally safe in pregnancy to eat 2 to 3 serves a week of any type of tuna or salmon, canned or fresh.

## Why

**Mercury.** Canned tuna usually has lower mercury levels than other tuna, because the tuna used for canning are smaller species caught when less than a year old. Canned salmon and canned tuna in oil are on NSW's list of fish low in mercury and high in omega-3 fatty acids.

**Listeria.** Canning is heat processing, which kills harmful organisms, so an unopened tin is shelf-stable and safe to eat. Once it's open, fish is a non-acidic food that can grow listeria if it gets contaminated.

## What to do

- **Count it as fish.** A serve is 150 g, and canned tuna or salmon counts toward your 2 to 3 serves of fish a week.
- **Best eaten straight after opening.** Put any you don't use in a clean, sealed container in the fridge and use it within a day.
- **Fresh tuna** sits under the same 2 to 3 serves a week. Cook it to at least 63 °C and eat it straight away; raw tuna in sushi or sashimi is a separate question.
- **Tuna sandwiches:** a pre-prepared or pre-packaged sandwich is on the NSW "do not eat (high risk)" list: see pre-made sandwiches.
