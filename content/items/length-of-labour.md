Nobody can say how long yours will be. The first stage is the longest and its length varies widely between births; it's usually longer for a first birth and shorter if you've given birth before.

## First stage

- **Latent phase** (early labour): can last for many hours or even days.
- **Active phase:** may take up to 8 to 18 hours for a first baby, and 5 to 12 hours if you've had a baby before.

## Second stage

From full dilatation, birth is generally expected within 3 hours for a first birth, or within 2 hours if you have given birth before. An epidural can make this stage take longer.

## When it's called slow

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says the evidence on what is "normal" and what is "slow" isn't strong, and experts have different views. Slow progress in the first stage is when the latent phase lasts longer than 20 hours in a first labour (14 hours if you've had a baby before), or in the active phase the cervix dilates less than 2 cm in 4 hours.

In the second stage it's when birth hasn't happened after 2 hours of full dilatation or 1 hour of involuntary pushing for a first baby (90 minutes and 30 minutes if you've had a baby before). If birth isn't close after 2 hours for a first birth, or up to an hour if you've had a baby, your care team will call a doctor to review how things are progressing.
