A GP is usually the first health professional to call when the baby is unwell. They check growth and development, give the routine childhood vaccines, refer to specialists, and can help with the adjustment to being parents. Some GPs have a special interest in child health.

Choosing one before the birth means you're not searching at 2 am with a sick newborn.

What to weigh up:

- **Getting there:** how close it is, parking or public transport, and whether a pram fits through the door.
- **Hours:** medical centres often open longer, but you may not see the same GP each time. Seeing the same GP matters more if the baby has an ongoing condition.
- **Cost:** ask whether they bulk bill babies or charge a gap. The childhood vaccines are free under the National Immunisation Program, though the visit may not be.
- **Recommendations:** ask family and friends, and your midwife.

**A paediatrician** is a specialist. To claim a Medicare rebate for a private paediatrician you need a referral, usually from your GP, so most families start with a GP. Your midwife or doctor will tell you if the baby needs a paediatrician after the birth.

**The child health nurse** is the other half: free checks of growth and development through your state's child health service until school. Your hospital will usually put you in touch after the birth.

The baby's first routine vaccines are due at 6 weeks. Bring the baby's Medicare card, once you have it, and the baby's health record book to every visit.
