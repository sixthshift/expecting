A model of care is who looks after you in pregnancy, where, and who is there for the birth. Hospitals call their versions different names (the Royal Women's in Melbourne has Team Maternity, MIST and Caseload, for example), so ask what yours calls them.

## The options

- **Public hospital care.** The most widely used model. Check-ups at the hospital's clinic or an outreach clinic, with midwives and doctors; it may not always be the same person. The birth is usually in hospital. With a Medicare card, most costs are covered.
- **Shared care.** Your check-ups are shared between your GP or a community midwife and the hospital, under an agreed plan. The birth is usually in hospital. Medicare usually covers the visits not held at the hospital.
- **Midwifery group practice (caseload).** One known midwife, with a small backup team, through pregnancy, birth and after. Publicly funded. The birth is usually in hospital, a birth centre or sometimes at home.
- **Private obstetrician.** The same specialist through pregnancy, birth and after, with check-ups in their rooms and the birth in a private or public hospital. Medicare covers only part; private health insurance may cover some or all of the rest.
- **Others.** A private midwife, a GP obstetrician (often in rural areas), team midwifery, a public birth centre, or a planned homebirth for a low-risk pregnancy.

## Things to weigh

- **Cost.** Public care costs little or nothing with a Medicare card; private care can be expensive, and costs vary. Check with your doctor, midwife, hospital and health fund what you'd pay out of pocket.
- **Seeing the same person.** The Royal Women's says growing evidence shows that having the same midwife from early pregnancy until around six weeks after the birth has significant and lasting health benefits. Caseload is popular and not available everywhere: check at the booking appointment.
- **Where the birth happens**: hospital, birth centre or home.
- **Your health.** Some options depend on your pregnancy being uncomplicated; a complex pregnancy may need specialist-led care.
- **Where you live.** Not every model is offered everywhere, and in regional or remote areas you may need to travel for some kinds of care.

## When to decide

Ask your GP, midwife or doctor at your first antenatal visit which models are available in your area, and think about your options before your booking appointment. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to take your time, and that it's OK to change your mind and find the care that's right for you.
