Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says relaxation methods can help ease pain in labour: they may distract you and help you focus away from the pain, and create a calm and peaceful environment. Options include music, yoga, breathing techniques and mindfulness. Hypnobirthing and hypnotherapy are listed among other non-medical methods.

## Why relaxing matters

RANZCOG says being relaxed has many benefits: your body works better, and your natural labour hormone (oxytocin) and natural pain-relief hormones (endorphins) are released more readily. Anxiety, fear, tension and resistance are a normal response when you feel overwhelmed or unsure what to expect, and relaxing and trusting that your body knows what to do will help you manage your pain. RANZCOG's advice is to learn how to relax, stay calm and breathe deeply.

## Touch and warmth

The Royal Women's says your pain can often be managed with relaxation techniques, letting go and trusting that your body knows what to do. Fear, tension and resistance are a normal response when you feel out of control or unsure what to expect next. Touch and massage, and heat and water, can also reduce tension.

## Music

The Royal Women's says music is a good distraction and can be very relaxing, but it can also be suddenly and unexpectedly annoying, so be prepared for any eventuality.

## Alongside other options

You may choose non-medical pain relief alongside medical pain relief, and it's your choice whether to use pain relief at all. Different combinations are available. Your doctor or midwife can explain the risks and benefits of each.
