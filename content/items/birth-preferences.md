Birth preferences (a birth plan) are a way of telling your healthcare team the kind of birth you'd like. You can write them down or just talk them through. Not everyone makes one; it's up to you. Research shows that people who prepare their birth preferences are more likely to be satisfied with how the birth goes.

## The options

Things people often think about:

- **Who's there:** your key support people, and when and how you'd like your support person to speak to staff for you.
- **Where:** hospital, birth centre or home.
- **Moving and positions:** staying active, the positions you'd like for labour and birth, labouring in a bath or shower, or giving birth in water.
- **Pain relief:** whether you'd like it offered early, or readily available, and any type you prefer.
- **If things change:** whether you'd prefer medical help if labour is taking too long, and what you'd want if you need an induction or a caesarean.
- **The birth itself:** RANZCOG says a birth plan may include wishes about the birth of your baby and the placenta.
- **Afterwards:** skin-to-skin, feeding, and who'll support you at home.
- **Culture:** any cultural practices you'd like followed.

You don't need answers to all of these, especially with a first baby.

## Things to weigh

- **Keep it flexible.** Labour may not go exactly as you'd like, and sometimes medical help is needed for you or the baby. The Royal Women's Hospital and RANZCOG both warn that sticking rigidly to a plan can add to a strong sense of disappointment if things happen differently. You can change your mind at any time, even when the baby is close to being born.
- **Check it's realistic.** Talk it through with your midwife or doctor. What's possible can depend on where you give birth and any health conditions.
- **Write it together.** The Royal Women's Hospital says a plan you've prepared together helps your partner feel able to speak up for you.
- **Make it easy to find.** Aim for a simple, clear list. Keep a spare copy, and ask for it to be put in your notes.

## When to decide

There's no specific time to prepare your birth preferences. A labour and birth class can help.
