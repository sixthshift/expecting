## What's usual

Pre-eclampsia is a serious condition of pregnancy. It usually comes on suddenly, after at least 20 weeks, and can also happen in the first few days after the birth. It raises your blood pressure and can affect your kidneys, liver and brain, and the placenta, which can slow the baby's growth. It can get worse very quickly. HELLP syndrome is its severe form and needs urgent medical attention.

Most people with pre-eclampsia notice no symptoms at all; some just feel unwell or "not themselves". It's usually found at a routine antenatal visit, which is why your blood pressure is checked at every one: in pregnancy it's high at 140 over 90 or more, and about 1 in 8 pregnancies has higher blood pressure than normal. If yours is high, you may have a urine test for protein and blood tests.

The signs of severe pre-eclampsia are:

- sudden swelling of your hands, face and feet
- a headache that doesn't go away with simple pain relief
- vision problems, such as flashing lights or spots
- severe pain below the ribs or in your upper tummy
- heartburn that doesn't go away with antacids
- feeling very unwell

## Call 000 or go to emergency

In labour, call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance if you have a severe headache or blurred vision.

## Contact your hospital now

If you have a severe headache, blurred vision, sudden swelling of your hands, feet or face, or pain under your ribcage, seek medical attention urgently or go to your local emergency department.

For any of the signs above, see your midwife, doctor or pregnancy care provider straight away.
