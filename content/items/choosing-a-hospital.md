Where you book often decides which models of care you can have, so the hospital and the model of care are worth choosing together.

## The options

- **A public hospital.** You may book at the local maternity hospital for your area. If there's more than one in your area, you can choose which one; your midwife or doctor can help you decide. With a Medicare card, the costs of giving birth in a public hospital are covered.
- **A private hospital.** You'll need a private obstetrician or GP obstetrician, and you book into one of the hospitals where your doctor works. So you're really choosing the doctor and the hospital together. The first step is a GP referral to an obstetrician.
- **A private patient in a public hospital.** Your chosen doctor cares for you, but a single room isn't guaranteed and you may pay hospital, medical and diagnostic costs.
- **A birth centre** (for a low-risk pregnancy) or **home**: see their own entries.

## Things to weigh

- **Models of care.** Which ones the hospital offers: its antenatal clinic, shared care with your GP, midwifery group practice.
- **Care for the baby.** A hospital gives access to anaesthetists for epidurals, newborn specialists, a caesarean if needed, and easy transfer to a special care or neonatal intensive care unit. Most major public hospitals can care for you and your baby if there are complications.
- **Distance.** How far you want to travel, including in labour.
- **Your pregnancy.** Your chance of complications may help decide where you should give birth. With twins, your midwife or doctor may recommend against a home birth or birth centre.
- **Cost**, and whether you'd like a private room.

Questions worth asking each hospital:

- Can we tour the maternity unit before the birth?
- When can we talk about a birth plan?
- What pain relief can I use?
- What birthing aids are there, such as a birthing chair or bean bags?
- How long will the baby and I stay?
- What happens if I need to be transferred from a birth unit to the hospital?

## When to decide

Early. If you want to give birth in a hospital or birth centre, book as early as possible. Birth centres are popular, so book in as early as you can. You can change your mind at any time.
