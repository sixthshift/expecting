At 28 weeks, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says your baby may be breech (head up, bottom down) but most babies move into the head-down position by the time they are getting ready to be born. At 35 weeks it says not to worry if your baby is still breech: most will gradually turn head-down during the last month.

RANZCOG says that near the due date only about 3 babies in every 100 are breech, and that if your baby is still breech then, the chances of turning without help are low. If you are 36 weeks pregnant and your baby is still breech, your obstetrician or midwife should discuss ECV (external cephalic version).
