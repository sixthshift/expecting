A baby's costs fall into three parts.

**1. The income while you're on leave.** Lay out the year week by week: employer-paid leave, Parental Leave Pay, annual and long service leave, and when each stops. Then the fixed costs that keep going: rent or mortgage, insurance, loans, phone and subscriptions.

**2. The pregnancy and the birth.** Public care with a Medicare card covers most costs. Private care adds the gaps, the excess and the hospital's charges; ask about gap payments before you're admitted.

**3. The things to buy.** You don't need everything at once, and much of it can be borrowed or bought second-hand.

**How to do it:**

- Use Moneysmart's free budget planner, together, with your real numbers.
- Give each thing on the list a figure, and a line for the unexpected.
- Mark what's being given, borrowed or handed down, so nobody buys it twice.
- Leave room for the costs that start later, and childcare if you'll use it: it's one of the biggest ongoing expenses.

A gift registry, a baby shower and hand-me-downs can cover a lot; a budget tells you what's left to buy.
