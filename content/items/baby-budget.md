A baby's costs fall into three parts, and the gear is usually the smallest.

**1. The income while you're on leave.** Lay out the year week by week: employer-paid leave, Parental Leave Pay, annual and long service leave, and when each stops. Then the fixed costs that keep going: rent or mortgage, insurance, loans, phone and subscriptions. Anything you can renegotiate is easier to do before the income drops.

**2. The pregnancy and the birth.** Public care with a Medicare card covers most costs. Private care adds the gaps, the excess and the hospital's charges; ask for an estimate in writing before you're admitted.

**3. The things to buy.** A short list really can't wait: a car seat, a safe place to sleep with a fitting mattress, nappies and wipes, and a way to feed. Most of the rest (the pram, a carrier, a monitor, a change table) can wait until you know you need it, and much of it can be borrowed or bought second-hand.

**How to do it:**

- Use Moneysmart's free budget planner, together, with your real numbers.
- Give each thing on the list a figure, and a line for the unexpected.
- Mark what's being given, borrowed or handed down, so nobody buys it twice.
- Leave room for the costs that start later: more groceries and power, and childcare if you'll use it.

A gift registry, a baby shower and hand-me-downs can cover a lot; a budget tells you what's left to buy.
