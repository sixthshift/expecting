## Why

Airlines set their own cut-offs. As at October 2026, Qantas and Virgin Australia allow a routine pregnancy to fly:

| | Flights of 4 hours or more | Flights under 4 hours |
|---|---|---|
| One baby | to the end of week 36 | to the end of week 40 |
| Twins or more | to the end of week 32 | to the end of week 36 |

These are the airline's minimum requirements, not a promise that flying is safe at that point; Qantas says so itself. RANZCOG says travel beyond 36 weeks is not recommended.

Other airlines can be stricter: Smartraveller warns that most restrict travel after 28 weeks. Check with yours before you book.

## What to do

- **Carry a letter** from your doctor or registered midwife giving your due date, whether it's one baby or more, and that there are no complications. Qantas wants it after 28 weeks; Virgin Australia wants it from 28 weeks, and also wants it to say you're fit to fly, dated no more than 10 days before travel. Keep it in your cabin bag.
- **Any complications** mean you need formal medical clearance as well. Qantas wants the form at least five days before departure.
- Check the rules of every airline you're booked with.
