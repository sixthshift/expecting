A pregnant employee who is eligible for unpaid parental leave can take **unpaid special parental leave** if they're unfit for work because they:

- are pregnant and have a pregnancy-related illness, or
- have a pregnancy loss after 12 weeks and the baby isn't stillborn.

It **doesn't reduce** the unpaid parental leave you can take later.

## How long it lasts

For a pregnancy-related illness, it ends when the pregnancy ends or when you're fit to return to your job, whichever comes first. After a miscarriage or termination, it can continue until you're fit for work.

If your baby is stillborn, you can't take unpaid special parental leave, but you can still take continuous or flexible unpaid parental leave, and compassionate leave.

## Sick leave too

Pregnancy isn't an illness or injury, but if you have a pregnancy-related illness or injury, you can take sick leave or unpaid special parental leave. In the Fair Work Ombudsman's example, an employee who has used up her paid personal leave takes unpaid special parental leave until her parental leave starts.

## Telling your employer

Tell your employer **as soon as possible** that you're taking it; that can be after the leave has started. Tell them how long you expect to be away. Your employer can ask for evidence, such as a medical certificate.
