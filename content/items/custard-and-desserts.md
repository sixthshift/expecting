It depends how the egg is cooked. Commercial custard is fine; desserts that use raw or lightly cooked egg are not.

## Why

Egg-based desserts can carry salmonella. If you get it while pregnant, it can cause miscarriage, though this is rare. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby names mousse and custard among the foods that may carry it when made with raw eggs, and says not to eat foods containing raw or undercooked eggs, such as mousses or raw cake batter. The NSW Food Authority puts raw egg in food, chocolate mousse included, under "do not eat (high risk)". Home-made custard may not get hot enough to be safe.

## What to do

- **Shop-bought custard:** eat with caution. It can be eaten cold if freshly opened. Once open, keep it in the fridge, reheat it to at least 60 °C and use it within a day. Check the use-by or best-before date.
- **Home-made custard:** cook it thoroughly, to at least 71 °C, and eat it hot. Keep leftovers in the fridge, always reheat them to at least 60 °C, and use them within a day.
- **Mousse and other desserts made with raw or undercooked egg:** don't eat them (NSW: "do not eat (high risk)"). FSANZ lists commercial custard and pasteurised eggs as the safer choices.
