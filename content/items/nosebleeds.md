## What's usual

Nosebleeds are more common in pregnancy, even if you haven't had many before, partly because hormone changes make the blood vessels in your nose more likely to burst. In most cases there's no need to worry and you can manage one at home:

- sit up and lean forward slightly
- pinch your nose firmly below the bridge and breathe through your mouth
- keep pinching for 10 minutes, then let go and see if it has stopped
- spit out any blood that enters your mouth

Don't tilt your head back, lie down, or put tissues or cotton wool up your nose. For the next 24 hours, avoid blowing or picking your nose, bending down, vigorous exercise, hot drinks, hot showers or baths, alcohol and NSAIDs. A humidifier, saline spray or saline gel may help prevent them.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you have a severe nosebleed that doesn't stop, contact your doctor immediately or call 000 and ask for an ambulance. Go to hospital or call an ambulance if it lasts longer than 20 minutes, there seems to be a large amount of blood, you've swallowed a lot of blood and vomited more than once, it started after you hit your head, you're having trouble breathing, or you feel weak or dizzy.

## See your doctor or midwife

If bleeding starts again, get medical advice; if it doesn't stop after 20 minutes, seek medical help. If you lose a lot of blood during a nosebleed, see your doctor, as it may be a sign of a bleeding or clotting disorder. Speak with your doctor or midwife if you're getting nosebleeds frequently.
