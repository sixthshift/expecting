If you're on a visa, you can still get care for yourself and your baby during and after pregnancy and birth. What it costs depends on your visa and your health insurance.

## Who can get Medicare

You can enrol in Medicare if you're an Australian citizen or permanent resident, a New Zealand citizen, in the process of applying for permanent residency, or on some types of temporary visa; check the list to see if your visa is included.

## Without Medicare

If you're not eligible, plan to pay for all the costs of your pregnancy, birth and postnatal care, whether you go to a public or a private hospital. With no insurance at all, you can still get care, but you'll pay the full cost for yourself and your baby. Call your local hospital to find out its fees, and speak to your doctor or the hospital before making an appointment so you understand the costs.

Public hospitals may offer financial support if you need help paying the out-of-pocket fees. Ask about it when you make your booking.

## Reciprocal health care agreements

Belgium, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Malta, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Slovenia, Sweden and the United Kingdom have agreements with Australia. If you're from one of them, you should be able to get free **urgent** treatment: emergencies and conditions that must be treated before you can go home. You can also apply for limited Medicare cover. Each agreement is different (for example, people on student visas from Finland, Malta, Norway or Ireland aren't included), so check how yours applies. For non-urgent treatment, ambulance and private care you'll need private insurance.

## Health insurance and the waiting period

If you can't get Medicare, take out Overseas Visitors Health Cover; some visas require it. Students on a temporary student visa must hold Overseas Student Health Cover for their whole stay, with dependants included on the policy.

Overseas Visitors Health Cover can cover pregnancy and birth, but you may need to wait **up to 12 months** for parts of the cover to start. Many private health insurance funds have a **waiting period of 12 months** before they cover maternity care, and if you get pregnant before it ends you may need to pay the full cost of your care. With Overseas Student Health Cover there is usually a waiting period of 12 months: you won't be covered for pregnancy care or birth for the first 12 months you're insured, though emergency treatment is covered. Some policies don't cover pregnancy and birth at all, and even with cover you might pay a gap. Check what yours includes before you become pregnant.

## Your baby

A baby born in Australia is an Australian citizen if at least one parent is a citizen or permanent resident. If neither parent is, the baby will not be a citizen and has the same visa as you (and the other parent, if they're on a visa); tell the Department of Home Affairs about the birth so your baby can be added to your visa or visa application. If your baby can't get Medicare, add them to your insurance as soon as you can: treatment after birth can cost a lot.

## Interpreters

TIS National, on 131 450, provides interpreters for health appointments, usually free of charge.
