## What's usual

Swelling in your ankles, feet and fingers can be a normal part of pregnancy: the Royal Women's Hospital says about 8 in 10 pregnant people have it. It tends to be more noticeable towards the end of the day and towards the end of the pregnancy, and it usually goes down overnight while you sleep. Shoes and rings may feel tight. It goes away soon after the birth.

Gradual, mild swelling doesn't need a doctor. To ease it: avoid standing for long periods, rest with your legs raised, wear loose, comfortable shoes and clothes, and wear compression stockings during the day (off at night).

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance if you have a headache together with changes in your vision.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your doctor, midwife or maternity hospital immediately if you have:

- **sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet**, or a very severe or dull headache that does not go away, changes to your vision (blurry vision, flashing lights or dots), severe pain just below your ribs, feeling sick or vomiting, high blood pressure, or heartburn that does not go away with antacids. These can be symptoms of pre-eclampsia, a very dangerous complication for you and your baby.
- **one arm or leg more swollen than the other**, with pain, redness or warmth. Contact them straightaway: this can be a blood clot, and you may need urgent care in hospital.
- **swelling in your legs that doesn't improve overnight**, after 20 weeks (healthdirect). Earlier in pregnancy, healthdirect says to see your doctor or midwife as soon as possible for this.

## See your doctor or midwife

Tell your doctor or midwife if the swelling is more than slight, isn't relieved by rest, or shows up in other parts of your body.
