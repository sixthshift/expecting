A baby born before 37 completed weeks is premature, or preterm. In Australia about 8 in every 100 babies are born early. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby counts full term as weeks 37 to 42; the AIHW counts a term birth as 37–41 completed weeks and a post-term birth as 42 or more completed weeks. Most premature babies thrive as they grow, though some have health and development problems.

## How early is early

- **Before 32 weeks (very premature).** These babies have a higher risk of health and developmental problems. Many need help breathing and are usually cared for in a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) at first.
- **32 to 37 weeks.** Babies may need care in a special care nursery (SCN). Most don't have any serious long-term problems.

## The chance of survival

Babies born at **24 weeks** have about a 1 in 2 chance of surviving if they're born in hospital and receive expert care in a neonatal unit, but a high chance of a disability such as cerebral palsy or blindness.

Born at **25 weeks** with expert care, a baby has about an 80% chance of surviving, though they would still need help to breathe for quite a while.

## Why every day counts

Each day your baby stays in the uterus, the better their health is likely to be. If labour starts early, it may be possible to slow it down or stop it. You may be offered medicines to delay the birth, and corticosteroid injections to lower your baby's risk of complications such as breathing difficulties. You may need to go to a hospital with a NICU or SCN; if your baby is born where there isn't one, they may be transferred.

## Corrected age

After an early birth, your baby's growth and development is measured against their corrected age: their age since birth minus the weeks they were born early. A 16-week-old born 8 weeks early has a corrected age of 8 weeks. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives two ages for when to stop: its premature baby page says most paediatricians correct until age 2; its corrected-age page says corrected age is useful until at least 2 to 3 years. Vaccinations still follow their age since birth.

## When to get help

If you're less than 37 weeks pregnant and have any signs of labour, contact your midwife, doctor or nearest birthing centre immediately. Signs include contractions, your waters breaking, mucus, blood or fluid leaking from your vagina, pressure in your pelvis, cramping or pain in your lower tummy, and lower back pain.
