## What's usual

Sciatica is pain that starts in your back and runs down your leg along the sciatic nerve, through your hip and buttock to your knee or foot. It can come with lower back pain, numbness or weakness, usually on one side, and is often worse when you cough, sneeze, twist, bend or breathe deeply. It happens when the nerve is irritated, inflamed or pressed on. In pregnancy, changes to your muscles and ligaments may increase your risk.

Most of the time it isn't caused by anything serious and goes away over time; many people recover within a few months.

What helps: stretches and low-impact activity such as walking or swimming, good posture, not sitting or standing for long, changing position often, and bending your knees, not your back, to lift. A physiotherapist can help. For pain relief in pregnancy, paracetamol is considered safe; don't take anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen unless your doctor advises it.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) immediately and ask for an ambulance if you have back pain with signs of cauda equina syndrome, a very rare compressed nerve in the spine: lost feeling or weakness in your limbs, numbness around your genitals or bottom, pins and needles or tingling, or problems controlling your bowels or bladder.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor straight away if the pain is severe lying down or at night, or comes with a fever or weight loss you can't explain.

See your doctor if you have symptoms of sciatica, if they haven't improved after 4 weeks of treatment, or you're worried about them.
