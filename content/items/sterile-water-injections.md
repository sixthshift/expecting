Many people have lower back pain that persists throughout labour. Midwives can use a technique with sterile water injections for this. Usually four injections are given in different places in your lower back, just beneath the skin.

## What it feels like

The Royal Women's Hospital says the injections cause a strong stinging sensation, like a bee sting, that lasts for up to 30 seconds before disappearing along with the back pain. They can bring up to two hours of pain relief to your lower back, but you'll still feel the contractions. RANZCOG describes it as a short bee sting sensation that may help your lower back pain.

## What the evidence says

The Royal Women's says there is still not enough research evidence to show that sterile water injections reduce the need for other pain relief. The Royal Women's says there are no side effects for you or your baby, and RANZCOG says they may help "without any side effects".

## Who gives them

Midwives can use the technique. The Royal Women's Hospital lists it under assisted non-medical pain relief, alongside TENS.

## Your choice

RANZCOG says you can change your mind about pain relief at any time, and can choose one method or a few, or change from one to another during labour.

## Availability

RANZCOG notes that not all birth places can offer every method of pain management, which can matter when you choose where to give birth, so ask your care provider what's available at your planned place of birth.
