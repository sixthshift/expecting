Avoid them. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid saunas, hot tubs and heated spas during pregnancy, and the Royal Women's Hospital says no spas or saunas.

## Why

Saunas can increase your risk of overheating, dehydration and fainting, and a significant rise in your temperature may affect your unborn baby. You may already feel warmer in pregnancy, because of hormonal changes and more blood flowing to your skin, and your body may be less effective at cooling itself down.

Not every source puts it so firmly. MotherSafe, at the Royal Hospital for Women, says that unless you're exposed to extremely high temperatures for a long time, a sauna is unlikely to raise your core temperature enough to cause problems, and it cites a 2019 review that found sitting in a sauna for up to 20 minutes was safe at any time in pregnancy. The general Australian advice is still to avoid them, and that's the advice we give.

## What to do

- **Skip the sauna** until after the birth.
- **Already had one?** MotherSafe's reading of the evidence is reassuring for a short session. If you're worried, ask your midwife or doctor, or call MotherSafe: Sydney metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848.
- **A warm bath is fine.** It's perfectly safe to have a warm bath when pregnant; just don't let it get too hot.
- **To stay cool generally,** avoid long stretches in hot places, wear loose clothing and stay hydrated.
