A new baby is a big change for an older child. Feeling left out, blaming the baby, or going backwards for a while (with toileting or sleep) is common and normal.

**Talking about it.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests not telling a toddler too soon: later in pregnancy, once the bump shows, is easier for them to grasp. Talk about "our baby", look at photos of when they were small, and read books about becoming a big brother or sister.

**Big changes early.** If they're moving rooms or out of the cot, do it well before the birth, and put it down to them growing up, not the baby needing their bed. Toilet training may be easier before the baby comes home, but don't push it if they aren't ready.

**The birth.** Labour can start any time, day or night:

- **Who minds them,** and a back-up if that person is away or sick.
- **What they'll need** packed: clothes, a favourite toy, their routine written down.
- **Telling them beforehand** that you'll go away to have the baby, and who will look after them.

**Meeting the baby.** A visit to the hospital to meet the baby helps. Many families have a small gift "from the baby" ready for that first meeting.

Afterwards, the parent not feeding the baby can make time just for the older child, and grandparents and friends can help them feel special too.
