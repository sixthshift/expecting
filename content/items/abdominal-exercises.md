Not sit-ups or crunches. Keep working your core, but in a different way.

## Why

Your abdominal muscles stretch over the growing baby, so they can't work the way they did before. The Royal Women's Hospital calls sit-ups and crunches ineffective in pregnancy, and inappropriate because they're done lying on your back, where the baby can rest on major blood vessels and make you dizzy. Better Health adds that they may worsen abdominal separation, a painless splitting of the muscle down the middle that's common in pregnancy.

## What to do

Draw your tummy in without moving your spine:

1. Get on your hands and knees and let your tummy relax.
2. Breathe in gently. As you breathe out, draw in the lower part of your tummy firmly. Hold for a count of three, then let go.
3. Repeat up to 10 times. Build up until you can hold strongly for 10 seconds, 10 times.

You can also do it sitting or standing with a straight back. When it gets easy, the Royal Women's suggests lifting one arm, then one leg, while you hold. You should be able to breathe and talk throughout.

In a class, clinical Pilates, fitball, aquarobics or a light-weights 'Pump' class are good choices.
