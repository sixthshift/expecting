A baby's arrival can trigger an influx of advice from family, friends and even strangers. The Gidget Foundation says most of it comes from people who want to feel useful or supportive. Some of it is welcome and helpful. Some of it is overwhelming, or confusing, particularly when it conflicts, and it can undermine the confidence of new parents. Health professionals can give conflicting advice too.

## Getting out of it

Gidget's suggestions for unhelpful or unsolicited advice:

- Deflect it with humour.
- Just say "thanks", or "there really is so much to consider".
- Give an acknowledging nod, then disengage from the conversation.
- Divert attention by asking the person to help with something.
- Ask a friend or partner to be your advocate if you need one.

Sometimes firmer words are needed, so come up with a couple of sentences and practise them. For example: "Thank you for trying to help. I really appreciate that you care about me and the baby. We are going to take our time with trying a few different things and we'll work it out." Or more directly: "I've noticed I get stressed when I get different advice from various people. Thanks for offering but we're figuring it out and I'll let you know if I need help."

## Social media

Gidget's tips for managing anxiety and stress say to refer to reputable sources of information and limit the time you spend on unhelpful social media sites. Social media can erode confidence and show an unrealistic view of parenthood.

## Worth keeping in mind

Most new parents struggle with this at some point. Parents' intuition plays a part, it's fine to try different things, and over time you'll work out what's best for you and your baby and whose advice fits. In the meantime, be kind to yourself.
