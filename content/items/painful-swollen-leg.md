## What's usual

Gradual, mild swelling in your ankles, feet and fingers can be a normal part of pregnancy, and leg cramps, most often in the calf, are common too. Swelling in one leg or arm and not the other is different: it can be a sign of a blood clot.

Being pregnant raises the chance of a deep vein thrombosis (DVT), a blood clot in a deep vein, most often in the leg. The area may feel sore, look red or discoloured, be swollen, or have more noticeable veins. Some people have no signs at all. Being pregnant or having recently had a baby also makes a pulmonary embolism more likely: that's when part of a clot breaks off and travels to the lung.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance if you have chest pain, trouble breathing, a racing or pounding heart, dizziness or fainting, or you cough up blood. These can be signs of a clot in the lung.

## Contact your hospital now

If one arm or leg is more swollen than the other and you also have pain, redness or warmth in it, contact your doctor, midwife or maternity hospital straight away.

Any time in pregnancy, pain or swelling in one leg means contacting your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately.
