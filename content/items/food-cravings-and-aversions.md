## What's usual

It's normal to lose your appetite or change what you like to eat, and this can change through pregnancy and between pregnancies. Aversions are more likely in the first trimester. Common ones are alcohol, coffee and tea, meat, fatty food, spicy food, eggs and strong smells; foods can taste different too. If you go off something important to your diet, try it again later: it usually settles as pregnancy goes on.

Up to 9 in 10 pregnant people have food cravings at least once, mostly in the second trimester (weeks 13 to 27). Hormones, tiredness and emotions can all play a part. Eating regular healthy meals, drinking water and keeping balanced snacks around can help. Cravings can't predict the sex of your baby; that's an outdated idea not supported by science.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor if nausea stops you eating well, especially with vomiting, being unable to keep food or fluids down, or weight loss.

Speak to your midwife or doctor if you crave non-food items such as chalk or clay. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says this is an eating disorder called pica, which has been linked to iron deficiency in pregnant women.
