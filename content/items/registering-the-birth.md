Registering the birth is the parents' job. In NSW, the registry says plainly that hospitals will not register your baby for you. You register with the Births, Deaths and Marriages registry of the state or territory where the baby is born, and it's free to register. A birth certificate is separate: in most states it costs extra, though Victoria gives a free digital one once the birth is registered.

**Why it matters.** Services Australia says to register the birth as soon as possible: if you don't, they may not be able to pay you. To get Parental Leave Pay for a newborn, you need to have registered, or applied to register, the birth. Registration gives your baby a legal name and identity, and NSW says the birth certificate is used for things like a passport.

**Decide the name first.** Registration records the baby's given names, so you'll need them to register.

## By when, and how, where you live

The rules differ by state and territory:

- **NSW:** you should register within the first 60 days. You can still register after that: the registry says it's never too late and you won't get in trouble. There's an online form.
- **Victoria:** births must be registered within 60 days. You register online with a Service Victoria account, and each parent fills in their own details separately.
- **Queensland:** you must register within 60 days. Apply online, by post or in person; both parents verify their ID for the online form.
- **WA:** the registration form must be lodged within 60 days. The hospital or midwife gives you information on how to register.
- **ACT:** you must register within 6 months. If your baby is born at the Centenary Hospital for Women and Children or the North Canberra Hospital, you may be able to register through myGov under the Newborn Enrolment Trial, by completing a consent form at the hospital. Otherwise, register online with Access Canberra.
- **SA:** babies must be registered within 60 days. It's free, the hospital won't do it for you, and you can apply online.
- **Tasmania:** register within 60 days. There's no fee, and you apply online.
- **NT:** you must register within 60 days, a stillbirth included. It's free, online or on a form.
