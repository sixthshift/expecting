Bringing the baby home is a big change, and it takes time to adjust, but your confidence will grow. This is a preview for the last weeks of pregnancy: what your part looks like once you're home.

## Share the care

Get involved in the baby's care from the start: dress, wash and settle them, and change nappies. Sharing the job helps you both bond with the baby and support each other, and one-on-one time with the baby gives your partner a chance to rest. Talk to the baby and copy their expressions. If you don't feel an instant, overwhelming attachment, Gidget says that's okay: connection takes time, and small moments like bathing, changing and helping with feeds build it.

## Back up the feeding

Your support can make a big difference, and active support can help your partner breastfeed for longer. Most newborns feed 8 to 12 times a day, each feed can take up to an hour, and it often takes up to 6 weeks to settle into a good routine.

- Be an extra pair of hands: bring a drink, a pillow, whatever they need, and keep them company.
- Encourage plenty of water and make healthy meals; breastfeeding makes people hungry and thirsty.
- **At night,** bring the baby to your partner, then burp, change and settle them back to sleep.
- After feeds, take the baby: they may settle better in your arms, away from the smell of milk.
- If feeding hurts beyond the first 30 to 60 seconds, it's not normal: encourage them to get help, and book the child health nurse or a lactation consultant.
- If you're bottle feeding, learn to make bottles safely, wash or sterilise them, and take turns.

## Run the house and the door

Be at home as much as you can. Do the meals, the laundry and other children. Manage visitors, and make sure they leave by the time your partner needs to feed or rest. Talk early about who does what, and about money.

## Watch their mood

The baby blues are common in the first few weeks. Encourage your partner to see a doctor if they show signs of postnatal depression: sadness, loss of interest, irritability, anger or guilt.

## Watch your own

Partners get postnatal anxiety and depression too; Gidget says around 1 in 10 new dads experience perinatal anxiety or depression. Eat well, keep moving, rest when you can, and share rest with your partner. Talk to your doctor if you're feeling very tired, irritable, angry, overwhelmed or like you can't cope. Gidget says if you've felt low, anxious, irritable or disconnected for two weeks or more, or things are getting harder, extra support may help: Gidget, PANDA, Beyond Blue, Dad Space, SMS4dads or Lifeline.
