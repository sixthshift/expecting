Yes, often. Being casual doesn't rule you out of [unpaid parental leave](unpaid-parental-leave), but there's an extra test.

## Who qualifies

You can take unpaid parental leave, and request flexible working arrangements, if you:

- have been working for your employer on a **regular and systematic basis for at least 12 months**, and
- had a **reasonable expectation of continuing** that regular and systematic work, had it not been for the birth.

The 12 months are counted back from the expected date of birth. Beyond that, the rules are the same as for everyone else, including for continuous and flexible parental leave.

## A safe job, whatever your service

All pregnant employees, casuals included, have the right to a [safe job](safe-job-and-no-safe-job-leave) if their usual work isn't safe for a time because of the pregnancy. That holds even if you haven't worked there long enough to get unpaid parental leave.

## Money while you're off

Casuals don't get most types of paid leave, so there's usually no annual or sick leave to fall back on. In some states and territories, long-serving casuals can get long service leave.

[Parental Leave Pay](parental-leave-pay) from the government has its own [work test](parental-leave-pay-work-test): 10 of the 13 months before the birth, and at least 330 hours in those 10 months, around one day a week.

## What to do and when

If you qualify, give your employer written notice at least 10 weeks before your leave starts, saying how much leave you want and its start and finish dates (see [telling your employer about your leave](employer-leave-notice)). If you can't give 10 weeks, give notice as soon as you can.
