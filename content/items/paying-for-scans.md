Medicare helps with the cost of routine pregnancy scans, but it doesn't always pay the whole bill. That's why a scan can still cost you money.

## How Medicare works for scans

Medicare covers free or subsidised ultrasounds, including routine pregnancy scans, when you have a Medicare card. If the practice bulk bills, you pay nothing. If it charges more than Medicare covers, Medicare pays part and you pay the rest: the gap, or out-of-pocket cost. Whether you pay one, and how much, depends on where you go for the scan.

**Ask when you book.** Ask your midwife or doctor whether to expect out-of-pocket costs in your situation, and ask the practice about its fees when you make the appointment.

## The scans most people have

- **The dating scan**, if needed, usually in the first trimester, though it can be done any time.
- **The nuchal translucency scan** at 11 to 14 weeks, part of combined first trimester screening. The blood test for that screening is covered by Medicare, but there may be costs for the ultrasound.
- **The morphology scan** at 18 to 22 weeks.

Extra scans are usually only done if they're needed, such as for a complication or worries about your baby's movements.

## Tests that aren't covered

Not every antenatal test is on Medicare. NIPT, the blood test that screens for Down syndrome and other conditions from 10 weeks, isn't covered and can be expensive.

## 3D, 4D and keepsake scans

Some people choose scans, including 3D and 4D scans, for non-medical reasons. Healthdirect's advice is to discuss having these, or whether an ultrasound is needed, with your obstetrician or GP.

## If costs add up

The Medicare Safety Nets can lower your out-of-pocket costs for out-of-hospital services once you reach a threshold. Couples and families can combine their costs by registering as a family.

If you're worried about the cost of antenatal tests, speak to your doctor or midwife. If you're not eligible for Medicare, you'll usually pay the full cost.
