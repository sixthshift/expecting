MotherSafe considers it safe to drink herbal teas in moderation, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests no more than 2 cups a day, and checking with your doctor or pharmacist first, especially if you take prescription medicine.

## Why

Herbal teas have no caffeine, but they're a form of herbal medicine, and natural doesn't necessarily mean safe. The amount of each herb can vary, and a tea may contain ingredients that aren't on the label, or contaminants. What's in it can cross the placenta to your baby or interfere with other medicines you take.

Large amounts of some herbs may be harmful in pregnancy: they could harm your baby or trigger contractions, raising the chance of preterm labour.

## What to do

- **Limit it to 2 cups a day,** and drink different types, so your baby gets less of any one herb.
- **Likely to be safe:** ginger tea in moderation, which can help with nausea; peppermint tea, which can ease nausea and wind and hasn't been shown to harm you or your baby (avoid very large amounts); and raspberry leaf tea, though it hasn't been shown to help prepare for labour.
- **Not enough known:** chamomile (best not drunk in large quantities), dandelion and rose hip.
- **Green tea isn't herbal.** It contains caffeine, so limit how much you drink.
- **Ask about the rest.** Some herbal teas are reported to be unsafe in pregnancy; ask your doctor or midwife which ones to avoid.
