Living a long way from a maternity unit changes the logistics more than the care. Your choices of care and where to give birth depend on where you live, and long travel for appointments and the birth can take time, energy and money. A bit of planning takes much of the pressure off.

## Moving closer for the birth

In some places you'll be asked to travel to the hospital ahead of the birth. In remote Northern Territory, care is at your nearest community care centre, and once you're 37 to 38 weeks pregnant you will need to travel to hospital for the birth; your local community clinic will arrange your transport and accommodation. In rural Queensland, you'll be advised to travel closer to your birthing hospital at 37 to 38 weeks.

Wherever you are, if you need to travel a long distance to give birth, plan ahead.

## Help with travel and accommodation costs

Every state and the Northern Territory has a scheme you may be able to claim some of the cost of travelling for care from:

- **NSW:** Isolated Patients Travel and Accommodation Assistance Scheme (IPTAAS), 1800 478 227
- **Queensland:** Patient Travel Subsidy Scheme (PTSS)
- **Victoria:** Victorian Patient Transport Assistance Scheme (VPTAS)
- **South Australia:** Patient Assistance Transport Scheme (PATS), 1300 341 684
- **Western Australia:** Patient Assisted Travel Scheme (PATS)
- **Tasmania:** Patient Travel Assistance Scheme (PTAS)
- **Northern Territory:** Patient Assistance Travel Scheme (PATS)

Depending on the scheme, they can cover private car use, public transport, taxis, air travel and accommodation. They don't always cover everything: in WA, you pay the difference between what you get and what the travel cost.

## Planning ahead

Gidget Foundation suggests a simple plan for appointments, travel, the birth, childcare and urgent care. Keep key phone numbers, appointment details, travel plans and urgent care contacts in one place, such as your phone, the fridge or the hospital bag. Think about who could help with transport, childcare, meals, animals and work if you need them. Ask about telehealth, grouped appointments and any transport, accommodation or financial support.

## Looking after yourself

Being far from family, friends and parenting groups can feel isolating, and in a small community you may worry about being seen or judged. You deserve confidential care, and health professionals are required to protect your health information. Your village might be neighbours, online groups, or family on video calls.

Telehealth can bring specialist support to your home. Gidget Foundation's **Start Talking** program gives expectant and new parents, and partners, 10 free support sessions a calendar year by video call, with a GP referral and mental health treatment plan.

## When to get help

If worries, low mood or stress last a while or make daily life harder, start with your GP, midwife, maternal and child health nurse or Aboriginal health worker. Lifeline (13 11 14) is there 24/7, and so is 13YARN (13 92 76) for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.
