Usually no. Calcium matters for your baby's growth, but there is usually no need for extra calcium supplements in pregnancy if you eat a healthy diet.

## Why

Your body adapts to make more efficient use of the calcium in your usual food. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby also says there is some evidence that calcium supplements lower your chance of high blood pressure during pregnancy. The national pregnancy care guidelines go further: they strongly recommend a calcium supplement for anyone at risk of high blood pressure in pregnancy.

## What to do

- **Aim for 3 to 4 serves of dairy foods a day,** or alternatives fortified with calcium (MotherSafe).
- **If that isn't possible,** MotherSafe says to consider a calcium supplement. Some preparations contain both vitamin D and calcium. Talk it over with your doctor or midwife.
- **Ask whether you're at risk of high blood pressure in pregnancy,** for example of pre-eclampsia. If you are, the guidelines say to take calcium.
- **Your midwife or doctor may suggest calcium** if your usual diet doesn't have enough or you have certain medical conditions.
