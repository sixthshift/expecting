At your first visit your midwife or doctor may suggest testing for chlamydia and gonorrhoea, depending on your age and circumstances. The national pregnancy care guidelines recommend it for everyone under 30 and anyone at increased risk, and the test can be a urine sample or a vaginal swab you take yourself. The test is a urine sample, a swab or a blood test, depending on the STI. You can also ask for it. Many people with an STI have no symptoms.

Chlamydia can pass to the baby during labour or birth, and some STIs raise the risk of eye and breathing infections in newborns. Bacterial STIs such as chlamydia can usually be treated with antibiotics, and early treatment reduces the risks.
