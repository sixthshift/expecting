## What's usual

**A cold** affects your nose and throat: a sore throat, headache, sneezing, cough, blocked or runny nose, and sometimes a fever (38 °C or higher). Colds aren't dangerous for you or your baby and usually get better on their own in a few days.

**The flu** has similar symptoms but often comes on faster, and can bring severe tiredness, muscle and joint aches, nausea and vomiting. It's more serious than a cold. In pregnancy you're more likely to catch it and to become seriously unwell, especially in the second or third trimester, and it can lead to pneumonia, premature labour or stillbirth.

To ease symptoms of either: rest, drink plenty of fluids, try warm drinks with lemon and honey, and use a saline nasal spray (for no more than 5 days). Paracetamol is considered safe at all stages of pregnancy. Some cold medicines are not: don't use ibuprofen unless your doctor prescribed it, or oral decongestants such as pseudoephedrine, and ask your doctor before taking a cough medicine. If you're not sure, ask your doctor or pharmacist.

The best protection is the flu vaccine. It's free in pregnancy under the National Immunisation Program, can be had at any stage, and is updated each year, so have it even if you had one last year.

## See your doctor or midwife

**See a doctor if you think you have the flu.** Your doctor may prescribe an antiviral medicine, which works better the earlier you take it, so go when you first notice symptoms. Antibiotics won't help the flu.

Also see your doctor if you're worried about your symptoms. Call ahead to tell the reception what's going on, and ask about a telehealth appointment to protect others in the waiting room.
