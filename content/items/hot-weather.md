Hot days need planning. Your metabolism rises in pregnancy and your body temperature with it, so you're more sensitive to hot weather. NSW Health lists pregnant people among the people hot weather can cause serious health problems for.

## Why

In the first trimester, becoming overheated can affect the baby's growth and development. And whenever you overheat, more blood flows to your skin to cool you, which may mean less goes to the placenta, the baby's supply of oxygen and nutrients.

## What to do

- **Stay out of the sun** and, if you can, spend the middle of the day somewhere air-conditioned.
- **Drink water regularly,** even if you're not thirsty. Dark urine is a sign you're dehydrated.
- **Cool down:** loose clothes, a cool shower, feet in cool water.
- **Move exercise to early morning or later in the day.** Swimming, walking and pregnancy classes are less likely to overheat you.
- **Feel unwell from the heat?** Stop, lie down somewhere cool, loosen your clothes and sip water. Heat exhaustion looks like heavy sweating, great thirst, headache, cramps, dizziness and pale, clammy skin.
- **See a doctor** if heat exhaustion doesn't improve after you've cooled down and rested, or see a doctor or urgent care clinic if you get dizziness, nausea, a fast pulse or shortness of breath.
- **Get emergency medical care for heatstroke (call 000):** hot, dry skin with no sweating, a temperature over 40 °C, vomiting, confusion or slurred speech, or collapse or seizures. It can be life-threatening.
