Unless your doctor or midwife tells you otherwise, you can start or continue regular exercise while you're pregnant. If you weren't very active before, start small and build up; if you were, choose safe activities at a level that feels right. As the pregnancy goes on, you may need to do less.

## How much

Be active most days, preferably every day, adding up to 2.5 to 5 hours of moderate activity a week (or 1.25 to 2.5 hours of vigorous activity), with muscle strengthening at least 2 days a week (Australian Government recommendations).

Moderate means you can still hold a conversation (the "talk test"). Good choices include walking, swimming and water aerobics, a stationary bike, dancing, pregnancy classes, yoga, Pilates and light weights.

## Why your body needs a lighter touch

- **Your heart** enlarges and pumps faster, so you're already doing an aerobic workout just by being pregnant. Keep the intensity moderate.
- **Your blood** volume rises and can pool in your legs; calf raises or walking on the spot help.
- **Heat:** your temperature runs slightly higher, and intense exercise can push your core temperature to a level unsafe for your baby. Drink water, wear light clothes, exercise somewhere cool, skip hot yoga, spas and saunas, and don't exercise with a fever.
- **Joints:** hormones soften your ligaments, especially around the pelvis and lower back. Avoid high-impact, twisting and jumping exercise.

## What to avoid

- **Lying flat on your back.** The weight of your bump can press on big blood vessels, making you feel faint and reducing blood flow to your baby. Avoid exercises on your back after 16 weeks.
- Contact sports and anything with a risk of being hit or falling (football, squash, horse riding, downhill skiing, gymnastics).
- Lifting heavy weights.
- Scuba diving and skydiving, and exercise above 2,000 m until you've acclimatised.

Specific activities have their own entries.

## What it does for you

Exercise can help prepare your body for birth and recovery, reduce back and pelvic pain, avoid excess weight gain, lower your risk of gestational diabetes, reduce the chance of mental health complications and make you fitter for labour.

## Check first

See your doctor or midwife before exercising if you have pre-eclampsia, placenta praevia, twins or more, bleeding in the second or third trimester, a weak or short cervix, waters that have broken, or a long-term condition such as type 1 diabetes or high blood pressure.

## When to get help

Call 000 if you have chest pain or severe trouble breathing. Stop exercising and see your doctor immediately if you have unexplained shortness of breath, dizziness, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or sudden swelling of the ankles, hands or face, or nausea and vomiting. Also stop if you notice vaginal bleeding or pain in your tummy, back or pelvis. If your baby is moving less, contact your midwife or birth unit straight away, don't wait: see [The baby is moving less](baby-moving-less).
