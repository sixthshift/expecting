Yes. Antihistamines are considered safe in pregnancy at recommended doses, though it's always best to check with your doctor or pharmacist first.

## Why

There's more safety information on the older, sedating antihistamines, such as promethazine and dexchlorpheniramine, and they may be used at recommended doses at any stage of pregnancy. Less sedating ones, such as loratadine, desloratadine, cetirizine, levocetirizine and fexofenadine, are also considered reasonable to use, because there's been adequate follow-up of their safety in pregnancy. healthdirect adds that non-sedating antihistamines like loratadine should be used for the shortest time possible.

Nasal sprays and eye drops get into your bloodstream in only very small amounts, so they're preferred in pregnancy, but tablets are considered safe too.

Taking sedating antihistamines regularly around the time of the birth may rarely make the baby drowsy or cause withdrawal symptoms.

## What to do

- **Avoid your triggers first,** where you can: pollens, dust mites, animal dander and moulds.
- **Take tablets as needed,** rather than every day. If one antihistamine doesn't work, another may work better.
- **For a blocked nose,** antihistamines generally won't help. Saline sprays, antihistamine nasal sprays (azelastine, levocabastine) and corticosteroid nasal sprays (such as budesonide and mometasone) are considered safe.
- **Check combined products** with your pharmacist: KEMH says oral decongestants such as pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine are not recommended in pregnancy (other sources differ; see Sudafed and oral decongestants).
- **While breastfeeding,** the non-sedating ones are preferred, and sedating ones are best avoided with younger babies.
