Keep it moderate. Use the talk test: during moderate activity you can still hold a conversation; during vigorous activity you'd find it hard (Australian Government guidelines).

## Why

Your heart already works harder in pregnancy: it enlarges and pumps faster, and your resting heart rate goes up. That leaves a smaller range between resting and a safe maximum, so the Royal Women's Hospital says to moderate the intensity.

Hard exercise also heats you up, and your core temperature can rise to a level that isn't safe for the baby.

## What to do

- **Very active already?** If you're an athlete or do more than the recommended amount, get advice or supervision from a qualified health professional.
- **Vigorous exercise is possible for some.** MotherSafe cites a review finding high-intensity exercise at 25 °C for up to 35 minutes safe at any stage, but says to weigh your fitness before pregnancy and check with your doctor or midwife first.
- **Regular beats occasional.** Moderate exercise often is better than occasional intense sessions.
- Avoid exercising in hot weather, especially when it's humid, and stay well hydrated.
