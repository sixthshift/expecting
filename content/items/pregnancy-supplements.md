A healthy, balanced diet gives you most of what you need in pregnancy. But two supplements are recommended for everyone in Australia: folic acid and iodine. Before adding anything else, talk to your pharmacist, doctor, midwife or dietitian.

## Folic acid

Folate lowers the chance of your baby being born with a neural tube defect, such as spina bifida. Your body needs twice as much in pregnancy, which is hard to get from food alone.

- Ideally, start **0.5 mg (500 mcg) of folic acid a day** at least one month before a planned pregnancy, and keep taking it for the first 3 months of pregnancy. A supplement with 400 mcg is another good option, says Pregnancy, Birth and Baby.
- Your doctor may recommend a **higher dose (5 mg)** if you have diabetes, are living with overweight or obesity, take anticonvulsant (epilepsy) medicine, have a family member affected by a neural tube defect, or are at risk of poor food absorption.

## Iodine

Iodine is important for your baby's brain development. Take a supplement with **150 mcg a day**, before, during and while breastfeeding. The Royal Women's Hospital says not to take kelp (seaweed) tablets, as they may contain too much iodine.

## Vitamin D

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says some people need a vitamin D supplement: if you're likely to be low, your midwife or doctor may test your levels and recommend one. The Royal Women's Hospital goes further, advising every pregnant person to take 400 IU (10 mcg) a day, with an extra tablet if a blood test shows you're low. Ask your midwife or doctor which applies to you.

## Iron, calcium, omega-3 and B12

- **Iron:** needs rise in the second and third trimesters. If a blood test shows you're low in iron or have anaemia, you'll likely be recommended an iron supplement.
- **Calcium:** usually no supplement is needed if you eat a healthy diet. Your midwife or doctor may suggest one if your usual diet doesn't have enough or you have certain medical conditions.
- **Omega-3:** your midwife or doctor may suggest it if you're low. Avoid fish liver oils: they contain too much vitamin A.
- **Vitamin B12:** your midwife or doctor may suggest it if you follow a vegetarian or vegan diet. The Royal Women's Hospital says that if you eat no animal foods you will need to take it.

## A pregnancy multivitamin

Pregnancy multivitamins often contain extra folate, iodine and iron, but amounts vary by brand, so read the label and check how many tablets to take. The Royal Women's Hospital says to take only a multivitamin designed for pregnancy, and that costlier ones aren't always better.

## What to avoid

More isn't better: high doses of some vitamins can be dangerous. It's best not to take vitamins A, C or E as supplements in pregnancy. Extra vitamin A might increase the chance of birth defects: don't take more than 10,000 international units a day, and avoid foods very high in it, such as liver and pâté. Only take a high-dose supplement if your doctor recommends it.
