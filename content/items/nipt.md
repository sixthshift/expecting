During pregnancy some of the baby's DNA passes into your blood. Non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) checks it, from a blood test taken from around 10 weeks. It estimates the chance of Down syndrome (trisomy 21), Edwards syndrome (trisomy 18), Patau syndrome (trisomy 13) and sex chromosome conditions such as Turner syndrome. It can also tell the baby's sex.

## The options

- **NIPT.** Identifies about 99% of babies with Down syndrome. More accurate than the nuchal translucency scan or combined screening. Comes as **genome-wide** (all chromosomes) or **targeted** (selected chromosomes); ask your doctor which suits you.
- **Combined first-trimester screening.** A blood test and the nuchal translucency scan; identifies about 85 to 90%. The blood test is covered by Medicare; the scan may cost.
- **Second trimester serum screening.** A blood test between 15 and 20 weeks; detects about 75%.
- **No screening.** It's your choice whether to have any of them.

## Things to weigh

- **Cost.** NIPT costs around $450 to $800 (as at 2026), depending on the provider and the tests you choose. Neither Medicare nor private health insurance covers it, and you may also pay for the doctor's appointment to get the referral.
- **It's still a screen.** It gives a chance, not a yes or no. A higher-chance result needs a diagnostic test, CVS or amniocentesis, to confirm it, and you may be offered genetic counselling.
- **It doesn't cover everything.** NIPT doesn't screen for all genetic conditions; ask what's included.
- **It doesn't replace the scan.** NIPT doesn't include the 11 to 13 week ultrasound, which has to be arranged separately if you want one.
- **The sex.** NIPT can tell you the baby's sex, if you want to know.

## When to decide

NIPT can be done from around 10 weeks. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests talking to your doctor, midwife or a genetic counsellor first and giving yourself some time to decide. If you want the scan too, remember its window: usually 11 weeks to 13 weeks 6 days.
