From 28 weeks until the birth, doctors recommend you sleep on your side. That means going to sleep on your side at night and when you nap during the day. Either side is fine, or both. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's important to do this to help reduce your risk of stillbirth.

## Why

Later in pregnancy, lying on your back puts the weight of the baby and uterus on major blood vessels. That can reduce blood flow to the uterus, restrict the baby's oxygen supply and affect their heart rate, and research suggests it brings a higher risk of stillbirth. RANZCOG says recent scientific studies have shown that people who go to sleep on their back have up to an 8 times higher chance of having a stillborn baby than those who go to sleep in another position. Research shows that sleeping on your side can reduce the risk.

Stillbirth has many different causes, and most are not in your control. Side sleeping is one simple thing that is.

## What to do

- **Go to sleep on your side.** The position you fall asleep in is usually the one you stay in longest, so that's the one that counts.
- **Woke up on your back?** Don't worry. Changing position in your sleep is normal. Just roll onto your side and go back to sleep.
- **Make it comfortable.** Bend your knees and put a pillow between them, and one behind your back so you don't roll over. A pillow under your belly, or a pregnancy pillow, can help too.
- **Don't lie awake over it.** RANZCOG's advice is to set yourself up with pillows and, if you wake on your back, simply settle back on your side.
