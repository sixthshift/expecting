A birth centre is an alternative to a hospital labour ward for your labour and birth. It treats pregnancy and birth as normal life events, takes a less medical approach, and often feels more relaxed and home-like than a hospital. Most are in a public hospital or close to one, so you can be transferred if you or your baby need more care.

## What it's like

Birth centres are usually run by midwives, and in many the same group of midwives looks after you through pregnancy and birth (continuity of care), so you get to know them. You'll often have your own room with a double bed and an ensuite; some offer water birth. Friends and family can be with you in labour, and some centres let your children be there too.

Your pregnancy check-ups happen at the centre or at the hospital it's attached to, and you may be able to see a doctor there if you need extra care.

## Who can use one

Birth centres generally take people without pregnancy complications: you and your baby are healthy and the birth is expected to be straightforward. The centre decides whether your pregnancy is low risk, so talk to them about whether you'd be suitable. A birth centre might not suit you if you have a higher chance of complications, or had complications in a previous labour.

## Pain relief and transfers

Birth centres aim for minimal medical intervention, so there are fewer pain relief options than in hospital. Gas and some medicines may be available, along with relaxation, massage, movement and water. There's no epidural: if you want one, or if there are complications, you'll be transferred to the hospital labour ward.

## After the birth

The midwives help you with feeding and caring for your baby. If all is well you may go home early, sometimes within 6 hours, and midwives can visit you at home in the first few days. If you need to stay longer, you may move to the hospital maternity ward.

## Cost

Most birth centres are publicly funded, so the cost is usually covered by Medicare. Ask about any extra costs when you book.

## Getting in

Each centre is different: you may need a doctor's referral, or you may be able to refer yourself. Birth centres are usually in demand, so book in as soon as you know you're pregnant. The first appointment is usually at around 10 to 16 weeks. If there's no birth centre near you, ask your doctor for suggestions.

Questions worth asking: can we tour it, what pain relief can I use, how long will we stay, and what happens if I need to transfer to the hospital?
