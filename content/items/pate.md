Best avoided. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid all types of pâté, and Food Standards Australia New Zealand lists refrigerated pâté and meat spreads among the foods to avoid in pregnancy.

## Why

**Listeria.** Pâté is one of the foods that can be contaminated with listeria, a bacterium that can grow even in the fridge. In pregnancy listeria can cause miscarriage, premature birth or stillbirth.

**Vitamin A.** Liver contains a lot of vitamin A (retinol), and too much can harm your baby. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests avoiding foods very high in vitamin A, which might include liver and pâté.

**Undercooked liver.** Outbreaks in Australia and overseas have been linked to poultry liver dishes such as pâté or parfait where the liver was undercooked. FSANZ says livers need cooking all the way through to kill harmful microorganisms, particularly campylobacter, and that lightly searing the surface is not enough.

## What to do

- **Skip pâté, chilled or not.** Canned or shelf-stable pâté carries less listeria risk than chilled, but liver pâté is still very high in vitamin A, so it's simplest to leave it until after the birth.
- **Pâté made from liver** may also be very high in vitamin A.
- **Already had some?** Listeria symptoms can be mild, like the flu: fever, headache, tiredness, aches. FSANZ says if you're pregnant and get them, seek medical attention immediately. If you're worried about something you've eaten, call your maternity care provider or doctor.
