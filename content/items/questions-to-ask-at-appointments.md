You'll be offered a number of tests and scans during your pregnancy. Your doctor or midwife is there to support you, so don't be afraid to ask them questions about the care they give you.

## Questions about any test or scan

**About the procedure**
- Is this routine?
- How does it work?
- Why might I need it?
- When should I have it?

**Benefits and risks**
- What are the benefits to me or my baby?
- Are there any side effects?
- Are there any risks to me or my baby?

**My choice**
- Is there an alternative if I don't want this test?
- Do I have to have it?

**About the results**
- When will I get the results?
- What happens after I get the results?
- What are the chances of the result being wrong?

**Before and after**
- Do I need to do anything to prepare?
- Do I need to care for myself afterwards?

**Costs and support**
- Are there any costs involved?
- What support is available if something is wrong?

## Your rights

It's your right to be fully informed about any tests or treatment you're asked to have, and to refuse any you're offered. You will be offered recommended antenatal tests and can decide whether you'd like them: this is called informed consent.

## Costs

Medicare covers some of the cost of routine antenatal tests, but not all. Depending on the test and where you have it, you may pay an out-of-pocket cost, so it's a good idea to check the fees when you book.

## Other things to bring up

Appointments are also a chance to ask about pregnancy symptoms, what to expect in each trimester, the birth itself and caring for your baby afterwards. It's a good idea for your birth support partner to come along, especially when you're discussing your birth plan.
