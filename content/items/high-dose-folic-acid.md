Ask your doctor. Most people take 0.5 mg of folic acid a day (or a supplement with 400 micrograms), from at least a month before pregnancy through the first 3 months. Some need ten times that: 5 mg a day.

## Why

Folic acid helps prevent neural tube defects, birth defects of the baby's brain and spine such as spina bifida. When the chance of one is higher, a bigger dose is used.

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says your doctor may recommend 5 mg if you:

- have diabetes
- are living with overweight or obesity
- take anticonvulsant (epilepsy) medicine
- have a family member affected by a neural tube defect
- are at risk of poor food absorption

MotherSafe adds a neural tube defect of your own, a family history on either the mother's or father's side, some antipsychotic medicines, and diabetes from before pregnancy.

For some, MotherSafe says 5 mg should be considered **throughout the pregnancy**: a multiple pregnancy, haemolytic anaemia, a BMI over 30, malabsorption or inflammatory bowel disease.

## What to do

- **If any of these fit you,** ask your doctor whether you need the higher dose.
- **Ask how long to take it.** For some, MotherSafe says it should be considered throughout the pregnancy.
