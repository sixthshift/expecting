Talk to your doctor before changing anything. MotherSafe's advice is that it's often advisable to stay on a regular mental health medicine if it keeps you well. If yours isn't recommended in pregnancy, your doctor can help you switch safely.

## Why

Pregnancy and the time after birth are when the risk of depression and anxiety is highest. Left untreated, they carry significant risk for you and the baby, including a higher risk of miscarriage, low birth weight and prematurity.

Many of these medicines have been studied in pregnancy and haven't been linked with birth differences or other harmful effects. Stopping suddenly isn't advisable: it can cause withdrawal symptoms and a relapse.

Some babies exposed throughout pregnancy are jittery or irritable, or have feeding or breathing issues, just after birth. Most are unaffected, and it's temporary.

## What to do

- **Talk to your GP, psychiatrist or psychologist,** ideally when you're planning a pregnancy, to review your medicines.
- **Don't stop in the third trimester.** It isn't recommended: the risk of depression or anxiety returning is high.
- **Plan to give birth in hospital** if you're taking an antidepressant, so there's extra support for your baby if needed.
- **Skip herbal alternatives.** Herbal medicines, St John's wort among them, are not recommended for depression or anxiety in pregnancy: most haven't been studied enough, and some have dangerous interactions with prescribed medicines.
- **For a specific medicine,** call MotherSafe: Sydney metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848. healthdirect also lists the Medicines Line, on 1300 633 424, for questions about medicines in pregnancy or breastfeeding.
