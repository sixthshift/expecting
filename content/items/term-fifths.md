Fifths above the brim is where your baby's head is in relation to your pelvis, found by examining your tummy. On Queensland Health's Pregnancy Health Record it has its own column in the visit notes, "Descent / Fifths above brim". Your midwife or doctor presses in with flat hands just above your pubic bone to feel how much of the head is still above the brim of your pelvis. It can be tender, and it's usually more relevant from 36 weeks (Peninsula Health's guideline for clinicians). So 3/5 means three fifths of the head can still be felt above the brim.

The head is engaged when its widest part has passed through the brim, which is measured in fifths above the brim. It usually happens from 36 weeks in a first pregnancy, and may not until labour in later ones. On the record, E or Eng means engaged, and NE not engaged.
