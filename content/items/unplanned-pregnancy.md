An unplanned pregnancy can bring confusing, conflicting feelings, sometimes fear and joy at once. That's very common, and an unplanned pregnancy may still be wanted. If you're pregnant, see your doctor as soon as possible to talk about your health and your options.

## Your options

- **Continue the pregnancy and keep the baby,** with a partner or as a single parent.
- **Have the baby and place them for adoption.** Adoption permanently transfers all legal rights and responsibilities to the adoptive parents. Nothing is confirmed until after the birth: you must have pre-adoption counselling before consenting, and after you sign there's usually around 30 days to change your mind. The laws differ between states and territories; a hospital social worker or your state's adoption service is a good place to start.
- **Have the baby and use foster or kinship care,** if you're unsure about adoption and unsure whether you can care for a baby. Foster care is another family caring for your child while you work towards caring for them yourself; kinship care is extended family raising your child. You can still see the baby in both.
- **Have an abortion.** Abortion is safe, common, and doesn't affect your ability to have children in future. It's legal in every state and territory, but the timing laws differ between them.

## Why timing matters

The earlier you talk to a doctor or family planning clinic, the more choices you have. A medical abortion (taking a medicine) is an option up to 9 weeks; a short surgical procedure up to 14 weeks, and a different procedure after that. Later in pregnancy, your doctor may need approval for an abortion after 20 weeks, and in most of Australia abortions after 22 to 24 weeks can only be done for certain reasons and need a second doctor's approval. If your doctor can't help, ask for the name of one who can.

## Making the decision

Sleep on it and don't rush, while remembering that some options depend on the weeks. Explore all your options, be kind to yourself if you feel differently on different days, and look after yourself. The best person to make this decision is you.

It's up to you how much to involve your partner. You are the only person who can consent to or refuse an abortion: **no one can force you to have an abortion or to continue a pregnancy.** Don't let anyone pressure you into a decision you're not happy with. For adoption, both birth parents usually need to consent.

## Who to talk to

Counselling should give you unbiased information and support whatever you decide. Your doctor or local family planning clinic is a good start. You can also call:

- 1800 My Options: 1800 696 784
- Children by Choice (Queensland): 1800 177 725
- MSI Australia: 1300 003 707, to make an appointment
- 1800RESPECT: 1800 737 732, if you've experienced sexual assault or domestic and family violence
- PANDA: 1300 726 306; Lifeline: 13 11 14 for crisis support; Beyond Blue: 1300 22 4636
