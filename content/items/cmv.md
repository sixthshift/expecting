Cytomegalovirus (CMV) is a common virus. Most people who catch it have no symptoms, and about half of adults in Australia have already had it. If you catch it for the first time while pregnant, it can cross the placenta and infect the baby; this is called congenital CMV. It's more likely to pass to the baby in the first half of pregnancy. There's no vaccine, so the way to lower your chance is hygiene.

The national pregnancy care guidelines say everyone who is pregnant should be told how to avoid CMV, early in the pregnancy. Many haven't heard of it: the guidelines put awareness among people who are pregnant at 10% to 50%.

## How it can affect the baby

About 6 in every 1,000 babies in Australia are born with CMV. Most have no symptoms or health problems from it. A small number do. Hearing loss is the most common problem, and it can come on later, in a baby who seemed well at birth. Others are poor vision, learning difficulties, developmental delays, cerebral palsy and seizures. Congenital CMV also raises the risk of miscarriage and stillbirth.

You can pass CMV to the baby after a first infection, when an old infection becomes active again, or by catching a different strain. A first infection in pregnancy is the most likely to pass on.

## How it spreads

CMV is in body fluids: saliva, urine, mucus from the nose, breast milk, vaginal fluid and semen. A common way pregnant people catch it is from the saliva and urine of young children, including their own. A child who has had CMV can carry it in their saliva and urine for years. It can also spread through coughs and sneezes, by touching things with body fluids on them, and through sex.

If you have young children at home or work in childcare, you're more likely to catch it if you haven't had it before.

## Lowering your chance

- **Wash your hands** well with soap and water after changing nappies, wiping noses, feeding children, and handling toys, dummies or anything with body fluids on it.
- **Wear gloves** to change nappies.
- **Don't share** food, cups, cutlery or toothbrushes with young children, and don't put their dummy in your mouth.
- **Don't kiss young children on the lips.**
- **Clean surfaces and toys** that have saliva, urine or mucus on them.

The guidelines say the evidence that these steps work is limited, but they're what's advised.

## Testing

A CMV blood test isn't routine in pregnancy in Australia. The national guidelines say to offer and recommend it:

- **early in pregnancy, if you're at higher risk:** you have young children at home who go to childcare, or you work in childcare
- **if you have symptoms** that could be CMV and aren't explained by another infection
- **if a scan suggests** the baby may have CMV

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby also lists healthcare workers with frequent contact with young children, and anyone who knows they've been exposed to someone with a new CMV infection. Ask your doctor whether a test is right for you. If you're at higher risk, ask at your first visit: the guidelines say as early as possible in the first trimester.

## If you have CMV in pregnancy

You'll be referred to a doctor with expertise in CMV in pregnancy. The national guidelines (December 2025) say there are treatments, such as the antiviral medicine valaciclovir, to lower the chance of the baby being infected, and that they may work better started earlier in pregnancy, which is why early testing and quick referral matter. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby (March 2026) says there's currently no treatment that clearly lowers that risk. Both lead to the same next step: see the specialist promptly and talk through the options.

You may be offered tests to see whether the baby has CMV too. Most babies who test positive during pregnancy won't have long-term problems. Your baby should be tested after the birth, and a baby with CMV may have regular hearing and eye checks.

## When to get help

See your doctor or midwife if you have symptoms that could be CMV, such as a fever, swollen glands, tiredness or a sore throat, and nothing else explains them. Ask whether a CMV test is needed.
