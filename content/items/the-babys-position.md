Late in pregnancy your midwife or doctor will start talking about which way your baby is lying. Three words do most of the work: presentation, position and engagement. People, including health professionals, sometimes mix up the first two, saying "breech position" when they mean breech presentation.

## Presentation: which part comes first

Presentation is the part of your baby facing down towards the birth canal.

- **Cephalic (head first).** Most babies present this way, and most of those are **vertex**: the crown of the head sits at the opening of the birth canal. Rarely a head-first baby is in face or brow presentation, which may not suit a vaginal birth.
- **Breech.** The bottom or feet come first. Breech has its own entry.

## Position: which way the head faces

Position is where the back of your baby's head (the occiput) is in relation to your body:

- **anterior:** the back of the head is at the front of your belly
- **lateral:** it faces your side
- **posterior:** it's towards your back, so your baby faces your belly

Each can be to the left or right. Anterior is the ideal for a vaginal birth, because the smallest part of the head goes down the birth canal first. If your baby stays posterior or lateral in labour, labour may be a little longer, with more back pain; being on your hands and knees may help with the back pain. But even in labour your baby is likely to adjust, and position can change with contractions.

## How it's checked

Your midwife or doctor can usually tell the presentation by feeling your belly, around 36 weeks, and may double-check with a portable ultrasound. In labour they confirm the head's position by feeling your belly, by ultrasound, and sometimes by a vaginal examination, feeling for the ridges and soft spots (sutures and fontanelles) on your baby's head. A vaginal examination also checks where your baby is in your pelvis, called the **station**.

Most babies are head first by 37 weeks; around 3 in 100 are still breech after 37 weeks.

## Engagement

Engagement is your baby's head moving down into your pelvis, a sign they're getting ready to be born. In a first pregnancy it has likely happened by 36 weeks. If you've had a baby before, the head may not engage until you have some contractions. It can happen any time from 37 weeks until the birth.

You may notice your bump has moved down slightly. Breathlessness should ease as your baby moves lower, but you might feel more pressure in your lower tummy and pelvis, and need the toilet more often.
