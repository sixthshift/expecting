This isn't financial advice, and it isn't a sales pitch: it's what Moneysmart, the government's money site, says to think through. For a decision about your own cover, Moneysmart points you to a financial adviser.

## Life cover

Life cover (also called term life or death cover) pays a lump sum when you die, so the people who rely on your income can pay the mortgage, clear debts and cover everyday costs. It helps when others depend on your income: a partner, children or other family. If no one depends on you financially, you may not need it.

**How much.** Add up what your family would **need**: the mortgage, credit cards and other debts, child care, school fees and ongoing living costs. Take away what they would **receive**: super, savings, investments you could sell, your paid leave balance, and support from extended family. The difference is the cover to aim for. Moneysmart's life insurance calculator does the sums.

**Inside or outside super.** You can get life cover through your super fund or directly from an insurer, broker or adviser. Default cover through super can be cheaper and easier, but might not match your needs. Premiums inside super come out of your balance. One of Moneysmart's questions for the fund: will cover end if you stop contributing, or your balance runs out?

## Income protection

Income protection usually replaces a share of your pre-tax income, for example 75% or 90%, if illness or injury stops you working. It can matter most if you don't have much paid leave to fall back on, or rely on your pay for the bills. To work out how much you need, do a budget. Most super funds offer default income protection, so check what you already have.

When comparing, look at:

- **the waiting period** before payments start, usually between 14 days and two years
- **the benefit period**, how long payments last: commonly two or five years, or to an age such as 65
- **minimum work hours**: some policies only cover you if you work, say, 15 hours a week
- **exclusions**, including for pre-existing conditions

## When you apply

Insurers ask about your age, job, medical and family history, smoking and lifestyle. Answer honestly: if you leave out important details, the insurer may change or cancel your cover, or refuse a claim. Read the product disclosure statement (PDS) for what's covered and what isn't.

## Name who gets it

Nominate your beneficiaries. If you haven't, your estate or the super trustee decides where the money goes. ASIC found almost 60% of super members hadn't nominated a beneficiary at all. See [wills and guardianship](wills-and-guardianship).
