A pram is for a baby too young to sit up: it usually keeps them lying flat. A stroller is usually for older babies and toddlers who can sit upright, though many adjust to lie flat too. Most carry a child up to 9 kg. A baby under 6 months should only go in a stroller if it reclines to at least 130°.

## The safety standard

Prams and strollers should meet the Australian Product Safety Standard, which is based on AS/NZS 2088:2000. Look for:

- a 5-point harness that goes around your baby's waist and through their legs
- simple, clearly visible safety warnings and information labels
- a tether strap on the handle
- a strong, balanced frame that won't tip over
- latches and locks so it can't collapse while in use
- a basket that doesn't affect the pram's balance
- one or more parking brakes that are red, clearly visible and out of your child's reach

Beyond safety, think about comfort, how easily it pushes and steers, how it folds (some fit in the car better than others), and sun protection: a mesh canopy with a UPF of 50+ is ideal.

## Buying second-hand

A second-hand or hand-me-down pram should also meet the standard. Check that:

- there are no signs of wear and tear
- the harnesses, brakes and tether strap all work properly
- the safety warning labels are still there and readable
- the set-up instructions are available
- the original maker can repair it if needed

Also check it hasn't been recalled, on the Product Safety Australia recalls list.

## Using it safely

- Always strap your child in.
- Put the brakes on whenever you take your hands off the handle.
- Use the tether strap around your wrist when walking or jogging, and if you run, use a stroller made for running.
- Never leave your baby unsupervised in a pram. It isn't a substitute for a safe cot.
- Don't hang heavy bags on the handles: the pram can tip, especially as you lift your child out. Use the tray underneath.

## In the heat

Don't cover the pram with a dry cloth to keep the sun off. On hot days, it can be up to 4 degrees hotter under a pram canopy than outside, which can be dangerous for your baby. Plastic rain covers also cut airflow and raise humidity, so leave enough ventilation. If your pram has no canopy, you can cover it with a damp muslin, checking and re-wetting it every 20 minutes.
