The nuchal translucency (NT) scan is an ultrasound, usually done between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days. It's offered to everyone, it's optional, and it's safe for you and the baby. It's sometimes called an early structural ultrasound.

**What it looks at.** The sonographer measures the baby's length, to make sure the timing is right, then the small fluid-filled space behind the baby's neck. That measurement helps estimate the chance of Down syndrome and other chromosomal differences. The scan can also find early signs of heart and other structural differences, and help screen for pre-eclampsia. It takes around 20 minutes, longer if the baby is moving a lot. The wand may go on your tummy or inside your vagina.

**The result.** It's a screen, not a diagnosis, reported as low or high risk. If the space is larger than expected, your doctor or midwife may suggest more tests or scans. Sometimes the picture isn't clear and you'll need to come back.

**With other tests.** It's part of combined first-trimester screening, alongside a blood test. If you choose NIPT instead, note that NIPT doesn't include the 11 to 13 week ultrasound: it has to be arranged separately if you decide to have one.

**Cost.** Medicare covers the combined screening blood test, but there may be costs for the ultrasound. Ask before you book.
