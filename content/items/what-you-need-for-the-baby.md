Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says newborns don't need much other than love, milk and warmth. It adds that you don't need to buy everything new: second hand, borrowed and pre-used baby furniture and equipment save money. With a first baby, expect to spend more time and money getting the essentials together.

## The essentials

- **Somewhere to sleep.** The safest place for your baby for the first 6 to 12 months is their own cot or bassinet in the same room as you or another adult caregiver. Whether new or second hand, check that it meets the safety guidelines and has a clean, firm mattress that fits tightly against the sides.
- **A car restraint.** You'll need a baby capsule secured correctly in your car before the baby arrives.
- **A way to feed.** Think about where and how you'll feed. If you're not planning to breastfeed, that means bottles, feeding and sterilising equipment and formula.
- **Nappies and somewhere to change them.** Decide on disposables or cloth, and how you'll store and dispose of them.
- **Clothes.** How many depends on your laundry setup, where you live and the season. All-in-ones, wraps for swaddling, and weather-appropriate singlets, beanies and socks.

## Also worth thinking about

A pram (buy or borrow), a bath or the kitchen sink, towels and washcloths, enough cot sheets to have a spare while one is in the wash, and toiletries. For you: maternity pads for postpartum bleeding, breast pads, comfortable clothing, and a big water bottle. Other things to consider: a baby monitor, dummies, a nappy bag and, if you'll express, a breast pump.

## Not gear

Cook and freeze meals, practise online grocery ordering, accept offers of food drops, and make a list of emergency and support numbers while you're calm.
