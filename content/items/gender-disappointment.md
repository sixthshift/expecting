Imagining your child, the things you'll do together and how they might look is part of becoming a parent, and it's hard not to imagine their gender too. The Gidget Foundation says having a preference is common, and gender disappointment is the distress some parents feel when their child's sex isn't what they'd hoped. It can happen to any parent, and at any stage: at conception, during pregnancy or at the birth. It's real, but can be hard to talk about.

For some, the feeling is temporary. For others it's unsettling and lasts. Many feel shame or fear of being judged, or guilt that they should be more grateful. The Gidget Foundation says the experience doesn't mean you won't be a good parent or can't love your child. It says early testing and gender reveal parties may make it harder to come to terms with, because you start to build a relationship with the pregnancy much earlier.

## What can help

The Gidget Foundation suggests:

- Remind yourself you can be happy about being pregnant and disappointed at the same time.
- Find a safe person to talk to, and allow yourself space to grieve the child you imagined. This doesn't take away from the child you'll have.
- Give yourself time. Bonds take time to develop, and with time and talking it through, the distress will most likely lessen as you get to know your newborn.
- If you're pregnant, decide where and when you want to find out the sex. For some, finding out at the birth helps; for others, working through feelings beforehand does.
- Try journalling or something creative, and take a break from social media, which can feed the cycle of loss.

## If you're supporting someone

Be supportive, validate their feelings, give them time, and avoid "but at least..." statements.

## Getting help

The Gidget Foundation provides free individual psychological therapy for expectant, new and potential parents, in person or by telehealth, up to 10 individual sessions per calendar year, subject to Medicare eligibility. It isn't a crisis service.
