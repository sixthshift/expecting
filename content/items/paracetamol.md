Yes. Paracetamol is the recommended treatment for pain or fever in pregnancy when used as directed. It's pregnancy category A in Australia: it has been taken by a large number of pregnant women without any proven increase in malformations or other harm to the baby.

## Why

Untreated pain and fever can themselves pose risks to the baby. A sustained fever above 38.9 °C for at least 24 hours has been linked with miscarriage and malformations such as spina bifida early in pregnancy, and stillbirth later.

**About the autism and ADHD claim.** In September 2025 the US Administration said paracetamol in pregnancy may be linked to autism and ADHD. Australia's Chief Medical Officer and the TGA, with other medicines regulators worldwide, rejected the claim: robust evidence shows no causal link, and several large studies directly contradict it. If you're worried, talk it over with your doctor or midwife.

## What to do

- **Lowest effective dose, shortest time.** The recommended dose is 2 × 500 mg tablets every 4 to 6 hours, and no more than 8 × 500 mg tablets (4 g) in 24 hours.
- **Watch for doubling up.** While you're taking regular paracetamol, don't take other medicines that contain it, such as cold and flu tablets.
- **With a fever,** drink plenty of fluids and rest, and contact your doctor if the fever goes on.
- **If pain keeps going,** see your doctor about the best way to manage it.
