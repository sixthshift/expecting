Some, if the trip calls for them.

## Why

The Australian Immunisation Handbook recommends non-live vaccines in pregnancy when the benefits of protection outweigh the risks. That includes:

- **Typhoid:** the injectable (Vi polysaccharide) vaccine if you're travelling to countries where water quality and sanitation are poor. The oral (live) typhoid vaccine is not for pregnancy.
- **Hepatitis A:** if you're travelling to areas where it's moderately to highly common.
- **Japanese encephalitis:** the inactivated vaccine (JEspect) if you're at high risk; the live one (Imojev) is not for pregnancy.
- **Rabies:** if needed.

Live vaccines, such as MMR and chickenpox, are not given in pregnancy.

**Yellow fever** is a live vaccine and isn't recommended in pregnancy. Avoid rural areas where yellow fever is common. If travel to one is truly unavoidable, the vaccine may be given after weighing the risks and benefits with your doctor, along with ways to avoid mosquito bites.

## What to do

- **See your doctor at least 6 weeks, and preferably 12 weeks, before you go,** and tell them you're pregnant. Talk to your GP and, if needed, a travel doctor.
- **Or change plans:** avoiding the disease is another way to lower the risk.
- **Planning a pregnancy?** These vaccines are ideally given before.
