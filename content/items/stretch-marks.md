## What's usual

Around 9 in 10 pregnant people develop stretch marks, most often on the abdomen but also the thighs, breasts, hips and upper arms. They're red, pink or purple lines, sometimes itchy at first, and often appear in the third trimester as your tummy grows. They're more likely if you gain weight quickly. They won't disappear after the birth, but they fade over time to silver or white and become less noticeable.

There are no skin products scientifically proven to prevent them. You can massage in oils or creams that are safe in pregnancy; it won't stop them but can make your skin feel soft. Stretch marks aren't harmful, so you don't need to treat them. Prescription vitamin A creams such as tretinoin can harm your baby and should not be used in pregnancy or while breastfeeding. Check with your doctor, midwife or pharmacist before using any cream.

Sometimes a very itchy rash starts in or around stretch marks on your tummy. This is called polymorphic eruption of pregnancy; it affects 1 in 160 pregnancies and goes away 4 to 6 weeks after the birth.

## Mention it at your next visit

If you're concerned about stretch marks, talk to your doctor or midwife at your next antenatal appointment.
