Ask before you fast. If you're pregnant, it's important to check with your doctor or midwife in the lead-up to a religious fast.

## Why

Research on the long-term effects of fasting is limited, and more is needed to know whether fasting in pregnancy is safe for you and your baby. Some studies have found it doesn't affect the baby's birth weight or raise the chance of a preterm birth. Others suggest it may lower amniotic fluid levels or affect the baby's wellbeing.

Fasting can also cause dehydration and fatigue in pregnancy. Many doctors don't recommend fasting in the second or third trimester.

The rules vary by religion and by your own health, and many religions that include fasting allow exceptions or adjustments for people who are pregnant or breastfeeding. Your religious leader can advise on that side.

## What to do

- **Talk to your doctor or midwife first,** especially if you have a medical issue or a complication such as gestational diabetes. Ask too when you should break your fast, such as if you feel unwell.
- **If you do fast, plan ahead.** Drink plenty of water before and after the fast, and keep your diet balanced and nutritious.
- **If you feel light-headed or dizzy** while fasting, at any time in the pregnancy, contact a healthcare professional.
