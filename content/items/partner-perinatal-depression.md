## What's usual

Pregnancy and the year after put the partner at higher risk too. Depression affects about 1 in 10 fathers from the first trimester through the baby's first year. Anxiety affects about 1 in 6 during the pregnancy and around 1 in 5 after the birth. The sources mostly talk about dads, but this is about any partner who isn't carrying.

Mixed, complicated feelings about becoming a parent are completely normal. Depression or anxiety is more than that: feelings that are more persistent and more severe than you'd expect. In partners it can look like:

- being short-tempered, resentful, frustrated or angry
- withdrawing from people
- using alcohol or drugs to escape or cope
- physical stress, such as headaches and muscle tension
- feeling sad, low, flat or numb
- worries you can't stop, feeling restless or tense, a racing heart or tight chest
- not sleeping, even when the baby is settled

These signs are often missed, or dismissed as a normal part of life with a new baby. Noticing them, in yourself or in your partner, is how your family gets help.

PANDA says men can find it harder to reach out, because of shame, cultural stigma and other barriers. It's nothing to be ashamed of.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 000. If you're having thoughts of self-harm or suicide, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, 24/7.

## See your doctor or midwife

If you notice these signs, talk to your GP. Be as honest as you can. You can also call the PANDA helpline on 1300 726 306. Almost 1 in 10 callers are men, and there are male counsellors if you'd rather talk to another bloke.
