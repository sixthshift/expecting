Perineal massage is a technique to help stretch the perineum, the space between the opening of the vagina and the anus. It may reduce your risk of tears or other perineal trauma during birth, especially with a first birth, and can lower your chance of needing an episiotomy. It's your choice, and some people choose not to.

## When and how often

Most people can start around week 34 or 35. Ask your doctor or midwife when to start. Do it once or twice a week, up to when your baby is born, for up to 5 minutes a session. Doing it more often than that is usually not recommended.

## How to do it

You can do it lying down, seated, standing or in the shower, and you or your partner can do it.

- Empty your bladder, wash your hands well and find a relaxing place. It helps to have a warm bath or shower, or a warm compress on the perineum, first.
- Use a water-soluble vaginal lubricant or a natural oil such as sweet almond oil. Avoid nut-based oil if you have known allergies.
- Gently insert your thumbs or 1 to 2 fingers 3 to 5 cm into your vagina.
- Press gently downwards (towards your anus) and to the sides.
- You may feel a burning sensation, which may lessen over time. Hold the stretch for up to 2 minutes, then release.

It may feel uncomfortable at first and get easier with practice, more comfortable after 2 to 3 weeks. It shouldn't cause you much discomfort or any pain. If your partner helps, they should have clean hands and be guided by you.

## When not to do it

Don't massage your perineum if you:

- are less than 34 weeks pregnant
- have a low-lying placenta (placenta praevia) or a short cervix
- are bleeding from the vagina
- have an infection such as thrush or herpes
- have severe blood pressure problems during your pregnancy
- have an open cut or infection on your hands or fingers

If you've had previous perineal surgery or your waters have broken, speak with your maternity care provider before starting.
