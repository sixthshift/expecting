Yes. Exercise is healthy in pregnancy: it can ease back and pelvic pain, help prepare your body for birth, and lower your risk of complications such as gestational diabetes. It's best to talk to your doctor or midwife before you start.

## Why

**How much.** The sources differ. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you can try to be active each day of the week, with strengthening exercises twice a week. Better Health Channel says at least 30 minutes of moderate activity on most, if not all, days. The Royal Women's Hospital suggests a session on most days or 150 minutes over a week. RANZCOG says 20 to 30 minutes, four to five times a week.

**How hard.** Your resting heart rate rises in pregnancy, so don't use a target heart rate. You're at a good level when you can talk normally but can't sing. Don't increase the intensity of your sport while pregnant.

**What to avoid:**

- contact sports, and anything you could fall from, such as cycling, skiing or horse riding
- scuba diving and skydiving
- getting too hot: hot yoga or Pilates, spas, or hard exercise on hot or humid days
- exercising lying flat on your back: the Royal Women's Hospital says it's best to avoid it after 16 weeks; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says lying flat on your back, particularly after 28 weeks, can be dangerous; Better Health Channel lists it among exercises to avoid, without a week

## What to do

- **New to exercise?** Start small, with 15-minute sessions, and build up.
- **Don't exercise with a fever** or when you're unwell. Drink plenty of water.
- **Check with your doctor first** if you have pre-eclampsia, placenta praevia, twins or more, bleeding in the second or third trimester, a weak or short cervix, or your waters have broken.
- **Stop and see your doctor immediately** if you have chest pain, dizziness or feeling faint, headache, calf pain or swelling, sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet, vaginal bleeding, contractions, fluid leaking, or a change in your baby's movements.
