If you're already pregnant and only just took out private health insurance, or upgraded it, it may not pay for this pregnancy. Here's how to tell, and what your options are.

## The 12-month wait

When you start a new policy or increase your level of cover, you have to serve waiting periods before you can claim benefits at the new level. The maximum waiting period for pregnancy and birth is 12 months. The pregnant parent needs to have served the full waiting period before being admitted to hospital to claim benefits.

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says most policies have a 12-month waiting period for pregnancy, and during it you can't claim any pregnancy-related health expenses. For private obstetric care, you'd need to have bought or upgraded relevant cover at least 12 months before becoming pregnant, or pay for private care yourself.

If the baby is due inside the waiting period, that leaves public care, which everyone with a Medicare number can get in a public hospital or birth centre, fully covered by Medicare, or paying privately.

## Does your policy cover pregnancy at all?

Hospital policies come in four tiers: Gold, Silver, Bronze and Basic. Pregnancy and birth is a minimum requirement only for Gold. Silver and Bronze policies may include it as an option, and Basic policies only on a restricted basis. Many less expensive covers don't include obstetrics, or pay restricted benefits that cover you only as a private patient in a public hospital. Check the policy carefully, and check with your fund.

## Why people hold cover anyway

- **The rebate.** Most people are eligible for the Private Health Insurance Rebate on their premiums.
- **Lifetime Health Cover loading.** If you take out hospital cover after the 1 July following your 31st birthday, you pay a loading on top of your premium, which increases for every year you're over 30.
- **The Medicare Levy Surcharge.** If you don't have private hospital cover and earn above a certain income threshold, you may have to pay it when you lodge your tax return.

## Cover for the baby

Covering the baby is a separate question. If you're on a single membership, or a dependant on a family policy, you'll need to move to a family or single parent membership for your newborn to be covered. Funds have different rules about how far ahead you need to change, and some ask for months; contact your insurer.
