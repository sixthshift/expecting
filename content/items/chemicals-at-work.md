Talk to your employer. If your work involves chemicals, they must carry out a risk assessment that takes your pregnancy into account, and by law make work safe for you and your baby. You're more likely to need changes if you work with chemical or biological hazards.

## Why

Chemicals you breathe in or swallow can enter your bloodstream and pass to your baby through the placenta. Large exposures can lead to miscarriage, stillbirth, premature labour, low birth weight and problems with your baby's development. Most everyday exposures are too small to matter; it's generally long-term exposure to large amounts that harms.

Some exposures to know about: some pesticides and herbicides are known to affect developing babies, and lead (in batteries and lead-based spray paint, for example) can cross the placenta, with no safe level.

## What to do

- **Raise it early** with your manager, HR or union representative. You may need to tell them sooner than you'd planned; you can do it confidentially.
- **Reduce exposure**: for hairdressing work, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you can lower your exposure by opening windows, using gloves, washing your hands before eating, and storing and disposing of chemicals safely.
- **Don't rely on a mask.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says masks may not fully protect you from breathing in chemicals, so good ventilation is still important.
- **If your usual job isn't safe**, you can move to a safe job at the same pay, or take no safe job leave if there isn't one.
- **Worried about an exposure?** Call the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26.
