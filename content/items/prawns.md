## Why

Chilled cooked prawns, the cold, ready-to-eat kind, are on the list of higher-risk foods for listeria. The NSW Food Authority rates ready-to-eat chilled peeled prawns "do not eat (high risk)", because the many stages of processing raise the chance of contamination.

Mercury isn't the worry. Crustacea, including prawns, lobsters and crabs, generally contain lower levels of mercury than fish, and they aren't eaten often enough to be a significant source of it, though large amounts eaten regularly may add to your intake.

## What to do

- Have prawns freshly cooked and eaten hot.
- If you buy cooked prawns, reheat them thoroughly until steaming hot.
- Cooked fish and seafood should be cooked to at least 63°C and eaten hot.
- Leftovers: into the fridge, and use within a day of cooking.
