Despite the name, morning sickness — nausea, with or without vomiting — can happen at any time of day. It's most common between about 6 and 14 weeks, and for many people it settles by the second trimester, though it can last to about 14 to 20 weeks, and for some people right through the pregnancy.

It ranges from mild queasiness to something much harder. When it's very severe it's called **hyperemesis gravidarum**: it becomes hard to keep enough food and fluids down, and it can lead to dehydration and weight loss.

If you can't keep any fluids down, go to your local emergency department: they can give you fluids through a drip. For vomiting that's severe or won't stop, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away: see [Can't keep anything down](severe-vomiting).

## Medicines

If the usual ways of settling it don't help, there are medicines to try. MotherSafe, the NSW medicines in pregnancy service, says the first medicine to try is doxylamine with pyridoxine (vitamin B6) tablets. Doxylamine is the first-line medicine for nausea in pregnancy in Australia and worldwide, because it works and has a strong safety record.

- **Where to get it.** Doxylamine (Restavit, Dozile) is sold at pharmacies as a sleeping aid. Some packs say not to take it if you're pregnant; MotherSafe says that warning isn't evidence based. Ask the pharmacist for tablets you can break in half, and check the dose with them or your doctor.
- **The pyridoxine** should be 25 mg tablets: higher strengths may be a problem in pregnancy. Don't take more than the recommended dose, and see your doctor if symptoms persist.
- **It can make you drowsy.**
- **If it doesn't help,** see your doctor: there's a range of prescription medicines that are safe in pregnancy. You can also call MotherSafe on 9382 6539 (Sydney) or 1800 647 848 (rest of NSW), weekdays 9am to 5pm.

## If you had it last time

Many people who had nausea and vomiting in their first pregnancy have it again in later ones, and MotherSafe says managing it as early as possible may make it less severe. If it was hard last time, talk to your doctor or midwife early.
