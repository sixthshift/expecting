A late miscarriage is the loss of a baby between 13 and 20 weeks of pregnancy. It's much less common than an early miscarriage, 1 to 2 in 100, and can be a shock. Often the cause can't be found, but it is unlikely to be anything you did.

Some people have bleeding, cramping or pain in the lower tummy or back; others have no symptoms and find out at a routine check.

## Why you give birth

After a late miscarriage you will usually need to go through labour and birth. Your doctor may recommend medicine to start labour; After 15 weeks this is usually one medicine (mifepristone), then another (misoprostol) 48 hours later. In some cases a surgical procedure is recommended instead. Contractions are painful but likely less intense and shorter than at full term. It's usually managed in hospital.

You'll likely give birth in a birth suite or labour ward, where other babies are being born. Tell your care team what you need: they may be able to offer a more private space or extra support.

## Your choices

Nothing is required; it's always your choice.

- **Seeing and holding your baby.** You'll usually be offered the chance. Many parents find it helps them grieve; others decide not to. Staff can describe your baby first if that helps.
- **Time and memories:** time with your baby with family, photos, hand and footprints, and cultural or religious rituals.
- **Burial or cremation:** most hospitals can arrange a respectful cremation, or you can make private arrangements. Tell the staff your wishes.
- **Tests:** your doctor may recommend tests, sometimes a post-mortem with your consent.

## Recovering at home

You may bleed for up to 2 weeks; use pads, not tampons. Pregnancy symptoms will go, and if you were close to 20 weeks your breasts may make milk, which can be very distressing; medicine may be offered to suppress it. Paracetamol, rest and support help. See your doctor 4 to 6 weeks afterwards.

## When to get help

**Call 000 or go to emergency immediately** if you're soaking 1 or more pads in an hour, bleeding you can't contain, or passing clots the size of a golf ball; or you have severe tummy pain, shoulder-tip pain, smelly discharge, a fever or chills, or feel faint, dizzy or unwell.

After you're home, seek medical help straight away for ongoing bleeding, fever or severe tummy pain; see your doctor if bleeding and pain continue 2 weeks on.

## Grief

Sadness, guilt, grief, anger and numbness are all valid, and you and your partner may grieve differently. Talk to your doctor, who can refer you on; Beyond Blue is on 1300 22 4636 and Medicare Mental Health on 1800 595 212. In crisis, call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 if anyone is at immediate risk.
