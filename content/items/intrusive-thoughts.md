## What's usual

Research suggests more than 80% of expectant and new parents have intrusive thoughts from time to time. They're images, thoughts or impulses that are negative, unwanted and often upsetting. They can be fleeting or keep coming back, and violent, sexual or disturbing. For new parents they can be about harming, or seeing harm come to, the baby or others.

**Having an intrusive thought doesn't mean you'll act on it.** The thoughts usually feel out of character and make no sense to you, and the distress they cause is itself a strong sign they're driven by anxiety, not something more serious. They can make you feel like you're going crazy, but you're not.

What can help: label it as an intrusive thought, a symptom of anxiety and not who you are; let it pass rather than pushing it away; distract yourself if it's too much; and talk about it with people you trust.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

**If at any time you feel you or your baby are not safe, call 000 or have someone take you to an emergency department.**

**If your thoughts feel out of your control, or you can't manage them, contact your healthcare provider immediately or call 000.**

If people have told you your thoughts worry them, but the thoughts seem real to you or make sense when others' don't, tell someone close to you how you're feeling and that it's an emergency.

For 24-hour crisis support, call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

## See your doctor or midwife

In more severe cases, intrusive thoughts can be part of perinatal depression and anxiety, and persistent, obsessive thoughts can be a sign of obsessive-compulsive disorder. If that sounds like you, or someone close to you, seek professional support. The Gidget Foundation (1300 851 758) offers free ongoing specialist counselling to expectant and new parents (up to 10 individual sessions per calendar year, subject to Medicare eligibility).
