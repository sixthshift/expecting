Yes, if you don't eat enough omega-3 from food. If your diet already has enough, MotherSafe says taking a supplement has no demonstrated benefit for you or your baby.

## Why

Omega-3 fats (DHA and EPA) support your baby's brain, nerve and eye development. Your body can't make them, and fatty fish such as sardines, salmon, mackerel, herring and trout are the best source. Flaxseed and canola oil contain some omega-3, but not DHA or EPA.

Enough omega-3 in pregnancy is linked with fewer premature births when you're carrying one baby, so a supplement is likely to lower that risk if your diet is short. A recent review found no benefit for the baby's IQ or development.

Some data suggest too much omega-3 may increase the risk of a premature birth.

## What to do

- **If you need a supplement,** the recommended dose is 1000 mg a day in total, from before 20 weeks. You can stop at the birth.
- **Vegetarian?** Oil from algae is a plant source of DHA and EPA.
- **Not cod liver oil.** It also contains vitamin A, so it should be avoided in pregnancy.
- **Check the label.** Fish oil isn't a major source of mercury, but make sure the one you take is low in mercury and other contaminants.
- **Not sure?** Your midwife or doctor may suggest omega-3 if you're low.
