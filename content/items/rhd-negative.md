Your blood group is checked at the start of pregnancy. The "negative" in a group like A negative means you're RhD negative: your red blood cells don't carry the D protein. Around 17 in every 100 people in Australia are RhD negative, and on its own it isn't usually a problem.

## Why it matters in pregnancy

If you're RhD negative and the baby is RhD positive (possible if the other parent is positive), a little of the baby's blood getting into yours can make your immune system produce antibodies against it. This is called a sensitising event. The antibodies usually won't affect your first pregnancy, but your immune system remembers, and in a future pregnancy they can cause serious complications for your next baby: severe anaemia, brain damage and even death in some cases. This is haemolytic disease of the newborn. It's rare these days because anti-D injections can usually prevent it.

## The plan

- **Anti-D at 28 and 34 weeks.** You may be offered an injection of anti-D immunoglobulin, a blood product made from plasma. It's safe for you and the baby.
- **After the birth.** The baby's cord blood is tested, and if the baby is RhD positive, you may be offered another dose.
- **RhD NIPT.** From 11 weeks, a blood test can predict, with more than 99% accuracy, whether the baby is RhD negative or positive. If the baby is negative, you might not need anti-D injections. Ask your doctor or midwife whether it's available to you. If you can't get it, you'll still be offered anti-D.

## Sensitising events

Anti-D may also be recommended after:

- CVS or amniocentesis
- a miscarriage, or a termination at 10 weeks or more
- heavy, frequent bleeding in pregnancy
- an injury or accident to your abdomen
- turning a breech baby (external cephalic version)
- an ectopic or molar pregnancy

Anti-D should be given within 72 hours of the event, though it can still be given after that.

There isn't enough evidence that bleeding in the first 12 weeks needs anti-D. But if the bleeding happens again, is heavy, or comes with stomach pain or a serious injury to the pelvis, anti-D may be recommended.

## When to get help

Always speak to your doctor or midwife if you have any bleeding in pregnancy, and tell them you're RhD negative.
