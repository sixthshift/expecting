## What's usual

Pelvic girdle pain (PGP), once called symphysis pubis dysfunction (SPD), is pain in the bones and joints of your pelvis. It's very common, can start at any time but usually between 14 and 30 weeks, and ranges from mild discomfort to severe pain. It can spread to your lower back, groin, bottom, hips, thighs or legs, and may get worse when you walk fast or a long way, get in or out of a car, rolling over in bed, on stairs or standing up.

It's not dangerous for you or your baby. It usually goes without treatment within 3 months of the birth, though symptoms may return in future pregnancies.

Changes that help:

- Wear low-heeled shoes, and dress sitting down.
- Take stairs one step at a time, take smaller steps, and avoid long periods of walking or standing.
- Sleep on your side with a pillow between your legs; to get up, keep your knees together and push up with your arms.
- Don't cross your legs, and keep your knees together standing up or getting in and out of the car.
- Rest, a heat or ice pack, and paracetamol if needed.
- A fitted pelvic support garment, worn for short periods.

Extended bed rest without a doctor's guidance isn't recommended.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor **immediately** if you:

- have pain that comes on without movement
- have an unexpected fever
- have bleeding or fluid leaking from your vagina
- are losing weight without trying
- have trouble controlling your bladder or bowels
- have numbness or weakness in your legs
- have had a fall or an injury

If you have back, pelvic or hip pain, see your doctor to check it's PGP. They may refer you to a physiotherapist.
