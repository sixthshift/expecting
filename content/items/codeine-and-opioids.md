Talk to your doctor. Opioids such as codeine, oxycodone and morphine are stronger painkillers for moderate to severe pain. If paracetamol isn't enough or isn't suitable, KEMH says short-term use of some opioids is considered safe in pregnancy, and MotherSafe says short-term or infrequent use may be considered. KEMH says you need to consult your doctor if you need one. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says codeine is not found in over-the-counter medicines.

## Why

Used as your healthcare provider recommends, opioids haven't been linked with a higher risk of birth defects or pregnancy loss.

The concerns are with regular use:

- **Withdrawal in the newborn.** Regular use of any opioid in the last trimester may raise the chance of withdrawal symptoms in the baby after birth.
- **Dependence.** Taken consistently, opioids can become less effective, and you can become dependent on them.
- **Side effects** for you include dizziness, nausea and vomiting, confusion, constipation and drowsiness.

## What to do

- **Start with paracetamol.** It's generally considered the safest medicine for short-term pain in pregnancy. For headaches, Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says your doctor may prescribe codeine for short-term use if paracetamol doesn't work.
- **If you're taking opioids regularly,** tell your healthcare providers, and make sure the baby's team knows, so they can check for withdrawal symptoms after the birth.
- **After the birth,** codeine can make a breastfed baby sleepy. If your baby becomes sleepier than usual, stop the codeine and speak to your doctor.
