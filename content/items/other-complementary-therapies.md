It depends on the therapy. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says some complementary therapies have a strong evidence base and others have less, and that therapies not proven to help may also be harmful if they delay or get in the way of necessary treatment.

## Why

- **Hypnotherapy:** limited research on benefits; it may help with managing pain during birth. You can safely practise it during pregnancy and birth.
- **TENS:** small electric pulses to relieve pain, considered safe in all 3 trimesters.
- **Reflexology:** limited research on safety and benefits. Some studies show it is probably effective and safe for labour pain, duration of labour and anxiety in pregnancy, but strong supportive evidence isn't available. Ask your doctor or midwife first.
- **Naturopathy:** limited research on safety and any benefits. Ask your doctor or midwife first.
- **Homeopathy:** no reliable evidence it works for any health condition.
- **Biofeedback:** may help you adjust your physical responses in labour, but there is limited high-quality evidence it reduces labour pain.

## What to do

Ask the practitioner about their qualifications, whether there is evidence the treatment works, whether it is safe at your stage of pregnancy, the risks, and the cost. Check AHPRA to see whether the practitioner is registered. Some therapies, including reflexology, Bowen therapy, kinesiology, naturopathy, homeopathy and aromatherapy, are no longer claimable on private health insurance.
