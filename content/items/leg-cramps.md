## What's usual

Leg cramps are sudden, painful tightening of a muscle, usually in your calf but sometimes your thigh or foot. They affect up to 3 in 10 pregnant people, usually at night, and are more common in the second and third trimesters. Nobody fully understands why.

During a cramp:

- pull your toes hard up towards the front of your ankle to stretch the muscle
- rub the muscle firmly, or ask your partner to massage it
- walk around
- if the muscle is still sore afterwards, paracetamol can help

Nothing prevents cramps completely, but these might help: stretch your calves before bed, drink plenty of water, avoid stretching your leg with your toes pointed, have a warm bath before bed and eat a balanced diet.

Some people take magnesium or calcium supplements for cramps. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says there's no clear evidence they work; the Royal Women's Hospital says studies of calcium have shown no improvement or relief. Talk to your doctor or midwife before trying any supplement.

## Contact your hospital now

Leg pain can sometimes be a sign of a blood clot. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have pain or swelling in one leg, pain that doesn't go away, or your leg is red or warm.

## See your doctor or midwife

If cramps are bothering you, or they're persistent and not going away, talk to your doctor or midwife.
