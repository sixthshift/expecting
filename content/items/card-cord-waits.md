Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says cord clamping usually happens after the cord has stopped pulsating, usually about a minute after birth, which lets your baby receive more blood from the placenta and may reduce the chance of iron deficiency later. Cutting the cord isn't painful for you or your baby. Your midwife or doctor may need to cut it immediately if you or your baby is unwell. If you'd like to keep the cord connected for longer, or your birth partner wants to cut it, tell your midwife in advance and put it in your birth preferences.
