Listeria infection is rare, but in pregnancy it can cause miscarriage, stillbirth or premature birth. Unlike most food bugs, listeria keeps growing in the fridge. Cooking it to 75°C kills it.

What Food Standards Australia New Zealand and the NSW Food Authority say to avoid, unless it's cooked until steaming hot and eaten straight away:

- **Soft and surface-ripened cheeses:** brie, camembert, ricotta, feta, blue cheese.
- **Deli meats:** ham, salami, and other ready-to-eat sliced meats.
- **Cold cooked chicken**, pâté, and anything cooked then chilled for a while.
- **Chilled seafood:** smoked salmon, raw oysters, sushi and sashimi, cold cooked prawns.
- **Pre-cut fruit and pre-made salads**, rockmelon, bean sprouts, and soft-serve ice cream.

The one rule that covers most of it: freshly cooked and eaten hot is safe. Keep the fridge at 5°C or colder, and eat leftovers within a day.

Symptoms can be mild and flu-like, and can start anywhere from 3 days to 10 weeks after eating. If you have a fever after eating something on the list, call your midwife or GP.

This holds for the whole pregnancy.
