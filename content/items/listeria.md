Listeria infection is rare, but in pregnancy it can cause miscarriage, stillbirth or premature birth. Unlike most food bugs, listeria keeps growing in the fridge. Cooking or reheating it until steaming hot (75°C) kills it.

What Food Standards Australia New Zealand says to avoid, unless it's cooked until steaming hot and eaten straight away:

- **Soft and surface-ripened cheeses:** brie, camembert, ricotta, feta, blue cheese.
- **Deli meats:** ham, salami, and other ready-to-eat sliced meats.
- **Cold cooked chicken**, and refrigerated pâté or meat spreads.
- **Chilled seafood:** smoked salmon, raw oysters, sushi and sashimi, cold cooked prawns.
- **Pre-cut fruit and pre-made salads**, rockmelon, raw seed sprouts, soft-serve ice cream, and unpasteurised milk and fruit juice.

The one rule that covers most of it: freshly cooked and eaten hot is safe. Keep the fridge at 5°C or colder, and eat leftovers within a day.

Symptoms can be mild and flu-like, and can start anywhere from 3 days to 10 weeks after eating. If you have a fever or flu-like symptoms after eating something on the list, contact your doctor or midwife straight away: symptoms in pregnancy may seem mild, but it's important to get checked immediately.

This holds for the whole pregnancy.
