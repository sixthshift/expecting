Breech means your baby is lying bottom or feet first, rather than head down (cephalic). It is very common earlier in pregnancy; most babies turn head down before the last month. Near the due date, only about 3 in 100 are still breech.

Your doctor or midwife checks your baby's position by feeling your tummy at around 36 weeks, with an ultrasound to confirm if needed. If your waters break and you have been told that your baby is not head-first, seek medical help immediately by calling triple zero (000) and asking for an ambulance. If your waters break when your baby is not head-first, there is also a higher risk of cord prolapse, which is an emergency.
