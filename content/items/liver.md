Best avoided. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid liver and liver products because they contain a lot of vitamin A, which can harm your baby. Further down the same page it puts it more gently, "try to limit how much you eat".

## Why

Too much vitamin A, also called retinol, can harm a growing baby. Extra vitamin A from supplements might increase the chance of birth defects, and supplements shouldn't give you more than 10,000 international units a day in pregnancy. It's also best to avoid eating foods that are very high in vitamin A, which might include liver and pâté.

## What to do

- **Skip liver, lamb's fry and chicken livers**, and liver products such as pâté.
- **Get your vitamin A from everyday foods.** The best way to get enough in pregnancy is from eggs, dairy, orange and yellow vegetables, green leafy vegetables, oily fish such as salmon, and meat.
- **Check supplements.** Vitamin A is one of the vitamins it's best not to take as a supplement in pregnancy. Check the label or ask your pharmacist, midwife or doctor if a pregnancy multivitamin is suitable for you.
- **Already eaten some?** If you're worried about something you've eaten, call your maternity care provider or doctor.
