A short cervix means a cervical length of less than 25 mm. Your doctor can find it on a routine pregnancy ultrasound, usually between weeks 16 and 24.

It can increase your risk of labour and birth before 37 weeks. Your doctor may recommend treatment to reduce that risk, and will monitor your cervix closely through the pregnancy. For a cervix of 10 to 25 mm, the national pregnancy care guidelines recommend vaginal progesterone, started as soon as possible between 16 and 24 weeks and continued until 34 to 36 weeks; for a cervix under 10 mm, a stitch in the cervix (cerclage) may be offered.

If you are less than 37 weeks and notice any signs of labour, contact your midwife, doctor or nearest birthing centre immediately.
