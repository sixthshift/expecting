Yes. ARPANSA, the national radiation safety agency, says there's no established evidence of any health effects from the electric and magnetic fields typically produced around computers, or from the wireless devices that go with them, and that the fields from laptops and tablets are very low. It doesn't mention pregnancy. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it is safe to work while you are pregnant unless your doctor tells you it is unsafe, though you and your employer may need to make some adjustments depending on your job.

## Why

ARPANSA's review of 18 studies looked at radiofrequency electromagnetic fields (the kind from mobile phones and base stations, and in some jobs) and pregnancy outcomes. Overall it found no or little association between maternal exposure and pregnancy outcomes, though the evidence was low to very low certainty. In the general-public studies it did find an association with small for gestational age (RR 1.13). That review is about radiofrequency exposure, not computer screens or laptops as such.

## What to do

- **Take regular breaks** if you can, and get plenty of rest at home.
- **Expect more aches in your hands and arms.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says carpal tunnel syndrome is more likely in pregnancy.
- **Ask about posture.** You can talk to your employer about help with correct posture and moving at work.
- **If something at work makes you feel dizzy, sore or unwell,** stop doing it and get medical advice from your doctor or midwife.
