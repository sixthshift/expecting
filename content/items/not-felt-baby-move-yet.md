## What's usual

You'll usually start to feel your baby move by about 20 weeks, or earlier if this isn't your first pregnancy. The first movements are often described as fluttering, like butterflies, or bubbling, swishing, rolling or tiny kicks. They're easy to miss at first, and they get more noticeable as the weeks go on. By about 28 weeks the movements should be regular.

Where the placenta sits makes a difference. With an anterior placenta, on the front wall of the uterus, movements tend to be felt at around 20 weeks and may not feel as strong. With a posterior placenta, on the back wall, they're often felt earlier and may feel stronger.

Once you can feel the baby regularly, get to know their own pattern.

## Contact your hospital now

At any point, if you're concerned about your baby's movement pattern or notice the movements slowing down, contact your midwife, hospital birth unit or doctor immediately. Don't wait until the next day. If you can't reach your maternity care provider, go to your nearest hospital birthing or emergency unit. See [The baby is moving less](baby-moving-less).

## See your doctor or midwife

If you haven't felt your baby move by 24 weeks, contact your doctor or midwife.
