Skin-to-skin contact means holding your undressed baby (in just a nappy, and maybe a hat) on your bare chest, with a blanket or your clothes wrapped around you both for warmth. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's encouraged in the first hour after birth, and many maternity hospitals encourage it straight after the birth if both you and the baby are well.

## What it does

It can help promote bonding, help with breastfeeding, keep your baby warm, and stabilise their heartbeat and breathing. The Australian Breastfeeding Association says straight after birth your baby can be placed directly on your bare chest and stay there until after the first breastfeed, giving them time to use their natural instincts to move towards the breast and begin the first feed.

## After a caesarean

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you should still be able to have skin-to-skin contact after delivery. The Association says to ask for your baby to be placed on your chest as soon as possible, and that many hospitals do this as standard practice.

## The partner can do it

If you need medical care, your partner can give skin-to-skin contact until you're well enough.

## It keeps going

If you missed it straight after the birth, the Association says it's never too late. Skin-to-skin at any time can trigger your baby's feeding instincts. Keep your baby skin-to-skin as much as possible in the early days so they can feed whenever they're ready. It matters even more for premature or special care babies, where it's called kangaroo care.
