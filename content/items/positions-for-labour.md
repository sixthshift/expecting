There is no one position that is best for labour and birth, and different people find different positions comfortable. Unless there is a medical reason, you can choose what makes you comfortable and move around throughout. Following your instincts can help you feel more in control, reduce pain and make the birth easier. You might like to practise some positions beforehand, and an antenatal class may teach you some.

It used to be common to encourage people to give birth lying on their back in bed. Research now shows being upright during labour and birth has benefits for both you and your baby.

## Early labour

Positions that may be comfortable: lying on your side, sitting with your feet up or in the bath, walking, or standing in the shower, upright or bent over, rocking or swaying. Resting helps save your energy.

It's generally not a good idea to lie on your back during the first stage: lying flat can reduce the blood supply to your baby and could lead to a longer labour.

## Active labour

Choose positions that are most comfortable and help with pain. Choosing an upright position can have some benefits: you are less likely to have an epidural or a caesarean section, you may have a shorter first stage and less pain, and it can help your baby's head descend into your pelvis. Ideas: kneeling in the bath or on hands and knees, using a chair or birthing ball for support, squatting with a birthing stool, ball or bar, swaying or walking in the shower, or lunging with one foot on a chair.

## Second stage

Without an epidural you can choose a position that's comfortable: kneeling, squatting while supported, or sitting leaning forwards. Upright positions can improve blood and oxygen flow to your baby, help your uterus contract more efficiently and open your pelvis. In bed, you can still kneel, go on all fours or lie on your side.

## What limits your movement

- **An epidural** usually makes your legs heavy and numb so you'll need to lie on the bed, though "walking epidurals" are available in some hospitals. You may still benefit from lying on your side rather than your back.
- **Continuous fetal monitoring** may limit movement. Ask if there's a mobile monitor.
- **Assisted delivery** with forceps or a ventouse means lying on your back, as do vaginal examinations and an episiotomy.
