A full-term pregnancy lasts around 9 months, and most people, including doctors and midwives, break it into three trimesters of around three months each.

## Where the lines fall

- **First trimester:** from the first day of your last period to week 12.
- **Second trimester:** about week 13 to week 26.
- **Third trimester:** from about week 27 until the baby is born.

The edges are approximate: you'll see them drawn a week either way.

The weeks are counted from your last period, which is why the first 12 or so are counted before you could know the date of conception.

## What each one is like

**First.** You may have nausea ("morning sickness", at any time of day), food cravings or aversions, tender larger breasts, tiredness, moodiness and needing to wee more. By the end of it your baby is a fetus whose heart has started to beat. You'll be offered health checks and genetic screening tests. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to get medical help for vaginal bleeding or abdominal pain, and to speak with your midwife or doctor about severe nausea and vomiting.

**Second.** Nausea usually settles and you may have more energy. Your uterus grows, your ligaments stretch and your belly skin may itch. You may start to feel your baby move by about week 19, possibly sooner if it isn't your first pregnancy. You should start sleeping on your side from 28 weeks.

**Third.** Skin and ligaments keep stretching, you tire more easily, sleep gets harder, and heartburn or breathlessness are normal. Check-ups are about every 4 weeks until 36 weeks, then every 2 weeks. Around 36 weeks, if it's your first baby, the baby may move lower into your pelvis. Babies born in weeks 37 to 42 are full term, and the trimester ends whenever your baby is born.
