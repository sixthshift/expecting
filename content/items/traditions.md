Many families mark a birth with a custom from their faith or culture, and some combine more than one. Each partner may grow up with different expectations, so talk about it before the birth, then with both families.

Some to think about:

- **A naming ceremony.** A non-religious welcome for the baby, often with "guideparents". Civil celebrants run them; good ones book out, so ask about dates early.
- **Baptism or christening.** Churches usually ask you to contact the parish, and many hold a preparation session for parents and godparents first. Ask what they need and how far ahead.
- **The confinement month.** In Chinese and several other Asian cultures, the birth parent rests at home for about a month, with special food and family help, often ending in a full-month celebration. Some families arrange a confinement nanny or a meal service; these book out, so arrange it in the second half of the pregnancy.
- **Customs in the first days.** Some faiths mark the first week or so: a brit milah on the eighth day in Jewish families, an aqiqah in many Muslim families, a naming ceremony in many Hindu families. If yours has one, arrange who will lead it before the birth, as the timing is set by the birth date.
- **A celebration later.** A party at one month or a hundred days, or a welcome with the wider family once things have settled.

Agree between you:

- Which customs you'll keep, and whose family's.
- Who's coming, and when. Visitors in the first weeks can be a lot.
- What can wait. Most ceremonies can be held months later.
