## Why

Smoked ready-to-eat seafood is one of the foods Food Standards Australia New Zealand says to avoid in pregnancy, because of listeria. Cold-smoked salmon counts as a raw fish dish: it's ready-to-eat raw fish kept in the fridge, and listeria can grow in the fridge. Listeriosis is rare, but in pregnancy it can cause miscarriage, premature birth or stillbirth.

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby puts it simply: don't eat raw or ready-to-eat fish or seafood, including smoked salmon.

## What to do

- Skip it cold: on a bagel, a platter or a salad.
- Cooked is fine. Smoked seafood freshly cooked into a hot dish, or thoroughly reheated until steaming hot, is the safer option.
- Leftovers: into the fridge, and eat within a day.
