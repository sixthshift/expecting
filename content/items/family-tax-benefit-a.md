Family Tax Benefit (FTB) is a 2-part payment that helps with the cost of raising children. Part A is paid **for each eligible child**, and how much depends on your family's adjusted taxable income and the ages and number of children in your care. To get FTB you must care for the child at least 35% of the time and meet an income test.

## The rates

Per child, per fortnight:

- **Maximum rate:** $235.48 for a child 0 to 12.
- **Base rate:** $75.60. This isn't the minimum: you may get less because of your income.

There's also a yearly **FTB Part A supplement**, paid after your payments are balanced at the end of the financial year: up to $970.90 per child for 2026–27. Your family's adjusted taxable income must be $80,000 or less to get it.

## The income test

- **$69,131 or less:** you may get the maximum rate.
- **$69,131 to $123,078:** the payment drops by 20 cents for each dollar over $69,131, until it reaches the base rate. With one child aged 0 to 12, you're down to the base rate at $89,973.
- **Over $123,078:** it drops by 30 cents for each dollar over $123,078, until it's nil.

With **one child aged 0 to 12**, FTB Part A stops once family income reaches **$129,648** ($136,218 with two children under 13). Above that, your family gets no FTB Part A.

If your income is close to the limit, Services Australia suggests a different payment choice, or claiming as a lump sum after the financial year ends, when you know your actual income, to avoid an overpayment.

## Worth knowing

- **Immunisation.** Your child must meet immunisation requirements, or your FTB Part A may be reduced. Services Australia contacts you first.
- **Health Care Card.** If you're on the maximum rate of FTB Part A, you'll get a Health Care Card in the post without applying.
- **Keep your estimate up to date.** Your payments are based on your family income estimate, and compared with your actual income at the end of each financial year.
