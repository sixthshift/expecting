TENS (transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation) is a small, portable, battery-powered machine wired to sticky pads, which send small electrical pulses through your skin. The Royal Women's Hospital says there's no harm in using one and many people find it helpful. It isn't clear exactly how it works, and it doesn't work for everybody.

## How it's used

The pads usually go on your lower back, and the pulses feel like a tingling that shouldn't be painful. You can adjust the frequency and strength yourself, or your midwife can. It uses no medicines or needles.

- You can keep moving, and use other pain relief if you need more.
- It can be used at home in early labour, and at any stage of pre-labour or labour for as long or as little as you want. Check with your doctor or midwife before using it at home.
- It can be uncomfortable, and the pads can irritate your skin.

## When not to use it

- Not in or around water: you can't use it in the bath or shower.
- Don't use one if you have a cochlear implant, a pacemaker or other implanted heart device, epilepsy or spinal metal rods.
- Don't put the pads over your abdomen. Place them only on your back, or as your doctor or midwife says.

## Getting one

Ask your doctor or midwife whether it's safe for you, where you can get one and what it costs to hire or buy. Not all hospitals and birthing centres have them. You can buy from manufacturers, medical equipment suppliers and some pharmacies: check the supplier is registered with the TGA. Private health insurance may help with the cost.
