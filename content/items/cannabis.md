No. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby lists cannabis among the drugs that shouldn't be used in pregnancy, because they can harm you and your baby.

## Why

Around one third of the cannabis in your system passes to the baby through the placenta. The Royal Women's Hospital says there's no link between cannabis and birth "abnormalities", but that use in pregnancy may still harm your baby: there are possible links with poorer growth, and with learning disabilities, behaviour difficulties and memory problems later in life. The more you use, the more your baby is affected.

Smoking it with tobacco adds tobacco's harms, such as less oxygen and nutrition reaching the baby. "Street" cannabis often contains toxic compounds.

After the birth, some babies show withdrawal: irritability, unsettled behaviour and feeding trouble. It can take a week or two to appear, so it may not show in hospital. Cannabis also passes freely into breast milk, at up to eight times the level in your blood.

## What to do

- **Talk to your doctor or midwife.** They're there to support you, including to cut down if you can't stop yet.
- **See a drug and alcohol counsellor.** Pregnancy is a good time to get help; drug and alcohol services can help you withdraw safely, sometimes with medication.
- **If you're using it for nausea or sleep,** talk to your doctor or midwife about other ways to manage them.
