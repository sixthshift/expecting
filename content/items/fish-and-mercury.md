Fish is worth eating in pregnancy: protein, iodine and omega-3. The NSW Food Authority suggests two to three 150 g serves a week of any fish or seafood that isn't on the list below. Salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring and canned tuna in oil are high in omega-3 and low in mercury.

Mercury builds up in a few large fish, and it can harm the baby's developing nervous system. The guidance:

- **Shark (flake) or billfish (swordfish, broadbill, marlin):** one 150 g serve a fortnight, and no other fish that fortnight.
- **Orange roughy (deep sea perch) or catfish:** one 150 g serve a week, and no other fish that week.

Flake is shark, so ask what the fish is at the fish and chip shop. Canning, freezing and cooking don't reduce mercury.
