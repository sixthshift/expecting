A screening test, whether combined first-trimester screening, NIPT or second-trimester serum screening, estimates the chance that your baby has a condition such as Down syndrome. It can't diagnose it. A high-chance result means it's more likely a difference is there, not that it is. Your doctor or midwife will talk with you about what happens next.

## What you'll be offered

**Genetic counselling.** A genetic counsellor can explain what the different tests can and can't tell you, talk through possible outcomes and next steps, and help you think about how your own values fit your options. Genetic counselling can help you decide whether you want further tests.

**NIPT, if you haven't had it.** If combined first-trimester screening showed an increased chance of Down syndrome, NIPT is one option before deciding on a diagnostic test. It's a blood test, safe and low-risk, but it's still a screening test, it isn't covered by Medicare or private health insurance, and it costs around $450 to $800.

**A diagnostic test.** These can confirm whether your baby has the condition:

- **Chorionic villus sampling (CVS)** tests a small sample of the placenta, usually between 11 weeks and 13 weeks 6 days.
- **Amniocentesis** tests a sample of the fluid around the baby, from 15 weeks.

Both carry a small risk of miscarriage.

## Your choice, at every step

It's your choice whether to have any further test. There is no single right decision: different options feel right for different people, depending on their values, beliefs, experiences and circumstances. Talk it over with your doctor, midwife or a genetic counsellor, and give yourselves some time to decide. A genetic counsellor can also support you if a condition is diagnosed during pregnancy.

Being offered a diagnostic test after a high-chance result can bring on worry and stress. These feelings are common. Some people find that a diagnostic test early in pregnancy reduces their anxiety, because it gives them time to understand the result and what it means for them.

## Support

- **Through the Unexpected** has information and support for parents who get unexpected news about their baby during pregnancy.
- **Down Syndrome Australia** can connect you with families who are happy to share what day-to-day life looks like: call 1300 881 935 and ask for your local organisation.
- **YourChoice** is a free online decision aid for thinking through screening and diagnostic tests.
- **PANDA** offers mental health support for parents during pregnancy.
- **Pregnancy, Birth and Baby** maternal child health nurses are on 1800 882 436, 7am to midnight (AET), 7 days a week.
