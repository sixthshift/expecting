## Why

Listeria is a bacterium that causes an illness called listeriosis. It's rare, but in pregnancy it can be serious: it can cause miscarriage, premature birth or stillbirth, and even a small amount in food can make you ill. Pregnancy lowers your immune system, so you're more likely to catch it than other healthy adults.

What makes listeria different is that it keeps growing in the fridge. So the risky foods are ready-to-eat ones that have been chilled and stored, possibly for a long time. Cooking food thoroughly kills it.

## What to do

Avoid, especially:

- cold meats from deli counters and sandwich bars, and packaged sliced meats
- cold cooked chicken
- raw oysters, sashimi, sushi, smoked seafood and cooked ready-to-eat prawns
- soft cheeses like brie, camembert, ricotta, blue and feta, unless thoroughly cooked
- refrigerated pâté and meat spreads
- pre-prepared or pre-packaged salads, pre-cut fruit, rockmelon and raw seed sprouts
- soft-serve ice cream and unpasteurised juice

The rule underneath it all: freshly cooked and eaten hot is safe. Reheat food until it's steaming hot (75°C), keep your fridge at 5°C or colder, and eat leftovers within a day.

Symptoms usually start about 3 weeks after eating, but can start anywhere from 3 to 70 days, and they can be as mild as a flu: fever, headache, tiredness, aches. If you're pregnant and get them, seek medical attention immediately.
