Coming back from parental leave, you'd normally return to the same hours, location and duties as before. If you want something different, you have a legal right to ask, and both parents can.

## Who can ask

Full-time and part-time employees who have worked for the same employer for **at least 12 months** can request flexible working arrangements if they're pregnant, or the parent of (or responsible for) a child who is school-aged or younger. **Casual employees** can too, if they've worked for the employer regularly and systematically for at least 12 months and expect to keep doing so.

Flexible arrangements include changes to your **hours** (part-time, start and finish times), **patterns** (split shifts, job sharing) and **location** (working from home).

## How to ask

Your request must be **in writing**, and explain what changes you want and why. The Fair Work Ombudsman has free templates and sample letters.

## How your employer must respond

Your employer must reply **in writing within 21 days**, saying whether it's approved or refused. If you agree on something different from what you asked for, they must confirm it in writing within 21 days of your request.

They can only refuse on **reasonable business grounds**, such as cost, being unable to change other employees' arrangements, or a significant loss of productivity or impact on customer service, and only after discussing it with you, genuinely trying to agree on an alternative, and considering the consequences for you. A refusal must explain the reasons, say what other changes they'd make (or that there are none), and tell you about help from the Fair Work Commission.

## If you can't agree

If your employer refuses, or doesn't reply within 21 days, and you can't resolve it at work, you can apply to the Fair Work Commission. It usually tries conciliation or mediation first, and can then arbitrate and make binding orders. If a state or territory law gives you a better entitlement, that law still applies.
