## What's usual

Stress is a normal response to a major change like pregnancy. It can come from an unplanned or unwanted pregnancy, previous negative experiences with a pregnancy, birth or parenthood (such as a miscarriage or the death of a baby), waiting for test results, relationship difficulties, money, moving house or changing jobs.

If you're worrying that your worry is hurting the baby: what Pregnancy, Birth and Baby describes is **chronic** stress, the ongoing kind that wears down your quality of life or leaves you overwhelmed and unable to cope. When chronic stress isn't managed well, there may be effects on the baby's growth and the length of the pregnancy, and a higher risk of problems in the child's later development and behaviour.

Where stress tips into a problem differs for everyone. Clues include feeling overwhelmed or on edge, not being able to stop worrying, mood changes or anger, changes in sleep or appetite, headaches or muscle tension, pulling away from people, or leaning on alcohol to cope.

What can help:

- slow down, rest, and don't put too much pressure on yourself
- talk to someone you trust
- regular pregnancy-safe exercise, yoga, meditation or breathing exercises
- time with people who make you feel calm
- accept offers of help

## See your doctor or midwife

**If you're having thoughts of self-harm or suicide, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 for immediate support.**

If you've tried relaxation and talking to someone you trust but still feel overwhelmed, check in with your doctor, midwife or a mental health professional. Stress isn't a diagnosis but a sign that something else is going on; chronic stress could point to depression, anxiety or another condition, and your GP can refer you to a psychologist or counsellor. PANDA is on 1300 726 306 and Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636.
