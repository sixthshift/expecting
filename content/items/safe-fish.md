Yes, and it's good for you. FSANZ says it's safe to eat two to three serves of fish a week in pregnancy. A serve is 150 g.

## Why

Fish is a good source of protein, minerals, vitamin B12 and iodine. It's low in saturated fat and has omega-3 fatty acids, which matter for the development of your baby's nervous system before and after birth.

Most fish in Australia are low in mercury. Only a few are high enough to limit: shark (flake), billfish (swordfish, marlin), orange roughy (deep sea perch) and catfish. Mercury isn't removed by cooking, freezing or canning, so the limits apply whichever way it's served.

## What to do

- **Eat 2 to 3 serves a week** of any fish or seafood not on the high-mercury list.
- **Good choices.** The NSW Food Authority lists mackerel, silver warehou, Atlantic salmon, canned salmon, canned tuna in oil, herrings and sardines as low in mercury and high in omega-3. Snapper, salmon and trout, trevally, whiting, bream, mullet, garfish, anchovy, prawns, lobster, bugs, squid and octopus are also low in mercury.
- **Cook it** to at least 63 °C and eat it straight away. Raw and ready-to-eat chilled seafood, such as sushi, sashimi, smoked salmon and pre-cooked prawns, is a listeria risk.
- **Had more than this before?** Go back to 2 to 3 serves a week and your mercury levels will return to normal fairly soon.
