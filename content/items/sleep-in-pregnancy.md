You need more sleep than usual when you're pregnant, just as it gets harder to come by. Sleep is often lighter, you wake more, and you may feel especially tired at the beginning and towards the end of the pregnancy.

## Why it's harder

In the first trimester, tiredness comes from the changes happening in your body. Towards the end, you're carrying more weight and not sleeping as well. Along the way, sleep is broken by:

- needing to wee more often (early on from hormones, later from the baby pressing on your bladder)
- morning sickness, temperature changes and a blocked nose
- leg cramps and back aches
- heartburn, as the baby presses on your organs
- more dreams than usual, which may be unusual or nightmares; that's common, and sometimes linked to stress or broken sleep

## Sleep on your side from 28 weeks

From 28 weeks until your baby is born, doctors recommend you sleep on your side, at night and for daytime naps. Either side is fine, or both.

Lying on your back in later pregnancy puts pressure on your major blood vessels. That can reduce blood flow to your uterus and your baby's oxygen supply, and research suggests it causes a higher risk of stillbirth. Stillbirth can happen for many reasons, and most aren't in your control, but research shows sleeping on your side can reduce the risk.

The position you fall asleep in is usually the one you stay in longest. Changing position is normal, so don't worry if you wake on your back: just roll onto your side and go back to sleep.

## Getting comfortable

- Bend your knees and put a pillow between them, and another under your belly. A pregnancy pillow can help.
- For heartburn, sleep with your head raised on pillows; ask your pharmacist about an antacid.
- Drink well during the day but not too much before bed.
- Ease a leg cramp by stretching and massaging your calf.
- Rest when you can, nap in the day, and go to bed earlier.

## Winding down

Keep regular times for bed and waking, and keep naps short (no more than 20 minutes). Exercise, but not in the evening, and keep caffeine to mornings. Do something relaxing before bed, deal with worries earlier in the day, and keep the bedroom quiet, dark and comfortable. Avoid screens in the bedroom and for about an hour before bed. If you can't sleep, get up and do something relaxing in another room until you feel sleepy.

## When to get help

See your doctor or midwife if:

- you're exhausted and more rest doesn't help, or tiredness feels severe
- you start snoring, or have more pauses in your breathing while you sleep: it could be a sign of a medical problem that can affect you and your baby
- your sleep is disturbed by a health condition such as heartburn or restless legs, or you have trouble sleeping and you're worried it's affecting your or your baby's health
- you feel anxious or depressed

See your doctor if heartburn causes severe discomfort. Heartburn with swelling of your hands, feet or face, a headache or distorted vision can be pre-eclampsia: contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately.
