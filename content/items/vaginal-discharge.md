## What's usual

More discharge than usual is normal in pregnancy. It's called leucorrhoea, comes from higher levels of oestrogen and progesterone. It should look like your usual discharge, just more of it: clear or white, without a strong smell.

Near the end of pregnancy you may have a **show**: the mucus plug that sealed your cervix coming away as it starts to open. It may be watery, sticky or jelly-like, and pink, brown or streaked with blood.

Your waters breaking is different: liquid trickling or gushing from your vagina that you can't hold in. It can be hard to tell from discharge.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your doctor or midwife **immediately** if you have:

- any bleeding or fluid leaking from your vagina
- blood that's bright red or not mixed with mucus (it's probably not a show)
- your waters breaking, especially before full term

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor if your discharge is unusual for you. It may be an infection if it:

- looks green, yellow, brown or grey
- smells bad
- is thick and lumpy like cottage cheese, thin and runny, or frothy
- comes with itching or pain

Thrush and bacterial vaginosis (BV) are both more likely in pregnancy. Some genital infections can cause pregnancy complications, and untreated BV can lead to premature birth or miscarriage, so getting the right treatment matters. Your doctor may do a swab and prescribe treatment. healthdirect lists unusual discharge among the signs to see your doctor or midwife about as soon as possible.
