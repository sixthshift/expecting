Avoid them. Keeping active is good for you in pregnancy, but contact sports, where there's a risk of being hit, are on the list to avoid.

## Why

A blow to the abdomen can lead to serious complications, including placental abruption (when the placenta separates from the uterus before birth) and preterm labour. From the second trimester, as the baby grows, it's no longer protected by your pelvis.

Pregnancy changes other things too. Your centre of gravity moves, so you're more likely to fall, and your ligaments loosen, so sudden movements and changes in direction are more likely to injure you.

The sources name these sports to avoid:

- **Contact or collision:** football, soccer, basketball, martial arts, judo, kickboxing and other competition sports.
- **Hard balls or sticks:** hockey, cricket, softball.
- **Court sports** with sudden changes in direction, such as tennis and squash.

## What to do

- **Swap to lower-risk activity:** non-contact, low chance of falling, low or moderate intensity. Brisk walking, swimming, aqua-natal classes and classes designed for pregnancy all fit.
- **Tell your maternity team** about any sport you play, and talk to your doctor or midwife before exercising.
- **Took a knock?** Contact your midwife or doctor straight away. Go to hospital straight away if the baby is moving less than normal, or you have vaginal bleeding or pain in your abdomen. See [A fall or a knock to the bump](falls-and-knocks).
