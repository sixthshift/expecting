Recovery after a caesarean usually takes longer than after a vaginal birth.

## Straight after

Midwives and nurses look after you in the recovery room until you're ready for the postnatal ward. They check your vaginal bleeding, your pain, and your wound and dressing. If you're awake, you can usually hold your baby straight away; after a general anaesthetic, you can start skin to skin, and possibly breastfeeding, as soon as you're alert. If you can't, your partner or support person can do skin to skin.

## In hospital

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's page on caesarean recovery says you'll usually stay 2 to 5 days, and its page on being at home says you're likely to go home 48 to 72 hours after a caesarean; King Edward Memorial Hospital says 24 to 72 hours if you and your baby are well enough.

- **Pain.** You'll feel pain for at least a few days. Tell the midwives: managing it helps your recovery, and they'll guide you on how much pain relief you can safely take.
- **Getting up.** You stay in bed until the epidural or spinal wears off, doing breathing and leg exercises. A nurse or midwife helps you out of bed the first time, usually in the first 1 to 2 days, and with your first shower. Then walk gently every day to lower your chance of a chest infection or blood clots.
- **Feeding.** You can usually start breastfeeding very soon. Your milk may come in a little later; often it just takes more time. A pillow over your wound, lying on your side, or holding your baby under your arm can be more comfortable. If you can't breastfeed yet, ask your midwife to help you express colostrum.

## At home

- Rest, eat well, take short walks on flat ground when you feel able, and **avoid lifting anything heavier than your baby.**
- Keep taking regular pain relief if you need it; ask your doctor, midwife or pharmacist which suits you.
- Keep the wound clean and dry, pat it dry after a shower, wear loose clothes and check it for redness or swelling. Staples come out around 7 days; stitches usually dissolve. Some wound pain and changed feeling around it are normal for months, usually easing by 3 to 6 months.
- Stick to gentle movement, and ask family and friends to help with cleaning, meals and other children.
- **Driving:** talk to your doctor or midwife first. It's usually after you've fully recovered, which may take 2 to 6 weeks. First make sure you can do an emergency stop, aren't too sleepy, and your car insurance covers you.

See your doctor around 6 weeks after the birth for your postnatal check.

## Feelings

An unexpected or emergency caesarean can be traumatic. Talk to a midwife about it straight after, and to your doctor, midwife or child health nurse about how you feel. Guilt, anxiety, nightmares, trouble sleeping or always feeling on high alert can be signs you're not coping: get support early. PANDA is on 1300 726 306 and Lifeline on 13 11 14.

## When to get help

**Contact your doctor or midwife straight away** if you have:

- a fever (38 °C or more), shivering or sweating
- redness or swelling around your wound
- smelly discharge from your wound or vagina
- pain when you wee
- heavy bleeding from your vagina
