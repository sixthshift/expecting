Most people are offered an HIV test at the first antenatal visit; at the Royal Women's Hospital it comes with counselling before and after. It's rare for a baby to be born with HIV in Australia.

If it's positive, antiretroviral treatment in pregnancy can keep the virus undetectable, which reduces the risk to your baby. Your specialist team plans the birth around your viral load, gives your baby medicine after birth, for about 2 weeks if the risk is low or 4 to 6 weeks if it's higher, and talks through feeding.
