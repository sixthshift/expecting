Yes. ARPANSA's review found no or little association between radiofrequency exposure, such as from mobile phones, and pregnancy outcomes, and microwave ovens have no established health effect when used and kept as they should be. There's no specific Australian advice on wifi or laptops in pregnancy.

## Why

ARPANSA, Australia's radiation protection agency, reviewed 18 studies (a 2024 review) of radiofrequency electromagnetic field exposure and pregnancy outcomes, mostly self-reported mobile phone use, distance from mobile base stations and some jobs. Overall it found "no or little association" between exposure and pregnancy outcomes. ARPANSA notes the evidence was low to very low certainty, and the studies had limits, such as relying on people reporting how much they used their phone.

One finding to know about: in the general-public studies, phone use wasn't linked to preterm birth or low birth weight, but there was an association with a baby being small for gestational age (relative risk 1.13).

On microwave ovens, ARPANSA says there is "no established evidence" that they cause any health effects when used according to the manufacturer's instructions and kept in good working order. The fact sheet isn't specific to pregnancy.

## What to do

- **Use the microwave as the manufacturer says.** Check that the door isn't damaged, fits squarely and opens and closes smoothly. If it doesn't fit squarely or shows damage or corrosion, ARPANSA says to have it repaired by a qualified technician or replace it.
- **Keep the door and seals clean.** Use water and a mild detergent, not abrasive pads.
