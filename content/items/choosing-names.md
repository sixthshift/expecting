Plenty of couples don't settle on a name until they've met the baby. That's fine, but a shortlist agreed beforehand saves a hard conversation in a tired week.

**Building the shortlist.** Each write down names you like, then swap lists and cross off anything the other can't live with. Keep what's left.

**Testing it out loud.**

- Say the whole name, first, middle and surname, as if calling it across a park.
- Write out the initials, and the likely nicknames.
- Think about how it's spelt: will they spend their life correcting it?
- Decide whether you'll share the shortlist. Some couples keep it to themselves until the birth to avoid opinions.

**The surname.** Decide whose surname the baby takes, or whether it's both, hyphenated or not. It goes on the registration with the first name.

**The rules.** You register the birth yourselves, with the Births, Deaths and Marriages registry of the state or territory where the baby is born. It's free, and due within 60 days of the birth (the ACT allows six months). Registration records the name, so the name needs deciding first.

Each registry sets its own naming rules. NSW, for example, won't register a name that is offensive, could be mistaken for a title or rank (like "Prince" or "Judge"), includes numbers or symbols, or is longer than 50 characters. Others are similar. If your favourite is unusual, check your registry's page before the birth.
