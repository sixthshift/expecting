Parental leave isn't only for the pregnant parent. If you're the partner and you work, you have your own entitlement, your own paperwork and your own deadlines, and they run on the same rules.

## Are you eligible?

You can take unpaid parental leave if you have, or will have, responsibility for the care of the child and you've worked for your employer for at least 12 months when the leave starts. If you started with a new employer between children, you need to work for that employer for at least 12 months before you can take parental leave.

If you're a casual, you're eligible only if you've been working for your employer on a regular and systematic basis for at least 12 months, with a reasonable expectation of continuing work on that basis had it not been for the birth.

## When you can take it

As the parent who isn't pregnant, your leave must start and end within 24 months of the birth. You can take it as one continuous block, as flexible days, or both, and you don't have to take it at the same time as your partner, though you can. Both parents can each take up to 12 months.

## Giving notice

The same two deadlines apply to you as to the pregnant parent:

- **At least 10 weeks before your leave starts:** written notice of how much leave you want, with the start and finish dates for continuous leave (or the total number of flexible days).
- **At least 4 weeks before:** confirm the start and end dates, and any changes.

If the baby comes early and you can't give the notice, you're still entitled to the leave as long as you give notice as soon as possible.

## The money

Unpaid leave is unpaid, so the pay comes from elsewhere:

- **Parental Leave Pay.** For a child born from 1 July 2026, 20 of the family's days are reserved for you. Parents can take up to 20 days at the same time, so you can be paid while you're both home.
- **Your employer.** Employers can provide parental leave payments in agreements, employment contracts or workplace policies. Ask yours what applies to you.
- **Annual leave.** You can take any annual leave you've built up during your unpaid parental leave.
