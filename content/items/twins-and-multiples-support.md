Surprise, delight, apprehension, disappointment, excitement: it's perfectly normal to feel some or all of these when you find out it's twins or more, and it can take time to adjust. Your partner may feel overwhelmed too. Start talking about it early.

## Find your people

It can be hard to find support if you don't know other families with multiples. You can join your local multiple birth club through the Australian Multiple Birth Association (AMBA) as soon as you find out. Clubs run an Expectant Parents Meeting and online forums, and can be the start of long friendships.

## What changes in the care

Most twin pregnancies go smoothly, but a multiple pregnancy is higher risk than carrying one baby, so it needs more care: more appointments, monitoring, ultrasounds and tests. It's very important to find out whether your babies share a placenta, because that needs careful monitoring. You'll get to know your midwife or doctor well.

Babies are more likely to come early. AMBA says a full-term pregnancy is generally 37 to 38 weeks for twins and 35 weeks for triplets. Your team may recommend against a home birth or birth centre, and may suggest a hospital that can care for twins, since premature babies sometimes need time in a neonatal intensive care unit or special care nursery. Caesareans are quite common in multiple births.

## Your body

Early symptoms such as nausea, tiredness and mood changes can be stronger, and backache, heartburn and swollen ankles are more likely. You'll need more protein, calcium, iron, folic acid and vitamin B12 than with one baby. Gentle, low-impact exercise helps; start slowly and stop if you feel cramping, pain or short of breath. Daily pelvic floor exercises reduce the risk of later bladder leaks and prolapse.

## Your mental health

AMBA says the emotional health of mothers of multiples is poorer, and they are at higher risk of becoming depressed, anxious and clinically exhausted after childbirth than mothers of single babies. It cites research finding that rates of clinical anxiety are three times higher, and rates of depression five times higher, among mothers of twins than among mothers of singletons. The intensity of caring, broken sleep, financial strain and relationship stress all play a part. Keep talking with your partner and ask for help if you need it; AMBA also suggests joining a club to meet your peers. If you're struggling, the hospital social worker or someone at AMBA may help.

## Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says identical twins who share a placenta and amniotic sac can sometimes get twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), where blood flows from one twin to the other, and that it can sometimes be very severe. It's rare, and most identical twins won't get it. If your twins have it, your care will be transferred to medical specialists and you'll be referred to a hospital that can care for you and your babies.
