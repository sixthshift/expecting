Congratulations: your baby is here. And if they are in the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) or special care nursery (SCN), this is probably a very different path from the one you expected. Mixed emotions are normal, and so is feeling overwhelmed. You don't have to do this alone.

## Day to day

Talk to your baby's healthcare team about what you can and can't do, and raise every worry you have: you are not bothering them. Ask what you can do to be part of your baby's care.

Many babies stay until they gain weight, can keep their temperature steady and feed well. The days and nights in the NICU can be very long, and long stays can feel lonely. Feeding, expressing and kangaroo care have their own pages, as does the money side.

## What helps, if people ask

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's list for family and friends is worth passing on:

- **Meals:** a meal paid for near the hospital, or meals frozen for when you get home.
- **Parking and lifts:** parking can be very expensive over weeks or months; a parking voucher, or lifts if you've gone home before the baby.
- **Updates:** someone to keep family and friends posted, with your consent, so you don't repeat yourself.
- **Small breaks:** a walk or a coffee near the hospital, for fresh air and a break from sitting by the cot.
- **Home:** looking after older children, cleaning, laundry, pets.
- **Staying in touch:** texts, calls and cards, especially after the first rush of contact fades.

## For the partner

If the birth was planned, unexpected or an emergency, you may be the one who follows the baby to the neonatal unit, and leaving the birth parent at that moment can leave you feeling very torn. You may be asked to consent to procedures in the first hours, under a lot of pressure. Ask the team questions, ask for regular updates on how your partner is doing, and ask how you can be included.

It can feel as if the focus is all on the birth parent, and you may feel lost. You are a very important part of your baby's life, and there is a lot you can do: kangaroo care; talking, singing or reading to your baby; changing nappies; doing cares like cleaning eyes and mouth; cleaning and sterilising the breast pump; taking photos; and looking after yourself. It may feel scary at first, and gets easier the more you do it.

SMS4dads sends free text messages for dads, including a set for dads in the NICU. Miracle Babies has a closed Facebook group for neonatal dads.

## When to get help

Miracle Babies' Nurture Line is free and open 24 hours, for families with a premature or sick newborn: 1300 622 243 (1300 MBABIES). For mental health support, PANDA is on 1300 726 306, Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636 and MensLine Australia on 1300 78 99 78.
