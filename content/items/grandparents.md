Grandparents can be the best help you have, or the most complicated. Most of the friction comes from expectations nobody said out loud, so say them early.

**Agree between you first.** Work out what you'd each like before either family hears it:

- **The help you'd actually use:** meals, washing, minding an older child, a lift, an afternoon so you can sleep.
- **The first days:** who comes to the hospital, who waits until you're home, and whether anyone stays with you.
- **Advice:** how much you want, and on what.
- **Later on:** whether they might help with care once leave ends, and whether they want to.

**Then each of you talks to your own family.** It lands better from their own child, and you're less likely to end up as the one who said no.

**What helps most.** The Gidget Foundation's advice to family is to ask the parents what support they want, check before visiting, respect a need for space, offer practical help like a meal, and generally hold back on parenting advice. It can be easier to share their sheet than to say it yourself.

**Distance.** If they live interstate or overseas, plan any visit around what you'll need, not just when the baby is due: babies rarely arrive on the day.

Their role will keep changing as the baby grows. Keep talking.
