## What's usual

Any bleeding in pregnancy, at any stage, is a reason to contact your doctor or midwife. This page is about how much is too much: when it's no longer a phone call but an ambulance.

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby gives two measures of heavy. Its page on bleeding in pregnancy says soaking 2 pads an hour, or passing clots the size of golf balls. Its page on miscarriage says soaking 1 or more pads in an hour, or bleeding you can't catch or contain. Go by the lower one: **1 pad in an hour**.

If you can, keep the pads or stained clothes to show the doctor or midwife.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance, or go to your nearest emergency department, immediately if you have:

- very heavy bleeding: soaking 1 or more pads in an hour, bleeding you can't catch or contain, or large clots
- strong pain in your belly, or pain in the tip of your shoulder
- feeling faint, dizzy or very unwell
- trouble breathing
- a fever, or discharge that smells bad

Go to your nearest emergency department immediately if you have chills or a fast heartbeat with bleeding.
