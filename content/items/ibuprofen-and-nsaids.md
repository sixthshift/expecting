Avoid them unless your doctor tells you otherwise. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory medicines (NSAIDs), such as ibuprofen (Nurofen), naproxen, diclofenac and indomethacin, are no longer recommended in pregnancy and should only be used under medical supervision.

## Why

There are different reasons to avoid NSAIDs at different stages of pregnancy. KEMH says they're not recommended in pregnancy, especially in the third trimester, and that includes creams and gels. Topical anti-inflammatories shouldn't be used in the last 3 months of pregnancy unless your doctor specifically directs you to.

If you're still trying to conceive: taking NSAIDs regularly or over a long time may stop an egg being released in some people. It reverses once regular use stops.

## What to do

- **Use paracetamol instead** for aches, pains and fever. It's considered the safest choice.
- **Took one before you thought about it?** If you've accidentally used an anti-inflammatory, you can generally be reassured.
- **If you think you need one,** talk to your doctor or midwife about whether you can take it. They should only be used under medical supervision.
- **After the birth, the answer changes.** Ibuprofen is generally safe while breastfeeding, as only small amounts pass into breast milk. Check with your doctor, pharmacist or child health nurse first.
