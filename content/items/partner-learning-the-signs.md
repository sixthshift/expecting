Most of what happens in a pregnancy is the pregnant parent's to feel and describe. But you're the one who might notice when something has changed, and the one with a free hand to make the call. This entry is the short list of what to watch for; each sign has its own entry with the detail.

## Your rule

If either of you is worried, you call. The advice is to ring for support and guidance any time you feel concerned or worried. Keep the hospital's or birth unit's contact numbers in your phone as well as theirs, so you're never hunting for them.

## The body and the baby

These are the signs to know by name. Each has its own entry, which says how urgent it is and who to call:

- a change in the baby's movements
- bleeding from the vagina
- a severe headache, or changes to vision
- fluid leaking from the vagina
- a fever
- itching

## The mind

**Thoughts of self-harm.** If the person you're supporting, or anyone close to you, is having thoughts of self-harm, or you're worried about the baby's safety, go to the nearest hospital emergency department or call 000 and ask for an ambulance. Lifeline (13 11 14) is there 24/7 for crisis support and suicide prevention.

**Postpartum psychosis, after the birth.** It's rare but serious. It can come on very suddenly, within hours of the birth, or up to 12 weeks after, in anyone who gives birth, even without any history of mental illness. Signs include:

- sudden mood swings, excessive happiness, irritation or depression
- speaking quickly, being confused, disorganised or forgetful, making unrealistic plans
- false beliefs, paranoia, or seeing or hearing things that aren't there
- being unable to sleep, sometimes for days
- being unable to care for the baby, or considering or planning harm to themselves or the baby

If you notice these, they need to see a doctor urgently: the GP or the hospital emergency department. With the right treatment, people are likely to make a full recovery.

## Safety at home

Danger at home is a warning sign too. 1800 737 732 is 24/7 support for sexual assault and domestic and family violence. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 000.
