A monitor is a convenience, not a safety device. If you get one, a camera or wifi monitor is a small computer in the baby's room, so choose and set it up like one.

## Not a safety device

Red Nose says there is no scientific evidence that any type of baby monitor prevents Sudden Unexpected Death in Infancy (SUDI). Monitors are only alarms: they do not save lives. Red Nose says room-sharing (not bed-sharing) for the first 6 months remains the most effective way to reduce the risk of SUDI; other safe sleep guidance says 6 to 12 months (see [Bassinet](bassinet)).

Mat and movement monitors can't detect obstructed breathing, so they don't prevent SUDI. Home monitors are only recommended under medical advice, for example after a serious event that needed resuscitation, or for babies with a diagnosed sleep-related breathing disorder. If a monitor is used, Red Nose says parents and carers should be trained in infant cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) (see [A baby first aid course](baby-first-aid-course)) and have a care plan. Commercial baby monitors sold in stores don't have to meet safety standards, and some may present strangulation or entrapment risks if they have leads.

## Choosing a wifi or camera monitor

The Australian Cyber Security Centre says to research a device before you buy, because manufacturers provide different levels of security:

- **Bought on or after 4 March 2026?** Check it meets the Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025. They require no default passwords, a clear way to report security issues, and a defined period of security updates.
- **The password.** Check you can change it, and use a strong one.
- **Updates.** Check the manufacturer provides security updates.
- **Your data.** Check what it collects, where it's stored (sometimes overseas) and who it's shared with, and turn off data sharing or features you don't need.
- **The maker.** Choose products from trusted manufacturers, sold by well-known stores.

## Keeping it secure

- Install software and firmware updates as soon as they're available, and keep the app and your phone up to date.
- Regularly review and remove apps, services or people that no longer need access.
- **Second-hand,** factory reset it before you use it, so the previous owner no longer has access. When you sell or pass one on, reset it, unlink your accounts and remove your data.
