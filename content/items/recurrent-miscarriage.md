Yes, from your second loss. In Australia the definition of recurrent miscarriage was updated, in line with international guidelines, to two pregnancy losses before 20 weeks, whether or not they were in a row. Around 1 to 2 in 100 people experience it. Losing one pregnancy after another is often highly distressing, and **you are not to blame**: most miscarriages come from things outside anyone's control.

## Tests

In Australia, testing for the cause of miscarriage isn't common unless you've had recurrent miscarriage (or have a personal or family history of a relevant condition). After two or more, your doctor may recommend:

- blood tests for hormones, thyroid function, and clotting or immune factors (such as antiphospholipid syndrome)
- chromosome testing for you and your partner, sometimes with a genetic counsellor
- a pelvic ultrasound, to look at the shape of the uterus or for fibroids
- testing of pregnancy tissue from a miscarriage, if it's available

Some hospitals have specialist recurrent miscarriage clinics that help find reasons and, where possible, start treatment. If you'd like testing, speak with your doctor or specialist.

## What might be found

The cause of most recurrent miscarriage can't be found. When something is, it may be chromosomal; a condition such as diabetes, thyroid problems, PCOS or a clotting disorder; the shape of the uterus, fibroids or scarring; an immune condition; an infection; or older maternal age.

## Treatment

It depends on the cause: medicines for hormone or thyroid problems, low-dose aspirin or blood thinners for clotting or immune conditions, surgery for some uterus conditions, and lifestyle advice where it helps. **Even when no cause is found, supportive care and early, frequent monitoring in the next pregnancy can make a difference.**

## Next time

Even after recurrent miscarriage, most pregnancies are successful. Around 1 in 5 people may have another miscarriage, but most do not.

## The toll

Repeated loss can bring grief, guilt, anger, anxiety and depression, and the effects can last longer than after one miscarriage and shape how you cope in later pregnancies. You and your partner may cope differently. If sadness, anxiety or guilt become overwhelming or last for weeks, speak with your doctor, who can refer you to a support group, counsellor or psychologist. Call Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636, or Medicare Mental Health on 1800 595 212 to connect with local services.

## When to get help

**Call 000 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately** with heavy bleeding (soaking 1 or more pads an hour, or bleeding you can't contain), severe tummy pain, shoulder-tip pain, smelly discharge, a fever, or feeling faint, dizzy or unwell. If you or someone close to you is in crisis, call 000; to talk to someone now, call Lifeline on 13 11 14.
