## What's usual

Hormone changes can trigger acne in pregnancy. It may flare up in the first trimester, and usually improves or even disappears by the third trimester. It can show up on your face, neck, back or chest. It won't affect your pregnancy or your baby.

It's important to use only acne treatments that are safe in pregnancy. For mild acne that can include over-the-counter products such as benzoyl peroxide, and your doctor may prescribe other treatments. Some medicines must be avoided because they can harm your baby, including retinoids (medicines similar to vitamin A), whether pills, creams or ointments, and spironolactone. Always ask your midwife, doctor or pharmacist before starting any new medicine or skin product.

Gentle care helps: wash with a soap-free cleanser, use oil-free make-up and moisturiser, don't pick or pop pimples, and use sun protection.

## See your doctor or midwife

If your acne isn't well controlled with non-prescription products, is moderate to severe, or is causing you distress.
