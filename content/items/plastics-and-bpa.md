Some plastics contain bisphenol A (BPA), found in water bottles, toys and the lining of some food and drink products. Exposure in pregnancy and infancy has been suggested to affect a baby's development, organs and immune system and to increase the chance of childhood asthma. But Food Standards Australia New Zealand says BPA in food packaging is unlikely to be a health risk.

## Why

Most chemicals you come across in daily life won't harm your baby, usually because the amount is so small you don't need to worry. Flame retardants, added to furniture, carpet, clothes and electronics to make them less flammable, can impact your baby's brain development.

## What to do

- **Keep your home well ventilated** and wash your hands regularly, to reduce chemical exposure in general.
- **Look for alternatives** if you want to: products with low levels of chemicals that may be harmful in pregnancy.
- **Don't microwave food in plastic** unless the container is made for microwave use: ARPANSA says high temperatures may break the plastic down and contaminate the food.
- **Non-stick pans and PFAS:** there's no specific Australian pregnancy advice on these.
