Hormonal changes in pregnancy lead to physical symptoms and can affect your mood, according to Pregnancy, Birth and Baby. Here are the main ones, in the order you meet them.

**hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin).** Made by the placenta once the embryo has implanted. It's what a pregnancy test detects, usually about 2 weeks after conception. It helps your body produce progesterone. hCG is highest towards the end of the first trimester, then gradually decreases.

**Progesterone.** Rises as soon as you conceive and prevents you having a period.

**Oestrogen.** Needed for pregnancy as well as puberty, the menstrual cycle and bone strength. In pregnancy the main type is oestriol, made mostly by the placenta. Higher oestrogen and progesterone are behind the extra clear or white vaginal discharge (leucorrhoea) you may notice. If it has a funny smell or colour, or comes with itching or pain, see your doctor or midwife, as it could be a sign of an infection.

**Hormones and your body.** Hormonal changes can cause nausea and vomiting, cravings and constipation, and pregnancy hormones soften the ligaments, which can affect your pelvis, lower back and balance.

## For the birth

- **Prostaglandin.** Before childbirth a higher level helps open the cervix and makes you more receptive to oxytocin.
- **Oxytocin.** Causes the contractions of labour, the contractions that deliver the placenta, and contractions during breastfeeding. Healthdirect adds that it plays a role in emotional bonding, including with your child.
- **Relaxin.** Softens and stretches the cervix, helps your waters break and lets the pelvic ligaments stretch.
- **Beta-endorphins.** Help with pain relief in childbirth and may bring joy or euphoria.
- **Adrenaline and noradrenaline.** "Fight or flight" hormones released just before birth, causing several strong contractions and a surge of energy.

## For feeding

When your baby sucks, your brain releases prolactin and oxytocin. Prolactin helps make the milk, and oxytocin pushes it out through the nipple (the let-down reflex).
