However far along you were, you're entitled to grieve and to honour your baby in any way you want. And if you don't feel the need to mark it, that's OK too: there's no right or wrong way.

## A certificate

A loss before 20 weeks isn't formally registered, so there's no birth or death certificate. But every state and territory offers an optional certificate recognising early pregnancy loss, through its Births, Deaths and Marriages registry. It isn't a legal document; it's a way of recognising your baby and your loss. The names differ:

- **ACT:** Early Pregnancy Loss Certificate
- **NSW:** Recognition of Early Pregnancy Loss Certificate
- **NT:** Commemorative Certificate of Early Delivery
- **Queensland:** Early Pregnancy Loss Recognition Certificate
- **South Australia:** Early Loss of Pregnancy Certificate
- **Tasmania:** Early Pregnancy Loss Recognition Certificate
- **Victoria:** Early Pregnancy Loss Commemorative Certificate
- **Western Australia:** Recognition Certificate of Early Pregnancy Loss

## A name, a ceremony, a keepsake

Naming your baby may help you process your grief.

Before 20 weeks there's no legal need for a funeral, burial or cremation, but you can have one if you choose. Most hospitals can arrange a respectful burial or cremation of the pregnancy tissue, some hospitals hold a memorial service for pregnancy loss, and you can make private arrangements through a funeral director or your religious or spiritual leader. Burial in a private garden is allowed if you own the land or have the owner's permission.

People keep an ornament, a piece of jewellery, a tattoo, or a box of scan pictures and tests; some plant a tree. Rituals that might seem unusual to others can be a way to heal, for as long as you need them.

## After 20 weeks

A loss from 20 weeks is a stillbirth. Your hospital healthcare provider may be able to help with keepsakes such as handprints and footprints, a lock of hair or your baby's cot name card, and your healthcare professionals can support you to arrange rituals that meet your cultural, religious and spiritual needs: blessings, naming, baptism, burial or cremation. There's no rush to decide about a funeral; the hospital can help with information.

## The reminders

Pregnant friends, babies and the dates can hurt, especially early on. That's normal. Face them at your own pace, and take a break from social media if it helps. If grief is so consuming you can't get through the day, reach out to a psychologist or other professional with bereavement training.
