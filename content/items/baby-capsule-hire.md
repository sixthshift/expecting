Babies up to 6 months old must travel in an approved rear-facing car seat, and the first trip is the drive home from hospital. A baby capsule (an infant carrier) is made for that stretch: Kidsafe SA describes its capsules as for newborns to about 6 months. Because it's used for such a short time, hiring one is an option.

## Why hire

Kidsafe Queensland puts it this way: with so many car seats and capsules available, hiring gives you breathing space before you invest in something that may not suit your car or your baby. It can also help if you're juggling several car seats in one car while an older child waits to move to their next seat.

## What hire includes

Two Kidsafe hire services, as examples:

- **Kidsafe Queensland** offers long-term capsule hire for up to 6 months at one fee, plus a refundable deposit, with additional per-month fees to extend beyond that. The hire includes installation by qualified fitters, who show you how to adjust and use the seat safely. It also offers short-term hire of convertible seats (birth to 4 years), by the week, for up to 4 weeks.
- **Kidsafe SA** hires capsules for periods from 1 week to 6 months, with free installation into your car by its qualified child restraint fitters, and a refundable deposit.

Both hire capsules made for low birth weight and premature babies, which Kidsafe SA says many restraints can't accommodate. Check the current prices and booking with the service near you.

## The alternative: a convertible seat from birth

A convertible seat can be used rear-facing from birth and later turned forward-facing. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby recommends keeping children rear-facing for as long as they fit the seat, and moving to the next type only once they've outgrown it. Kidsafe Queensland notes young children have to reach the height markers before a convertible seat can be fitted forward-facing.

## Whichever you choose

- It must meet the Australian standard, AS/NZS 1754, and be installed properly.
- Buy from an outlet that only stocks restraints meeting Australian Standards, and buy new where possible.
- Follow the manufacturer's instructions for your seat. If you're unsure, contact an authorised restraint fitting station in your state or territory.
- Never swaddle your baby in their car seat.
