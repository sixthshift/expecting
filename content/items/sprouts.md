Not raw. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says not to eat any type of raw or lightly cooked bean sprouts, and the NSW Food Authority calls them "do not eat raw or lightly cooked (high risk)".

## Why

The way sprouts are grown means they can be contaminated with E. coli, listeria and salmonella. That covers alfalfa, broccoli, onion, sunflower, clover, radish, snow pea, mung bean and soybean sprouts. In pregnancy, listeria can cause miscarriage, premature birth or stillbirth, and salmonella can cause miscarriage, though this is rare.

## What to do

- **Cook them thoroughly.** FSANZ lists thoroughly cooked sprouts as the safer alternative. Eat them straight away, or cool and refrigerate leftovers, use them within a day, and reheat them thoroughly.
- **Watch for raw sprouts in a dish.** Leave them off, or ask for them to be left out.
- **Already eaten some?** If you're worried about something you've eaten, call your maternity care provider or doctor.
