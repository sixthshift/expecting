What you pay to have a baby depends mostly on one choice: public or private care. Here's where the money goes in each, and what to ask before you're committed.

## Public care

If you have a Medicare card and have your baby at a public hospital, birth centre or public homebirth program, most of your pregnancy care costs are covered by Medicare. Medicare covers many of the costs of having a baby, including doctor and midwife visits, ultrasounds, blood tests and having your baby in an approved facility.

Sometimes you may still have to pay an out-of-pocket cost, where the full cost of a service isn't covered. Some antenatal tests aren't covered by Medicare at all, such as non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT).

## Private care

If you're looked after by a private provider, Medicare covers only part of your costs, and private health insurance might cover some or all of the rest.

When you're admitted to hospital as a private patient, Medicare pays 75% of the Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS) fee for each item, and your insurer pays the other 25% if your policy covers it. But doctors are free to charge more than the MBS fee, and many do: there's no cap. The extra is the "gap", and you may have to pay it.

On top of the doctors' fees:

- hospital fees (such as accommodation and theatre) are separate from the doctors' fees, and you may have out-of-pocket costs for them
- your policy may have an excess or co-payment to pay for your or your baby's admission
- your baby is charged separately for their nursery care
- things like obstetrician check-ups, care outside hospital and the paediatrician's visits may not be covered by your fund, though some can be claimed back from Medicare

Without private cover, having your baby in a private hospital means high out-of-pocket costs: Medicare pays part of a private obstetrician's or eligible midwife's fee, but you pay the hospital costs.

## A private patient in a public hospital

With private health insurance, you can choose to be a private patient in a public hospital. You won't be guaranteed a single room, and you'll still be responsible for hospital, medical and diagnostic costs.

## What to ask

- **An estimate, before you go to hospital.** Ask your doctor for the MBS item numbers and an estimate of your out-of-pocket costs, preferably in writing, and whether other doctors (such as an anaesthetist) will be involved and what they charge. Then check with your insurer what your policy pays. This is informed financial consent.
- **The Medicare Safety Net.** You may want to register for it: if you spend above a certain amount on healthcare in a calendar year, you can get more back from Medicare.

If the costs are hard to follow, talk to your midwife, doctor, hospital or health fund, or call Pregnancy, Birth and Baby on 1800 882 436.
