## What's usual

It's common to feel tired during pregnancy. In the first trimester it's because of the changes happening in your body, and your energy often improves after 14 weeks. Towards the end of pregnancy it can come back, because you're carrying more weight and not sleeping as well as usual.

Sleep changes too: you wake more often (for the toilet, or when the baby kicks), you may be uncomfortable or get heartburn lying down, and you might develop restless legs. From 28 weeks, it's important to sleep on your side, not your back.

Resting when you can, asking for help with chores, gentle exercise, regular balanced meals with enough iron and protein, and avoiding caffeine later in the day can all help.

## See your doctor or midwife

- you're exhausted and getting more rest doesn't help
- you start snoring, or have more pauses in your breathing during sleep
- your sleep is disturbed by a health condition such as heartburn or restless legs
- you're feeling anxious or depressed

Tiredness can be a symptom of anaemia (low iron), which is common in pregnancy because your body needs more iron. If you or someone you know is having suicidal thoughts and is in immediate danger, call 000. For support, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 or PANDA on 1300 726 306.
