Second-trimester serum screening (also called maternal serum screening) is one of three ways to screen for Down syndrome, alongside combined first trimester screening and NIPT. It's a blood test in the second trimester. Sources differ on the window. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's Down syndrome page and the Royal Women's Hospital say 15 to 20 weeks; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby's neural tube defects page and blood tests page say 14 to 20, and RANZCOG's leaflet gives both 15 to 20 (in its text) and 14 to 20 (in its table).

**What it looks for.** It estimates the chance of your baby having Down syndrome or a neural tube defect such as spina bifida; the Royal Women's Hospital says it also estimates the chance of trisomy 18. Like every screening test, it can't diagnose any of them: it tells you whether the chance is higher, and it can miss some babies who have the condition.

**If the chance is high,** your doctor or midwife will talk with you about what comes next. You may be offered genetic counselling and information about a diagnostic test, such as amniocentesis (done after 15 weeks); the Royal Women's Hospital says it offers amniocentesis and an ultrasound.

**Other options.** NIPT, a blood test that screens for Down syndrome and other conditions, can be done from 10 weeks. It isn't covered by Medicare and can be expensive. The morphology scan at 18 to 22 weeks also checks the baby's growth and development.

**It's your choice.** Screening is offered to everyone and isn't compulsory. Before deciding, think about why you want it and how you might feel about the results, and talk it over with your partner, your doctor or midwife, or a genetic counsellor. Ask your doctor to explain the costs of each test.
