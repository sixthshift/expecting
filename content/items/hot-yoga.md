Avoid it. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says to avoid exercise classes held at intentionally higher temperatures, such as hot Pilates or yoga, and the Royal Women's Hospital lists "hot" yoga among the activities to avoid in pregnancy.

## Why

Your body runs slightly warmer when you're pregnant, and intensive exercise may push your core temperature up to a level that's unsafe for the baby. A hot room makes that more likely. High temperatures can also tire you more quickly, and tiredness puts you at greater risk of injury.

## What to do

- **Keep doing yoga or Pilates, just not hot.** Both can be good for you in pregnancy, and classes designed for pregnancy often include pelvic floor exercises. Ask your doctor or midwife whether they suit you.
- **The rule is the room, not the name.** Avoid yoga or Pilates in any room that's too hot or humid, and exercise in cool, ventilated places. That goes for water temperature too.
- **Keep the effort moderate,** drink water before, during and after, and wear light clothing.
- **Tell your teacher** you're pregnant and how many weeks along you are, and make sure they're properly qualified.
