With a private obstetrician, the doctor you choose looks after you through pregnancy, labour and the weeks after. Your check-ups are in their rooms. The birth is usually in a private hospital, though some obstetricians care for private patients in public hospitals.

## What it's like

In labour, you'll mostly be cared for by the hospital's midwives. Your obstetrician may visit during labour, should be there for the birth, and manages any complications. They'll usually check on you before you leave hospital and book you in for a 6-week check-up.

The Royal Women's lists the advantages: the doctor of your choosing throughout, a practice that may be more convenient, and usually shorter waits than public clinics. Some private obstetricians offer shared care with your GP or a midwife, which can cost less.

## Getting started

- **Get a GP referral.** You need one to see a private obstetrician and to claim a Medicare rebate. It lasts up to 12 months.
- **Find one.** Ask your GP, call local private maternity hospitals for a list of the doctors who practise there, or use the RANZCOG or Pregnancy, Birth and Baby service finder.
- **Ask about fees before the first appointment.** It can be expensive, and costs vary a lot depending on the care you get. Ask too about out-of-pocket fees for the hospital admission, anaesthetist and paediatrician, and ultrasounds and blood tests.

If their approach doesn't suit you, you can choose a different obstetrician or midwife.

## Insurance

Private health insurance only applies once you're admitted to hospital for the birth; it doesn't cover your pregnancy visits, though Medicare covers part of those. Most policies have a 12-month waiting period for pregnancy. Check your policy covers obstetrics: some don't, or only cover you as a private patient in a public hospital. Without private cover, having your baby in a private hospital means high out-of-pocket costs. The Royal Women's notes that services free to public patients, such as blood tests, ultrasounds and access to diabetes educators and physiotherapists, may carry a charge.
