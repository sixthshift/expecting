[Registering the birth](registering-the-birth) and getting a birth certificate are two different things. The law says the birth must be registered, and Victoria says there's no fee to register. The certificate is optional, and you can't get one until the birth is registered.

## Why you'll want one

The certificate presents the registration in an official document you can keep, and it's often needed as a primary form of ID. NSW lists what it makes easier: Medicare, enrolling in childcare and school, organised sport, opening a bank account, a driver licence or [passport](baby-passport), and government benefits like parental leave.

## How to get it

You don't get a certificate automatically when you register. You can order one with the registration or separately later. Paper certificates have a fee, set by each state and territory's registry, so check yours.

- **Victoria** sends a free digital birth certificate once the birth is registered, unless you opt out, through the Service Victoria app. It has the same legal standing as paper, but the registry still recommends a paper one, since some businesses and other governments may not accept a digital one yet. Registration and a certificate take up to 4 weeks there.
- **The ACT** processes certificate applications within 15 business days of getting all the documents, plus postage.

## Commemorative certificates

These are decorative keepsakes, for display. As Victoria's registry puts it, they're not legal documents and can't be used for official purposes such as proving identity. If you want one, order it as well as, not instead of, the real certificate.
