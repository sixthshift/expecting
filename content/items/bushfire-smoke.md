Limit your exposure. Bushfire smoke can cause serious health effects, and healthdirect lists being pregnant among the things that put you more at risk during a bushfire. NSW Health says that if you are pregnant you may be more sensitive to the effects of smoke.

## Why

Smoke can cause shortness of breath, headache, cough or wheezing, chest pain or tightness, and anxiety or depression. Many of these problems can be serious.

NSW Health says brief exposure to bushfire smoke during pregnancy is unlikely to affect you or your baby. If you are continuously exposed to smoke for more than one week, it may increase the risk of preterm birth, lower birth weight and developing gestational diabetes.

## What to do

- **Stay indoors when smoke is in the air:** Queensland Government says that when smoke is in the air but a fire is not directly threatening you, stay indoors and close all windows and doors. If you use an air conditioner, set it to recycle or recirculate.
- **Take steps to reduce your exposure** to all sources of smoke during pregnancy, and talk to your doctor or midwife about managing your health when there is bushfire smoke about (NSW Health).
- **If you need to go outdoors**, wear a P2 mask from a chemist or hardware store, fitted correctly with a tight seal around your mouth and nose. RANZCOG says every mask makes breathing harder, so check with your doctor or midwife before using one in pregnancy.
- **Don't smoke or burn candles or incense** during a bushfire.
- **In hot weather**, spend time indoors with the windows and doors closed, preferably with air conditioning, drink plenty, and skip intense outdoor activity.
- **Have a plan**: if you live, work or travel near bush, grassland or farmland, make a bushfire survival plan and an emergency kit that covers your health needs.
- **If you have any symptoms**, speak to your doctor. **If you have trouble breathing or chest pain, or symptoms are severe or getting worse, call triple zero (000)** and ask for an ambulance.
