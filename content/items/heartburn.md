## What's usual

About 8 in 10 pregnant people get indigestion or heartburn at some point. Indigestion is discomfort in your stomach after eating or drinking; heartburn (reflux) is a burning pain moving up your chest towards your throat, sometimes with a bitter taste.

Hormones relax the muscle that keeps your stomach contents down, and your growing uterus pushes against your stomach. It's uncomfortable, but it won't cause problems for your baby.

Changing how you eat usually helps:

- Eat smaller meals more often, slowly.
- Don't eat for 2 or 3 hours before exercise or bed.
- Steer clear of your triggers: big meals, fatty or spicy food, chocolate, citrus juice and caffeine are common ones.
- Sit up straight to eat, and don't lie down right after.
- Raise the head of your bed or sleep on at least 2 pillows.

Antacids work quickly and are safe in pregnancy at no more than the recommended dose; ask your pharmacist which suits you. If they don't help, your doctor can prescribe other safe medicines.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If what feels like heartburn comes with chest pain or shortness of breath, call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance: chest pain is treated as your heart until it's proven otherwise.

## Contact your hospital now

After 20 weeks, heartburn that doesn't go away with antacids can be a sign of pre-eclampsia, which can cause pain under your ribs and a feeling of heartburn. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if antacids don't fix it, or it comes with severe pain below the ribs, sudden swelling of your hands, face or feet, a headache simple pain relief doesn't fix, or vision problems.

Seek immediate care too if you're vomiting up blood, losing weight, or find swallowing painful or difficult.

## See your doctor or midwife

Also see your doctor if your heartburn gets severe, persistent or worse.
