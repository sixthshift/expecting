How many appointments you have, and when, depends on your health, any risk factors for you or the baby, your stage of pregnancy and any problems along the way. You get the plan at your first visit.

## How many

- **First pregnancy, no problems:** about 10 appointments.
- **If you've had uncomplicated pregnancies before:** about 7.

If your pregnancy becomes complicated, your midwife or doctor may need to increase the number of appointments, and you may need more tests and scans. You can also organise to see your doctor or midwife whenever you have a problem or concern.

## The first visit

It's best to have your first visit before 10 weeks, ideally at about 6 to 8 weeks. It's a longer appointment than the rest, and your doctor or midwife will talk through your model of care and how many visits you'll have and when.

## Who you'll see

- **Public hospital:** likely a doctor or midwife at the hospital.
- **Birth centre:** probably the midwife there.
- **Private hospital:** most likely your obstetrician in their rooms.
- **Home birth:** probably a midwife, at home, the hospital or elsewhere in the community.

## Visits and tests with something extra

The timing of some visits and tests can shift slightly with your type of care, whether you've been pregnant before and any complications. For an uncomplicated pregnancy, the usual ones are:

- **10 to 13 weeks:** screening options such as a nuchal translucency scan (11 to 14 weeks).
- **18 to 22 weeks:** an ultrasound scan of your baby's body and organs.
- **24 to 28 weeks:** a glucose tolerance test for diabetes.
- **28 weeks:** a blood test for anaemia and platelets, and RSV vaccination offered from 28 weeks.
- **35 to 37 weeks:** a possible group B strep swab, and a check of your baby's presentation.
- **41 weeks:** if you haven't had your baby, a heartbeat check and extra ultrasounds offered.
