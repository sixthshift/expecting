## What's usual

Mild headaches are common in pregnancy, most of all in the first few months, when your hormones are changing. They usually get better as the pregnancy goes on. Poor sleep, stress, dehydration, low blood sugar and eye strain can all set one off.

If you get migraines, pregnancy may change them: up to 7 in 10 people who get migraines find they improve, usually in the second and third trimesters.

Try rest and fluids first: a drink of water, a nap or rest with your eyes closed, a cold or heat pack on your forehead or neck, a walk in fresh air. If it doesn't settle, you can take paracetamol, for the shortest time you can. It's best not to take anti-inflammatories such as ibuprofen or aspirin; check with your doctor, pharmacist or midwife before taking any medicine.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

Call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance, or go to your nearest hospital emergency department, if you have a sudden, severe headache, or a headache with vomiting, confusion, a stiff neck or changes in your vision.

## Contact your hospital now

After 20 weeks, a headache can be a sign of pre-eclampsia. Call your doctor or midwife straightaway if you have a very bad headache, blurred vision, pain below your ribs, vomiting, or sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet.

If you have a severe headache that isn't usual for you, seek medical help immediately.

## See your doctor or midwife

Tell your doctor or midwife if you start getting frequent headaches. If you have a headache that begins suddenly or is different to your past headaches, speak to your doctor.
