Yes. KEMH says suppositories and ointments containing an anaesthetic and/or steroid (for example Proctosedyl and Rectinol) are considered safe in pregnancy. MotherSafe says widely used over-the-counter products are not considered to increase risks to your baby at any stage of pregnancy, and that ointments and suppositories are both safe. They ease the symptoms but won't cure the haemorrhoids themselves.

## Why

Haemorrhoids are common in pregnancy, particularly in the second and third trimesters. Hormone changes, pressure from the baby, changes in blood flow and constipation all contribute. Most go away after the baby is born.

## What to do

- **Use as directed:** KEMH says up to three times a day, best applied after a bowel motion, for no longer than seven days. MotherSafe says some products carry maximum-use warnings because steroids and local anaesthetics may change the skin or raise infection risk with prolonged use, and that it may be preferable to switch products over time.
- **Treat the constipation.** It's the most common treatable cause, and laxatives are considered safe in pregnancy.
- **Try the simple things:** a deep warm bath, ice packs on an external haemorrhoid, fibre and fluids, and not straining.
- **See a doctor** for diagnosis, particularly if there is bleeding and significant pain, to rule out other conditions.
