In about 1 in 12 pregnancies after 37 weeks, the waters break before labour starts (term prelabour rupture of membranes, or PROM). Once the membranes have broken, infection can reach you and the baby, and that risk is why the timing matters. If your waters break, put on a pad and call your midwife, doctor or birth centre; they'll check the diagnosis, your baby's position and how you and the baby are.

## The options

- **Induction (active management).** Starting labour, usually with an oxytocin drip in Australia; prostaglandins may be used if your cervix isn't ready. RANZCOG (C-Obs 36, current July 2021, review due July 2026) recommends induction within 24 hours where practical. RANZCOG concludes that planned early birth leads to fewer infections for you and the baby and greater satisfaction, without an increase in caesareans.
- **Waiting (expectant management).** Letting labour start on its own. RANZCOG says some people may choose this after being told about the increased risks.

## Things to weigh

- **How soon labour usually comes.** RANZCOG: about 70% start labour within 24 hours, 85% within 48 hours and 95% within 96 hours. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says most go into labour within 24 to 96 hours, and most give birth within 24 to 48 hours of their waters breaking.
- **Group B strep.** If you carry GBS, RANZCOG recommends antibiotics and an early planned birth (its plain-language summary says labour induced straight away); Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says your team may recommend induction as soon as possible.
- **Other things that complicate it:** a previous baby with GBS disease, an infection, meconium in the fluid, or a previous caesarean.
- **If you'd rather wait.** RANZCOG says that ideally the baby is head down and settled, you're GBS negative, there are no signs of infection, the baby's heart trace and movements are normal, the fluid is clear, you can get to hospital reliably, and there's a clear plan for review. You'd check your temperature every 4 hours while awake, and watch your fluid loss and the baby's movements.
- **After 18 hours.** If your waters break 18 hours or more before the birth, your baby will need their vital signs checked for their first 24 hours.

Your choices, preferences and cultural needs will always be respected, and your team will weigh your baby's wellbeing, your history and any risk factors with you.

## When to get help

**Call your doctor, midwife or birthing unit immediately for advice, or go to hospital,** if the fluid is green, brown or blood coloured.

**Call your midwife, doctor or hospital immediately** if your waters have broken and:

- they smell bad
- you're bleeding from your vagina
- you have a temperature of 37.5 °C or above
- you feel unwell or have flu-like symptoms
- your baby's movements change
- you have regular painful contractions or abdominal pain

If your waters break **before 37 weeks**, contact your midwife or doctor and go to the hospital.

## When to decide

When your waters break: if it's after 37 weeks, your midwife or doctor will discuss the options with you then.
