Early blood tests check the pregnant parent's blood group. If the pregnant parent is **RhD negative** and the baby is RhD positive, the body can make antibodies that affect this or a future baby. It's rare these days because it can usually be prevented with anti-D injections.

Anti-D is usually offered at about **28 and 34 weeks**, and again after the birth if the baby turns out to be RhD positive. It's also offered after some events, such as heavy bleeding, an injury to the belly, or procedures like amniocentesis.

There's now a blood test (RhD NIPT), from about 11 weeks, that can predict the baby's RhD type — so some people won't need the injections at all. Ask your midwife or OB.
