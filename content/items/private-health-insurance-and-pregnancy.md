**The waiting period.** When you start a policy or raise your level of cover, you must serve waiting periods before you can claim. For pregnancy and birth (obstetrics) the maximum is 12 months, and most policies use it. During that time you can't claim any pregnancy-related expenses. To have private obstetric care paid for, you need the right cover at least 12 months before you become pregnant, or you pay for private care yourself.

**What it pays.** Private health insurance may cover part or all of a private hospital stay, some out-of-pocket fees for private doctors or midwives, and some antenatal tests Medicare doesn't. It only applies once you're admitted to hospital for the birth, not to your pregnancy visits. Your obstetrician's consultations and check-ups, and a portion of the hospital fees, may still be out of pocket.

**Check the policy.** Some don't cover obstetrics or midwifery at all, or pay restricted benefits, such as covering you only as a private patient in a public hospital. Your fund may also charge an excess or co-payment.

**Cover for the baby.** Ask your fund whether the baby is covered automatically or whether you need a family policy. Many funds ask you to upgrade to a family policy 1 to 3 months before the baby is born; some ask up to 12 months before, so any congenital conditions are covered. A healthy baby usually isn't formally admitted, but a premature or unwell one may be, and that can raise costs significantly on a single policy. Twins or more can increase costs significantly.

**No cover?** A private hospital birth means high out-of-pocket costs. Everyone with a Medicare number can have high-quality care in a public hospital or birth centre, fully covered by Medicare.
