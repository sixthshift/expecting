The hours after the birth are not a good time to work out who to tell. Settle it now.

**Who hears first.** Usually the grandparents and siblings, by phone, before anything is written. Agree the order between you, and whether it's straight away or after you've had some time alone with the baby.

**Who tells whom.** Often the partner does the calls and messages while the birth parent rests. Ask the first people you tell to keep it to themselves until you've told everyone you want to tell yourselves.

**What to share.** The name (if you've decided), the date and time, the weight, and that everyone's well. Some people leave out details or wait to share the name. You don't owe anyone the birth story.

**Social media.** Agree whether you'll post, when, which photo, and ask family not to post before you do. Some couples keep the baby's face, full name or birth date off public posts.

**Get it ready now.**

- A list of who to call and who to message, with numbers in one phone.
- A message drafted with gaps for the name, time and weight.
- A group chat set up for the wider family, so it's one message, not twenty.

**Later.** Printed announcement cards, a newspaper notice or a post with the newborn photos can come weeks later. Many people send cards with the thank-you notes.

If things don't go to plan, nobody has to be told anything until you're ready. Someone close can let others know for you.
