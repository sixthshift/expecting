If you have private health insurance, you can choose to be treated as a private patient in a public hospital rather than going to a private hospital. As a private patient you'll usually have your baby in a private hospital, but this is the other option. Electing to be private lets you choose your obstetrician or midwife.

## What you get

- **Your own obstetrician, sometimes.** It's sometimes possible for your private obstetrician to attend your birth in a public hospital. If you have a particular obstetrician in mind, find out where they practise.

## What you don't get, and what you pay

- **No guaranteed single room.**
- **Hospital, medical and diagnostic costs** are yours: the hospital stay, medical fees, and tests such as blood tests and ultrasounds. Your insurance may not fully cover the hospital and medical costs of the birth.
- **Pregnancy visits** aren't covered by insurance at all, though Medicare covers part of them.

## Check your cover first

- Most funds need you to have been a member for at least 12 months before they cover maternity services.
- Some policies only pay restricted benefits, for example covering you only as a private patient in a public hospital, so read yours carefully.
- Ask your fund, the hospital and your obstetrician what out-of-pocket costs to expect. Most hospitals have liaison officers who can help with questions.

If you have a Medicare card, you can always be a public patient instead: care in a public hospital or birth centre is covered by Medicare.
