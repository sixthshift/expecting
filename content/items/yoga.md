Yes. Yoga for pregnancy can build muscle strength, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says both yoga and Pilates have been shown to improve mental health through focused breathing and mindfulness. Make sure your instructor knows you are pregnant, or choose a class designed for pregnant people, ideally with an instructor trained in prenatal yoga.

## Why

Your body changes as the pregnancy goes on, so some poses stop being suitable in the second and third trimesters. Heat is a concern too: you shouldn't let your body temperature get too high.

## What to do

- **Skip hot or humid rooms.**
- **Don't lie on your back** after the first trimester. The weight of your baby may push against your blood vessels, which can cut off supply to your baby or make you feel faint.
- **Don't push stretches.** Hormonal changes relax your ligaments, so you can be injured if you stretch too hard. Warm up and warm down.
- **Avoid movements that could unbalance you,** make you fall, make you jump and bounce, or change direction suddenly. Your centre of gravity changes as your baby grows.
- **Walking lunges and wide squats** are not recommended.
- **Stop** if you feel pain or discomfort.
- **If a bulge develops down the middle of your abdomen** (diastasis recti), avoid abdominal exercises.
