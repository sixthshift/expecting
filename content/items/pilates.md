Yes. Pilates for pregnancy can build muscle strength, and pregnancy classes often include pelvic floor exercises. Let your instructor know you are pregnant, or choose a class designed for pregnant people. Look for an instructor trained in prenatal Pilates; Pregnancy, Birth and Baby points to the Pilates Alliance Australasia.

## Why

The Royal Women's says clinical Pilates is one of the best group options for working your abdominals. Ordinary sit-ups and crunches are inappropriate and ineffective in pregnancy, it says, because they are done lying on your back and your stretched abdominal muscles can't work the way they used to.

## What to do

- **Don't exercise lying on your back** after the first trimester. The weight of your baby may push against your blood vessels, which can cut off supply to your baby or make you feel faint.
- **Avoid hot or humid rooms.**
- **Avoid movements that could unbalance you,** make you jump and bounce, or change direction suddenly.
- **Stop** if you feel pain or discomfort.
- **If a bulge develops down the middle of your abdomen** (diastasis recti), avoid abdominal exercises.
