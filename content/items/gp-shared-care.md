Your GP is likely to be the first health professional you see when you think you're pregnant, and some GPs offer 'shared care', where you see your GP for some of your routine pregnancy care visits. This is called shared care, and it's one of the four most common models of maternity care in Australia.

## How it works

Your pregnancy care is shared between your local doctor or midwife and the hospital's maternity team, working together under an agreed plan. Check-ups happen at your GP clinic or midwifery practice, or at the hospital's outpatient clinic, on a schedule usually agreed in advance so everyone knows who does each part. Your tests and scans may be done at the hospital or organised by your GP.

The birth usually happens in the hospital, with midwives and doctors. In some places, especially rural or regional areas, your community doctor or midwife may also be involved. Early care after the birth is usually in hospital, with some support sometimes continuing in the community.

GPs with or without extra obstetric training may provide shared care, in partnership with local midwives, Aboriginal Health Workers or even a private obstetrician. The Royal Women's describes shared care as being with a local doctor or midwife who is affiliated with the hospital, so ask whether yours is.

## Who it suits

Shared care is a popular choice if you're healthy and have a low-risk pregnancy. It can be more convenient, and cheaper.

## What it costs

Medicare covers bulk-billed GP visits, so you pay nothing for those. Many GPs charge more than the Medicare rebate, and you pay the difference, the 'gap'. The Royal Women's says Medicare usually covers the cost of the visits that aren't at the hospital.

## GP obstetricians

Some GPs do extra training in obstetrics and women's health to become GP obstetricians. They often provide pregnancy care and deliver babies in rural areas.

## Getting started

Ask your GP how shared care works locally. They can refer you to the hospital, and a referral helps share information between your GP and whoever cares for you in pregnancy.
