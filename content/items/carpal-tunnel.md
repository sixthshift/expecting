## What's usual

Carpal tunnel syndrome is common in pregnancy: up to 1 in 2 develop it, more often in the second and third trimesters. Hormones cause fluid to build up and soften your ligaments, and swelling in your wrist squashes the nerve that runs through it.

You might have pins and needles, pain, numbness or weakness in your hand, burning fingers, or pain darting from your wrist or spreading to your arm or shoulder. It mostly affects the thumb, index and middle fingers, in one or both hands, and is often worse at night or early in the morning. In most cases it gets better within 4 weeks of the birth.

What helps:

- raise your hands when you can, and keep your wrist straight, not bent forwards or back
- avoid repetitive tasks and heavy lifting; at a computer, keep good posture and take a break every 20 minutes
- don't sleep with your wrist bent under your chin or the sheets; sleep on the side of your less affected hand
- ice wrapped in a tea towel for 20 to 30 minutes every 2 to 3 hours, or cold running water

## Contact your hospital now

After 20 weeks, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately if you have sudden, severe swelling in your hands, feet or face.

## See your doctor or midwife

If you have symptoms of carpal tunnel syndrome, see your doctor or midwife; early treatment helps. They may send you to a physiotherapist or occupational therapist for exercises and a wrist splint or brace, which is one of the best ways to relieve symptoms, especially worn overnight. A steroid injection is another option.
