Surrogacy is when one person carries a pregnancy for someone else, the intended parents. After the birth, the intended parents receive the baby and become the legal parents. There are many legal and medical steps, so get professional advice and make sure you have support. In Expecting, the two of you may be the intended parents rather than the person carrying.

## The rules

- **Surrogacy arrangements must be altruistic.** The surrogate is not paid for carrying the pregnancy. However, the intended parents must pay for the surrogate's pregnancy-related costs, which is legal in Australia. Generally they're expected to cover reasonable costs, including the surrogate's medical, legal, counselling and travel expenses.
- **Commercial surrogacy is banned** in Australia (that's when a surrogate is paid for their services, on top of being reimbursed for pregnancy-related costs), and in some states it's also illegal for residents to use it overseas.
- **Each state and territory has its own law,** with eligibility criteria that can include age, residence, health, relationship status, sexual orientation, gender, and whether the surrogate has had children. Some states don't allow advertising for a surrogate. Get legal advice before you start.
- **A formal surrogacy agreement** is made, usually before starting, but it can't be enforced: neither the surrogate nor the intended parents can be forced to give up or take the baby. However, the surrogate can force the intended parents to pay them back for any costs involved in the process.
- **Most Australian surrogacy is gestational:** the embryo comes from the intended parents or donors, so the surrogate isn't genetically related to the baby. Traditional surrogacy, with the surrogate's own egg, is allowed only in some states.

## Parentage after the birth

At birth the surrogate, and their partner if they have one, is legally the baby's parent. Parentage passes to the intended parents by court order, and the process differs by state and territory: another reason for legal advice early.

## Counselling and the relationship

The surrogate, the intended parents and their partners must all have counselling before starting. It helps everyone understand the issues, feel comfortable with the process, and think through practical and emotional questions you might not foresee. Rainbow Families' Australian Surrogacy Process Chart describes surrogacy here as relationship-centred, built on trust and open communication within the team.

The surrogate has the right to choose how they manage the pregnancy and birth, even though the baby is intended for you.

## Cost

Surrogacy Australia estimates around $55,000 to $75,000 over 2 years, covering legal fees, IVF, counselling, the surrogate's costs, insurance and travel, and the parentage order. Medicare doesn't subsidise surrogacy; check what your private health insurance covers.

## Getting started

See a fertility specialist; your GP can refer you. The federal Attorney-General's Department's Surrogacy in Australia website explains the laws.
