Placenta accreta is a rare but serious complication where the placenta implants too deeply into the wall of the uterus, so it stays attached after the birth. This can cause heavy, life-threatening bleeding. It is more likely after a caesarean, fibroid removal or several D&Cs.

It can be difficult to diagnose: it may be found on a routine antenatal ultrasound, with an MRI sometimes used to confirm, or only after the birth. If it is found beforehand, a team of specialists plans your care. It's usually best to give birth in a major hospital, in most cases it is safest to deliver by caesarean, and it usually means having your baby early.
