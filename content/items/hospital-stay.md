You might spend a few hours or up to a few days in hospital. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says how long depends on your recovery, whether you've had a caesarean, and whether you or your baby have any complications. Stays vary by hospital, so treat what follows as the shape of it, not a promise.

## Typical stays

- **Uncomplicated vaginal birth.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you may be able to go home 4 to 6 hours after the birth in a public hospital or birth centre. King Edward Memorial Hospital (Perth) says you can expect to go home from 4 hours after an uncomplicated vaginal birth, if you and your baby are well enough.
- **Caesarean.** King Edward Memorial Hospital says between 24 and 72 hours.
- **Longer.** You may need to stay longer after an assisted birth (forceps or vacuum), a caesarean, or if you or your baby is unwell. Private hospitals generally allow a longer stay than public ones, so ask your hospital what to expect.

## Going home early

If you go home early from a public hospital, midwives will usually visit you at home with care for you and your baby, including breastfeeding advice. Most public hospitals offer at least one home visit, then hand over to community care such as your child health nurse and local doctor.

## Before you leave

King Edward Memorial Hospital lists what's done before you go home. For you: a health check, eating and drinking, passing urine, any pain managed, moving around, and referrals or instructions for follow-up. For your baby: a health check, a feeding plan, vitamin K and hepatitis B considered, birth registration paperwork and the Health Record given and explained. For going home: discharge medication arranged, transport and a baby car seat, and a safe sleeping environment for your baby discussed.

## Visitors and support people

Visiting hours can vary between hospitals, so Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's a good idea to check when you can have visitors.
