Parental Leave Pay is the Australian Government's payment for parents caring for a newborn, under the Paid Parental Leave scheme. You claim it through Services Australia, but in many cases your employer delivers it to you; sometimes Services Australia pays you directly.

## How much

It's paid at the national minimum wage: **$200.94 a day before tax, or $1,004.70 for a 5-day week**, for days taken in 2026–27. It is taxable.

The rate usually changes each 1 July, and you get the rate for the financial year the days fall in, not the year your baby was born.

## How many days

It depends on your baby's date of birth. For a child born:

- from 1 July 2026: up to **130 days** (26 weeks of 5-day weeks)
- from 1 July 2025: up to 120 days
- from 1 July 2024: up to 110 days

That's for the whole family. If you have a partner, some of those days are reserved for them, and you decide together how to use the rest. Twins, triplets or more get one child's worth of days.

## Who can get it

All of these must apply. You must:

- be caring for the newborn
- meet the [income test](parental-leave-pay-income-test)
- meet the [work test](parental-leave-pay-work-test)
- meet the residence rules
- have registered, or applied to register, your baby's birth with your state or territory registry.

You can't work on a day you get Parental Leave Pay, except for allowable reasons.

## Fitting it around other leave

You can take Parental Leave Pay before, during or after any paid or unpaid leave from your employer, including maternity or parental leave, annual leave and long service leave.

## What to do, and by when

Claim before the birth: you can lodge up to 3 months before the due date, which lets you choose your start date. How to do it is in [Claim Parental Leave Pay before the birth](parental-leave-pay-claim). After the birth, at least one parent must claim within 52 weeks, and all the days must be used within 2 years of the birth.
