If you take a medicine every day for your mental health, the first rule is simple: don't stop it or change the dose without first talking to your doctor. That's a conversation to have early, ideally before you start trying for a baby, or as soon as you know you're pregnant.

## What to know

- **Some medicines are safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding,** including most SSRIs, which are used for depression and anxiety.
- **Others may need changing.** For some, such as some medicines for bipolar disorder, there isn't enough evidence to say whether they should be stopped. Valproic acid is not recommended in pregnancy.
- **Treatment matters.** The Royal Women's Hospital says an untreated mental illness can have long-term effects on your health and wellbeing and your baby's, and that there are safe and effective treatment options for depression during pregnancy and while breastfeeding.

## How the decision gets made

Your doctor will explain the risks and benefits of your treatment options: what the medicines can do in pregnancy, and also how stopping them might affect your mental health. Then you decide together. This is called shared decision making, and your wishes, culture and health needs all count.

Medicine is one part of it. Other treatments include talking therapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, yoga, relaxation and mindfulness, and childbirth and parenting classes.

Your doctor may suggest a referral to a perinatal mental health team, who specialise in mental illness during pregnancy and after the birth.

## Who to ask, and when

Talk to your doctor or midwife:

- before you start trying for a baby, to plan your care
- during the pregnancy, if you or the people around you are worried you're not your usual self
- towards the end of the pregnancy, to plan for after the birth and for breastfeeding if you plan to

## When to get help

It's OK to feel overwhelmed, anxious or low. If those feelings don't go away or are getting worse, reach out: your perinatal mental health team, midwife, GP or obstetrician. To talk to someone about your mental health, call Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636.
