If you've chosen to breastfeed, the first feed usually happens during skin-to-skin time soon after the birth. Your baby is placed straight onto your bare chest, and from there many babies can move towards the breast, attach and start feeding on their own. This is sometimes called baby-led attachment.

## Skin to skin first

Being close to you helps regulate your baby's temperature, heartbeat, breathing and blood sugar. A settled baby is more able to stay alert and start feeding. If you have a caesarean, you should still be able to have skin-to-skin contact after the birth.

If you can, the Australian Breastfeeding Association suggests staying skin to skin for at least the first hour after the birth, and until after the first feed, even if your baby feeds sooner. Some routine checks, such as weighing and measuring, can often wait, and so can cuddles with your partner or family. If skin to skin has to pause for medical care, your baby can have it with another adult until you're ready.

## Watching your baby get ready

Many babies are quietly alert after the birth. You might see your baby move their head from side to side, bring their hands to their mouth, suck their fingers, lick or nuzzle your breast, and wriggle closer to the nipple. There's no need to rush. For some babies the first feed comes within the first hour; for others it takes longer, and resting, licking and nuzzling are all part of getting there.

Some babies need more time because of medicines or interventions used during the birth, or simply go at their own pace. They may rest, then wake and try again. Your midwife can help check your positioning and attachment.

## Colostrum

The first milk is colostrum: thick, yellow and made in only small amounts, which is perfect for your baby's very small tummy. Your baby may want to feed often, perhaps every hour; as a rough guide, 8 to 12 times or more over the first 24 hours. After the first feed, many babies fall into a deep sleep and may not wake to feed for a while.

## If the first feed is delayed

You can still breastfeed if skin-to-skin time is delayed. If needed, your midwife will show you how to express breastmilk until your baby is ready to breastfeed.

For help, talk with your midwife or a lactation consultant, or call the Australian Breastfeeding Association's Breastfeeding Helpline on 1800 686 268.
