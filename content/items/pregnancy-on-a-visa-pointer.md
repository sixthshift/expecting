Yes. If you're on a visa, you can get care for yourself and your baby during and after pregnancy and birth. What it costs depends on your visa and your health insurance.

## Medicare

Medicare covers many pregnancy and newborn costs: doctor and midwife appointments, ultrasounds, blood tests, birth in a public hospital, hospital care for your baby and your baby's vaccinations. You can enrol if you're an Australian or New Zealand citizen, a permanent resident, applying for permanent residency, or on some temporary visas. Services Australia lists which visas are included.

## A reciprocal healthcare agreement

Belgium, Finland, the Republic of Ireland, Italy, Malta, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Slovenia, Sweden and the United Kingdom have reciprocal agreements with Australia. If you're from one of them, you should be able to get free urgent medical treatment and can apply for limited Medicare cover. Each agreement is different: for example, people on student visas from Finland, Malta, Norway or the Republic of Ireland aren't included. You'll need private insurance for anything non-urgent, ambulances and private care.

## Private insurance

If you can't get a Medicare card, you may have to pay the full cost of any treatment, so take out Overseas Visitors Health Cover (OVHC), even if your visa doesn't require it. Some policies don't cover pregnancy and birth, and there can be a waiting period of up to 12 months, so check before you become pregnant. Students need Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for their whole stay; it can cover pregnancy and birth, usually after a 12-month wait, though emergencies are covered.

## With no insurance

You can still get care in pregnancy, at the birth and afterwards, but you'll pay the full cost for you and your baby. Call your local hospital to find out its fees.

## Your baby

A baby born in Australia is an Australian citizen if at least one parent is a citizen or permanent resident. If you and the other parent are on visas, your baby automatically has the same visas as you, and you need to tell the Department of Home Affairs about the birth. If your baby can't get Medicare, add them to your health insurance as soon as you can: treatment after the birth could cost a lot without it.

## Language help

TIS National (131 450) provides interpreters for healthcare appointments, usually free.
