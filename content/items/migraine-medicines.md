Migraines can be treated in pregnancy, and you don't need to suffer needlessly. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist about which medicines suit you.

## Why

Untreated migraine can lead to poor nutrition, dehydration, lack of sleep, depression and anxiety.

- **Paracetamol, or an opioid such as codeine,** is the recommended first choice.
- **Sumatriptan:** if those don't help or aren't suitable, occasional use is generally considered safe. There's considerable data on sumatriptan, but not on other triptans.
- **Anti-inflammatories** such as ibuprofen, aspirin and naproxen are no longer recommended in pregnancy and should only be used under medical supervision.

## What to do

- **If you take a preventer,** don't just stop. MotherSafe suggests people with frequent migraines consider staying on their preventer, and talk through its safety with a health professional. If a preventer is prescribed, propranolol, cyproheptadine or amitriptyline are generally considered the safest.
- **After 20 weeks, contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately** if you have a very bad headache, pain below your ribs, vomiting, or sudden swelling in your face, hands or feet: these can be signs of pre-eclampsia. A headache with changes in your vision is a 000 call.
- **Tell your doctor or midwife** if you start getting frequent headaches. Unexplained, frequent headaches later in pregnancy or within 4 weeks of the birth could be a sign of pre-eclampsia.
