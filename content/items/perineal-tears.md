As the baby is born, the skin and muscles between your vagina and anus (the perineum) stretch, and sometimes they tear. Tearing is common: about 8 in 10 people who give birth vaginally have some form of tear.

## The degrees

- **First degree:** small and skin-deep, in the perineum or labia. These can often heal without stitches.
- **Second degree:** deeper, into the muscles of the perineum. Stitches are usually recommended, though a small one can heal well without.
- **Third degree:** through the deeper muscles to the anal sphincter, the muscle that keeps the anus closed.
- **Fourth degree:** reaching the lining of the anus or rectum.

## What makes a tear more likely

It's your first baby, you're of Southeast Asian background, you've had a third or fourth degree tear before, the baby weighs more than 4 kg or lies with their back against yours (posterior), the baby's shoulders get stuck, or you need forceps or other instruments.

## What may lower the chance

Pelvic floor exercises and perineal massage from 35 weeks may help. In labour, warm compresses on the perineum in the second stage, breathing techniques and moving around can encourage a slow, controlled birth. Ask your hospital what it does to lower the chance of a serious tear.

## The repair

After the birth your doctor or midwife checks for tears, and may offer a finger in your bottom to make sure none were missed. They won't repair a tear without talking to you first. Most first and second degree tears are stitched in the room where you gave birth, under your epidural or a local anaesthetic. Third and fourth degree tears are recommended to be repaired surgically, often in an operating theatre, to reduce the chance of incontinence.

## Recovery

Pain or soreness is normal for 2 to 3 weeks, especially walking or sitting. The stitches usually dissolve within 3 weeks, and full healing can take 6 to 8 weeks. Rest on your side, keep the area clean and dry, change pads often, avoid constipation and straining, and start gentle pelvic floor exercises 2 to 3 days after the birth, or when it's comfortable.

## When to get help

Contact your doctor or midwife straight away if you have a temperature of 38°C or more.

Most people have a postnatal check at 6 weeks, but talk to your doctor earlier if you're worried about healing, or if:

- there's an unusual smell or discharge from the tear
- the area becomes inflamed, swollen or red
- it still hurts after 2 to 3 weeks
- you can't control your bowels or wind
- sex is painful
- you have a heavy, dragging feeling in your vagina
