Yes, when your doctor prescribes them. A common reason in pregnancy is a urinary tract infection (UTI), the most common bacterial infection in pregnancy, and UTIs are treated with antibiotics that are safe in pregnancy.

## Why

An untreated infection is the bigger worry. If a UTI isn't treated, you may develop kidney problems, and in pregnancy a bladder infection is more likely to lead to a kidney infection. Bacteria in the urine during pregnancy carry a small risk of preterm labour or a baby that's underweight. That's why treatment matters even when you have no symptoms.

MotherSafe says that, in general, all the antibiotics commonly used to treat UTIs are not considered to be of concern in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

## What to do

- **See your midwife or doctor** if you have any symptoms of a UTI, such as pain or burning when you wee, needing to go more often, or a fever. It's important not to delay getting antibiotics: an infection can develop quickly and lead to problems for you and your baby.
- **Let your doctor choose.** They pick the antibiotic based on the bacteria in your urine sample.
- **Asking about a particular antibiotic?** Contact MotherSafe (Sydney metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848) or ask your healthcare provider.
- **A repeat urine test** a few weeks after you finish the antibiotics may be recommended, to make sure the infection has gone.
