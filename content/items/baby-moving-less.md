## What's usual

Once you can feel your baby regularly, they have their own pattern, and they keep moving every day right up to the birth. It's easiest to feel them sitting quietly or lying on your side, especially in the evening.

There's no number of kicks to reach: counting kicks or keeping a chart is no longer recommended. What matters is a change from this baby's own pattern: moving less and less, movements that aren't as strong, or no movement at all.

Don't try to check for yourself. A home doppler or an app can't tell you the baby is well, and eating or drinking something doesn't make a baby move.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you need urgent medical help, call triple zero (000).

## Contact your hospital now

If the movements slow down, weaken, change or stop, contact your midwife, doctor or hospital birth unit straight away. Don't wait until the next day. If you can't reach them, go to your nearest hospital birthing or emergency unit.

They'll usually ask you to come in, and check the baby's heart rate with a CTG machine; sometimes you'll have an ultrasound as well.

Call again every time it happens, even if you've already been checked. You are not wasting their time.
