A bond is an emotional connection between you and your baby. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says you don't need to wait until your baby is born to start one, and that one of the best times to start is during pregnancy. The Gidget Foundation adds that, like any relationship, bonds take time to develop.

## Ways in

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests you can:

- talk and sing to your baby
- gently touch, rub or massage your belly
- respond to your baby's kicks by tracing your belly where they kick
- play calming music

Your baby's hearing becomes fully functional at around week 25, and they may be soothed by your voice. It also suggests giving yourself time to think and reflect, going for a walk, having a warm bath, or writing a diary or stories to the baby.

## For partners

Partners, family members and close friends can bond with your baby during pregnancy too. If you're not the pregnant parent, you can massage the bump if they're happy for you to, feel the baby kicking, attend ultrasound appointments, and read and talk to the baby so they get used to your voice. Supporting your partner through the pregnancy and birth is also a way of bonding.

## Other children

You can talk to them about the baby, read them stories about pregnancy and babies, let them feel the kicks, take them shopping for baby supplies, and let them help set up the nursery.

## If you're struggling with your feelings

Instead of excitement, you might feel stressed, confused, anxious or depressed. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says up to 1 in 5 people have depression while pregnant, and that you should talk to your doctor or midwife if you've had a mental health issue before, are very stressed, or are struggling with difficult feelings. They can make a treatment plan and help you find support. You can also call Beyond Blue on 1300 22 4636.
