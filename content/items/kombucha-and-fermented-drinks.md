Fermented drinks such as kombucha, kefir, kvass and ginger beer can contain alcohol left over from fermentation. The NSW Food Authority names all four as drinks that present a higher risk in pregnancy. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby lists kombucha, kefir and ginger beer under alcohol on its foods-to-avoid page.

## Why

There's no safe level of alcohol in pregnancy. Alcohol can harm your unborn baby: it can cause fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD) and increase the risk of miscarriage, stillbirth or premature birth.

How much alcohol a fermented drink holds isn't always what you'd expect. MotherSafe says commercial products generally contain only trace amounts, but some have been recalled because they contained more significant amounts.

Home-brewed drinks have no regulation and should be avoided in pregnancy: their alcohol levels may be higher, and they may also contain other contaminants.

## What to do

- **Leave home-brewed kombucha, kefir and ginger beer** until after the pregnancy.
- **Shop-bought ones too.** The NSW Food Authority puts kombucha, kvass, kefir and ginger beer under "do not drink (high risk)": they may carry leftover alcohol and have a higher contamination risk.
- **If you've already had some,** MotherSafe says the risk is likely to be low after small amounts of alcohol, and stopping at any time in the pregnancy reduces the risk. For individual advice, call MotherSafe: Sydney Metro (02) 9382 6539, NSW non-metro 1800 647 848.
