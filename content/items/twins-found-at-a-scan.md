Most people having twins have a healthy pregnancy and healthy babies. There are more risks than with one baby, though, so your care changes. An early scan can confirm whether you're carrying one baby or more.

## Do they share a placenta?

This is the question that shapes the plan. Fraternal (non-identical) twins each have their own placenta and sac. Identical twins can be:

- **DCDA:** each with their own placenta and sac. This is the most common type of twin pregnancy.
- **MCDA:** sharing a placenta, each in their own sac.
- **MCMA:** sharing a placenta and a sac. This is rarer.

Finding out whether your twins share a placenta is very important, because that kind of pregnancy needs careful monitoring.

## What changes in your care

You'll need more health checks and ultrasounds, so problems show up early. You're also more likely to have backache, heartburn, morning sickness, swollen ankles, tiredness and varicose veins, and a twin pregnancy can be more tiring, physically and emotionally.

Your midwife or doctor may recommend against a home birth or birth centre, because of the higher risk of complications at the birth. They may recommend a hospital with services to care for twins, because twins are more likely to be born before 37 weeks and may need time in a neonatal intensive care unit or special care nursery.

## The higher chances

Some problems are twice as likely with twins: anaemia, gestational diabetes, high blood pressure and pre-eclampsia, hyperemesis gravidarum, miscarriage and congenital conditions (more likely in identical twins). Bleeding after the birth, low birth weight and premature birth are also more likely.

Twins who share a placenta can get **twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS)**, where one baby gets too much blood and the other too little. It's rare. Twins sharing one sac can have their cords tangle.

## A vanishing twin

Sometimes an early scan shows twins and by the next scan one can't be seen, because that twin miscarried early. Most of the time this doesn't cause problems for the other twin.

## Costs

A multiple pregnancy can increase your costs significantly. Twins are more likely to be born early and admitted to hospital, and some multiple births involve care in the NICU for days or weeks, which can be very expensive. Check with your hospital about likely costs and with your health fund about what's covered. As a public patient in a public hospital, most costs are covered by Medicare.

## When to get help

**Contact your doctor immediately** if your tummy feels tight, hurts or suddenly gets bigger, or you put on weight very quickly or are short of breath. These could be signs of TTTS.
