Ask your doctor or midwife first. Chiropractic and osteopathy might help, but there isn't much evidence about how safe they are in pregnancy.

## Why

There is some evidence that chiropractic therapy during pregnancy might reduce lower back and pelvic pain, and that osteopathic manipulation might reduce back pain. But the evidence on safety is limited for both, and there are reports of negative effects after spinal manipulation. Before any manipulation, the therapist should assess you to make sure it's safe.

## What to do

- **Check with your doctor or midwife** before booking a chiropractor.
- **Choosing someone:** ask your doctor or midwife for a referral or recommendation, and check the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) website that they're registered. Ask what the treatment is for, how you'll know it's working and what it costs. Don't go ahead if you're not comfortable with the answers.
- **A physiotherapist** can help you manage backache, with stretches and exercises that suit you. For pelvic girdle pain your doctor may refer you to one: they can teach you exercises and suggest changes to daily activities to avoid pain.
- **Injured a muscle or joint?** See your doctor, who may recommend a physiotherapist.
- Stop any exercise that causes or worsens your pain.
