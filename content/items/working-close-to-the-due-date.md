Yes, if you're fit for work. There isn't one right time to stop: it depends on your pregnancy, your health, your finances and your job. Parental leave usually starts up to 6 weeks before the due date, or earlier if your employer agrees.

## Why

If you want to work in the **6 weeks before your due date**, your employer can ask you for a medical certificate, **within 7 days**, saying:

- whether you're fit for work
- if you are, whether it's not recommended that you keep doing your current job, because of an illness or risk from your pregnancy or hazards in the job.

Your employer can direct you to start continuous parental leave if you don't provide the certificate within 7 days, if it says you're not fit for work, or if it says you shouldn't continue in your job and you haven't given the notice and evidence needed for unpaid parental leave. Directed leave counts towards your total parental leave.

## What to do

- **Fit, but not for this job?** If the certificate says you're fit for work but it isn't safe to continue in your normal job, you're entitled to a safe job, or no-safe-job leave if there isn't one.
- **Flexible days.** If you're working in those last 6 weeks, you can start taking flexible unpaid parental leave days then.
