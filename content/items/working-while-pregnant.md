Yes. It's safe to work while you're pregnant unless your doctor tells you it isn't. Depending on your job, you and your employer may need to make some changes.

## Why

Employers must protect pregnant workers from risks at work. SafeWork NSW lists the potential risks as chemicals and materials, excessive travel, shift work, heavy lifting, and physically or mentally exhausting work. Changes are more likely if you work with chemical or biological hazards or do physically demanding work.

Your employer needs to do a new risk assessment that takes your pregnancy into account, and repeat it at different stages or if your work changes: what isn't a risk early on can become one later. That may mean telling them sooner than you'd planned; you can do it confidentially.

## What to do

- **Raise any concerns** with your manager, HR or union representative.
- **If your usual job isn't safe**, every pregnant employee, casuals included, can move to a safe job at the same pay and hours. If there isn't one, you may get no safe job leave. Your employer can ask for a medical certificate.
- **If a task makes you dizzy, sore or unwell**, or you're not sure it's safe, stop and get advice from your doctor or midwife.
- **Look after yourself**: regular breaks, comfortable shoes and clothes, water, and a workspace set up for your changing body. Aches in your hands and arms (carpal tunnel syndrome) are more likely in pregnancy.
