A single umbilical artery (SUA), or two-vessel cord, is when the umbilical cord has one artery instead of the usual two. It may be associated with other birth defects, and it can be hard to see on an ultrasound, depending on the baby's position.

If it is found, the usual care is a thorough scan, including of the baby's heart, with chromosome testing sometimes considered. If nothing else is found, the advice is close follow-up of the baby's growth with repeat ultrasound scans as indicated clinically, and a routine examination after birth that includes a kidney ultrasound unless the kidneys were well seen on the scans in pregnancy. This guidance is from 2017 and may be past its review date, so check with your doctor or midwife for current practice.
