This one is for you, the partner. It matters, even if you smoke outside: the best thing you can do for the baby is to quit. Until you do, keep the smoke well away from the pregnant parent and the baby.

## Why

Passive smoke and vapour are never safe, and they're especially harmful to babies, children and pregnant people. In pregnancy, breathing in someone else's smoke can affect the baby's growth, health and lung development, and raise the risk of the baby being born early or stillborn.

After the birth, babies around smoke are more likely to get asthma, ear infections, bronchitis, croup, bronchiolitis and pneumonia. Passive smoke can also contribute to sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS). Red Nose says that if the baby's father smokes, the risk of sudden unexpected death in infancy (SUDI) is higher, and that a baby who shares a sleep surface with someone who smokes is at higher risk even if they never smoke in the bed.

Going to another room isn't enough: smoke moves through the house. Smoke particles also cling to your clothes, skin and hair, and settle on walls, furniture, carpet and toys. That's called third-hand smoke, and it can harm people too.

## What to do

- **Quit if you can.** Talk to your doctor, and call Quitline on 13 7848. The national pregnancy care guidelines say partners who smoke or vape should be offered a referral to Quitline too.
- **Ask about quitting medicines.** Varenicline and bupropion aren't recommended for the pregnant parent, but Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says they may suit other members of the household. Your doctor can tell you whether they suit you.
- **Until then, smoke outside.** Close the doors and windows so the smoke doesn't come in, and move away from other people while you smoke or vape.
- **Never smoke in the car.** In every state and territory it's illegal to smoke in a car with a minor in it (under 16, 17 or 18, depending on where you live).
- **Keep the baby smoke-free.** Red Nose says not to let anyone smoke near the baby: in the house, the car, or anywhere else the baby spends time.
- **Think about where the baby sleeps.** Red Nose says sharing a bed or other sleep surface with someone who smokes raises the baby's risk, even if no one smokes in the bed.
- **Ask family and friends** who smoke or vape to do it outside, away from the pregnant parent and, later, the baby.
