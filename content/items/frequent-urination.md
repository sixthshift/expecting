## What's usual

Needing to go to the toilet more often is normal, and common at every stage of pregnancy. Early on it's hormonal changes. Later, your growing uterus pushes against your bladder and makes it harder to wait, and in the last few weeks you may find it hard to empty your bladder completely. Towards the end you may leak a little when you cough, sneeze or lift something, because pregnancy can weaken your pelvic floor.

What can help:

- pelvic floor exercises: everyone benefits, during pregnancy and after, and a continence physiotherapist can help you train
- less caffeine and fewer fizzy drinks
- less high-impact exercise

It can take time after the birth for your pelvic floor to recover: some people improve in the first 3 to 6 months, others take longer.

## See your doctor or midwife

**Get care from a doctor or midwife straight away** if you see anything green or brown in fluid leaking from your vagina. It may be amniotic fluid with meconium (baby poo) in it, not urine. Amniotic fluid smells different from wee and may be pink, clear, green or brown rather than yellow; talk to your doctor or midwife if you think your waters are leaking or have broken early.

Talk to your doctor or midwife if you feel stinging, burning or pain when you wee. It may be a urinary tract infection, which needs treatment as soon as possible.

## Mention it at your next visit

If how often you need to wee worries you, or it's affecting your daily life, speak to your doctor or midwife. They can tell you what treatments and supports are available.
