In Australia, a stillbirth is the birth of a baby who has died any time from 20 weeks of pregnancy, or who weighs 400 grams or more if the length of the pregnancy isn't known. About 6 babies are stillborn every day in Australia. Often, no one knows why.

This page is here so you know what happens, gently, if it is ever you. What lowers the risk (knowing your baby's movements, going to sleep on your side from 28 weeks, not smoking) has its own pages; the Safer Baby Bundle is the national initiative behind that advice.

## If your baby has died

A stillbirth is diagnosed when the baby's heart has stopped, which an ultrasound can show. Shock, disbelief, deep sadness, anger and emptiness are all normal; there's no right or wrong way to feel. Your care team may include a bereavement midwife, a bereavement counsellor, a cultural, religious or spiritual advisor, and an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander health worker.

You will still need to give birth. In most cases your team will suggest inducing a vaginal birth rather than a caesarean, and you may be able to go home for a day or 2 first. Your feelings and preferences about the birth will be considered too.

## Time with your baby

If you choose to, you can spend time with your baby: hold, bathe and dress them, take photos and videos, and name them. You don't have to decide straight away, and you can change your mind. Some families spend a few days with their baby, or take them home for a while. The hospital can help collect keepsakes: a lock of hair, the cot card, the ID bracelet, hand and footprints.

## The decisions, in your own time

- **A funeral or memorial:** there is no rush. The hospital can help with information, and with rituals that meet your cultural, religious and spiritual needs.
- **Finding out why:** your team may suggest tests, including an autopsy. You don't have to say yes to any of them. They won't always give an answer.
- **Registration:** the hospital will give you information and support about registering your baby's birth and death, and about financial support from Centrelink. The hospital's social worker or bereavement midwife can help with the paperwork.

## Your body after

Your body will go through the usual changes after a birth, which can be a painful reminder. Your milk may come in; cold packs, a firm bra and expressing can ease it, and you can stop gradually. Bleeding can last up to 6 weeks. See your doctor about 8 to 12 weeks after the birth.

## When to get help

See your doctor immediately if you have signs of mastitis: a painful or warm breast, which may have a red patch, or flu-like symptoms such as a fever, aches or chills. Seek help from your doctor or midwife if, more than 24 hours after the birth, the bleeding suddenly increases, you pass large clots, it turns bright red, you feel dizzy, weak or sweaty or have trouble breathing, you have a high temperature, or you're worried it isn't normal. If your feelings are overwhelming and you're not coping, get professional help; parents are at higher risk of anxiety, depression and post-traumatic stress.

Red Nose Grief and Loss is on 1300 308 307, 24 hours a day. Gidget Foundation offers therapy to birthing and non-birthing parents after a stillbirth. If you or someone close to you is in crisis, call 000, or Lifeline on 13 11 14.
