A year of leave isn't one budget, it's several: the weeks when employer pay comes in, the weeks of government pay, and the weeks after both stop. Moneysmart's five steps for a budget work for each of them.

## Step 1: what comes in, and until when

Moneysmart's first step is to list every bit of income: wages, government payments, anything else. For leave, write each one against the weeks it covers:

- **Paid parental leave from your employer**, if you get it.
- **[Parental Leave Pay](parental-leave-pay)** from the government: for a child born from 1 July 2026, up to 130 days for the family, at $200.94 a day before tax, or $1,004.70 for a 5-day week, in 2026–27. It's taxable, and the rate usually changes each 1 July. If you have a partner, 20 of the days are reserved for them, and you decide together how to use the rest.
- **Annual leave and long service leave.** You can get Parental Leave Pay before, after or at the same time as them.
- **[Family Tax Benefit Part B](family-tax-benefit-b)** can't be paid on the days Parental Leave Pay is paid.

Then mark the week the money stops. If an income varies, Moneysmart suggests working out an average.

## Step 2: what goes out

List the essentials (rent or mortgage, groceries, power, gas and phone, medical costs, insurance premiums, transport), then debt repayments, then irregular costs such as car repairs and annual bills. Your bank statements show what you've missed.

## Step 3: compare, and plan the gap

The planner shows whether you'll have money left over or spend more than you earn. If you're spending more, Moneysmart points to simple ways to save, starting with one of the biggest regular costs, groceries. Savings are the safety net for unexpected costs, so you don't rely on credit or loans when an emergency happens; the planner lets you test different savings amounts.

## Super while you're off

For a child born from 1 July 2025, the ATO pays a super contribution on Parental Leave Pay, at the superannuation guarantee rate, as a lump sum after the financial year ends.

## Keep it going

Moneysmart says to review your budget when your income changes, which on leave is often, and to check it each month. The [budget planner](https://moneysmart.gov.au/budgeting/budget-planner) runs in the browser, or there's an Excel version you can use across multiple devices.
