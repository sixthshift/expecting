If you've been through IVF to get here, you've often been on a long road, and you can start the pregnancy feeling emotionally running on empty. Up to 75% of people having IVF report anxiety or depression during treatment, and it can put a great deal of stress on relationships.

## Why you might not feel how you expected

After IVF you may worry about the pregnancy despite medical reassurance, and find you can't enjoy it the way you hoped. Other people may expect you to just feel grateful, so you may not feel able to share your worries, and that can be isolating. You may also feel you aren't "allowed" to complain. That isn't true: everyone can find parts of pregnancy and parenting hard, however they got there.

Knowing that infertility makes perinatal mental health challenges more likely can help you prepare. Notice how you're feeling, name it, and let go of how you think you "should" feel. Jealousy, anger and isolation are understandable, and hormones can play a part.

## Is the pregnancy itself different?

All pregnancies carry a chance of complications. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says people who become pregnant through IVF are more likely to have some, including vaginal bleeding, blood clots in early pregnancy, high blood pressure, gestational diabetes, premature birth, a caesarean birth, and a child with a congenital disorder. Transferring more than one embryo raises the chance of twins or more. Your risk depends on your situation: ask your doctor or fertility clinic.

## The two of you

Relationships go through a lot during treatment, and you may each cope differently: one wants to talk, the other focuses on what they can control. Don't take a different style personally. Regular catch-ups that focus on the present, and being clear about what each of you needs, can help.

## Where to get support

- Talk to someone you trust. PANDA's National Helpline is 1300 726 306.
- Ask your clinic about IVF support groups, or look for one online.
- If your relationship is struggling, Gidget Foundation suggests seeing someone who specialises in couple's therapy.
- If your mood has dropped, your anxiety is too high or you're not coping, speak to your GP, who can refer you, including to the Gidget Foundation's bulk-billed sessions.
