Grief after the loss of a pregnancy or a baby doesn't completely go away, though you learn to live with it. The day your baby was due, the anniversary of the loss, pregnant friends, babies: reminders can hurt at any time, not only early on. That's normal, and part of going through such a profound loss.

## Facing the reminders

At first you may not be able to face pregnant people or be around babies. That's normal too. Go at your own pace; there's no rush, and these triggers gradually become more bearable.

When something catches you, acknowledge that it still hurts. Talk to someone if you can, or shift your focus: music, exercise, yoga or a hobby you enjoy.

## Ways to look after yourself

- Don't do anything that doesn't feel right, especially early on.
- Set aside time to think about your loss if that helps: a journal, painting or drawing.
- Mark it in your own way. Rituals that might look unusual to others can help you heal, for as long as you need them.
- Turn off social media and phone alerts for a while, and take a break from the pressure to reply.
- Find a trusted friend and tell them how you're feeling. Withdrawing from people is a common reaction; reconnect at your own pace.

Those close to you may want to know the dates that matter. Miscarriage Australia suggests friends and family note the day of the loss and the due date as times to show support.

## When to get help

If your strong feelings last a long time, or you think you might have depression, reach out. If grief is so consuming you can't function or get through daily life, contact a psychologist or other professional with bereavement training. Your doctor or midwife can help, Beyond Blue is on 1300 22 4636, and if you're in crisis, call Lifeline on 13 11 14 or 000 if anyone is at immediate risk.
