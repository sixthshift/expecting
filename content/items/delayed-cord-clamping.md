During the third stage of labour the umbilical cord is clamped and cut. This isn't painful for you or your baby.

## What usually happens

Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says clamping usually happens after the cord has stopped pulsating, usually about a minute after your baby's birth. This lets your baby receive more blood from the placenta, which may reduce the chance of iron deficiency later.

## When it's cut sooner

Your midwife or doctor may need to cut the cord immediately if you or your baby is unwell at birth.

## Why it matters

Because the cord is left until it stops pulsating, your baby gets more blood from the placenta. That may reduce the chance of iron deficiency later.

The evidence comparing the two ways of managing the third stage is limited, so Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's helpful to talk with your midwife or doctor during pregnancy about your options.

## Your preferences

If you'd like the cord kept connected for longer, or your birth partner wishes to cut the cord, tell your midwife in advance and include it in your birth preferences. With physiological management of the third stage, cord clamping is delayed until the cord stops pulsating or the placenta is out.

## Cord blood banking

You can't bank cord blood if you delay cord clamping, because the cord must be clamped early to capture as many stem cells as possible. If you'd like both, talk it through with your midwife.
