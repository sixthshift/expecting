A doula is a non-medical companion who supports you and your family before, during and after childbirth. One who works with you after the birth is called a postpartum doula.

## What a doula does

- **Before the birth:** gives you information to prepare for labour and birth, and helps you make a birth plan.
- **In labour:** reassures and motivates you, keeps you informed about progress, helps you find comfortable positions and get in and out of the shower or bath, helps with pain through position changes, stretches, massage and breathing techniques, offers drinks or ice, and supports your partner.
- **After the birth:** some visit at home and may help with housework, meals and older children, and with feeding, settling and caring for your baby. Ask what yours offers.

## How they fit with others

If you have a birth support partner, a doula won't replace them. They work with your partner to support you. Doulas can also help you communicate with your doctors and midwives.

## What they aren't

Doulas don't have medical training and it's not part of their role to give medical or health care advice. They're not a midwife. If you're having a home birth you should have a trained midwife with you.

## Cost and qualifications

Doulas charge a private fee. Check with Medicare or your private health insurance to see what's covered. The industry is unregulated and doulas aren't required to hold any specific qualifications, though many complete training. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby suggests listing questions to ask a doula, starting with what training or experience they've had.
