Ask your doctor first. There's limited evidence on cosmetic surgery in pregnancy, and cosmetic surgery that involves local anaesthesia is not recommended. If a procedure is only for cosmetic reasons, the advice is usually to wait until after your baby is born.

## Why

Local anaesthetic injections may cross the placenta, so the effects may reach your baby. Depending on how far along you are and the anaesthetic used, the risks include slowing your baby's heart rate, triggering early labour and birth differences. Adrenaline, used to stop the anaesthetic spreading, can reduce blood flow to the uterus and baby in a high dose, and may cause your baby to be born early.

## What to do

- **Small procedures** like removing skin tags, warts and other benign skin lesions are thought to be safe.
- **An urgent skin procedure**, such as removing a skin cancer, is best in the second trimester or after the birth if possible: the baby's organs are developing in the first trimester, and the third brings a risk of premature birth.
- **If you must have cosmetic surgery**, the recommendation is to wait until the second trimester. Your doctor can explain the risks for you.
- **Varicose veins** often go away on their own after the birth. Sclerotherapy is best left until you've finished breastfeeding.
- **Tell every health and beauty professional** you're pregnant or breastfeeding before you book.
