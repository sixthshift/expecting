Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says about 8 in 10 women have some form of perineal tear in a vaginal birth. A tear is more likely if it's your first baby, you have an instrumental (forceps or vacuum) delivery, or your baby weighs more than 4 kg. Tears are graded 1 to 4: first-degree tears are small and skin-deep and can often heal without stitches, second-degree tears usually need stitches, and third- and fourth-degree tears need surgical repair. Regular pelvic floor exercises and perineal massage from 35 weeks may help prevent perineal injury.
