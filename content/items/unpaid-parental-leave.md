Under the National Employment Standards, employees can access up to 12 months of unpaid parental leave, and can request up to a further 12 months: up to 24 months in all.

**Who's eligible.** You need to have, or be going to have, responsibility for the care of the child, and to have worked for your employer for at least 12 months before the expected date of birth (if you're the pregnant employee) or before the leave starts. If you started with a new employer between children, you need to work for that employer for at least 12 months before you can take parental leave.

**Casual employees** are also eligible if they've been working for their employer on a regular and systematic basis for at least 12 months, with a reasonable expectation of continuing work on that basis had it not been for the birth.

**How it's taken.** As one continuous period, as flexible days (up to 130 days for a child born on or after 1 July 2026), or a mix. For the pregnant employee, continuous leave can start up to 6 weeks before the expected birth (or earlier if the employer agrees), and has to end within 24 months of the birth.

**Both parents** can each take up to 12 months, with the same or different employers. They don't have to take it at the same time, but they can.

**What builds up while you're away.** Employees don't usually build up paid leave while on unpaid parental leave, except during a keeping in touch day or a period of paid leave (such as annual leave) taken within it. Long service leave may build up, depending on your state or territory's rules.

**What to do.** Leave needs written notice at least 10 weeks before it starts, and the dates confirmed at least 4 weeks before. See Telling your employer about parental leave.
