Having the right support people can shorten labour, lower how much pain relief you need, lower the chance of an assisted birth or caesarean, and make the experience better. Who that is, is up to you.

## The options

- **Your partner.** It's common, but you don't have to. If your partner might find it hard to see you in pain, you might choose someone else as well. RANZCOG suggests it may be better for an anxious partner to step outside for some of the labour, with someone else there to support them and you.
- **A friend or relative:** a parent, sibling or close friend. Someone who's had a baby or seen a birth before can help.
- **A doula:** a paid birth support professional. Doulas aren't medical professionals; they give emotional and practical support, and may meet you before the birth and help afterwards.
- **More than one.** Some people have more than one support person, or want someone for only part of the labour.

## Things to weigh

- **Comfort and honesty.** Choose someone you feel safe with and can be honest with, who's calm, positive and willing to help, not just there to watch.
- **Can they speak for you?** They may need to tell the team your wishes.
- **Not too many.** There are times you'll need peace and quiet, so it may be best not to have too many people around.
- **Hospital limits.** Check with your hospital or birth centre how many support people are allowed in the room.
- **A backup.** Your partner may be unwell, away, or needed at home with other children when labour starts. A backup person covers that.
- **A caesarean.** Usually you can have one support person, such as your partner, in the operating theatre. Not if you need a general anaesthetic, because you'll be asleep.

If someone you've asked doesn't feel they can give the support you need, it's best to talk about it beforehand.

## When to decide

Before labour, so your support people can come to antenatal classes and go over your birth plan with you.
