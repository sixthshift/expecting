## What's usual

Pain in the belly is common in pregnancy, and often it's nothing serious. Round ligament pain, constipation and Braxton Hicks contractions are all pregnancy causes of everyday aches. Because your body and baby are changing so fast, it can be hard to tell what's behind a pain: some pains are normal and some need checking.

Some pregnancy causes are serious: an ectopic pregnancy, a miscarriage, pre-eclampsia (and its severe form, HELLP syndrome), or placental abruption. Pain can also come from things that have nothing to do with pregnancy, such as appendicitis, a kidney infection or gallbladder trouble.

If the pain is mild and not a sign of something serious, rest, drink enough fluids and avoid lifting heavy things.

## Call 000 or go to emergency

If you have severe pain in your belly along with signs of preterm labour, vaginal bleeding or infection, or after any trauma to your belly (a car accident, a fall or violence), go to your nearest hospital emergency department or call triple zero (000) and ask for an ambulance.

## Contact your hospital now

Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital immediately for severe pain in your belly at any time in pregnancy, and, after 20 weeks, for pain under your ribs. Severe pain below the ribs or in the upper tummy can be a sign of severe pre-eclampsia.

Seek urgent care if you have belly pain with:

- persistent diarrhoea or vomiting, or signs of dehydration
- signs of early labour
- vaginal bleeding
- your baby moving less than usual
- signs of infection

## See your doctor or midwife

For other pain, depending on your symptoms, see your doctor, midwife or obstetrician so they can check that everything is OK.
