Avoid them. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says oral decongestants such as pseudoephedrine are not safe to use in pregnancy. KEMH says oral medicines such as pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine are not recommended in pregnancy, and healthdirect says you should not use oral decongestants such as phenylephrine.

## Why

Pseudoephedrine and phenylephrine narrow blood vessels, which may raise blood pressure. A nasal spray does the same job with far less reaching your bloodstream, so it's the better choice in pregnancy.

They're the decongestants in many combination cold and flu tablets, so check the ingredients on the box.

## What to do

- **For a blocked nose,** try steam and saline nasal sprays or drops.
- **Decongestant nasal sprays** (oxymetazoline or xylometazoline) are considered safe in pregnancy because so little is absorbed. Use them for no more than 3 days at a time, because overuse can make the congestion worse.
- **If you've already taken one** at the recommended dose, it isn't regarded as a cause for concern.
- **Not sure what's in a product?** Ask MotherSafe, your doctor or your pharmacist about its ingredients.
- **While breastfeeding,** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says decongestants aren't recommended either: they can reduce your milk supply and make the baby irritable.
