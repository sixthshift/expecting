Ask your employer for a review. SafeWork NSW names shift work, physically exhausting work and mentally fatiguing work among the potential risks to a pregnancy at work.

## Why

Your employer must do a new risk assessment of your job that takes your pregnancy into account, and repeat it as the pregnancy goes on: what isn't a risk early on can become one later. You may also need to manage pregnancy symptoms such as morning sickness or fatigue while at work (Pregnancy, Birth and Baby).

## What to do

- **Talk to your manager, HR or union representative** if you think your roster is affecting your pregnancy.
- **Discuss your hours and duties.** If your usual job isn't safe you can move to a safe job at the same pay and hours, and you and your employer might also agree on different working hours (SafeWork NSW).
- **Take regular breaks** where you can, and get plenty of rest at home.
- **If you're not sure something is safe**, or a task makes you dizzy, sore or unwell, stop and get advice from your doctor or midwife.
