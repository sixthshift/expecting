Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says every pregnancy is unique, and it's normal to have different symptoms and different emotions the second time.

## What can be different

- **Your body.** Your bump might appear earlier, because your stomach muscles have already stretched, and you might feel your baby move earlier, because you recognise the feeling. Pregnancy sickness can be different: you might have it this time even if you didn't before. Some people notice more Braxton Hicks contractions, and your pelvic floor muscles may be weaker, which can cause leaking.
- **Your energy.** You might feel more tired and find you have less time for yourself, especially if you're caring for another child. It's important to ask for help and take time to rest when you can.
- **Your emotions.** You may feel more confident, or have new worries, especially if you had complications last time. It's also common to feel guilt about not giving your full attention to your first child, the pregnancy, work or your partner.
- **The birth.** If you had a vaginal birth, you'll probably go through the stages of labour more quickly. A previous caesarean doesn't always mean you'll need another.

## Looking after yourself

Try to take a few hours by yourself for self-care each week, and plan some quality time with your partner if you have one. It's important to attend your antenatal appointments even if you feel you know what to expect, and to rebook any you miss as soon as you can.

If you feel very anxious or overwhelmed, or have symptoms of depression, it's always best to get support early, from your doctor or midwife, a psychologist, a counsellor, or friends and family.

## The admin

Think ahead about what extra equipment you'll need (cot, car seat, double pram), where the baby will sleep, who can help care for your eldest child, and how much time to take off work. Try to plan your new routine, but stay flexible.
