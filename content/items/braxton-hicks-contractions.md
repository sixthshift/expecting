If your belly goes tight and hard for a little while and then lets go, it's probably Braxton Hicks: sometimes called false labour or practice contractions. They're a normal part of pregnancy.

## What they are

Braxton Hicks happen when the muscles of your uterus contract. They tone those muscles, getting your body ready for the birth. They don't cause labour, aren't a sign that labour is starting, and don't open (dilate) your cervix.

They can happen from early in pregnancy, but you may not feel them until the second trimester. They're most often felt in the third, and may come more often or feel stronger towards the end.

## What they feel like

The muscles across your belly feel as if they're pulling tight: a weak or uncomfortable tightening or ache around the bump. Braxton Hicks usually:

- come at irregular times, no more than once or twice an hour, a few times a day
- last about 30 seconds, though they can last up to 2 minutes
- are uncomfortable, but not usually painful
- don't get stronger or closer together, and walking doesn't make them stronger

## Braxton Hicks or labour?

Labour contractions are different. They open the cervix, become very regular, get closer together, last longer and get stronger over time. They're painful or need all your attention, and walking makes them stronger. Lying down doesn't make them go away, where Braxton Hicks usually stop when you change position or activity.

## Easing them

If they're uncomfortable, try:

- lying down or changing position
- going for a walk
- a warm bath or shower
- a drink of water, and a wee

## When to get help

**Contact your doctor or midwife immediately** if you're less than 37 weeks pregnant and feel pain or discomfort in your pelvis, belly or lower back, or have any signs of labour: before 37 weeks, contractions can be a sign of premature labour.

**At any stage, contact your doctor or midwife immediately** if you have persistent belly pain or vaginal bleeding, are concerned about your baby's movements, or feel very unwell.

**Call your midwife or doctor, or go to hospital,** if your waters break, or your contractions are getting stronger, closer together and more regular.

If you're not sure whether it's Braxton Hicks or labour, contact your doctor or midwife: if in doubt, don't hesitate to call.
