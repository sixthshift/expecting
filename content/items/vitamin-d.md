Ask your midwife or doctor. Vitamin D helps your body take up and use calcium, for your bones and your baby's. Your skin makes most of it in sunlight.

## Why

Many pregnant women in Australia are low in vitamin D, and if you're low, your baby will be low at birth too.

You're more likely to be low if you:

- spend little time outdoors, or live somewhere with less sunlight
- have dark skin
- wear clothes that leave little skin exposed
- have obesity or gut absorption problems, or take some medicines

But many people are low without any of these.

**The advice differs.** Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says that if you're likely to be low, your midwife or doctor may suggest a blood test, and a supplement depending on the result. MotherSafe says routine testing isn't considered necessary in Australia, and notes that recent international guidelines recommend all pregnant women take at least 400 IU a day as a supplement. The Royal Women's Hospital says all pregnant women should take 400 IU (10 micrograms) a day, and more if a blood test shows you're low.

## What to do

- **Check your multivitamin.** Most pregnancy multivitamins contain some vitamin D, though the amount varies. MotherSafe says 1000 IU a day can be taken on its own if you aren't on one, or yours has 200 IU or less.
- **Higher doses** are often recommended if you're at high risk or a test shows you're low. Check the dose with your doctor or midwife.
- **Supplements at recommended doses** haven't been shown to harm you or your baby.
