Portable cots are designed to be used temporarily, and they're handy when you're travelling or visiting. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says they might not be as safe as a permanent cot: because they fold, it's easier for a child to climb out or become trapped.

## The standard

Only use a portable cot if it meets the Australian safety standard. Since 19 January 2026, a folding cot sold new must meet the mandatory standard for infant sleep products, which for a folding cot means AS/NZS 2195:2010 or its US or European equivalent (ACCC Product Safety). Second-hand ones aren't covered by it, so check an older one's instructions or label.

## Setting it up

Make sure:

- it works properly
- it has a locking mechanism that stops it collapsing
- you set it up following the instructions exactly, so it can't collapse

## The mattress

Don't add a thick mattress or an extra layer. Your baby could get trapped between the mattress and the stretchy side of the cot. Use a mattress that matches the cot's dimensions.

## Weight

Only use a portable cot while your baby weighs less than 15 kg.

## Like any cot

Keep it free of pillows, doonas, loose bedding, lamb's wool or sheepskin, bumpers and soft toys, which can be climbing aids, cause choking or suffocate your baby. Place it away from curtains, blind cords and other cords your baby could get tangled in.
