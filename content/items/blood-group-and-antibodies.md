A blood test at your first antenatal visit for your blood group (A, B, AB or O), RhD factor and antibodies. Your group matters in case you ever need blood, for example after heavy bleeding.

Almost 2 in 10 people in Australia are RhD negative. If you are and your baby is RhD positive, your body may make antibodies against the baby's blood, usually affecting a later pregnancy. You'll be offered anti-D injections at 28 and 34 weeks to prevent it.
