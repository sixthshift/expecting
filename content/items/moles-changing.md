## What's usual

Your moles may change in pregnancy, especially in the first trimester. Stretching skin and hormones can make them larger and darker, particularly on your tummy and breasts. They aren't more likely to become cancerous because you're pregnant, and they should go back to how they were within 12 months of the birth.

There is still a very small risk of any mole becoming melanoma, a type of skin cancer, so keep checking your moles while you're pregnant for any worrying features. An itchy mole is a sign to look out for. If you're not sure, see your doctor.

If you already know you have slightly irregular moles, you need scheduled skin checks; your doctor will tell you how often.

## See your doctor or midwife

See your doctor if a mole:

- is asymmetrical (not round)
- has an irregular border
- has different colours or shades in it
- is larger than 6 mm
- is raised
- has changed in size, shape or colour during your pregnancy

If you see a midwife, they may refer you to a doctor to examine your skin. If your doctor is worried about a mole they'll take a small sample (biopsy) to test it.
