Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says choosing the right time to tell family and friends is hard, and there is no right answer. If you have a partner, who to tell, when and how is best decided with them.

## The "12-week" habit

Many people delay announcing at least until the end of the first trimester (12 weeks). This is usually because of concerns about the risk of miscarriage, and Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says up to 1 in 4 pregnancies end in miscarriage before 14 weeks.

It suggests thinking about how you'd handle a miscarriage if it happened. Some people would be too distressed to talk about it with others. On the other hand, by telling people you're pregnant, support may be available if it does happen.

## Who first, and how

- You may decide to tell close family and friends first, so they can support you as early as possible, and plan who you'll tell and how.
- Close friends and family may prefer to be told personally.
- If you announce on social media, you may have little control over who sees it and when, and it can draw advice and stories you didn't ask for.
- Friends who have lost a baby or are having trouble getting pregnant may find the news difficult. It may help to tell them in private, before others, and let them know you realise it may not be easy for them.

## Work

There's no law saying you have to tell your employer at any specific time, though Pregnancy, Birth and Baby says it's a good idea to tell them before they hear it from somebody else.
