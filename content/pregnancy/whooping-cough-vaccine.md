Whooping cough (pertussis) can be very serious, even deadly, for young babies — and they can't have their own first dose until 6 weeks of age.

The whooping cough vaccine is recommended between **20 and 32 weeks of every pregnancy**, though it can be given up until the birth. The pregnant parent's antibodies cross the placenta, so the baby has some protection from birth until their own vaccines start.

It's free under the National Immunisation Program (NIP), though some providers charge a consultation fee. It can be given at the same visit as the flu and RSV vaccines. Ask your midwife or OB.
