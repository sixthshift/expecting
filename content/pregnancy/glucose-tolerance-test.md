Gestational diabetes is diabetes that starts in pregnancy. It often has no symptoms, so everyone is offered screening between **24 and 28 weeks**. Some people are tested earlier, before 20 weeks, if they've had it before or an early blood test suggests it.

The usual test is the oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT): 8 to 10 hours of fasting (usually overnight), a blood test, a sugary drink, then more blood tests at 1 and 2 hours. You stay at the pathology lab the whole time, so bring a book — and something to eat afterwards.

If it's diagnosed, the care team will help manage it, and there's usually a follow-up test after the birth. Ask your midwife or OB.
