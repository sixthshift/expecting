Group B streptococcus (GBS) is a common bacterium. About 1 in 5 women carry it in the vagina, usually with no symptoms or problems. Rarely, it can pass to a baby during birth and cause a serious infection.

Many care providers offer a swab of the vagina and rectum at about **35 to 37 weeks**; it can often be self-collected. That timing gives the most accurate picture close to labour. There isn't one standard practice in Australia — some providers skip the swab and decide based on risk factors instead.

If the swab is positive, or risk factors come up, antibiotics through a drip during labour are usually recommended, and the choice is yours. Ask your midwife or OB.
