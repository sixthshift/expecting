Screening tests estimate the *chance* of conditions such as Down syndrome; they don't diagnose. They're optional, and it helps to talk it through before the window opens.

- **Combined first-trimester screening (CFTS)** — a blood test plus an ultrasound measuring fluid at the back of the baby's neck (the nuchal translucency), at about 11 to 14 weeks. Medicare covers part of the cost.
- **NIPT** (non-invasive prenatal testing) — a blood test that looks at the baby's DNA in the pregnant parent's blood, from about 10 weeks. Not covered by Medicare or private insurance; around $450 to $800.

If a screen shows a higher chance, diagnostic tests (CVS or amniocentesis) can be offered. Ask your midwife or OB.
