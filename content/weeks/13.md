## The baby

Fully formed, and moving about vigorously: arms and legs, a fist, a thumb to suck. The organs are starting to work, including the liver and pancreas, the vocal cords are beginning, and there is soft hair on the head and eyebrows. The testes or ovaries are fully developed, so by the 20-week scan your doctor might be able to see the sex, if you want to know.

## Your body

This is the start of the second trimester. A bump might be showing, and you may feel the ligaments stretching in your tummy and groin. More visible veins, cravings or aversions, heartburn and constipation are all common, as are dark patches on the face (melasma) and a dark line down from the belly button; both usually fade after the birth.

The chance of miscarriage drops significantly from here.

## For the partner

Many people decide to share the news now. Agree how you want to do it, and be ready for reactions that are not quite what you hoped. This is also a good week to look into parental rights and entitlements: find out what leave your employers offer and what Parental Leave Pay looks like, so the later paperwork is no surprise. If childcare is on the cards, many centres have waiting lists, so start well before you think you will need care.
