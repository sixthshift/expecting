## The baby

The brain is growing rapidly and the senses with it: the taste buds are more sensitive, the eyes respond to light, and the baby can hear sounds from outside. The lungs are growing branches and getting ready to make surfactant. Preterm babies born at 24 weeks have about a 1 in 2 chance of surviving with expert care in a neonatal unit, but a high chance of disability.

## Your body

The top of the uterus is just above your belly button. By now you should be feeling the baby move; if you have not, see your doctor or midwife. Braxton Hicks contractions may start, not regular and probably stopping when you shift position.

Always seek medical attention for contractions that become stronger, more frequent or longer, persistent pain or pressure in your tummy, back or pelvis, any vaginal bleeding, or the baby's movements slowing or stopping. The glucose tolerance test for gestational diabetes is offered between 24 and 28 weeks. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

Two things to book this week. The glucose tolerance test needs an appointment between now and 28 weeks, fasting for 8 to 12 hours beforehand, so book it for a morning. And written notice of parental leave to each of your employers is needed at least 10 weeks before leave starts: start drafting it. People will begin commenting on the bump and even touching it; the pregnant parent can speak up if that is uncomfortable, and people should ask for consent first.
