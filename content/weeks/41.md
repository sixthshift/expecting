## The baby

Pregnancy normally lasts about 40 weeks and is full term from 37; if labour has not started by 42 weeks, it is considered overdue. If it does go overdue, there is a risk that the placenta will not work well enough to supply the baby with oxygen and nutrients, which is why checks begin. Movements matter more than ever: it is very important to pay attention to them.

## Your body

Going past the due date is very common: only about 5 per cent of babies arrive on the exact date. You are more likely to go overdue if you have never given birth before. If it happens, the midwife or doctor will recommend regular checks, possibly twice a week: an ultrasound, and a CTG to check the baby's heartbeat. A membrane sweep (a "stretch and sweep") may be offered to start labour, and induction may be suggested; it is recommended when the risks of continuing the pregnancy outweigh the risks of inducing, and it is your choice whether to have treatment.

Let your doctor or midwife know straight away about any change in the baby's movements: faster, slower or stopped. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

This week is about staying in touch with the birth unit and turning up to every check. Go along, and bring the questions the source suggests: the risks to the pregnant parent or baby, what might bring on labour, when, where and how an induction would happen, what the alternatives are, whether you can have written information, and how long you have to decide.
