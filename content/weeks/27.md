## The baby

Growing and maturing fast, with muscles developed and a well-proportioned body, but still very thin; a lot of weight goes on over the next month. The skin is no longer transparent: red, very wrinkly, and covered in waxy vernix that protects it from infection and from the amniotic fluid around it.

## Your body

Most people put on quite a lot of weight from now until 36 weeks, though it is different for everyone, and eating for two is not needed. Stretch marks, pink or purple lines, may appear on the tummy, thighs or breasts, and the skin can become sensitive and itchy; they are not harmful and fade over time.

If you have not already had the whooping cough vaccine, now is the time (it is recommended between 20 and 32 weeks). It is free under the National Immunisation Program, and your antibodies pass to the baby, protecting them before they can be vaccinated themselves. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

The pre-birth claim for Parental Leave Pay can be lodged in myGov from now (up to 3 months before the due date), so set up or check your myGov accounts this week, for both of you. The whooping cough vaccine is the pregnant parent's to book; the flu vaccine is free and can be done at the same visit if they have not had this year's. The employer's written notice of leave is needed at least 10 weeks before leave starts.
