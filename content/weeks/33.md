## The baby

Gaining a lot of weight, ready to be born. The lungs are maturing and making surfactant, so the baby will be able to breathe on its own outside. The brain and nervous system are fully developed; the baby can suck and swallow, though the two will not coordinate for another week or so. It is storing iron in its liver, which it will use for about six months after the birth.

## Your body

The discomforts are likely to grow from here: leg cramps, backache, indigestion, heartburn, and swollen feet and legs. The weight of the baby can bring bladder and bowel problems, including haemorrhoids and leaking; daily pelvic floor exercises are what the source recommends.

Contact your doctor or midwife straight away if swelling suddenly gets much worse, if you have it first thing in the morning, or if your face or hands are swollen. You will probably know the baby's pattern by now; if you notice a sudden change, or the baby stops moving, talk to your doctor or midwife immediately. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

Learn the baby's pattern too: very active at certain times, often at night, and still while the pregnant parent is moving about in the day, is typical. Keep the car seat and the feeding plan moving, both due by week 36, and check the employer's leave dates are what you both expect.
