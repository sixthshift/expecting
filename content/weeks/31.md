## The baby

The brain is sending a lot of messages to the body. The eyes can open and focus, and the baby hears sounds from outside; a loud noise may make it startle. Talking to the baby, or playing some of your favourite music, can help build your connection before the birth.

## Your body

It is normal to feel tired and lack energy at this stage, and many people feel more emotional in the third trimester: hormones, broken sleep, and the thought of becoming a parent can all play a part. Breasts may leak more as the birth approaches, which is normal; breast pads from the supermarket or pharmacy help.

Anxiety and depression are common during pregnancy and after the birth, and it is important to seek help. If you feel very emotional and stressed, have a chat with your doctor or midwife.

## For the partner

This week's job is the car restraint. By law, babies under six months must be in a properly fastened rear-facing restraint with an inbuilt harness that meets AS/NZS 1754, and your car needs the right anchor points. Buy or hire one, and book a professional fitting, because it can take a while to organise and the baby may come early. If the pregnant parent is low, PANDA (1300 726 306) and Beyond Blue (1300 22 4636) are there.
