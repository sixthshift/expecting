## The baby

Most of the body is now fully formed, including the inner ear and the retina, though the irises have no colour yet. There are eyelashes, and the nails reach the fingertips. The baby is probably lying across your tummy, but it changes position all the time, and the movements get stronger as the muscles mature.

## Your body

Some people develop haemorrhoids during pregnancy, from constipation or the pressure of the growing baby and uterus; fibre and water help. Bloating and gas, varicose veins, backache, bleeding gums and stretch marks are among the discomforts you may have. Braxton Hicks contractions, where the uterus tightens and goes hard, may begin: they are not regular and usually go away if you change position.

Contractions that are painful, regular or getting stronger could be a sign of premature labour. Always contact your doctor or midwife if you have contractions along with pain, pressure or discomfort in your pelvis, abdomen or lower back. Low mood or worry lasting more than two weeks could be a sign of depression or anxiety, and is worth raising too. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

Learn the difference between Braxton Hicks and the real thing now, so neither of you has to work it out in the moment: not regular and fading with a change of position is Braxton Hicks; painful, regular or getting stronger is a call to the doctor or midwife. Antenatal classes often fill up quickly, so book now for a course around 28 weeks. If either of you is struggling, Lifeline is 13 11 14 and Beyond Blue 1300 224 636.
