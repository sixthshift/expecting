## The baby

Fully developed and tightly curled, a week from being considered full term. In a first pregnancy the head has probably moved down into the pelvis, called engagement, a sign the baby is getting ready; in later pregnancies it may not engage until contractions start. Movements should carry on in their normal pattern right up to the birth.

## Your body

The uterus reaches your ribcage, though if the baby has engaged the bump may sit lower, with more pressure in the lower abdomen and pelvis and more trips to the toilet. Breathlessness in this last month should ease as the baby moves lower. More discharge is normal.

Tell your doctor or midwife if the discharge is smelly, green or brown, or makes you itchy or sore. If you are concerned about a change in the baby's movements, contact your midwife or doctor immediately. A test for group B strep may be offered this week. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

This is the week most of the tasks come due: the car seat bought and fitted, the hospital bag packed, a feeding support contact saved in both phones, and your leave dates confirmed with work. The Parental Leave Pay claim should be in by now if it is not. Tick them off this week, because from next week the baby is considered full term.
