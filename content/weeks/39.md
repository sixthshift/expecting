## The baby

Weight gain slows now that the baby is almost ready, but development goes on fast, most of all in the head: the brain is about 30 per cent bigger than a month ago and keeps growing quickly for the first three years, which is why the head is a third of the baby's weight. There is a lot of fat under the skin, and a coat of creamy white vernix.

## Your body

The cervix is thinning and will gradually soften and open until it is 10 cm wide. Discharge may be heavy, and a show, the mucus plug that kept the cervix closed, sometimes with a little old blood in it, is part of the first stage of labour. The placenta can weigh as much as 1 kg by now, and birthing it is the third stage; how that is managed is a conversation to have.

If you notice you are bleeding, call your doctor or midwife straight away. Unless you have been told otherwise, stay at home as long as you can when labour starts, going in when contractions become more intense with less than 3 to 5 minutes between them, or if you are not coping.

## For the partner

Check the bag against the list: clothes, toiletries and pads for the pregnant parent, clothes and nappies for the baby. If you are planning a home birth, you'll want clean towels and sheets, a large plastic sheet, and drinks and snacks for both of you. Make sure you know the "when to go in" rule that has been agreed with the midwife, so you are not the one guessing.
