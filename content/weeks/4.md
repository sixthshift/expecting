## The baby

By the end of this week the fertilised egg, now a ball of cells called a blastocyst, implants itself into the wall of the uterus. It is about 0.2 mm wide and made of about 200 cells, in three layers: the outer will become the nervous system and brain, the middle the heart, blood vessels, muscles and bones, and the inner the breathing and digestive systems. Small tentacles on its outside reach for your blood supply; over the coming weeks they become the placenta.

## Your body

For many people the first sign is a missed period at the end of this week. You probably will not notice anything else yet, but your hormones are already changing: more progesterone, which stops your period, and the pregnancy hormone hCG, which is what a test detects.

A missed period can bring every feeling at once, from excitement to worry. All of them are normal.

## For the partner

A test can be done the day after a missed period. If it is positive, the first job is yours to help with: book a GP appointment to confirm the pregnancy and talk through care options. Expect a mix of feelings, and give it room.
