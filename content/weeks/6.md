## The baby

Growing very quickly and about to double in size, looking more like a tadpole with a large head and a tail. The organs are starting to form, the jaw and eyes are developing, and buds that will become arms and legs are showing, with vertebrae forming along the back. If you have an ultrasound this week, you may be able to see the heart beating.

## Your body

If you did not realise you were pregnant last week, you have probably noticed a missed period by now. Tiredness, tender breasts and nausea, even vomiting, are common. Not everyone has morning sickness, but it is more likely between weeks 6 and 14; it can happen at any time of day, and it usually clears up by about 3 months into pregnancy.

If you are feeling very unwell, or the vomiting is severe and does not stop, talk to your doctor or midwife.

## For the partner

You can't see anything yet, but this is a hard stretch: tired and queasy. Small, frequent meals help, and gentle exercise such as walking may help. The admin job is the first appointment: pregnancy care should start as soon as you realise, so book the GP, who will confirm the pregnancy with a blood test and talk through the care options, and go together if you can. Who to tell, and when, is a choice to make together; many people wait until the second trimester.
