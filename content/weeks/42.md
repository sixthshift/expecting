## The baby

At 42 weeks the pregnancy is considered overdue, also called postdates or prolonged pregnancy. The risk of stillbirth or neonatal death gets higher the longer it goes on, though it is still low in Australia, and there may be a higher chance of breathing problems for the baby and of a large baby. Monitoring is usually offered more often from here, and closer monitoring during labour and birth is recommended.

## Your body

Induction may be offered; when depends on your health and your doctor's or hospital's policy. Your midwife or doctor should explain the options, including the risks and benefits, taking in test and health check results, how overdue you are, whether the cervix is ready for labour, and your preferences. It is your choice whether to have treatment or not. If tests show the baby is well and your health is good, you might choose to wait and see whether labour starts naturally; if they show the baby's health is at risk, induction may be recommended.

Keep paying attention to movements. Let your doctor or midwife know straight away about any change: movements suddenly speeding up, slowing down or stopping altogether.

## For the partner

Be at the conversation about induction, and ask for written information on the options if that helps. Know where, when and how it would happen, what the alternatives are, and how long you have to think. Stay reachable, and keep the birth unit's number handy.
