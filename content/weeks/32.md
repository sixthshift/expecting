## The baby

Quite squashed now, but still moving in its usual pattern. The weight has almost doubled in the past four weeks, and from here weight grows faster than length. Most babies are head down by 32 weeks; some stay bottom down until the last month.

## Your body

More amniotic fluid and a fast-growing bump can bring pelvic pain that makes moving around hard; a physiotherapist can help if it is bad. Your belly button may flatten or stick out, and the source suggests taking out any piercing now.

A baby born now would be premature but unlikely to have long-term health differences. Keep a watch out for signs of premature labour: contractions, waters breaking, bleeding, a show of mucus, or a sudden decrease or change in the baby's movements. If you are concerned about movements, contact your midwife or doctor immediately. If the whooping cough vaccine has not happened, it is very important to have it now, and a flu shot too is a good idea depending on the season. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

Plan your reserved Parental Leave Pay days this week; they are reserved for you to use. Learn the signs of premature labour listed above, so you can keep a watch out too. A feeding plan is worth starting now: how you hope to feed, and what plan B is, with a support contact saved in both phones by week 36. Antenatal classes often fill up quickly, so check yours is booked.
