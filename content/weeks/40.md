## The baby

Every part is well developed, bones included, though the skull bones stay a little soft and flexible to fit through the birth canal, which is why a newborn's head can look pointed at first. A well-grown term baby can weigh anything from 2.9 to 4.2 kg. Movements stay important right to the end and into labour.

## Your body

The due date, and only a small number of babies arrive on it. Feeling fed up and uncomfortable is understandable, and there is usually no need to worry: you and the baby are monitored carefully. If you go more than 10 to 12 days past, or there is a risk to your own or the baby's health, induction may be recommended, and any decision is made in consultation with you.

A change in the baby's pattern of movements may be a sign it is unwell: if you are concerned, contact your doctor or midwife immediately.

## For the partner

Only a small number of babies are born on their due date, so keep the plan ready. After the birth the baby is offered a newborn screening heel prick, a vitamin K injection, a hepatitis B vaccination and a hearing test; consent may be asked for before you go to hospital, so read about them now. Reading up on the early signs of labour, feeding and the first days home is a good use of the waiting.
