## The baby

The eyelids open for the first time this week. The heart beats at about 140 times a minute, the brain waves are regulating what the baby sees and hears, and all the senses are improving. The brain, lungs and digestive system are formed but not finished; a baby born now would have about an 80 per cent chance of surviving with expert care in a neonatal unit, and would still need help to breathe for quite a while.

## Your body

As the uterus expands upwards you may feel uncomfortable around your ribs. You might also have indigestion and heartburn; smaller meals more often help. Leg cramps are another common problem, especially in bed; stretching the leg and pulling the toes back towards the knee, or standing up and putting your weight on the leg, eases them.

Heartburn that is bothering you is worth mentioning, because sometimes it can be a symptom of a more serious condition, such as pre-eclampsia. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

The source's job for this week is the plan for getting to hospital: how you will get there (the pregnant parent does not drive themselves), what happens if they cannot reach you when labour starts, and who the backup is. Things can move faster than you expect, so write it down. If the glucose test is not booked yet, do it now; the window closes at 28 weeks.
