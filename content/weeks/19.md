## The baby

The skin is becoming covered in vernix, a white, sticky coating that protects it from the amniotic fluid, and fat is forming underneath. The head may have hair. The baby now has clear waking and sleeping cycles, like a newborn: about 18 hours asleep and 6 hours moving about.

## Your body

Most people look obviously pregnant by now, though bumps come in every shape and size. Your feet may grow as ligaments stretch, your breasts are larger, and you are likely curvier overall. Your doctor or midwife will feel and measure your tummy at each visit to follow the baby's growth.

Anxiety and depression can start in pregnancy, and they are common and treatable. Feeling low, on edge or exhausted for more than two weeks is something to tell your doctor or midwife. Thoughts of hurting yourself need help straight away: call Lifeline on 13 11 14, or 000 in an emergency.

## For the partner

A changing shape can change how the pregnant parent feels about themselves, and about you. Talk about it openly. Keep an eye on mood too: worrying all the time, losing interest in people or sleeping badly for a couple of weeks is a reason to raise it gently, and PANDA (1300 726 306) is there for both of you.
