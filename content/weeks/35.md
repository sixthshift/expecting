## The baby

Cramped, with the legs likely bent up to the chest, but still able to change position and still moving in its normal pattern. The kidneys are mature: the baby swallows about a litre of amniotic fluid a day and passes it back out as urine. If it is still breech, most turn head down in the last month; if not in the next week or two, a procedure to turn the baby (ECV) may be recommended.

## Your body

Braxton Hicks contractions can come as often as every 10 to 20 minutes in late pregnancy, a tightening or cramping that normally goes away if you move position. They may be real contractions if they get stronger or closer together, last longer, are stronger when you walk, or bring pain or pressure in the pelvis, abdomen or lower back; labour now would be premature and you would need medical attention straight away.

Pre-eclampsia is a serious complication that can develop in late pregnancy. Contact your doctor, midwife or hospital straight away if you develop a bad headache, pain in the tummy, blurry vision or sudden swelling of the hands or feet.

## For the partner

Get the hospital bag packed: get a few things ready a few weeks ahead, whether the birth is at home, in hospital or at a birth centre. Many providers offer a Group B strep swab at about 35 to 37 weeks, so ask about it at the next visit. If there are complications, visits get more frequent from here.
