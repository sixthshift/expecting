## The baby

Eyebrows, hair and fingernails, and the baby can yawn and hiccup. The nerves are growing their myelin coating, and the bowel is filling with meconium, which will be the first poos. The baby moves around a lot: you may notice it as little flutters, called quickening, which sharpen into kicks, jabs and elbows over the coming weeks.

## Your body

Some people feel light-headed about now, especially getting up from lying down; the growing uterus can press on an artery, and standing brings a rush of blood. Weight gain becomes noticeable, and how much is normal depends on where you started.

The morphology scan is offered between 18 and 22 weeks. It checks the baby's organs, growth, heart and the placenta's position, and can show the sex if you want to know. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

The scan is close, if not this week: be there. Decide beforehand whether you want to know the sex, because the source says the sex is only shared if you want to know. Getting up slowly is the trick for the dizziness, so do not rush her off the couch.
