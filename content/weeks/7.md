## The baby

The brain is developing rapidly and the head is much bigger than the body. There are large eyes, a wide forehead, nostrils, lips and buds where the ears will be, and the stomach, kidneys and lungs are forming. The baby lies inside the amniotic sac, a bag of fluid that protects it, and the heart beats at around 150 to 180 times a minute.

## Your body

Your hormones are very different from before, and that can make you more emotional than usual, as well as tired and short on energy. Breasts may be swollen and tender, with darker areolas and larger nipples. Some people have problems with constipation at this stage.

If you have not seen a doctor yet, this is the time: the first visit confirms the pregnancy, works out the due date, checks your health and orders blood tests. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

Nothing shows, and a lot is going on. Rest is what the pregnant parent needs most: take the evenings, and the other children if there are any. If the first GP visit is not booked yet, book it now, and go along. Ask about an early dating scan while you are there; it is most often done between 6 and 8 weeks.
