## The baby

The major organ systems are now fully developed. The lungs are mature enough to breathe air and are making surfactant, and the baby may be seen on an ultrasound practising its breathing movements. The brain and hearing are developing fast, and the baby recognises familiar voices, including yours, and may startle at loud noises. Hiccups are normal, and the baby may still be bottom down; most turn head down before birth. Movements become regular by about now.

## Your body

The third trimester starts, and you will probably see your doctor or midwife more regularly. Many people find things get more uncomfortable: back pain, leg cramps, indigestion and heartburn, and swollen hands and feet. Sleep on your side from now on, since research shows it can reduce the risk of stillbirth by half.

Make time each day to notice your baby's movements. If you are concerned, including if they slow down, change or stop, contact your midwife or doctor immediately; do not wait until the next day. Several things fall around now too: the glucose test, anti-D if your blood group is RhD negative (it may be offered at 28 and 34 weeks), the RSV vaccine from 28 weeks, and the whooping cough vaccine (recommended 20 to 32 weeks). Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

The paperwork starts here: Parental Leave Pay can be claimed before the birth, and your employer needs written notice of your leave at least 10 weeks before it starts. Save the birth unit's number in your phone now. Talking or singing to the baby is a nice way for you both to bond before the birth.
