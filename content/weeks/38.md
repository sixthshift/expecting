## The baby

Ready to be born. Most of the lanugo has gone, and the baby is probably still covered in creamy vernix. The meconium in its bowel is sometimes passed during labour, which turns the fluid green and means closer monitoring.

## Your body

Labour can start any time between now and 42 weeks. Early signs are pressure in the pelvis, cramps or tightening like period pain, backache or diarrhoea; a show, when the mucus plug comes away; or the waters breaking, as a trickle or a gush. You are in labour when the contractions are strong, regular and last at least a minute each.

Some people get a burst of energy in the last weeks; don't overexert yourself, and make time to rest. Your doctor or midwife will have discussed when to go to hospital or the birth centre, and it is a good idea to call them when you think you are in labour; if you are unsure whether to stay home or go in, ring and speak to a midwife. If your waters break, call the birth unit and they'll tell you when to come in; any bleeding from the vagina is a call straight away. If you need urgent medical help, call triple zero (000).

## For the partner

The plan for when labour starts is yours to have ready. The midwife will have said when to come in; ring when you think it has begun. Know how you will get there, and the backup if you cannot be reached, because the pregnant parent should not drive themselves. Numbers saved in both phones. The hospital bag should be packed by now, so check it rather than pack it.
