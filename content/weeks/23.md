## The baby

Covered in fine lanugo hair that is darkening, with colour coming into the hair on the head and eyebrows. The lungs have started making surfactant, the substance that will keep them inflated after birth, and the baby practises breathing, though all its oxygen still comes through the placenta. It can now recognise light, sound and pain, and will know your heartbeat and your voice.

## Your body

The uterus may press on your bladder enough that you leak a little when you cough, laugh or sneeze; it is usually temporary, and pelvic floor exercises are what the source recommends, every day through the pregnancy and after the birth. Feeling warm is common, from the extra blood. Your centre of gravity is shifting forward, so balance changes.

Starting a new exercise programme, such as pregnancy yoga or Pilates, is worth checking first. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

This is the week the source says to start planning for parenthood in practical terms: who will be at the birth, who is taking time off and when, how the chores and any other children get shared in the first weeks. Have the conversation now. Work out who you can ask for help from family and friends; the leave paperwork begins next week.
