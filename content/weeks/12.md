## The baby

Almost doubled in size over the past 4 weeks and now fully formed, with all organs, muscles, limbs and bones in place; the baby fills the whole uterus. The head is about half the length of the body, the arms look more in proportion and the legs are still short. The kidneys and digestive system are starting to work: the baby swallows the amniotic fluid around it and passes it back out as urine.

## Your body

Your uterus has shifted, so there is less pressure on your bladder and you may need the toilet a little less. You may feel less tired and have more energy to get back to activities you enjoyed. For most people morning sickness settles by about 14 weeks, though for some it carries on into the second trimester.

Pelvic floor exercises are important in pregnancy, when hormones soften your muscles and the weight of the baby adds strain. If you are struggling to do them on your own, ask a physiotherapist, continence nurse, doctor or midwife.

## For the partner

Week 12 is about when routine antenatal visits start, with a doctor, midwife or obstetrician depending on the model of care. Help get them booked and go along when you can: they matter even when all is going well, so any risks can be spotted early. A scan around 12 weeks is often recommended, especially if you are thinking about screening, so come to that too.
