## The baby

Considered full term. The lungs are fully mature, the grip is firm, and the gut holds sticky green meconium that will form the first poo. The baby may engage in the pelvis any time from now, more likely in a first pregnancy. Movements should stay in the same pattern right through to the birth, and even during labour.

## Your body

Breasts often leak colostrum, your first milk, now; you might choose to start expressing and storing some before the birth. Early signs of labour can come any time between now and 42 weeks: cramps like period pain, backache, diarrhoea, waters breaking, or a show of sticky mucus. Nobody can predict when yours will start.

If you are concerned about the baby's movements, contact your midwife or doctor immediately; a slowing down may be a sign the baby is unwell. Ask your midwife or OB.

## For the partner

If the pregnant parent has not stopped work, the source says they will probably want to now; leave can start up to six weeks before the due date, and an employer can ask for a medical certificate saying they are fit to work. Save the support numbers in both phones this week if you have not. Stock the fridge for the first week home, so nobody has to cook with a newborn.
