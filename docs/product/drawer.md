# The Drawer

The handy things: tools you use together, each brought by an extra. Now
the names game and Your people. Emergency info and It's time sit beside
settings, not here ([emergency.md](emergency.md), [its-time.md](its-time.md)).

How it's built is [`architecture.md`](../architecture.md), the Drawer's row,
Extras, and The names deck. What an extra is: [extras.md](extras.md).

## What it holds to

- **Things you do, not things to read.** A tool earns a place here when
  it's something the two of you do together and come back to, not another
  entry.
- **The tools you've turned on first;** the rest as a line each saying
  which extra brings them.
- **Hidden is on trust.** Who's holding the phone is a choice, not a login:
  switch to the other one and their votes are there. Fine for a game
  between the two of you, so it's said plainly, and never used for
  anything that has to stay secret.
- **Put away while something has happened,** never deleted.

## The names game

A swipe game, each on your own phone. What makes it work is being dealt
names you'd never have thought to type.

- **A name goes on the shortlist only when you've both liked it,** so
  nobody has to admit to the name the other would hate. You never see a
  list of your votes, or the other's: you just keep swiping. Undo takes
  back the last swipe, because thumbs slip.
- **A superlike, one a day each,** for a name you love: it goes on the
  shortlist straight away, whatever the other thinks. The shortlist stars
  it but doesn't say whose. One a day keeps it from overriding the game.
- **Either of you takes a name off the shortlist,** and it isn't dealt
  again to either of you.
- **The deck is Western naming, not one country's.** Australia names its
  babies much as the rest of the English-speaking world does, so the deck
  is drawn from several countries' registers of births, each counting the
  same, so it isn't whichever country has the most babies. It keeps names
  people actually chose (common enough over a decade since 1950, or in a
  country's top 100 now), not one-offs and typos. Names common in only one
  place (Aroha, Seán) are kept but not dealt, for dealing by region one
  day. Spelled as the registers write them, accents and all.
- **Your own order.** Each of you gets your own shuffle, so you aren't
  swiping in lockstep, with familiar names a little likelier near the
  front so the first cards aren't all rarities. A name the other liked
  comes round sooner, mixed in among the first few dozen, never first,
  which would give away what they liked; one they passed is never shown
  early.
- **Girls' or boys' names, from what you know.** The colours question
  decides it. Not knowing, it's all of them, or as you choose. A name given
  to the other sex at least a fifth of the time is dealt as either.
- **A card says one true thing:** "Top 10 in England & Wales and Ireland",
  "Most given in the 1950s", "About 1 in 2,400 babies now", or "Rare now".
- **No count.** It never says how many are left. It's something for a
  spare moment, and should feel endless.
- **Add your own names,** which likes them; the other is dealt them first,
  since you asked for them.

## Your people

Visitors, Birth announcement, Lining up help and Thank-you notes all
promised a list, and turning one on used to give you a task telling you to
keep one somewhere else. They're the same thing: people you know, each with
something about them. So it's one list, and each of those extras adds its
own view of it.

- **A person is a name** and a few words ("Sam's sister"; a number if you
  like, with no phone field). Kept by either of you.
- **Each view sets one thing about them:**
  - **Visitors:** when they're welcome (in hospital, the first weeks, not
    yet).
  - **Hearing first:** when they hear of the birth (first, next, with
    everyone), and the words for it, written ahead.
  - **Help:** what they'll help with, and the meal train's roster (a day
    and what they're bringing).
  - **Gifts:** what they gave, and whether they've been thanked. Many per
    person; the view's name says how many are left to thank.
- **It hands off rather than sends.** Friends can't log in and Expecting
  sends nothing: on the day, the announcement goes to the phone's share
  sheet a round at a time, and the meal train's roster is copied out for
  whoever's organising it.
- **The extras' tasks open their view** ("Start a shared list of gifts"
  opens Gifts), left to tick when you're done.
- **Turning an extra off hides its view;** the people stay.
- **"Your people" in the app,** `circle` in the code.

## Not yet

- **The shortlist's checks** (said aloud with your surname, initials,
  nicknames and meaning), **secret mode** and **a veto each.** Meanings
  are mostly copyrighted; Wikidata has nicknames and variants.
- **Passed names coming round again** later in the pregnancy, and dealing
  by region.
- **Importing contacts:** the Contact Picker is Android Chrome only;
  typing names is enough.
- **Reminders on the day** for visitors and meals: the weeks after the
  birth aren't built.
- **More tools** (Who does what, A question a week, Pregnancy bingo,
  minding cards): [`plan-extras.md`](../plan-extras.md).
