# The Journal

What happens, kept and given back. Notes and letters, movements, sickness
and cravings, how you're feeling, photos and scans, results and papers, the
firsts, and what you got done in the Planner, as one story under its weeks.
It's built for the reasons you open it, not shaped like the store: you keep
things with the "+" ([capture](capture.md)), and the Journal gives them
back.

How it's built is [`architecture.md`](../architecture.md), the Journal's row
in The pillars, and Uploads.

## Why you open it

1. **"Did it go in?"** Straight after keeping something.
2. **"What's happened so far?"** Looking back, often together.
3. **"How has it been?"** A pattern, before an appointment: the midwife's
   "since when?", "how often?", "is it getting better?".
4. **"Where's that paper?"** The blood group at the 28-week visit, the birth
   plan at triage.
5. **"What did they add?"** What the other one kept since you last looked.

None of them is "choose a kind, then fill in its form". The first two are
reading back, and the story answers them; the third and fourth are looking
something up, often under pressure, and the records answer them. Two ways
in, at the top of the Journal: Story (`/journal`) and Records
(`/journal/records`). The fifth has no answer of its own since the
highlights went (below); a quiet mark on the other's lines is the likely
one.

## What it holds to

- **Not a paper journal.** A paper journal has a prompt on page 20 because
  it's page 20, and an empty frame for every week. Expecting knows the
  week, what's kept, who's holding the phone and what just happened, so the
  page is made for the moment.
- **What's kept, never what's missing.** Some keep nothing, some one kind,
  some everything, and each gets a Journal that's theirs:
  - No empty slots. A lens, a pattern or a part of a recap appears only once
    its kind has something. Never "no movements yet".
  - A pattern needs enough to be one: movements on three days of the
    fortnight, sickness in two weeks, a craving kept twice, three check-ins,
    bump photos in two weeks. Below that they're lines in the story.
  - A series is yours to start. The empty bump frame shows only once there
    are bump photos in two weeks.
  - A week with nothing kept is a faint line, never marked as missing.
  - The story has a backbone: what was ticked in the Planner, on its day,
    and the firsts. Someone who never opens the "+" still has a Journal.
- **Full on day one.** Before anything is kept, it's written from what
  Expecting already knows: each week's title, the baby that week, the
  chapters to come. What you keep is written into it.
- **The tone: doing, not asking.** Expecting has already begun the book and
  shows what it's making; it never tells you to start a habit. Said once,
  prominently while the Journal is young, then one quiet line. Confident
  and understated: "Kept for you", "Ready when you are". Never "Don't
  forget!", "Unlock", a count of what's missing, a streak, a reminder or an
  exclamation mark.
- **No made-up words.** The potential is shown with your own facts (names,
  dates, each week's title) and the shape of the book, never with sample
  entries that aren't yours.
- **Never about her body beyond what she keeps.** No scores, no "you've
  grown", no "normal", no advice on what to eat. It's directive only where
  its sources are (the call) and about admin (a question for the
  appointment, a paper kept handy).
- **Shared, mostly.** Both phones see everything, and either of you changes
  any note, movement, sickness, craving, photo or paper. A feeling is the
  exception (below), and the one private corner: there are no private
  notes and no private papers. With one password, a "just me" would only
  be a courtesy, and on a medical record a courtesy reads as protection it
  isn't. Two voices show only when there are two.
- **Who kept it isn't whose it is.** Sickness and cravings are her body's
  and movements the baby's, whoever held the phone (often the partner, at
  3 am), so their lines never carry a name: "Alex · 3:12 am · Threw up"
  reads as Alex being sick. Inside the entry it says "Kept by Alex". A
  photo says the same. A name sits on the line only where the person is
  the point: a note, a letter, a feeling, a first's words.

## The page

One story, newest first, made for the moment: it opens on now.

- **This week is the story's first page,** not a part beside it: the week,
  its note's title, the baby this week, the firsts that usually come now
  (each with its question, to write or put off for the week), the Keep
  row, and what's kept this week; then every week before it. "Did it go
  in?" and "what's now?" are why most visits happen. Until 2026-11-02 the
  week and the story were two parts, the story leaving this week out and
  the week hiding its days while the story was in a lens or zoomed: one
  story has no seam to keep in step. In a lens, zoomed or searched, that
  is the story instead, this week's page with it.
- **The book, already begun.** From the first visit there's a cover (Our
  pregnancy, your names, the due month and year) and contents: the
  pregnancy's chapters, each with the firsts that usually come in it.
  Every pregnancy journal starts early, because a moment not kept then is
  missed for good, and the chapters show what it could become without
  listing what's missing: a chapter is begun, now, still to come (faint),
  or passed with nothing kept (faint, its moments still keepable). The
  book on the page, the recaps and the print at `/journal/book` are one
  book seen at different times, not three things. It's large while the
  Journal is young (fewer than five things kept, ticks aside), then one
  line ("Our pregnancy · 4 chapters begun"): said once. With no due date
  the cover asks for one, since the due date is what writes the weeks in.

  | Chapter | Weeks | Its moments |
  |---|---|---|
  | Finding out | 1–6 | Found out |
  | The first scan | 7–13 | The first scan, heard the heartbeat, the first craving |
  | Telling people | 14–17 | Told the family, the first outfit |
  | Halfway | 18–22 | Started showing, found out boy or girl |
  | Feeling them move | 23–27 | The first kick, felt from the outside |
  | Getting ready | 28–36 | The cot is up |
  | The last weeks | 37–42 | The bag is packed, it started |
  | The birth | | Drawn, never filled: the birth is out of the first version |

- **Places ready,** at the moments that matter: the dating and morphology
  scans' pictures, the glucose test's result, a bump series from week 12.
  One sentence each ("Your dating scan is usually around now. There's a
  place ready for the picture."), the place itself the way in, gone once
  used or its weeks pass. It shows what Expecting can do at the moment it
  matters, instead of asking for a habit.
- **Catching up, as a courtesy:** "Some of this has already happened. You
  can still keep it, on the day it was." So opening the Journal for the
  first time at week 14 misses nothing for good.
- **No Keep row.** There was a row of the "+"'s Journal choices (Moved,
  Sick, Craving, Note, …) on this week's page. Taken out (2026-11-02): it
  was drawn as the same pills as the ways to look at the story, under
  them, so adding read as filtering, and the "+" is on every page for it.
  Kept from the "+" on the Journal, the toast says so with no way there:
  it's on this week's page, in front of you.
- **A call to make, always first,** outside the count: a movement felt as
  slower, weaker or stopped in the last two days, or can't keep fluids
  down today.
- **No column of highlights.** There were Now cards (just kept, since you
  looked, a first suggested from what's kept, a recap, last week, this day
  weeks ago) and Lately (the patterns, each opening its lens) beside the
  story. Taken out (2026-11-02): they showed again what the week and the
  story already show, and they steered the story from beside it. The page
  is one story, in one column; the patterns are in the records; the recaps
  are on the whole pregnancy's line.
- **The story, like a diary, not rows.** A list of records reads like a
  database, so the day sits in the margin, the words run as writing,
  photos are large, a day's movements are one line of times, a day's same
  craving is one chip ("Pickles, twice"), and a line has no buttons: tap
  it to change or delete it. Every week back to the first is on the spine
  with its note's title. One row of ways to look at it, one
  at a time: Days (all of it), Weeks (a tile a week), Whole pregnancy
  (weeks 1–42 as one line, the firsts as landmarks), then a kind once
  there's something of it (Notes, Photos, Firsts). They were two rows, the
  lenses and the zoom, though only one could be open: one choice, one
  row. The search under it covers everything, in days: typing leaves a
  zoom or a kind.
- **Recaps,** a part only when it has something: the firsts, the bump as a
  flipbook, the scans, the most craved, both voices, the letters, what got
  done. Stepped through card by card, or printed as the book.

## The records

What's kept, said short, to look up rather than read back: the answer to
the midwife's "since when?", "how often?", "is it easing?", and the paper
asked for at triage. The lines are counts, days and weeks from what's kept
(`src/lib/records.ts`): never "normal", never a score, never advice; a
kind with nothing kept has no record.

- **An answer first.** Each record leads with one short answer, large
  enough to read across a room ("10 of the last 14 days", "None since week
  13", "Mostly good"), and the lines behind it under it, quietly. Four
  blocks of sentences of the same weight read as a report; nobody finds
  the answer in one at an appointment.
- **Now first.** Lately (anything in the last fortnight), then Earlier. At
  week 38, sickness from weeks 6 to 13 is history: it goes under Earlier.
- **The papers to hand at the top,** the handy ones, to open at triage;
  the latest three at the bottom, and every one from there.
- **Copy as text,** the whole page as plain lines, to paste into a message
  or the hospital's form: what's on the page, nothing more.
- **Each opens at full size:** its pattern, its tools, then every one
  (`?show=movements`, `appetite`, `feelings`, `papers`), with All records
  to go back.

The records:

- **Movements:** how many of the last fortnight's days; any felt as
  slower, weaker or stopped lately, first; the parts of the day they're
  kept in; the last; since when. The fortnight's pattern under it.
- **Sickness:** how many of the last fortnight's days, or none since when;
  each spell (a gap of more than a fortnight starts another), the week or
  weeks with the most, the days it threw up or couldn't keep fluids down.
- **Cravings:** the most craved (all of them, when they tie); then
  everything craved and gone off, most often first.
- **Feelings:** over four weeks, mostly what (yours, if you checked in);
  each of you who did, how many days and mostly what; your own words most
  used, which only your phone has.

While something has happened, the records keep to what stays, as the
story does: feelings and papers. Movements and sickness and cravings are
folded away, even asked for by address.

These were lenses on the story until 2026-11-02: tabs beside Notes and
Photos, under a diary, where the one thing wanted at an appointment was a
scroll and a tab away.

## What's kept, kind by kind

### Notes and letters

A note is anything, on its day and time, by whoever is holding the phone.
A note can be a letter to the baby, from the note form; letters are in the
story, the Notes lens and the recaps.

- **A letter is signed.** It's read one day as from one of you, or, once
  there are two of you, "From both of us": a letter written together
  isn't one person's.
- **No sealing, yet.** A letter neither reads until the birth needs the
  birth to open it, which is out of the first version.

### Movements

Australian advice is to know your baby's usual pattern, not to count kicks.
So:

- **One tap is enough:** the baby moved, now (changeable), a few words if
  you want. Either of you: partners feel kicks too.
- **A change is the careful part.** "Slower, weaker or stopped?" keeps the
  entry and shows the call: contact the midwife or birth unit straight
  away, not tomorrow, with the birth suite's number. The sheet doesn't
  close on its own while it shows.
- **It never reassures.** No counts, no tally, no target, no "a good day",
  no line marking what's usual. A day without one is a day nobody wrote one
  down.
- **The pattern:** the last two weeks as a row per day, each movement a dot
  at its time of day, so the baby's own rhythm shows (the evenings, after
  meals). One marked as a change gets a ring, never red.
- **One rhythm, the baby's.** Who felt it isn't on the line, and two kept
  within five minutes (both of you, for the same kick) are one dot and one
  time, ringed if either was a change. Each stays its own entry.
- **From week 16,** where the advice's weeks start, or earlier once one has
  been kept.

### Sickness and cravings

For 3 am on the bathroom floor, one hand, half asleep, often the partner
holding the phone; and for the midwife's "since when?" weeks later.

- **Sick or queasy, in one tap:** Queasy, Threw up, Can't keep fluids down,
  worst last. Each time is its own tap, so the days count themselves. No
  "how many times", no hours of nausea, no score (the clinic's PUQE score
  is the clinic's).
- **Can't keep fluids down shows the call:** your doctor, midwife or
  hospital now; the emergency department can give fluids through a drip.
- **A craving, or gone off something:** what, then Keep. What you've craved
  before sits under the box as chips, so the second pickle is one tap.
- **Craving something that isn't food** (ice, chalk, soil, soap, paper…) is
  kept like any other, with one quiet line: it's been linked to low iron,
  worth mentioning to your midwife or doctor, and "Add to your questions".
  Not an alert.
- **Sick often** (threw up on four of the last seven days): one quiet line,
  "there is treatment", and a question for the appointment written for you
  ("I've been sick on 5 of the last 7 days, since 8 Sep. What can help?").
  Never red.
- **Shown back:** since when and the last one; the weeks as rows of days,
  each day's worst as the accent at rising strength, which answers "how
  often?" and "getting better?" by looking, without the app saying
  "easing"; and the fun list, "Pickles · 14 times · from week 14", then
  what you've gone off.

### How you're feeling

- **It asks you; you don't go looking.** Once a day This week asks
  whoever is holding the phone "How are you today?"; one tap keeps it. It's
  also always in the Journal.
- **Five steps, as words with a face:** Rough, Low, Okay, Good, Great. No
  numbers anywhere (a "3/5" is a score), no red or green: a low day is
  quieter, not an alarm. The dots are a row a day, never a line chart (a
  line going down reads as a grade).
- **Words after the tap, only if you want them:** fourteen to pick from
  (tired, anxious, worried, overwhelmed, teary, irritable, flat, lonely,
  scared, calm, happy, excited, connected, grateful) and a box to say more.
  Anxiety is the common struggle in pregnancy and isn't low mood, so it's a
  word ("anxious, 8 days"), not a second scale.
- **Both of you.** Partners get perinatal depression too.
- **The step is shared, the words are yours.** The other sees your step
  (that's how you look out for each other); your words go only to your
  phone, so you can be honest. A courtesy, not security: with one password,
  anyone can say they're you. Only the person a feeling is about changes
  or deletes it.
- **Rough is never met with alarm:** one quiet line, someone to talk to
  (PANDA, and Lifeline if it's a crisis).
- **A hard fortnight** (at least four days checked in over fourteen, most
  Low or Rough, or "anxious" on most): This week shows the person it's
  about, and only them, a card suggesting their GP or midwife. Put away, it
  stays away for a fortnight. It never names a condition and never asks a
  screening questionnaire's questions.

### Photos and scans

- **The weekly bump photo:** same wall, same pose, usually the partner
  holding the phone, and the payoff is weeks later. In the camera roll they
  get lost; here they sit a week each, the first and the latest side by
  side, with a scrubber to drag (or play) through the weeks.
- **The scan printout,** photographed walking back to the car, because
  thermal paper fades and the printout creases. Hospital car parks have
  poor signal, so a photo is kept on the phone and goes up when it can:
  one taken through the app's camera isn't in the camera roll, so losing
  it offline would lose it for good.
- **A 3D or 4D scan** emailed by a private clinic: chosen from the phone's
  photos or dropped on the desktop page, several at once.
- **What is it?** Bump, Scan or Something else, one tap, nothing chosen
  until you choose (a scan filed as a bump spoils the row). On the day the
  camera says it was taken, so one chosen a week late lands right.
- **The original is kept untouched,** full size: it's the keepsake and the
  backup.
- **Deleting asks first,** with no undo: a photo is the one thing here that
  can't be typed again.

### Results and papers

Expecting keeps the papers and finds them. **It never reads them:** no
"normal", no ranges, no green ticks, no field for a value, nothing drawn
from what a result says. A field for a blood group or a GTT number would
be read as a verdict, and Expecting isn't the one to give it.

- **Who reaches for one:** the midwife asking for a blood group from week
  8; triage at 2 am wanting the birth plan; the first hospital visit with
  the GP's letter left at home; the discharge summary the GP wants at six
  weeks. Often it's the partner who carries the folder.
- **A paper is pages:** a photo a page (a letter is one thing, not four) or
  a PDF. What it is (a result, a letter or referral, a plan, a record or
  summary, something else), what it's about (linked to its Library entry,
  where it shows under "Your papers"), its name, and the date on it, which
  is rarely today.
- **Both of you see every paper,** said as you keep one: the private
  choice is not to keep it here.
- **Keep handy:** on top, on Emergency info ("Papers to hand"), and kept on
  each phone for when there's no signal. On by default for a plan, the
  blood group, the hospital referral and the discharge summary.
- **Found fast:** handy first, then by date; by kind; by search.
- **A PDF opens in the phone's own viewer,** which pinches and scrolls
  better than anything drawn in the page.
- **Having the paper isn't the admin done:** a GTT result doesn't tick
  "Book the glucose tolerance test".

### Firsts

Found out, the first craving, the first scan, heard the heartbeat, told the
family, started showing, the first kick, found out boy or girl (only if you
know), felt from the outside, the first outfit, the cot is up, the bag is
packed, it started. Each has the weeks it usually comes in and a question
("Where were you?"), answered by each of you in your own words. A first
has one day, the shared moment's, even when it came to each of you apart
("She told me that Sunday" is in the words); the ones that truly differ are
already two firsts (the first kick, felt from the outside). A task that
keeps a first (the cot, the bag) has a way to mark it beside its tick;
nothing is marked on its own.

## While something has happened

Nothing about what the pregnancy would have become, and nothing lost.

It's drawn as its own page, not the Journal with parts taken away: from the
same data, written as what it is, so a change to how one looks can't reach
into the other. They share only the story's parts.

- **Kept, as it was:** notes, letters, feelings, papers and what was done.
  Papers are admin, and after a loss there are more, not fewer: the section
  stays open and the "+" still offers one, but nothing is suggested as what
  it's about and nothing is handy by default.
- **Folded behind one quiet line,** "What you kept before", opened only
  when asked: movements, sickness and cravings, photos and firsts. Never
  deleted. Inside, one plain "Add a photo", offering Scan and Something
  else, never Bump: some parents want to keep a scan or a photo of their
  baby afterwards.
- **Gone:** no week counted, nothing about the baby, no book cover or
  contents, no places, no catching up, no recaps, no "weeks ago", no suggested firsts, no bump frame, no
  scrubber.
- **A Rough check-in** points to the loss support entries rather than PANDA
  alone. This week asks nothing.
- **With hard news,** whose pregnancy goes on, photos and papers are as
  usual.

## Not doing

- **Reading a paper:** no OCR, no values, no flags. Ever.
- **Reminders or streaks:** no reminder to check in, keep a bump photo or
  write. The empty frame is the only nudge, and only once there's a series.
- **Advice about her body:** no "try ginger", no what helped, no weight
  unless it's someday switched on.
- **Sharing out:** no public link for grandparents or the GP. Friends and
  doctors can't log in, and a public link is a hole in the gate.
- **Editing pictures:** crop and rotate happen in the phone's editor first.
- **A summary to show the midwife** ("sickness on 5 days, easing"): comes
  with appointments, which aren't built, and will read from what's kept.

## Not yet

- **A way from a timeline week to the Journal at it.** The week's line
  already opens This week; a second link inside it isn't possible, so it
  wants its own design.
- **Video** (4D scan clips), on the same store.
- **Adding a page to a kept paper,** or reordering pages: delete and keep
  it again, until it's missed.
- **What's behind a feeling** (sleep, work, money, us): a second row of
  words, if the first proves light enough.
- **A stronger lock for medical papers** than the shared password: still
  the hosting question in [`intent.md`](../intent.md).

## First guesses, to judge on a phone

Not from a source; change them if they feel wrong in use.

- The hard fortnight: four check-ins, most Low or Rough.
- Sick often: threw up on four days of seven.
- The fourteen feeling words (shorter is better than complete).
- Movements from week 16 (a second baby may be felt earlier; keeping one is
  harmless).
- "Threw up" or "Vomited" at 3 am; "Something else" for a photo; "Record or
  summary" for a discharge summary; "Keep handy" or "Bring along".
- Display copies at 2048 px and thumbnails at 480 px, on a real scan
  printout.
