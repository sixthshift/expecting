# It's time

The page for the night it starts. It's 2:40 am: her waters might have
broken, or the tightenings have been coming for an hour and they're
getting harder to talk through. Neither of you has slept, and everything
Expecting has taught you is spread across a dozen Library entries,
Emergency info and the bag. On the night you won't read any of it.

So It's time is one page: **what's happening, who to ring, what they'll
ask, what to grab, and how to get there**, in that order, with everything
Expecting already knows filled in. It doesn't teach; it's a set of
instructions, and it works with no signal.

How it's built is [`architecture.md`](../architecture.md), The pillars (It's
time).

## What it holds to

- **For the two of you, on the night,** holding one phone or both. Big
  targets, one hand, readable through tears and at arm's length in a car.
- **Directive about the admin, never about her body.** It says who to
  ring, what to tell them, what to take and where to park. It never
  decides whether this is labour or says "go now": the birth suite does
  that. The only times it tells you what to do are when the Library's
  sources say plainly "call now" or "call 000", and then it's a call, not a
  diagnosis, in the Library's words, never its own.
- **No verdict on the contractions.** The timer gives the numbers in the
  shape the midwife asks for (how often, how long, how many in ten
  minutes) and never says "it's time" or "5-1-1": there's no single rule,
  and your hospital's advice comes first, so what your hospital told you
  sits under the numbers. Strength is a word if you want one, never a
  scale.
- **Not a second Emergency info.** That's the handover, for a paramedic or
  a doctor. It's time reads from it and links to it, and never keeps the
  same field twice.
- **Not a lesson.** Each part can link to its entry for the why, but
  nothing on the page needs reading through.
- **The run-through matters more than the button.** Before a start, the
  page is the practice run: what the night still needs, each a link to
  where it's filled in, and the night's page greyed and filled in as far
  as it can be. Unlike the Journal it says what's missing, because this is
  admin.
- **Called It's time:** what people say on the night, short enough for a
  pill, and it covers the waters breaking before labour ("Labour"
  wouldn't). It isn't the birth plan.

## Where it shows

- **A labelled pill** beside the call button on a phone, a row in the
  sidebar on a desktop: on the night you shouldn't have to work out what an
  icon means.
- **From week 36,** where the Library starts on it and the bag is packed
  by; from week 28 it would turn into wallpaper. **From 32 for twins or
  more,** who come early more often. Also whenever a start is under way.
- **At any week from Emergency info** ("Labour started early?"). Before 37
  weeks it opens with one line: contractions or waters this early means
  calling the birth suite and going in.
- **On This week at 36** (32 for twins), once on each phone until it's
  opened: look through it together, so it's familiar on the night.
- **Never while something has happened.** It isn't drawn, and its address
  goes to Emergency info.

## The night

1. **Where it's at, and the calls.** Started when, the waters when and
   what colour, the week. The birth suite (or for a home birth, the
   midwife) and 000. If she's holding the phone and didn't start it, "Call
   Sam" comes first. If the partner's holding it, it reads to them: you
   ring, you drive, you time. She plans not to drive.
2. **Call now, don't wait, if:** the red flags from the Library's entries,
   open until folded. Not red: the danger colour is kept for a call your
   own tap set off. Waters breaking with a baby you've been told isn't
   head-first puts 000 first (cord prolapse). A planned caesarean and
   labour starting first is a call.
3. **Contractions:** one big button, tap at the start and the end. Either
   phone can tap, so swapping phones loses nothing; the screen stays on;
   undo a stray tap.
4. **The waters:** one tap keeps the time, the first thing they'll ask.
   Then the colour: green, brown or blood keeps the call at the top, the
   danger colour's one use. "Not sure if they've broken" rings the birth
   suite all the same.
5. **When you ring, they'll ask:** the phone call, already answered, in the
   order a midwife asks: name and record number, how many weeks, first
   baby, a caesarean before, twins, the waters, the contractions, and
   anything else she wrote in Emergency info. "Baby moving?" is "Ask her",
   never inferred from the Journal. Nothing is read from papers. The birth
   plan is one tap away, offline.
6. **Before you go:** ring ahead, the bag's last things (chargers, pillows,
   snacks), the car seat (needed to drive home), the papers, who's minding
   the kids and pets with their number, and your own lines, kept between
   starts. Ticked for this start only, not a second packing list. "We're
   leaving" keeps the time and moves getting there up.
7. **Getting there:** the hospital and a maps link, getting in (parking,
   the after-hours entrance, the floor: what everyone forgets), a backup
   driver and a taxi, and the 000 line for a baby coming on the way.
8. **The end, asked twice** so a stray tap doesn't end the night: we're
   there, or false alarm, we're home. A false alarm is common, and it's
   said kindly, once.

**A home birth** turns it round: the midwife first, no trip and no bag,
"before the midwife arrives", and the hospital kept as the backup for a
transfer. A birth centre is like a hospital here.

## The bag

The hospital bag is It's time's own list, not one of the Planner's: by
section (papers, for her, for the baby, for the support partner) and its
last things, ticked on the night. To pack, set aside, packed.

## What's asked

- **Where you're giving birth,** at 20 weeks, settled by then.
- **Whether you've been told the baby is head down,** at 36 weeks, when
  it's checked and the page appears: what you've been told, never what she
  feels. Asked again on "not sure" or "not head-first", as a baby can turn.
  Not asked with a planned caesarean.
- **In Emergency info:** the partner's number, getting in, what the
  hospital said about when to come in, a minder and a backup driver, each a
  name and a number.

## Kept

A start that ends "We're there" is one line in the Journal's story on its
day, the times only ("Waters broke 2:14 am (clear) · timing from 2:30 ·
left 3:05 · there 3:40"), opening its contractions, and suggests the first
"It started". False alarms stay in It's time, folded into the real night's
line once there is one. Nothing here is about the birth itself: the weeks
after the birth aren't built.
